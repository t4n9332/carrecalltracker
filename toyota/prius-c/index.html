Toyota Prius C DC-DC Converter May Cause Drive Loss medium

Reported Jun 26, 2019 · Campaign 19V491000 · Model years: 2018

Toyota is recalling certain Prius C vehicles because the DC-DC converter may fail. If it fails, the vehicle's smaller battery stops getting power from the converter, which can cause the car to lose the ability to drive. This means the vehicle could unexpectedly stop moving while you are driving it.

Toyota Prius C Missing Airbag Warning Label low

Reported Mar 15, 2018 · Campaign 18V176000 · Model years: 2018

Some Toyota Prius C vehicles sold in Puerto Rico may be missing a label that identifies the front passenger air bag and shows the recommended seating positions for children. Without this label attached to the vehicle, occupants may not have the information they need to know how to properly seat and restrain children, or to follow the correct guidance for that passenger seat.

Toyota Prius C recall questions

How many recalls does the Toyota Prius C have?

NHTSA lists 2 safety recalls covering the 2018 Toyota Prius C. 0 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Toyota Prius C DC-DC Converter May Cause Drive Loss" (NHTSA campaign 19V491000), reported Jun 26, 2019.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Toyota Prius C?

No. None of the 2 Toyota Prius C recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Toyota Prius C has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Toyota dealer, including for second owners.
