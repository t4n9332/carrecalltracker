Toyota Sienna Hybrid recalls

8 recalls covering model years 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Rollx Wheelchair Vans: Restraints May Not Lock Wheelchair high

Reported 2025-12-17 · Campaign 25V876000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

These are vans converted by Rollx for wheelchair use. The straps that hold a wheelchair use retractors, which work like a seat belt: they pull in slack and then lock so the chair cannot roll. On these vans, the QRT-Deluxe and QRT-Max retractors may fail to lock. If they do not lock, the wheelchair and the person in it can shift or slide while the van is moving, which raises the risk of injury. Rollx and the restraint maker will check the retractors and replace them if needed.

Wheelchair van restraints may not lock in Odyssey, Pacifica, Sienna high

Reported 2025-12-17 · Campaign 25V877000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025

These minivans were converted by Vantage Mobility so a wheelchair can ride inside. The wheelchair is held down by belt-style restraints with retractors that are supposed to lock, much like a seat belt locks. On these vans, the retractors may fail to lock, so the wheelchair is not really tied down. If it is not secured, the chair and the person in it can roll or tip while the van is moving, braking, or turning.

Toyota, Lexus and Subaru rearview camera may freeze medium

Reported 2025-10-30 · Campaign 25V744000 · Model years: 2025

Toyota is recalling certain 2022 through 2026 Toyota and Lexus vehicles and the Subaru Solterra that have the Panoramic View Monitor camera system. Models include RAV4, Highlander, Prius, Sienna Hybrid, Camry Hybrid, Mirai, bZ4X and the Lexus NX and TX Hybrid. A software error can cause the backup camera picture to freeze or go blank when the car is in reverse, so these vehicles do not meet the federal rear visibility standard.

Toyota Sienna Hybrid second-row seat rails welded badly high

Reported 2025-10-07 · Campaign 25V668000 · Model years: 2025

The rails that the second-row seats are mounted on may have been welded incorrectly at the factory. A bad weld can let the seat shift or come loose in a crash, so the seat may not hold the person sitting in it the way it should, and that passenger could be hurt worse. Dealers will replace the second-row seat rails free of charge. Toyota is handling this recall in phases, so parts may not be available for every owner right away.

Toyota Sienna Hybrid third-row seatback bolts may be loose medium

Reported 2025-02-13 · Campaign 25V086000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025

On these Sienna Hybrid minivans, the bolts holding the seatbacks of the third-row seats may not have been tightened enough at the factory. A loose seatback may not hold a person in place in a crash, and it also affects the head restraints and the anchors used for child car seats, so the vehicles do not meet federal safety rules. A Toyota dealer will tighten the bolts for free. The repair is just tightening the bolts already in the van, so no new parts are needed.

Toyota and Lexus front passenger airbag may not inflate high

Reported 2023-12-20 · Campaign 23V865000 · Model years: 2021

These Toyota and Lexus vehicles have a sensor in the front passenger seat that tells the airbag system whether someone is sitting there. A short circuit can develop inside that sensor. If it does, the front passenger airbag may not inflate during a crash, so whoever is riding in that seat could be hurt worse than they otherwise would be. The dealer will inspect the sensor and replace it if needed, free of charge. Owner letters are being mailed in stages.

Toyota and Lexus stability control may stay switched off high

Reported 2022-04-13 · Campaign 22V239000 · Model years: 2021, 2022

Toyota is recalling certain Toyota and Lexus vehicles because of a problem with the software that controls the skid control computer. If the driver turns off the vehicle stability control system, the software may fail to turn it back on the next time the vehicle is started. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal rule for electronic stability control systems. Dealers will update the software so the system switches back on as it should.

Toyota Sienna second-row seat belts may tear high

Reported 2021-11-17 · Campaign 21V889000 · Model years: 2022

Your 2022 Sienna was built with seating for eight, and the second-row seat belts on the outside seats may have the wrong webbing guide — a small part the belt strap slides through. In a crash, the belt tightens fast, and the wrong guide can damage the strap and tear it. A torn belt cannot hold you the way it is supposed to. Dealers will replace both outboard second-row seat belt assemblies at no cost.
