Toyota Corolla Hybrid recalls

4 recalls covering model years 2020, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Toyota Corolla steering shaft can crack and break apart critical

Reported 2024-11-21 · Campaign 24V878000 · Model years: 2024

The metal shaft that connects your steering wheel to the steering gear was made with cracks in it. Over time that shaft can break, and it may come apart from the steering column. If that happens while you are driving, the steering wheel stops turning the front wheels and you cannot steer the car. The cracks came from how the shaft was made at the factory. Your dealer will replace the whole shaft with a new one at no cost.

Toyota Corolla Hybrid may lose brake power assist high

Reported 2024-09-20 · Campaign 24V708000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

These hybrids use a computer to help boost the force you put on the brake pedal. A software error in that computer can cut off the power assist while you are turning a corner. When the assist drops out, the brakes take much more effort and your car needs a longer distance to come to a stop, which makes a crash more likely. Toyota dealers will load corrected software into that brake control computer at no charge to you.

Toyota Corolla and C-HR rear seat belts may not lock medium

Reported 2019-12-11 · Campaign 19V877000 · Model years: 2020

The rear seat belts in these cars have a locking part that is supposed to grab and hold the belt when it is pulled hard. On the affected cars that part may not lock the way it should. In a crash where the car is hit more than once, the belt may not hold the person in the seat properly, so a back seat passenger could be hurt worse. A dealer will check the build dates of the rear belts and replace them if needed.

Toyota and Lexus hybrids can lose braking assist high

Reported 2019-07-24 · Campaign 19V544000 · Model years: 2020

A pump that helps power the brakes can fail on these vehicles. If it does, the system that keeps the car from sliding or spinning in a turn stops working, and the brakes lose their power assist. The brake pedal will feel much harder to push and the car will take longer to stop. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for stability control. A dealer will check the brake booster pump and replace it if needed.
