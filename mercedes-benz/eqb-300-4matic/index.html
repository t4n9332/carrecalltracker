Mercedes EQB High Voltage Battery Can Catch Fire critical

Reported 2026-02-06 · Campaign 26V073000 · Model years: 2022

Certain Mercedes-Benz EQB vehicles have a high voltage battery that may fail internally. This kind of internal battery failure can lead to a vehicle fire, and this can happen whether the vehicle is parked or being driven at the time.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Mercedes-Benz EQB Electric Battery Fire Risk critical

Reported 2025-12-19 · Campaign 25V894000 · Model years: 2022

The high-voltage battery in some EQB 300 4MATIC and EQB 350 4MATIC vehicles can fail on the inside. This internal failure can lead to a fire while the vehicle is parked or being driven. Some vehicles that were already repaired under an earlier recall will need to be repaired again. Mercedes-Benz dealers will update the software that manages the battery, free of charge.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Mercedes-Benz EQB Loss Of Drive Power Risk critical

Reported 2025-07-25 · Campaign 25V487000 · Model years: 2022

Some Mercedes-Benz EQB 300 4MATIC and EQB 350 4MATIC electric SUVs have a bus bar in the high-voltage battery that may be secured with the wrong screws. This can cause the vehicle to suddenly lose drive power while you're driving, without any warning beforehand.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Mercedes EQB Battery Failure Can Cause Fire critical

Reported 2025-02-07 · Campaign 25V050000 · Model years: 2022

Certain Mercedes-Benz EQB electric vehicles have a high voltage battery that may fail internally. This kind of internal failure can lead to a vehicle fire, and this can happen whether the vehicle is parked or being driven.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Mercedes EQB 300, 350 Front Axle Gear Defect high

Reported 2024-05-10 · Campaign 24V331000 · Model years: 2022

The welded spur gear inside the front axle electric drivetrain on these vehicles may not have been welded correctly. A weld that was not done properly can leave the gear weaker than it should be. Over time or under stress, that weak weld can cause the gear itself to fail, affecting how the front axle drivetrain holds together and functions in the vehicle.
