Mercedes-Benz AMG G63 recalls

10 recalls covering model years 2021, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Mercedes G63 Radar Sensor May Disable Safety Features high

Reported 2025-10-17 · Campaign 25V711000 · Model years: 2025

Some AMG G63 and G580 SUVs have a front radar sensor with a circuit board that can fail. This sensor helps power several driver assistance features, including automatic emergency braking, active brake assist, lane keeping assist, and Distronic cruise control. If the sensor fails, these features may stop working. Without automatic emergency braking working as intended, the vehicle may not brake automatically to help avoid a crash, increasing the risk of a collision.

Mercedes-Benz G-Class Stability Warning Light May Be Wrong medium

Reported 2025-04-17 · Campaign 25V254000 · Model years: 2025

If the electronic stability program has a failure, the warning light on the dashboard may not display correctly. This means the light may not properly show that there is a problem with the stability control system.

Mercedes G63 and G550 Rear Door Latch Defect high

Reported 2024-09-09 · Campaign 24V659000 · Model years: 2021

Mercedes-Benz is recalling certain G 550 and G63 AMG vehicles that had a replacement right-side rear door latch installed during a service visit or an earlier recall. Parts inside that latch may keep the rear door from latching or opening the way it should. The door can look closed even when it isn't fully latched, and in some cases the latch may also keep the door from being opened from inside the vehicle.

Mercedes-AMG fuel pump may shut down, causing power loss high

Reported 2023-06-26 · Campaign 23V445000 · Model years: 2021

The fuel pump in these Mercedes-AMG models may shut down on its own while the vehicle is being driven. When the pump shuts down, the engine no longer gets fuel, and the vehicle loses the power that keeps it moving. Mercedes-Benz dealers will replace the fuel pump at no charge to the owner. Owner notification letters were mailed June 14, 2024, and owners can contact Mercedes-Benz customer service to schedule the free repair.

Mercedes G63 wiring damage may disable ABS, stability control high

Reported 2023-02-17 · Campaign 23V097000 · Model years: 2021

On some Mercedes-Benz G550 and AMG G63 vehicles, the front axle wiring harness may chafe against an oil cooler and become damaged over time. If this wiring is damaged, it can cause a loss of the vehicle's anti-lock braking system and electronic stability control system. When these systems stop working, the vehicle no longer has this protection, which can increase the risk of a crash occurring.

Mercedes-Benz vehicles may lose automatic emergency call system medium

Reported 2022-05-20 · Campaign 22V365000 · Model years: 2021

Mercedes-Benz is recalling a wide range of its cars and SUVs, including many AMG models. A software error on the SIM card inside the vehicle's communication module can cause the car to lose its mobile network connection. When that happens, the emergency call system is disabled. That is the system an occupant uses to reach an emergency services call center. The software will be updated either over the air or by a dealer, free of charge.

Mercedes-Benz AMG side-impact airbag sensors may loosen high

Reported 2021-07-12 · Campaign 21V527000 · Model years: 2021

This recall affects certain Mercedes-Benz AMG CLS53, AMG E53, and AMG G63 vehicles. The connectors for the side-impact crash sensors may come loose. If that happens, it could delay the airbags and seatbelts from activating the way they're supposed to during a side crash, which means they may not respond as quickly as needed when the vehicle is hit from the side.

Mercedes-Benz rear seat belts may not lock child seats medium

Reported 2021-06-25 · Campaign 21V483000 · Model years: 2021

Mercedes-Benz is recalling a number of 2021 and 2021-2022 models, including the G550, E350, E450, CLS450 and several AMG versions. The rear seat belts have a locking feature used to hold a child seat tightly in place. That lock may switch off too early, which can keep the child seat from being held securely. Because of that, these vehicles do not meet the federal rule on protecting people in a crash. Dealers will check the rear seat belts and replace them if needed.

Mercedes-Benz G-Class Rear Door Latch Defect high

Reported 2021-05-14 · Campaign 21V353000 · Model years: 2021

Some Mercedes-Benz G550 and G63 AMG vehicles have a problem with the rear door latches. A door may look and feel closed even though the latch has not fully engaged, so it could open on its own while the vehicle is being driven. In some vehicles, damaged latch parts can also make it hard to open a rear door from inside the car.

Mercedes-Benz seat switch may stop airbags working properly high

Reported 2021-04-01 · Campaign 21V230000 · Model years: 2021

Mercedes-Benz is recalling certain 2021 C-Class, E-Class, E-Class Coupe and Convertible, GLC, CLS, AMG GT 4-door Coupe and G-Class vehicles. A faulty electrical contact inside the seat position switch may keep the driver and passenger airbags and the seat belt force limiters from working properly during a crash. The notice points owners to the Mercedes-Benz recall report for the exact list of models covered. The fix is to replace the seat position switches.
