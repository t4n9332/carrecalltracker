Audi A5 recalls

11 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Audi backup camera screen may go blank in reverse medium

Reported 2022-10-05 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2021 and 2022 Audi cars and SUVs because of a software problem with the backup camera display. When the vehicle is put into reverse, the screen may go blank or black instead of showing the view behind the vehicle. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for rear visibility. Dealers will update the software in the infotainment unit, the system that runs the center screen.

Audi rear axle alignment may not have been checked medium

Reported 2022-01-26 · Campaign 22V034000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2019 through 2021 Audi cars and SUVs, including A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, Q5, Q7, Q8 and S and RS versions. These vehicles had repairs done under an earlier recall, and the rear axle alignment may not have been checked afterward as it should have been. Rear axle alignment that is off can wear the tires out early or wear them unevenly.

Audi passenger airbag may be switched off by faulty cable high

Reported 2021-11-10 · Campaign 21V874000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020

Audi is recalling certain 2017-2020 cars because of a fault in the cable that connects the passenger seat heater to the system that detects whether someone is sitting in the front passenger seat. A poor connection can make the software read a fault that is not there and switch off the passenger airbag. Dealers will replace the cable and either the seat heating mat or the whole seat cover. This recall expands an earlier recall, and vehicles already repaired under that one need to return for the new repair.

Audi and VW rear belts may not secure child seats high

Reported 2021-08-05 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen and Audi are recalling certain 2021 and 2022 cars and SUVs because of a problem with the rear seat belts. The part of the belt that locks it in place for a child seat may switch off too early, so the child seat may not be held securely. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal rule on protecting people in a crash. Dealers will check the middle rear seat belts and replace them if needed.

Audi Cars and SUVs: Rear Suspension Nut Can Break high

Reported 2021-04-26 · Campaign 21V295000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

On these Audi cars and SUVs, a lock nut holds part of the rear suspension — the trailing arm — to the rear axle. That nut can corrode under stress and break. If it breaks, the rear axle can shift out of alignment, and the car can become hard to keep straight in a way the driver may not be able to correct. That can lead to a loss of control and a crash. A dealer will replace the lock nuts, and the bolts with them if needed, at no cost.

Audi A5 and S5 rear light wiring may chafe medium

Reported 2020-11-25 · Campaign 20V737000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

The wires that feed the rear lights run near the spare tire well, and on these cars the protective covering that keeps them from rubbing may be missing. If the car is hit from behind, those unprotected wires can be damaged. When that happens, the doors may not open from the outside and the hazard lights may quit working, which can make it harder for help to reach you after a crash. A dealer adds the missing protection.

Audi A4, A5, S4, S5 passenger airbag may shut off high

Reported 2019-07-24 · Campaign 19V547000 · Model years: 2018

These Audis have a sensor system in the front passenger seat that tells the car whether someone is sitting there. The cable connected to that sensor can corrode. When it does, the car's software can wrongly decide something is broken and switch off the front passenger airbag. If a crash happens while the airbag is switched off, the person in that seat has less protection and can be hurt more badly. Dealers will add cable ties to the wiring so the cable is protected.

Audi A5 Sportback front wheel may come loose critical

Reported 2018-12-17 · Campaign 18V893000 · Model years: 2018

The bolts holding the front suspension together may not have been tightened enough when the vehicle was built. If they are too loose, a front wheel can work its way loose while you are driving and you could lose control. This affects certain 2018 A5 Sportback models. An Audi dealer will inspect the front suspension fasteners and replace any that are not right, at no cost. The recall began January 23, 2019.

Audi A5 and A4 Allroad passenger airbag screw loose medium

Reported 2018-11-27 · Campaign 18V836000 · Model years: 2018

On these Audis, a screw holding the front passenger airbag module in place may not have been tightened enough at the factory. If the screw is loose, the airbag may not deploy the way it is supposed to in a crash, so a front passenger might not get the protection the airbag is designed to give. Dealers will inspect that mounting screw and tighten it or replace it as needed, free of charge to the owner.

Audi A5 Coupe side curtain airbags may misfire high

Reported 2017-12-20 · Campaign 17V822000 · Model years: 2018

These cars have head curtain airbags, the ones that drop down along the side windows to protect your head and help keep you inside the car in a side crash or rollover. On the affected cars, the curtain may not unfold the way it is supposed to. You would not notice anything during normal driving, because the problem only shows up in a crash. Audi dealers will modify the curtain airbags so they open correctly, free.

Audi A4, A5 door speaker trim leaves sharp edges low

Reported 2017-10-25 · Campaign 17V675000 · Model years: 2018

The aluminum trim around the door speakers on some 2017-2018 Audi A4, A4 allroad, and A5 Sportback models can come loose. Once a piece lifts away from the door panel, it leaves a sharp metal edge where people put their hands, reach for the door, or brush past while getting in and out. That edge can cut someone. Nothing about how the car drives, steers, or stops is affected. Dealers replace the trim free.
