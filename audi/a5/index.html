Audi backup camera screen may go blank in reverse medium

Reported Oct 5, 2022 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2021 and 2022 Audi cars and SUVs because of a software problem with the backup camera display. When the vehicle is put into reverse, the screen may go blank or black instead of showing the view behind the vehicle. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for rear visibility. Dealers will update the software in the infotainment unit, the system that runs the center screen.

Audi rear axle alignment may not have been checked medium

Reported Jan 26, 2022 · Campaign 22V034000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2019 through 2021 Audi cars and SUVs, including A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, Q5, Q7, Q8 and S and RS versions. These vehicles had repairs done under an earlier recall, and the rear axle alignment may not have been checked afterward as it should have been. Rear axle alignment that is off can wear the tires out early or wear them unevenly.

Audi passenger airbag may be switched off by faulty cable high

Reported Nov 10, 2021 · Campaign 21V874000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020

Audi is recalling certain 2017-2020 cars because of a fault in the cable that connects the passenger seat heater to the system that detects whether someone is sitting in the front passenger seat. A poor connection can make the software read a fault that is not there and switch off the passenger airbag. Dealers will replace the cable and either the seat heating mat or the whole seat cover. This recall expands an earlier recall, and vehicles already repaired under that one need to return for the new repair.

Audi and VW rear belts may not secure child seats high

Reported Aug 5, 2021 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen and Audi are recalling certain 2021 and 2022 cars and SUVs because of a problem with the rear seat belts. The part of the belt that locks it in place for a child seat may switch off too early, so the child seat may not be held securely. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal rule on protecting people in a crash. Dealers will check the middle rear seat belts and replace them if needed.

Audi rear axle lock nut may break high

Reported Apr 26, 2021 · Campaign 21V295000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

On certain Audi models, the lock nut on a rear axle trailing arm may break because of corrosion. If this happens, the rear axle can become misaligned, affecting how the vehicle handles.

Audi A5, S5 Rear Wiring Lacks Scuff Protection high

Reported Nov 25, 2020 · Campaign 20V737000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

On some A5 and S5 Sportback vehicles, the wiring for the rear lights may be missing a protective covering near the spare tire area. Without that covering, the wires could be damaged in a rear-end crash. If that happens, the doors might not open from the outside, and the hazard lights could stop working. Dealers will add the missing protection at no cost.

Audi Passenger Air Bag May Be Disabled high

Reported Jul 24, 2019 · Campaign 19V547000 · Model years: 2018

Corrosion (oxidation) can build up on the cable connecting to the Passenger Occupant Detection System. When this happens, the system's software may wrongly decide there is a malfunction, and in response it disables the passenger air bag. With the air bag disabled, it will not deploy the way it's supposed to if the vehicle is in a crash.

Audi A5 Front Suspension Bolts May Be Loose high

Reported Dec 17, 2018 · Campaign 18V893000 · Model years: 2018

Some A5 Sportback vehicles may have front suspension fasteners that were not tightened enough when the vehicle was built. Over time, this could cause a front wheel to become loose. A loose front wheel can affect how the vehicle handles and increases the risk of a crash.

Audi A4 Allroad, A5 Passenger Airbag May Not Deploy high

Reported Nov 27, 2018 · Campaign 18V836000 · Model years: 2018

The screw that holds the passenger air bag module in place may not have been tightened enough at the factory. Because of this loose screw, the air bag may not deploy the way it is designed to if the vehicle is in a crash. An air bag that does not deploy correctly when it is needed cannot protect the passenger the way it should.

Audi A5: Side Curtain Airbags May Not Deploy Properly high

Reported Dec 20, 2017 · Campaign 17V822000 · Model years: 2018

Some 2018 Audi A5 Coupe vehicles were built with head curtain airbags that may not unfold properly when needed. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet a federal safety standard covering occupant ejection protection in a crash. Dealers will modify the head curtain airbags so they deploy properly.

Audi A4 and A5 door trim can expose sharp edges low

Reported Oct 25, 2017 · Campaign 17V675000 · Model years: 2018

Volkswagen is recalling certain Audi A4 sedan, A4 allroad and A5 Sportback cars. The aluminum trim on the front and rear doors can come loose from around the door speakers. Once the trim is loose, it leaves sharp edges exposed on the inside of the doors. Audi will notify owners, and a dealer will replace the aluminum trim that covers the door speakers at no cost to the owner.

Audi A5 recall questions

How many recalls does the Audi A5 have?

NHTSA lists 11 safety recalls covering the 2018–2021 Audi A5. 8 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Audi backup camera screen may go blank in reverse" (NHTSA campaign 22V742000), reported Oct 5, 2022.

Which Audi A5 model year has the most recalls?

The 2018 Audi A5 has the most, with 6 recalls. The 2019 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 3. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Audi A5?

No. None of the 11 Audi A5 recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Audi A5 has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Audi dealer, including for second owners.
