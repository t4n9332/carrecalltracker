Audi A6 recalls

16 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Audi A6, A7 and Q8 lane departure warning shuts off low

Reported 2026-03-04 · Campaign 26V125000 · Model years: 2019

These vehicles were already repaired once under an earlier recall, but that work left a software bug behind. Because of it, the lane departure warning — the feature that alerts you when you drift out of your lane — can switch itself off. There is no clear sign that it has turned off, so you could be counting on an alert that never comes. Everything else about the car works normally; only this warning feature is affected.

Audi rearview camera image may fail to display medium

Reported 2025-12-19 · Campaign 25V900000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023

A software problem can keep the backup camera picture from showing up on the dashboard screen when you shift into reverse. Without that image, you cannot see what is directly behind the car — a person or an object — so you are more likely to hit something while backing up. Audi says the vehicles do not meet the federal rear visibility rule. The dealer will update the software for free.

Audi Sedans and SUVs May Lose Dashboard Display medium

Reported 2025-03-31 · Campaign 25V201000 · Model years: 2021

A software error can make the main dashboard display go blank. That screen is where your speedometer, fuel gauge and warning lights live, so if it quits you will not know how fast you are going or whether the car is trying to warn you about something else. A dealer reloads the instrument panel software at no charge, and no parts are needed. Audi mailed owner letters on April 24, 2025, so the fix is available now.

Audi A6 and A7 may have mislabeled brake fluid cap medium

Reported 2023-08-25 · Campaign 23V601000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Some of these cars left the factory with the wrong label on the brake fluid reservoir cap. That cap tells whoever tops up the brakes which fluid to use, so a wrong label can lead a repair shop — or you — to pour in the wrong type. The wrong brake fluid can weaken how well the car stops. A dealer will look at the cap and replace it free of charge if it is the wrong one. Letters went out in October 2023.

Audi A6 and A7 lose engine power from spills high

Reported 2022-11-21 · Campaign 22V861000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

If someone spills a drink in the back seat of these Audis, the liquid can soak through and reach an electronics box underneath called the gateway control module. Liquid on that module can make it shut down, and when it does the engine suddenly loses power while you are driving — a real problem if you are merging or passing. Dealers will fit a protective cover over the module so spills cannot get to it.

Audi backup camera may show blank black screen medium

Reported 2022-10-05 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

A software problem in the infotainment system can leave the screen blank or black when you shift into reverse, instead of showing the view from the backup camera. With no picture, you cannot see a person, a child, or an object directly behind the car while you are backing up, so it is much easier to hit something you never saw. The dealer fixes it by updating the infotainment software; nothing needs to be replaced.

Audi fuel gauge may show wrong fuel level medium

Reported 2022-03-14 · Campaign 22V155000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Certain Audi A6, A7, S6, S7, RS6 Avant, RS7 and A6 Allroad vehicles have a sensor in the fuel tank that can stick. When it sticks, the fuel gauge on your dashboard shows the wrong amount of fuel, so you may believe you have more gas than you really do. You could then run out of fuel unexpectedly and have the engine shut off while driving. Dealers will install a new sensor in the tank free of charge.

Audi rear axle alignment may cause uneven tire wear medium

Reported 2022-01-26 · Campaign 22V034000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

These Audis were part of an earlier recall (21V-295) that involved work on the rear axle. Afterward, the shop was supposed to check that the rear wheels were still lined up correctly, and on some cars that check was skipped. If the rear axle is out of alignment, the tires can wear out early or wear unevenly, which increases the chance of a crash. Dealers will inspect the rear axle, adjust the alignment if needed, and replace any badly worn tires at no charge.

Audi and Volkswagen rear belt may not hold child seat high

Reported 2021-08-05 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

In these Audi and Volkswagen models, the rear seat belts have a locking feature you use to hold a child safety seat tightly in place. On these vehicles that lock can switch off too early, so the belt no longer keeps the child seat secure. A child seat that can shift or move means a child is less protected in a crash. Federal occupant protection rules are not met. Dealers will inspect the middle rear seat belts and replace them free if needed.

Audi Cars and SUVs: Rear Suspension Nut Can Break high

Reported 2021-04-26 · Campaign 21V295000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

On these Audi cars and SUVs, a lock nut holds part of the rear suspension — the trailing arm — to the rear axle. That nut can corrode under stress and break. If it breaks, the rear axle can shift out of alignment, and the car can become hard to keep straight in a way the driver may not be able to correct. That can lead to a loss of control and a crash. A dealer will replace the lock nuts, and the bolts with them if needed, at no cost.

Audi A6 and A7 passenger airbag may not deploy right high

Reported 2021-03-10 · Campaign 21V159000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

The passenger airbag has a built-in seam, a thin line in the cover that is supposed to split open cleanly when the bag inflates. On these Audis that seam may have been made wrong. In a crash the airbag might not open the way it should, and it could throw bits of plastic into the cabin at the person sitting there. A dealer will look at the airbag and fix it if needed.

Audi A6 and Q7 door sensors may delay airbags high

Reported 2020-12-02 · Campaign 20V749000 · Model years: 2021

Each front door on these Audis has a sensor that tells the vehicle it has been hit from the side. On some vehicles, the electrical plug can come loose from that sensor. If it does, the side airbags, the curtain airbags above the windows, and the seat belt tighteners may go off late in a side crash, so they protect the people inside less than they should. A dealer will check both front door sensors and replace them if needed, free.

Audi A6 and A7 passenger airbag may not open properly high

Reported 2020-05-13 · Campaign 20V274000 · Model years: 2020

The front passenger airbag is packed inside the dashboard and is meant to burst out through a hidden seam when it inflates. On these vehicles, that seam may not tear open the way it should. In a crash, the airbag may not open and fill correctly, so it may not protect the passenger as designed. Small pieces of plastic from the dashboard could also be thrown into the cabin. Dealers replace the whole dashboard panel at no cost.

Audi A6 starter generator can short out and catch fire critical

Reported 2020-03-19 · Campaign 20V153000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

Some 2019-2020 Audi A6 models with the mild-hybrid system and the 2.0-liter turbo engine have a starter generator — the part that starts the engine and helps power the car — that can let moisture get inside. If water reaches the electrical parts, they can short out. A short circuit there can overheat and start a fire. Volkswagen's fix is to replace the whole starter generator with a new one, and you pay nothing for the part or the labor.

Audi Q7, A6 and A7 fuel rails can leak gasoline critical

Reported 2019-01-16 · Campaign 19V035000 · Model years: 2018

The fuel rails are the metal tubes that carry gasoline under pressure to the engine. On these Audis, the left and right fuel rails can leak. Gasoline escaping near anything hot or sparking can start a fire. Audi has already notified owners, and dealers will replace both the left and the right fuel rail at no cost to you. This recall began in February 2019, so the free repair has been available for some time.

Audi A6, A7 and S6 passenger airbag may not deploy high

Reported 2018-06-04 · Campaign 18V370000 · Model years: 2018

A pressure-sensing mat inside the front passenger seat tells the car whether someone is sitting there and how large they are, so the airbag knows how to react in a crash. Over time that mat can wear out or be stressed, and the control unit that reads it can malfunction. If that happens, the front passenger airbag may not go off the way it should in a crash, leaving whoever is in that seat less protected.
