Audi Q8 recalls

14 recalls covering model years 2019, 2020, 2021, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Audi A6, A7 and Q8 lane departure warning shuts off low

Reported 2026-03-04 · Campaign 26V125000 · Model years: 2019

These vehicles were already repaired once under an earlier recall, but that work left a software bug behind. Because of it, the lane departure warning — the feature that alerts you when you drift out of your lane — can switch itself off. There is no clear sign that it has turned off, so you could be counting on an alert that never comes. Everything else about the car works normally; only this warning feature is affected.

Audi rearview camera image may fail to display medium

Reported 2025-12-19 · Campaign 25V900000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021, 2024, 2025

A software problem can keep the backup camera picture from showing up on the dashboard screen when you shift into reverse. Without that image, you cannot see what is directly behind the car — a person or an object — so you are more likely to hit something while backing up. Audi says the vehicles do not meet the federal rear visibility rule. The dealer will update the software for free.

Audi Q7 and Q8 seat belt buckles missing rivet high

Reported 2025-11-12 · Campaign 25V777000 · Model years: 2025

The buckles for the driver's and front passenger's seat belts may be missing a rivet — the small metal pin that fastens the buckle to the short strap anchoring it to the seat. Without that rivet, the buckle may not hold as designed in a crash, so the belt might not keep you properly in your seat and you could be hurt more badly. Dealers will replace both front seat belt buckles at no cost to you.

Audi Sedans and SUVs May Lose Dashboard Display medium

Reported 2025-03-31 · Campaign 25V201000 · Model years: 2021

A software error can make the main dashboard display go blank. That screen is where your speedometer, fuel gauge and warning lights live, so if it quits you will not know how fast you are going or whether the car is trying to warn you about something else. A dealer reloads the instrument panel software at no charge, and no parts are needed. Audi mailed owner letters on April 24, 2025, so the fix is available now.

Audi front passenger airbag may switch itself off high

Reported 2024-04-03 · Campaign 24V251000 · Model years: 2024

An electrical connection inside the front passenger seat can work loose. That connection belongs to the system that senses whether someone is sitting in the seat, and if it comes loose the front passenger airbag is switched off. In a crash, that airbag would not inflate to protect the person riding up front, so injuries could be worse. The dealer will replace the seat's occupant detection control unit free of charge.

Audi Q7 and Q8 driver seat airbag may be loose high

Reported 2023-12-20 · Campaign 23V868000 · Model years: 2024

There is a side airbag built into the driver's seat that is supposed to inflate between you and the door in a side crash. On these vehicles it may not have been mounted to the seat frame correctly at the factory, so the car does not meet the federal side-impact protection rule. If the airbag is not held in place properly, it may not hold you the way it should in a crash, and you could be injured more badly.

Audi backup camera may show blank black screen medium

Reported 2022-10-05 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

A software problem in the infotainment system can leave the screen blank or black when you shift into reverse, instead of showing the view from the backup camera. With no picture, you cannot see a person, a child, or an object directly behind the car while you are backing up, so it is much easier to hit something you never saw. The dealer fixes it by updating the infotainment software; nothing needs to be replaced.

Audi Q7 and Q8 fuel pump can fail high

Reported 2022-07-20 · Campaign 22V516000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

A part inside the fuel pump can break, which can make the pump stop working. The fuel pump is what feeds gasoline to the engine, so if it fails the engine can shut off while you are driving. That can happen without warning, in traffic or at highway speed. This covers certain 2019-2021 Audi Q8 and 2020-2021 Audi Q7 vehicles. Dealers will replace the fuel pump at no cost to you.

Audi rear axle alignment may cause uneven tire wear medium

Reported 2022-01-26 · Campaign 22V034000 · Model years: 2021

These Audis were part of an earlier recall (21V-295) that involved work on the rear axle. Afterward, the shop was supposed to check that the rear wheels were still lined up correctly, and on some cars that check was skipped. If the rear axle is out of alignment, the tires can wear out early or wear unevenly, which increases the chance of a crash. Dealers will inspect the rear axle, adjust the alignment if needed, and replace any badly worn tires at no charge.

Audi and Volkswagen rear belt may not hold child seat high

Reported 2021-08-05 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

In these Audi and Volkswagen models, the rear seat belts have a locking feature you use to hold a child safety seat tightly in place. On these vehicles that lock can switch off too early, so the belt no longer keeps the child seat secure. A child seat that can shift or move means a child is less protected in a crash. Federal occupant protection rules are not met. Dealers will inspect the middle rear seat belts and replace them free if needed.

Audi Cars and SUVs: Rear Suspension Nut Can Break high

Reported 2021-04-26 · Campaign 21V295000 · Model years: 2021

On these Audi cars and SUVs, a lock nut holds part of the rear suspension — the trailing arm — to the rear axle. That nut can corrode under stress and break. If it breaks, the rear axle can shift out of alignment, and the car can become hard to keep straight in a way the driver may not be able to correct. That can lead to a loss of control and a crash. A dealer will replace the lock nuts, and the bolts with them if needed, at no cost.

Audi Q8 transmission fluid leak can make roads slippery high

Reported 2020-05-13 · Campaign 20V272000 · Model years: 2020

A pipe that carries your automatic transmission's fluid may have a weak weld where it was joined together. That weak spot can let transmission fluid leak out of the vehicle. If the fluid drips onto the pavement while you are driving, it leaves a slick patch on the road behind you, which can cause a vehicle to lose grip. A dealer will look at the pipe and fit a new one if it is faulty.

Audi Q7 and Q8 may suddenly lose steering control critical

Reported 2020-03-16 · Campaign 20V149000 · Model years: 2019

The bolt that connects the steering shaft to the steering gear can work itself loose. That shaft is what carries your turning of the steering wheel down to the front wheels, so if the bolt loosens you can lose the ability to steer the vehicle. Audi dealers will inspect that bolt and replace it if necessary, at no cost to you. The recall began in March 2020, so the free repair has been available for a while.

Audi Q7, Q8 and S5 front shock parts can crack high

Reported 2019-02-20 · Campaign 19V114000 · Model years: 2019

The forks that hold the front shock absorbers in place can develop cracks. A shock absorber is the part that soaks up bumps and keeps the wheel settled against the road. If a fork cracks, the shock it holds can work itself loose. A loose shock on the front of the car changes how the front wheels behave, so the steering can feel wrong or stop responding the way you expect it to.
