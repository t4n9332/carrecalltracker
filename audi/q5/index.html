Audi Q5, SQ5 Seat Belts May Not Restrain high

Reported Apr 24, 2026 · Campaign 26V266000 · Model years: 2025

Some Audi Q5, Q5 Sportback, SQ5, and SQ5 Sportback vehicles may have driver and front passenger seat belts that were manufactured incorrectly. Because of this manufacturing problem, the affected seat belts may not properly hold the driver or front passenger in place during a crash. This means the seat belts may not restrain these occupants as they are designed to.

Audi Q5 Cylinder Head Cover Screws Can Loosen critical

Reported May 7, 2025 · Campaign 25V294000 · Model years: 2023, 2024

The screws that hold the cylinder head cover in place on these SUVs may not have been tightened correctly at the factory. Over time, those screws can work themselves loose. Once they loosen, engine oil can start to leak out from under the cover. An oil leak like this raises the chance that a fire could start.

Audi Q5 High-Voltage Battery May Overheat critical

Reported Feb 12, 2025 · Campaign 25V080000 · Model years: 2021, 2023, 2024

In these Audi Q5 and A7 E Hybrid vehicles, the high-voltage battery that powers the car can overheat. An overheating high-voltage battery like this creates a risk of fire, according to the notice.

Audi Q5 Hybrid Battery May Overheat And Catch Fire critical

Reported Nov 27, 2024 · Campaign 24V898000 · Model years: 2023

Certain Audi Q5 and A7 plug-in hybrid vehicles have a high-voltage battery that may overheat. An overheating high-voltage battery increases the risk of a fire starting in the vehicle. This affects the battery that powers the vehicle's electric drive system.

Audi SUVs and A4 Allroad Risk Passenger Airbag Shutoff high

Reported Apr 3, 2024 · Campaign 24V251000 · Model years: 2023, 2024

An electrical connection in the front passenger seat for the occupant detection system may loosen. If it loosens, the system can deactivate the front passenger air bag, meaning the air bag would not be active for that seat.

Audi portable charging cables can overheat and cause fire critical

Reported Dec 13, 2023 · Campaign 23V842000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024

Audi is recalling certain electric and other models over the portable charging system supplied with the vehicle. When the industrial 220-volt/240-volt plug is used at the 100% charge setting, some home outlets cannot handle the electrical current the charging system needs. The outlet or the charging cable can then overheat. Dealers will supply a new 220-volt/240-volt portable charging cable with a temperature sensor built in, free of charge.

Audi Q5, VW Atlas Engine Bearings May Fail critical

Reported Oct 7, 2022 · Campaign 22V753000 · Model years: 2021

Some Audi Q5 and Volkswagen Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport vehicles may have damaged connecting rod bearings inside the engine. Damaged bearings can cause the engine to fail while driving. If the engine failure also causes an oil leak, that leak can raise the chance of a fire.

Audi backup camera screen may go blank in reverse medium

Reported Oct 5, 2022 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2021 and 2022 Audi cars and SUVs because of a software problem with the backup camera display. When the vehicle is put into reverse, the screen may go blank or black instead of showing the view behind the vehicle. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for rear visibility. Dealers will update the software in the infotainment unit, the system that runs the center screen.

Audi rear axle alignment may not have been checked medium

Reported Jan 26, 2022 · Campaign 22V034000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2019 through 2021 Audi cars and SUVs, including A4, A5, A6, A7, A8, Q5, Q7, Q8 and S and RS versions. These vehicles had repairs done under an earlier recall, and the rear axle alignment may not have been checked afterward as it should have been. Rear axle alignment that is off can wear the tires out early or wear them unevenly.

Audi Q5 and SQ5 water damage may cut power high

Reported Dec 8, 2021 · Campaign 21V947000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021

This recall covers certain Audi Q5 Sportback, SQ5 Sportback, Q5, and SQ5 vehicles. A liquid spill in the rear seat, or water getting in through a gap in the underbody seam, may reach the gateway control module and cause it to shut down.

Audi and VW rear belts may not secure child seats high

Reported Aug 5, 2021 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen and Audi are recalling certain 2021 and 2022 cars and SUVs because of a problem with the rear seat belts. The part of the belt that locks it in place for a child seat may switch off too early, so the child seat may not be held securely. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal rule on protecting people in a crash. Dealers will check the middle rear seat belts and replace them if needed.

Audi rear axle lock nut may break high

Reported Apr 26, 2021 · Campaign 21V295000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

On certain Audi models, the lock nut on a rear axle trailing arm may break because of corrosion. If this happens, the rear axle can become misaligned, affecting how the vehicle handles.

Audi, VW Front Seat Frame Weld Defect medium

Reported Aug 26, 2020 · Campaign 20V505000 · Model years: 2020

Volkswagen is recalling certain Audi Q5 and SQ5 SUVs, and Volkswagen Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport, Jetta, Jetta GLI, and Tiguan vehicles. Parts of the front seat frame and the backrest adjuster may not have been welded together correctly during manufacturing, meaning the weld holding these seat parts together may not be as strong as it's supposed to be.

Audi Q5 and SQ5 rear wheel arch trim can detach medium

Reported Oct 16, 2019 · Campaign 19V737000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020

Audi is recalling certain Q5 and SQ5 vehicles. The trim cover over the rear wheel arch, the curved panel above the back wheel, can work loose and fall off the vehicle while it is on the road. Dealers will replace the four clips that hold the front part of that rear wheel arch trim in place, and the work is free for owners. Audi is contacting owners about the repair.

Audi Q5 and SQ5 Passenger Air Bag Damage Risk high

Reported Jun 19, 2019 · Campaign 19V473000 · Model years: 2019

Volkswagen is recalling certain Audi Q5 and SQ5 vehicles. In a crash where the passenger frontal air bag deploys, the instrument panel carrier inside the dashboard may break because it was not welded correctly. If that carrier breaks, it can damage the passenger frontal air bag itself, so the air bag may not work the way it is supposed to when it is needed most.

Audi Q5 and SQ5 Wheel Arch Trim May Detach low

Reported Jun 5, 2019 · Campaign 19V416000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

On some Q5 and SQ5 SUVs, the trim covering the wheel arch may loosen and come off the vehicle.

Audi Q5 and SQ5 Brake Master Cylinder Defect critical

Reported Apr 8, 2019 · Campaign 19V277000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

On some Q5 and SQ5 SUVs, the casting used to make the brake master cylinder may be too short. The electronic parking brake and its emergency brake function are separate and are not affected.

VW and Audi Brake Calipers May Reduce Braking critical

Reported Jun 1, 2018 · Campaign 18V369000 · Model years: 2018

Certain 2018 Volkswagen Atlas, Passat, Beetle, Beetle Convertible, Golf, Golf SportWagen, GTI, and Audi Q5 and SQ5 vehicles may have brake caliper pistons without enough protective coating. This lack of coating can reduce how well the brakes work.

⚠ NHTSA advises: do not drive this vehicle until repaired.

Audi Q5 Seat Belt Pretensioner May Not Activate high

Reported Dec 29, 2016 · Campaign 16V955000 · Model years: 2018

Some 2018 Audi Q5 vehicles are part of a wider Volkswagen/Audi recall. For the Q5, the issue is with the seat belt pretensioner, the part that pulls the seat belt tight in a crash. In the affected vehicles, this pretensioner may not activate properly when needed.

Audi Q5 recall questions

How many recalls does the Audi Q5 have?

NHTSA lists 19 safety recalls covering the 2018–2025 Audi Q5. 14 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Audi Q5, SQ5 Seat Belts May Not Restrain" (NHTSA campaign 26V266000), reported Apr 24, 2026.

Which Audi Q5 model year has the most recalls?

The 2021 Audi Q5 has the most, with 8 recalls. The 2025 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 1. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Audi Q5?

Yes. 1 Audi Q5 recall carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA: VW and Audi Brake Calipers May Reduce Braking (18V369000). These apply only to the affected vehicles, so check your VIN.

How do I check whether my Audi Q5 has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Audi dealer, including for second owners.
