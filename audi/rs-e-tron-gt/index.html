Audi RS E-TRON GT recalls

5 recalls covering model years 2024. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Audi rearview camera may fail to show image medium

Reported 2025-12-19 · Campaign 25V900000 · Model years: 2024

Audi is recalling certain 2019 through 2026 vehicles, including A6, A7, Q7, Q8 and e-tron models. A software error can keep the backup camera picture from showing on the screen the way it is supposed to. Because the rear camera view may not appear, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule covering what a driver must be able to see behind the vehicle.

Audi e-tron GT battery can short circuit and burn critical

Reported 2024-09-30 · Campaign 24V726000 · Model years: 2024

On certain 2022-2024 e-tron GT and RS e-tron GT models, a section inside the large battery that powers the car can short out, which can cause a fire. The permanent fix is new monitoring software a dealer installs at no charge. Until that is available, Audi either watches your battery data remotely or has a dealer inspect it and replace battery sections if needed. Cars already repaired under the earlier recall still need this new repair.

Audi e-tron GT front brake hoses can tear and leak high

Reported 2024-06-20 · Campaign 24V465000 · Model years: 2024

The brake hoses at the front wheels of some 2022 through 2024 Audi e-tron GT and RS e-tron GT cars can develop tears. A torn hose leaks brake fluid, and that knocks out part of the braking system. You may find the brake pedal sinking farther than usual and the car taking much longer to stop. Dealers will replace the front brake hoses free of charge. Owner letters were mailed in August 2024.

Audi e-tron GT battery can short circuit and burn critical

Reported 2024-03-22 · Campaign 24V228000 · Model years: 2024

On some 2022-2024 Audi e-tron GT and RS e-tron GT electric cars, the big battery that powers the car can short circuit inside one of its modules, and that can start a fire. Until the car is repaired, Audi says to charge it only up to 80 percent. At the dealer, technicians read the battery's data and replace any battery modules that need it, free of charge.

Audi electric models: portable charging cable can overheat critical

Reported 2023-12-13 · Campaign 23V842000 · Model years: 2024

These Audis came with a portable charging cable that plugs into a 220/240-volt outlet, the kind used for a large appliance. At the 100% charge setting, the cable can draw more electricity than some home outlets can handle, and the outlet or the cable can get dangerously hot. Overheating like this can start a fire. Audi says to stop using that cable for now and charge with the 110-volt cable or at a public charging station until you receive a free replacement.
