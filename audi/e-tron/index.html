Audi E-TRON recalls

11 recalls covering model years 2019, 2020, 2021. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Audi rearview camera may fail to show image medium

Reported 2025-12-19 · Campaign 25V900000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Audi is recalling certain 2019 through 2026 vehicles, including A6, A7, Q7, Q8 and e-tron models. A software error can keep the backup camera picture from showing on the screen the way it is supposed to. Because the rear camera view may not appear, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule covering what a driver must be able to see behind the vehicle.

Audi e-tron brake pedal can detach from booster critical

Reported 2024-08-19 · Campaign 24V621000 · Model years: 2019

The fastener that joins the brake pedal to the brake booster, the part that passes your foot pressure on to the brakes, may have been put together wrong at the factory. If it lets go, the pedal comes away from the booster and you can lose braking. That can happen suddenly while you are driving. A dealer will inspect the brake booster and repair it at no cost. Audi mailed owner letters in October and November 2024.

Audi e-tron battery modules can overheat and catch fire critical

Reported 2023-12-20 · Campaign 23V867000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Some of the modules inside the large high-voltage battery pack can get too hot. A battery that overheats can catch fire. Audi's fix is to install onboard software that keeps watch on how each battery module is performing so a problem can be caught, and to replace any modules that turn out to be affected. Owner letters went out in April 2025, so the repair is available now at no cost to you.

Audi electric models: portable charging cable can overheat critical

Reported 2023-12-13 · Campaign 23V842000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

These Audis came with a portable charging cable that plugs into a 220/240-volt outlet, the kind used for a large appliance. At the 100% charge setting, the cable can draw more electricity than some home outlets can handle, and the outlet or the cable can get dangerously hot. Overheating like this can start a fire. Audi says to stop using that cable for now and charge with the 110-volt cable or at a public charging station until you receive a free replacement.

Audi A6 Allroad and E-Tron backup camera blank medium

Reported 2022-10-26 · Campaign 22V806000 · Model years: 2021

The main unit that runs the screen in the dashboard can be damaged inside when you shut the car off. The next time you start the car, the backup camera picture may not appear on the screen. Federal rules require a working rear view image, so these vehicles no longer meet that standard. Without the camera you cannot see small children, pets, or objects directly behind the vehicle when reversing. Dealers replace the unit at no charge.

Audi backup camera screen may go blank in reverse medium

Reported 2022-10-05 · Campaign 22V742000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen is recalling certain 2021 and 2022 Audi cars and SUVs because of a software problem with the backup camera display. When the vehicle is put into reverse, the screen may go blank or black instead of showing the view behind the vehicle. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for rear visibility. Dealers will update the software in the infotainment unit, the system that runs the center screen.

Audi E-Tron brake booster corrosion weakens braking power high

Reported 2021-09-13 · Campaign 21V707000 · Model years: 2021

Rust can build up inside the brake booster — the part that makes your brake pedal easier to press. If it corrodes, several systems can quit at once: power brake assist, the anti-lock brakes that keep the wheels from locking up, the stability control that helps you hold the road, and the speedometer display. The brakes still work, but the pedal gets much harder to push and the car takes longer to stop. Dealers replace the brake booster.

Audi and VW rear belts may not secure child seats high

Reported 2021-08-05 · Campaign 21V606000 · Model years: 2021

Volkswagen and Audi are recalling certain 2021 and 2022 cars and SUVs because of a problem with the rear seat belts. The part of the belt that locks it in place for a child seat may switch off too early, so the child seat may not be held securely. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal rule on protecting people in a crash. Dealers will check the middle rear seat belts and replace them if needed.

Audi e-tron glove box may block knee airbag medium

Reported 2021-08-02 · Campaign 21V595000 · Model years: 2021

The pin that holds the right side of the glove box lid on its hinge may not be fastened properly. If it isn't, the trim on the glove box lid can get in the way of the knee airbag when it inflates. That airbag sits behind the dashboard and is meant to cushion the passenger's legs in a crash. An airbag that can't open the way it should leaves you less protected, so injuries in a crash could be worse.

Audi E-Tron brake booster part can come apart high

Reported 2021-07-26 · Campaign 21V569000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

A part inside the braking system — a rotor attached to a shaft — was not welded correctly at the factory. The two pieces can come apart while you drive. If that happens, the power assist that makes the brake pedal easy to press is reduced, and the anti-lock brakes and stability control stop working. You would have to push much harder on the pedal, and the car could be harder to control in a hard stop or on a slippery road.

Audi e-tron battery can take on moisture and catch fire critical

Reported 2019-06-10 · Campaign 19V434000 · Model years: 2019

A faulty seal on the charging socket can let moisture get into the big high-voltage battery that powers the car. Water inside that system can cause a short circuit, and that can start a fire. If warning lights come on along with the message "Electrical system: fault," stop driving right away, park outdoors away from buildings, do not plug the car in, and call Audi Roadside Assistance at 1-800-411-9988 for a tow. Dealers replace the seal and any damaged parts free.
