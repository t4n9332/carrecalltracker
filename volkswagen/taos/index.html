Volkswagen Taos recalls

8 recalls covering model years 2022, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Volkswagen Taos fuel sensor can come loose in crash critical

Reported 2026-04-22 · Campaign 26V258000 · Model years: 2025

On some 2025-2026 Taos models, a sensor that monitors fuel pressure can break loose from the gas tank if the car is hit from behind. Once that sensor is knocked out of place, gasoline can leak out, especially if the vehicle rolls over, and leaking fuel raises the chance of a fire. These vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for fuel system integrity. Dealers will add a short wiring extension so the sensor stays attached.

Volkswagen Jetta and Taos instrument panel may go blank high

Reported 2026-03-25 · Campaign 26V185000 · Model years: 2025

On some 2025 Volkswagen Jetta and Taos models, the display screen behind the steering wheel can go blank or fail to show information. That means you may lose your speedometer, fuel gauge, and warning lights, so you would not know how fast you are going or if the car is telling you something is wrong. Federal rules require these displays to work. Dealers will update the display's software and, if that does not fix it, replace the panel at no cost.

Volkswagen Taos fuel pump can fail, stalling the engine critical

Reported 2023-03-29 · Campaign 23V214000 · Model years: 2022

The pump that sends gasoline from the tank to the engine can break. If it fails while you are driving, the engine can quit suddenly, with no chance to pull over first. Losing power in traffic or at highway speed can easily lead to a crash. A Volkswagen dealer will replace the fuel pump free of charge. Owner letters were expected to go out on May 26, 2023, but you do not need the letter to book the repair.

Volkswagen Taos engine may stall when coming to stop high

Reported 2022-09-14 · Campaign 22V687000 · Model years: 2022

This affects all-wheel-drive versions of this small SUV. The computer that controls the engine has a software problem, and because of it the engine can shut off on its own as you slow down and come to a stop. If the engine quits in traffic, you lose power right when you may need to move. The repair is simple: the dealer reloads corrected software into the engine computer, and there is no charge for it.

Volkswagen SUVs and sedans: backup camera may go blank medium

Reported 2022-07-20 · Campaign 22V514000 · Model years: 2022

A memory chip inside your Volkswagen's infotainment screen was made incorrectly, and because of that the backup camera picture may not come up when you shift into reverse. Instead of seeing what's behind you, the screen can stay blank. Federal safety rules require a working rear view, so these vehicles don't meet the standard. A dealer can fix it by updating the infotainment software — no new parts needed, and it doesn't cost you anything.

Volkswagen Tiguan, Taos rear suspension part can break high

Reported 2022-03-23 · Campaign 22V176000 · Model years: 2022

On certain 2022 Volkswagen Tiguan and Taos vehicles, and 2021 long-wheelbase Tiguans, a metal part that holds a rear wheel to the suspension can rust, crack and finally break. If it breaks while you are driving, the vehicle can suddenly become unstable and hard to control, which can cause a crash. Dealers will inspect the left and right rear parts and replace one or both if needed. Volkswagen is sending a second letter when repair parts are available.

Volkswagen Taos fuel line connector can leak gas critical

Reported 2021-08-20 · Campaign 21V651000 · Model years: 2022

On some 2022 Volkswagen Taos, the quick-connect fitting that joins the fuel line under the hood can pop off. If that happens, gasoline can leak out into the engine compartment. The engine may stall while you are driving, and leaking fuel near anything hot or sparking can catch fire. Dealers will look at the connection and, if needed, put in a new fuel line and connector at no cost to you.

Volkswagen Taos all-wheel drive may stall when stopping high

Reported 2021-08-06 · Campaign 21V615000 · Model years: 2022

Certain Volkswagen Taos models with all-wheel drive have a software problem in the engine control unit, the computer that runs the engine. Because of this software, the engine can shut off on its own as you slow down and come to a stop. There is no warning before it happens, and you would have to restart the engine to get going again. Dealers will reload corrected engine software at no cost to you.
