Ford Explorer, Lincoln Aviator Seats May Recline medium

Reported Jul 21, 2026 · Campaign 26V470000 · Model years: 2025

Some Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles have a driver's seat that may recline on its own when no one is sitting in it, such as when the vehicle is remotely unlocked or remotely started. If the seat back moves unexpectedly, it could trap a person sitting in the second row.

Ford, Lincoln Second-Row Seats May Shift Unexpectedly high

Reported Jul 14, 2026 · Campaign 26V451000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025

Ford is recalling certain Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles because the switch for the easy-entry second-row outer seats may bind or stick. This can cause the seat to unlatch, tip, or slide unexpectedly. A seat that moves unexpectedly may not properly restrain a passenger in a crash. This is a phased recall — the repair will become available at different times depending on the model and model year. Dealers will inspect and replace the switch bezel as necessary, at no cost.

Ford, Lincoln SUVs: Transmission Park System May Fail high

Reported Jun 24, 2026 · Campaign 26V402000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

On affected vehicles, a part called the park pawl, which is supposed to lock the transmission in place when you put the vehicle in park, may engage while the vehicle is still moving. This can damage the park system, the mechanism that normally keeps the vehicle from rolling once it has been shifted into park.

Ford Explorer and Lincoln Models Camera and ADAS Loss medium

Reported Mar 19, 2026 · Campaign 26V165000 · Model years: 2025

In these Ford Explorer and Lincoln vehicles, a part called the Image Processing Module A may unexpectedly reset itself multiple times. When this module resets, it can cause the vehicle to lose the rearview camera image. It can also cause the vehicle to lose several driver assistance features at the same time, including pre-collision assist, lane-keeping assist, and blind-spot monitoring.

Ford, Lincoln Seat Belt Anchor Bolts May Loosen high

Reported Mar 17, 2026 · Campaign 26V159000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025

Ford is recalling certain Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles because the seat belt anchor bolts at the second-row outer seating positions may not be properly secured. A loose seat belt buckle may not properly restrain a passenger during a crash. Dealers will inspect the second-row outer seat belt lower anchor bolts and repair or replace them as necessary, at no cost to the owner.

Ford and Lincoln Rearview Camera Image May Invert medium

Reported Mar 3, 2026 · Campaign 26V123000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

The image on the center display may flip or turn upside down, showing an incorrect rearview image when the vehicle is put into reverse. Because the rearview image is not displayed correctly, these vehicles do not meet federal safety standards for rear visibility. To fix the problem, Ford dealers will update the software in the accessory protocol interface module at no charge to the owner.

Ford, Lincoln Wiper Motor May Fail To Work medium

Reported Mar 3, 2026 · Campaign 26V117000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

Ford is recalling certain Explorer, Escape, Lincoln Aviator, and Lincoln Corsair vehicles because the windshield wiper motor may fail. If the motor fails, the windshield wipers may stop working properly, which can make it harder for the driver to see the road. Dealers will inspect the front wiper motors and replace them if necessary, at no cost to the owner. A final repair is expected to be available in October 2026.

Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator Windshield Bubbles high

Reported Oct 24, 2025 · Campaign 25V730000 · Model years: 2025

Certain Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles have windshields with visible air bubbles in the glass. These bubbles can block or blur part of the driver's view out of the windshield.

Ford Explorer, Lincoln Aviator Have Loose Hitch Bolts high

Reported Oct 10, 2025 · Campaign 25V687000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

On some Explorer and Aviator SUVs, the bolts holding the trailer hitch in place may not be tight enough. If you are towing a trailer, loose hitch bolts can make the vehicle harder to control, and in some cases the trailer could come loose and detach from the vehicle. Either of these problems raises the chance of a crash while towing.

Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator Trailer Lights May Fail medium

Reported Aug 22, 2025 · Campaign 25V543000 · Model years: 2025

Some Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles have a body control module that was not manufactured correctly. When you tow a trailer, this problem can cause the trailer's tail lights to fail to turn on the way they should. Because these lights are not working properly, the vehicle does not meet federal safety standards for lights and reflective equipment. This mainly affects visibility for the trailer being towed behind the vehicle.

Reported Jul 25, 2025 · Campaign 25V484000 · Model years: 2025

Some 2025 Lincoln Aviator vehicles have rear side windows that may not work correctly with the automatic closing feature. When one of these windows is closing and encounters an object in its path, it may exert too much force before automatically reversing direction. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet a federal safety requirement for how power windows are supposed to behave when they meet resistance while closing.

That rubbing can wear a hole through the liquid fuel line, and once the line is worn through, fuel can leak out of it.

Lincoln Aviator recall questions

How many recalls does the Lincoln Aviator have?

NHTSA lists 49 safety recalls covering the 2020–2025 Lincoln Aviator. 28 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Ford Explorer, Lincoln Aviator Seats May Recline" (NHTSA campaign 26V470000), reported Jul 21, 2026.

Which Lincoln Aviator model year has the most recalls?

The 2020 Lincoln Aviator has the most, with 30 recalls. The 2024 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 8. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Lincoln Aviator?

No. None of the 49 Lincoln Aviator recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Lincoln Aviator has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Lincoln dealer, including for second owners.
