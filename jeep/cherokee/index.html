Jeep Cherokee recalls

21 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Jeep Cherokee power transfer unit can fail, causing rollaway high

Reported 2026-05-07 · Campaign 26V290000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023

The part that sends power to the wheels, called the power transfer unit, can break on certain Jeep Cherokees. If it fails, the vehicle may suddenly lose the ability to move under its own power, or it may roll away on its own even when you think it is parked. Chrysler is still finalizing the full fix. Dealers will update the drivetrain and electronic parking brake software, and replace the power transfer unit if needed, at no cost.

Jeep Cherokee power transfer unit may fail, rollaway risk high

Reported 2025-04-29 · Campaign 25V011000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

A part called the power transfer unit, which helps send power to the wheels, can become damaged. If it breaks, it can disconnect the transmission from the differential. That means the car may lose power and stop moving while you are driving. It also means Park may not actually hold the vehicle, so the Jeep could roll away on its own after you get out. A dealer will update the software and replace the part if it needs it.

Jeep Cherokee right curtain airbag connector not properly secured medium

Reported 2024-07-08 · Campaign 24V516000 · Model years: 2021, 2022

The plug that connects the right-side curtain airbag was not pushed in and secured properly. That is the bag which drops down over the right-side windows in a side crash or rollover. With a loose connection, the airbag may not go off when it is needed. Because of this the vehicles do not meet the federal rules for side impact protection and for keeping people from being thrown out. A dealer secures the connector at no charge.

Jeep Cherokee turn signals may not shut off automatically medium

Reported 2024-03-28 · Campaign 24V239000 · Model years: 2022

After you finish a turn, your blinker is supposed to click off by itself. On some 2022 Jeep Cherokees, it may not. That means the signal can keep flashing while you are driving straight, or act in ways other drivers do not expect. When your blinker does not match what you are actually doing, drivers around you cannot tell which way you plan to go, and that can cause a crash. Dealers will check the steering column control module and replace it if needed.

Jeep Cherokee transmission hose can leak and cause fire critical

Reported 2021-05-04 · Campaign 21V310000 · Model years: 2021

On some 2021 Jeep Cherokees, the hose that carries transmission fluid to the cooler was not made correctly at the factory. The rubber was not cured the right way, so the hose can leak. A transmission fluid leak raises the risk of a fire. A dealer will inspect the hose and replace it at no charge. Because fire is involved, this one should not wait; get it looked at as soon as you can.

Jeep Cherokee front control arms can break while driving critical

Reported 2020-10-27 · Campaign 20V657000 · Model years: 2021

On certain 2021 Jeep Cherokee Latitude and Limited models, one or both of the front lower control arms — the metal arms that hold the front wheels in place — can crack and break while you are driving. If that happens, you can lose the ability to steer, with no warning beforehand. Chrysler says not to drive these vehicles until a dealer has inspected them. The dealer checks both arms and replaces them if needed.

Jeep, Ram, Chrysler backup camera image stays on screen low

Reported 2020-09-10 · Campaign 20V191000 · Model years: 2020

The software in your 8.4-inch or 12-inch radio screen has a bug. After you back up and shift out of reverse, the picture from the backup camera can stay on the screen instead of switching back to the normal display. That means you can be driving forward while a rear-facing picture is still showing, which can pull your eyes and attention off the road ahead. The fix is a software update for the radio screen.

Jeep Cherokee steering gear may break, steering loss critical

Reported 2019-08-29 · Campaign 19V637000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

The steering rack is the part that turns your front wheels when you turn the wheel. In these Jeeps, the gear inside it can break. If it does, you can lose the ability to steer the vehicle. There is no warning before it happens, and losing steering control while driving can easily cause a crash. A dealer will replace the entire steering gear. This recall started back in October 2019, so an older Cherokee may still be waiting for the repair.

Jeep Cherokee crash sensors may fail, affecting airbags high

Reported 2019-06-13 · Campaign 19V449000 · Model years: 2019

Your vehicle uses small sensors to tell when it has been hit hard enough to need the airbags. On these Cherokees, those sensors can fail, so the system may not recognize certain crashes the way it should. If that happens in a collision, the airbags may not go off when they should, or may not go off correctly, leaving people less protected. Dealers will replace the faulty sensors at no charge.

Jeep Cherokee front passenger seat bolts may be loose medium

Reported 2019-03-28 · Campaign 19V248000 · Model years: 2019

On some 2019 Jeep Cherokees, the bolts that hold the front passenger seat to the floor may not be tightened all the way. Day to day you probably would not notice anything, but in a crash a seat that is not bolted down firmly can shift, and the person sitting in it is more likely to be hurt. The fix is quick: Chrysler has already sent letters, and a dealer simply tightens the mounting bolts, at no charge.

Jeep Cherokee gauges and warning chimes may stop working medium

Reported 2018-12-20 · Campaign 18V917000 · Model years: 2019

The instrument cluster is the display behind the steering wheel with your speedometer, fuel gauge, warning lights, and chimes. On some Cherokees it can quit working, so you would get no warning sounds, no messages, and no gauges. You would not know how fast you are going, or that something is wrong with the vehicle, and that makes a crash more likely. Dealers will check the cluster and replace it if needed, at no charge.

Jeep Cherokee transmission software can cause sudden engine stall high

Reported 2018-10-19 · Campaign 18V739000 · Model years: 2019

The computer that controls the automatic transmission on some 2019 Jeep Cherokees with the 2.4-liter engine was programmed with the wrong settings. Because of that bad programming, the engine can shut off on its own while you are driving. Nothing is broken or worn out, so no parts need to be replaced. The dealer simply connects a computer to your Cherokee and loads corrected software into the transmission controller, which takes care of the problem.

Jeep, Ram, Dodge, Chrysler engine computer may cause stalling high

Reported 2018-08-09 · Campaign 18V524000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

The computer that runs the engine has a small part inside, a voltage regulator chip, that can fail. When it does, the engine can shut off while you are driving, or the vehicle may not start at all. A stall in traffic leaves you without engine power and can put you in the path of other vehicles. Dealers will replace the whole engine computer at no charge.

Dodge and Jeep rear brake calipers may weaken braking high

Reported 2018-08-09 · Campaign 18V523000 · Model years: 2019

The pistons inside the rear brake calipers may not have enough protective coating. Without it, pockets of gas can form in the rear brake system, and that can make the rear brakes less effective than they should be. You may not notice anything day to day, but the vehicle could take longer to stop, which raises the chance of a crash. Dealers will bleed the brake system to flush the gas pockets out, free of charge. This recall started September 29, 2018.

Chrysler Pacifica and Jeep Cherokee transmission may stop working high

Reported 2018-07-26 · Campaign 18V492000 · Model years: 2019

A part inside the automatic transmission may not have been welded properly. The transmission is what sends engine power to the wheels. If that weld breaks, the connection is lost and the vehicle simply stops moving — the engine may still be running, but the wheels get no power. Losing drive without warning, especially in traffic or at speed, raises the risk of a crash. Chrysler dealers will replace the entire transmission at no cost to you.

Jeep Cherokee front axle shaft may break high

Reported 2018-07-26 · Campaign 18V494000 · Model years: 2018

The right front halfshaft, the shaft that carries engine power to the front wheel, has a small part inside that may not have been hardened properly when it was made. That part can break, and the shaft can then stop delivering power. If that happens, your Cherokee can suddenly lose drive while you are on the road, and it can also roll away on its own even with the shifter in Park. Dealers will replace the shaft.

Jeep Cherokee 2.0-liter engine parts may cause stalling high

Reported 2018-05-25 · Campaign 18V344000 · Model years: 2019

Some 2019 Jeep Cherokees with the 2.0-liter engine were built with parts missing or installed backwards. A small clip that holds an engine valve in place may be missing, which lets the valve drop down inside the cylinder and wreck the engine. A cap over the camshaft, the bar that opens and closes those valves, may have been fitted the wrong way around and can chew up its bearing. Either problem can make the engine quit while you are driving.

Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep and Ram cruise control may stick on high

Reported 2018-05-17 · Campaign 18V332000 · Model years: 2018

These vehicles have cruise control, which holds a set speed for you. A short circuit in the wiring can lock the cruise control on. If that happens, tapping the brake pedal or pressing the off button may not shut it off, and the vehicle can keep its speed or even speed up on its own. Chrysler tells owners to stop using cruise control until the free computer software update is done at a dealer.

Jeep Cherokee fuel tube may leak into engine critical

Reported 2018-05-01 · Campaign 18V282000 · Model years: 2018

Some 2018 Jeep Cherokees with the 2.4-liter engine were built with a fuel tube that can leak gasoline into the engine compartment. The engine bay gets very hot and has electrical parts in it, so fuel leaking there can catch fire. A gasoline smell, wet spots under the hood, or fuel disappearing faster than normal are warning signs. Dealers inspect the fuel tube and replace it if needed, at no cost to you.

Jeep Cherokee and Compass oil pump may fail, stalling engine high

Reported 2017-10-24 · Campaign 17V670000 · Model years: 2018

The housing around the oil pump may be cracked. The oil pump is what pushes oil through the engine, and if it quits, the engine loses oil pressure and can shut off while you are driving. A stall can happen at any speed and you lose power without much warning, which is dangerous in traffic. A dealer checks the oil pump and puts in a new one if yours has the problem.

Jeep Cherokee missing tire and rim size label low

Reported 2017-08-31 · Campaign 17V543000 · Model years: 2018

Certain 2017-2018 Jeep Cherokee vehicles were built without the tire and wheel size information that is supposed to be printed on the vehicle's certification label. That label is what a tire shop or owner uses to pick the right tires and wheels. Without it, the wrong tire and rim combination could be put on the vehicle, which can lead to a crash. Because of the missing information, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety standard for tire and rim selection. Chrysler will send a corrected label.
