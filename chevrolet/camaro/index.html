Chevrolet Camaro recalls

6 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

GM cars may lose electric power steering assist high

Reported 2025-03-20 · Campaign 25V175000 · Model years: 2018

Certain Cadillac CTS and ATS models and Chevrolet Camaro and Corvette models may lose their electric power steering assist. When that help goes away, the steering wheel becomes much harder to turn, which is most noticeable when you are driving slowly or parking. You can still steer, but it takes considerably more muscle and can catch you off guard. Dealers will replace the power steering gear assembly at no charge. GM mailed owner notification letters on April 15, 2025.

Cadillac and Camaro transmission may lock the wheels high

Reported 2025-03-06 · Campaign 25V148000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

Certain Cadillac CT4, CT5, CT6 and Chevrolet Camaro models with the 10-speed automatic transmission can suffer internal damage inside the transmission. If that happens, the wheels can suddenly lock up, meaning they stop turning while you are driving and you lose control of the car. GM dealers will install new monitoring software in the transmission's computer so the problem can be caught, and the work costs you nothing.

Chevrolet Camaro steering wheel emblem may break loose in crash medium

Reported 2021-03-25 · Campaign 21V211000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021

The plastic Camaro badge sits on the cover of the driver's airbag, in the middle of the steering wheel. If the airbag goes off in a crash, that badge can break away from the cover instead of staying put. Pieces of plastic can then be thrown toward the driver at the same moment the airbag is inflating, which can cause injuries on top of those from the crash itself. The dealer replaces the whole driver airbag unit, free.

Chevrolet Camaro axle shaft may separate while driving high

Reported 2021-03-18 · Campaign 21V188000 · Model years: 2021

Some 2021 Chevrolet Camaros left the factory with a small retaining ring missing from the left or right axle shaft. That ring is what holds the shaft in place. Without it, the axle shaft can come apart while you are driving, and the car would suddenly stop sending power to the wheels. Dealers will replace the affected axle shaft free of charge. The recall started April 21, 2021, and Chevrolet has notified owners.

GM cars and trucks may have wrong seat belt bolts high

Reported 2020-12-23 · Campaign 20V811000 · Model years: 2021

Seat belts are bolted to the vehicle body and to the seats. On these vehicles, the wrong bolts may have been used at one or more of those spots, so a belt may not be attached securely. If a belt pulls loose in a crash, it may not hold the person wearing it, which can mean more serious injuries. Dealers will find and replace any wrong bolts at no charge.

GM trucks and SUVs fuel pump leak fire risk critical

Reported 2019-11-21 · Campaign 19V837000 · Model years: 2020

Some of these vehicles left the factory with a fuel pump that is missing its pressure regulator — the part that keeps fuel pressure in check. Without it, pressure can build too high, crack the fuel pump, and let gasoline leak out. Leaking fuel near anything hot or sparking can start a fire. Your dealer will replace the fuel pump at no cost to you. This recall began back in December 2019, so a used vehicle you bought may still need it.
