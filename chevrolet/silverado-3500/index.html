Chevrolet Silverado, GMC Sierra Roof Air Bag Inflator Risk high

Reported May 21, 2026 · Campaign 26V325000 · Model years: 2019

The end cap on the roof rail air bag inflator, on the left or right side, may come loose, or the inflator itself may rupture. This can let compressed gas escape and send the end cap or inflator pieces into the vehicle.

Chevy Silverado and GMC Sierra Roof Air Bag Defect high

Reported Mar 19, 2026 · Campaign 26V166000 · Model years: 2019

Certain Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra crew cab trucks have a roof rail air bag whose inflator may have a problem. The end cap on the left or right side inflator could detach, or the inflator itself could rupture. If that happens, it can release compressed gas and send the end cap or pieces of the inflator into the vehicle.

Silverado and Sierra 3500 Fuel Pump May Stall Engine high

Reported Mar 5, 2026 · Campaign 26V129000 · Model years: 2025, 2026

On these trucks, which are built with gasoline engines and have two separate fuel tanks, the rear tank's fuel pump may not transfer an adequate amount of fuel over to the front tank. Because of this, the front tank may not receive enough fuel to keep the engine running properly, and the engine can stall while the truck is being driven.

GM vehicles may be missing the electronic owner's manual low

Reported Feb 26, 2026 · Campaign 26V114000 · Model years: 2026

General Motors is recalling a wide range of Cadillac, Chevrolet, GMC and Buick vehicles, including the Escalade IQ and IQL, OPTIQ, Colorado, Equinox EV, Silverado 3500, Canyon and Sierra 3500. During production, the radio may not have been set to the correct status to download the electronic owner's manual, so the manual is not there for the owner to use. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety standard covering occupant protection.

Chevrolet Silverado, GMC Sierra Transmission Wheel Lockup Risk high

Reported Feb 12, 2026 · Campaign 26V083000 · Model years: 2020

Certain Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra trucks may not have been fully fixed by an earlier recall that addressed this same transmission problem. In these trucks, the transmission control valve can fail. When that happens, the rear wheels may lock up suddenly while the truck is being driven.

Chevrolet Silverado Trucks Missing Owner's Manual low

Reported Nov 25, 2025 · Campaign 25V815000 · Model years: 2026

General Motors is recalling certain 2026 Chevrolet Silverado 1500, 2500 and 3500 trucks that may not have been given an owner's manual when they were built. The manual contains information owners need to safely use and operate the vehicle. Without it, an owner may not have that information, which increases the risk of injury in a crash.

Silverado, Sierra Roof Airbag Inflator May Rupture high

Reported Jun 26, 2025 · Campaign 25V432000 · Model years: 2019

General Motors is recalling certain Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra pickup trucks. These trucks have airbags built into the roof rail, above the doors. On the left or right side, the metal end cap on the airbag inflator may come loose and detach, or the inflator itself may crack open, or rupture.

Silverado and Sierra Tailgates May Unlatch medium

Reported Nov 26, 2024 · Campaign 24V894000 · Model years: 2024

Some Chevrolet Silverado HD and GMC Sierra HD trucks have a power tailgate release switch that can short out if water gets into it. This can cause the tailgate to unlock itself even while the truck is parked. If that happens while you're driving, the tailgate could swing open and any cargo in the bed could fall out, creating a hazard for other drivers.

Diesel GM trucks, SUVs risk rear wheel lock-up high

Reported Oct 24, 2024 · Campaign 24V797000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

On certain diesel Chevrolet, GMC and Cadillac trucks and SUVs, the transmission control valve may fail. If it fails, the rear wheels can lock up while the vehicle is being driven.

Chevrolet Silverado, GMC Sierra Side Airbag Inflator Defect high

Reported Oct 10, 2024 · Campaign 24V756000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

Certain Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra crew cab trucks have a problem with the roof-rail side curtain airbag. The end cap on the left or right side inflator may separate from the inflator, or the inflator's sidewall may split open. If this happens, compressed gas inside can escape suddenly, sending the end cap or other pieces flying into the vehicle.

Silverado, Sierra Tailgate May Unlatch While Driving high

Reported Feb 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V060000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

The tailgate's electronic release switch can short circuit if water gets into it. This can cause the tailgate to unlatch on its own while the truck is parked, and it may then open while driving. If that happens, unsecured cargo could fall out, creating a hazard on the road. Dealers will replace the exterior touchpad switch assemblies to fix the problem, and this repair is free of charge.

Chevrolet, GMC Trucks: Steering Gear May Fracture critical

Reported Aug 3, 2023 · Campaign 23V549000 · Model years: 2024

On these Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra heavy-duty trucks, the steering gear shaft may fracture and separate from the steering arm.

Silverado, Sierra Rear Fuel Tank May Stall Engine high

Reported Feb 2, 2023 · Campaign 23V047000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

Certain Silverado and Sierra trucks with the dual fuel tank option can have a rear fuel tank that collapses. When this happens, the tank can prevent the fuel pump from moving fuel from the rear tank to the front tank, which can cause the engine to stall while driving.

Silverado, Sierra Brake Module Fire Risk critical

Reported Sep 29, 2021 · Campaign 21V758000 · Model years: 2021, 2022

Some Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra trucks have a brake control module that was replaced during assembly. The bolts holding two parts of this module together may not have been tightened enough, so water can leak inside. Water getting into the module can cause an electrical short, which increases the risk of a fire.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Silverado, Sierra Block Heater Cord Fire Risk critical

Reported Jul 1, 2021 · Campaign 21V496000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

Certain Duramax diesel Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra trucks equipped with the optional engine block heater cord can develop an electrical short-circuit. This can happen in the heater cable itself, or in the terminals that connect the cable to the block heater. A short-circuit in this wiring increases the risk of a fire starting in the vehicle.

GM Vehicles Seat Belt May Use Wrong Bolts high

Reported Dec 23, 2020 · Campaign 20V811000 · Model years: 2021

Incorrect bolts may have been used at one or more places where the seat belts attach to these vehicles. If the wrong bolt was used at any of these points, the seat belt may not be properly attached to the vehicle. GM dealers will inspect the fastening locations and replace any bolts that are found to be incorrect, so the seat belts are properly secured to the vehicle as they should be.

Chevrolet and GMC Trucks and SUVs Seat Belt Bracket Issue high

Reported Dec 17, 2020 · Campaign 20V792000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

In the front-row center seating position, the seat belt bracket may not be properly attached to the seat frame. If this bracket is not secured, the seat belt in that seat may not hold a passenger properly. GM dealers will check both the left and right front-row center seat belt bracket attachments and put them back together correctly if they find a problem.

GM SUVs, Trucks: Roof-Rail Air Bag May Underperform high

Reported Jul 30, 2020 · Campaign 20V446000 · Model years: 2020

On affected vehicles, a part called the diffuser, inside the roof-rail air bag inflator, may not have been properly attached to the inflator. This means the diffuser could come apart from the inflator when the air bag deploys.

Chevy, GMC Truck Hoods May Open While Driving high

Reported Jun 4, 2020 · Campaign 20V324000 · Model years: 2020

On these Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra trucks, some replacement hoods installed under an earlier recall, or fitted during other service such as collision repair, may have hood-latch striker wires that were not properly heat-treated. These wires may fracture.

Silverado, Sierra Trucks: Hood May Open While Driving high

Reported Mar 12, 2020 · Campaign 20V142000 · Model years: 2020

On some 2020 Silverado 2500 and 3500 and Sierra 2500 and 3500 trucks, the wires that hold the hood latch striker in place may not have been heat-treated the correct way, which could cause them to fracture over time. If a striker wire fractures, the hood could open unexpectedly while the truck is being driven, which increases the chance of a crash on the road.

Silverado and Sierra Seat Belt Fire Risk critical

Reported Nov 14, 2019 · Campaign 19V814000 · Model years: 2020

These trucks have carpet floor covering and front seat belt pretensioners. When a seat belt pretensioner deploys, it releases hot gas. In these vehicles, that hot gas may escape through an opening in the pretensioner's mounting bracket instead of staying contained. If that hot gas reaches the carpet inside the vehicle, it could ignite the carpet material and start a fire inside the cabin.

GM Diesel Trucks: Block Heater Cord May Short Circuit high

Reported Apr 25, 2019 · Campaign 19V328000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

These GM diesel trucks can be equipped with an optional engine block heater. The cord that plugs into this heater, or the terminals where the cord connects to the block heater, may short circuit and fail. If a short circuit happens, it can damage engine components nearby and increases the chance that a fire could start in the engine area of the vehicle.

Silverado, Sierra 3500 Dual Fuel Tank Can Overfill critical

Reported Feb 7, 2019 · Campaign 19V088000 · Model years: 2018

These trucks have two fuel tanks, and a sensor in the front tank tells the system when it's full. That sensor can stick, so the rear tank keeps pumping fuel into the front tank after it's already full. The extra pressure can make the front tank swell and touch the driveshaft, which can wear a hole in the tank. Fuel leaking from that hole can catch fire if it reaches a spark or flame.

Chevrolet and GMC Trucks Have Faulty Seat Belt Retractors high

Reported Sep 14, 2017 · Campaign 17V567000 · Model years: 2018

Certain Chevrolet Silverado 2500/3500 and GMC Sierra 2500/3500 trucks have front seat belts built with the wrong internal part. Because of this, the front seat belt retractors on both sides may not work the way they should if the truck is in a crash. These trucks do not meet the federal safety standard that applies to seat belts. Dealers will replace both front seat belt retractor assemblies.

Chevrolet Silverado 3500 recall questions

How many recalls does the Chevrolet Silverado 3500 have?

NHTSA lists 24 safety recalls covering the 2018–2026 Chevrolet Silverado 3500. 21 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Chevrolet Silverado, GMC Sierra Roof Air Bag Inflator Risk" (NHTSA campaign 26V325000), reported May 21, 2026.

Which Chevrolet Silverado 3500 model year has the most recalls?

The 2020 Chevrolet Silverado 3500 has the most, with 8 recalls. The 2023 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 1. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Chevrolet Silverado 3500?

Yes. 1 Chevrolet Silverado 3500 recall carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA: Silverado, Sierra Brake Module Fire Risk (21V758000). These apply only to the affected vehicles, so check your VIN.

How do I check whether my Chevrolet Silverado 3500 has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Chevrolet dealer, including for second owners.
