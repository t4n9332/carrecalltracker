BMW, Mini Integrated Brake System May Malfunction high

Reported Jul 1, 2026 · Campaign 26V422000 · Model years: 2023, 2024

The integrated brake system in these vehicles may malfunction. This can cause a loss of power brake assist, which affects how the brakes work. It can also cause the Antilock Brake System and the Dynamic Stability Control system to stop functioning properly. To fix this, the integrated brake system will be inspected and, if necessary, replaced, free of charge for owners.

BMW X1, X2, Mini Countryman Seat Belt Retractor Damage high

Reported Sep 17, 2025 · Campaign 25V616000 · Model years: 2025

In certain BMW X1, X2, and Mini Countryman S ALL4 vehicles, the torsion bar inside both front seat belt retractors may have been damaged during manufacturing.

BMW X1 B-Pillar May Lack Crash Strength high

Reported Nov 4, 2024 · Campaign 24V829000 · Model years: 2025

On certain BMW X1 xDrive28i and X1 M35i vehicles, the reinforcement plates in the left and right B-Pillars may have been manufactured with insufficient structural strength. Because these pillars may not be as strong as they should be, they may not properly protect passengers if the vehicle is ever in a crash. This increases the risk of injury to people riding inside the vehicle if a crash happens.

BMW Brake System May Lose Power Assist high

Reported Oct 2, 2024 · Campaign 24V739000 · Model years: 2023

Certain BMW models with an integrated brake system may have the system malfunction. This can cause a loss of power assist for the brakes, meaning it takes more pedal effort and distance to stop the vehicle. The malfunction can also stop the Antilock Brake System and Dynamic Stability Control from working properly, which can affect the driver's ability to keep control of the vehicle.

BMW and MINI Brake System Weld May Fail high

Reported Sep 18, 2024 · Campaign 24V697000 · Model years: 2023, 2024

The welds inside the servomotor of the integrated brake control module may break. If this happens, the vehicle can lose power assist for the brakes and lose function in the rear brakes. The antilock brake system and the dynamic stability control system may also stop working properly. To fix the problem, BMW dealers will replace the entire integrated brake system with a new one.

BMW, Mini Brake System May Lose Power Assist high

Reported Feb 12, 2024 · Campaign 24V104000 · Model years: 2023, 2024, 2025

The integrated brake system in these vehicles may malfunction. This can cause a loss of power brake assist, which affects how the brakes work. It can also cause the Antilock Brake System and the Dynamic Stability Control system to stop functioning properly. To fix this, dealers will replace the integrated brake system, and this repair is free of charge for owners.

BMW X1 Second-Row Seat Belts May Be Pinched high

Reported Apr 12, 2023 · Campaign 23V260000 · Model years: 2023

On some BMW X1 vehicles, the seat belts for the second-row outboard seats may have been pinched by an interior trim panel during assembly. A pinched seat belt may not function the way it is supposed to.

BMW iX and X1 Driver Air Bag May Not Deploy high

Reported Feb 14, 2023 · Campaign 23V079000 · Model years: 2022, 2023

Some BMW iX and X1 vehicles have a front driver's air bag that may have been built incorrectly. Because of this manufacturing problem, the air bag might not inflate the way it's designed to if the vehicle is in a crash. This means that in a collision, the driver may not get the protection the air bag is meant to provide, since it may not work correctly when it's needed most.

BMW X1, X3 Fuel Rail May Crack, Leak Fuel critical

Reported Jul 20, 2022 · Campaign 22V513000 · Model years: 2022

BMW says the high-pressure fuel rail on certain X1 and X3 vehicles may develop a crack. If the fuel rail cracks, fuel can leak out inside the engine compartment.

BMW, MINI and Toyota Supra passenger seat belt retractor fault medium

Reported Jul 21, 2021 · Campaign 21V554000 · Model years: 2021

BMW is recalling certain BMW, MINI, Rolls Royce and Toyota Supra vehicles. The passenger seat belt has a locking feature that can switch off too early, before the belt webbing has pulled all the way back in. Because of this, these seat belt assemblies do not meet the federal safety standard for occupant crash protection. Dealers will inspect the seat belt retractors and the seat belts themselves, and replace the retractors if that is needed.

BMW X1/X2 and MINI airbags may fail in rollover high

Reported May 20, 2020 · Campaign 20V283000 · Model years: 2020

BMW is recalling certain X1, X2, and MINI models because of a problem with the sensor that detects a rollover, located inside the airbag control unit. If the vehicle is in a rollover crash, this problem could mean the head airbags, seat belt pretensioners, and other safety systems don't activate the way they're supposed to.

BMW X1, X2, MINI Cooper Steering Tie Rod May Break high

Reported Aug 14, 2019 · Campaign 19V601000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

Some BMW X1, X2, and MINI Cooper vehicles may have a steering tie rod that wasn't assembled correctly. Over time, this can cause the tie rod end to wear down more than it should, and it could eventually break.

BMW X1 B-pillar may not absorb crash impact high

Reported May 9, 2019 · Campaign 19V349000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

In a crash where an occupant's head strikes the B-pillar, the frame section between the front and rear doors, the pillar may not absorb enough of the impact.

BMW X1 Taillights May Loosen or Detach medium

Reported Jan 30, 2019 · Campaign 19V074000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

Some BMW X1 vehicles have a manufacturing problem where contaminated hardware can cause the brackets holding the taillight housings to crack. Over time, this can make a taillight become loose, and it may even come off the vehicle entirely. A taillight that falls off no longer warns drivers behind you, and a detached light sitting on the road can also be a hazard to other traffic.

BMW, MINI: Crankshaft Sensor May Cause Stalling high

Reported Jul 12, 2018 · Campaign 18V465000 · Model years: 2018

On certain BMW and MINI vehicles, the crankshaft sensor may be installed with incorrect firmware. This incorrect firmware can prevent the sensor from properly reading signals from the crankshaft reluctor ring. As a result, the vehicle may stall while it is being driven. BMW will notify owners, and dealers will replace the crankshaft sensor, free of charge, to correct the problem.
