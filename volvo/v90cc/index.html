Volvo rearview camera image may not appear in reverse medium

Reported 2025-12-23 · Campaign 25V908000 · Model years: 2022

Volvo is recalling a range of recent Volvo cars and SUVs. When the driver shifts into reverse, the picture from the rearview camera may not come up on the screen. Because of that, these vehicles do not meet the federal rear visibility safety rule. A dealer can install updated software, or the update can be sent to the car over the air. This recall replaces an earlier Volvo recall, and cars already repaired under that earlier recall need the new repair too.

Volvo rearview camera image may not appear in reverse medium

Reported 2025-05-08 · Campaign 25V282000 · Model years: 2022

On these Volvo vehicles, the backup camera picture may not show up on the screen when you shift into reverse. The camera view is supposed to appear every time the vehicle is put in reverse, and because it may not, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule for rear visibility. Volvo will fix this with a software update, either at a dealer or sent to the vehicle over the air.

Volvo V60CC, V90CC Fuel Pump Can Fail high

Reported 2021-06-02 · Campaign 21V414000 · Model years: 2020

On affected vehicles, a fuse for the low-pressure fuel pump can blow, causing the pump to stop working. If the fuel pump fails while you are driving, the engine can stall.

Volvo V60, V90 Cross Country Braking System Issue high

Reported 2020-03-12 · Campaign 20V144000 · Model years: 2020

Some Volvo V60 Cross Country and V90 Cross Country vehicles have a software and hardware incompatibility in the Automatic Emergency Braking (AEB) system. Because of this incompatibility, the AEB system may not detect obstacles and engage the brakes as it is designed to do.

Volvo V60 and V90 Cross Country may lose emergency GPS medium

Reported 2018-11-13 · Campaign 18V800000 · Model years: 2018

The software in these Volvo vehicles' connectivity module may have an error that keeps the telematics and driver support systems from working correctly. One result is that the car will not send its GPS location to emergency personnel during an emergency. In other words, if help is called, responders may not get the location information the car is supposed to provide. Dealers correct the software at no cost, and owners were notified starting in December 2018.
