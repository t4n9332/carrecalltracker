4 recalls covering model years 2023. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Nissan Ariya may lose drive power from software glitch high

Reported 2024-07-25 · Campaign 24V560000 · Model years: 2023

The Ariya's inverter is the part that sends power from the battery to the electric motor. Its software may read a problem that is not really there. When that happens, the system can shut itself down and the car loses drive power, so it stops pulling and you cannot accelerate. This can happen while you are driving, including in traffic. The fix is a software update, so the dealer checks the version and reloads it if needed.

Nissan Ariya may suddenly lose drive power high

Reported 2024-06-03 · Campaign 24V391000 · Model years: 2023

Some 2023 Ariya electric SUVs have rubber seals, called O-rings, inside the front electric drive motor that may have been damaged when the motor was put together. Damaged seals can let oil leak inside the motor. That internal leak can cause the vehicle to unexpectedly lose drive power, meaning the car could stop pulling while you are driving. Nissan dealers will replace the entire front traction motor assembly at no cost to you.

Nissan Ariya can suddenly lose drive power high

Reported 2023-09-28 · Campaign 23V657000 · Model years: 2023

In some 2023 Ariya electric SUVs, the software that runs the inverter — the part that sends power from the battery to the motor — can wrongly decide there is an electrical short. When that happens, it shuts down the electric drive system and the vehicle loses power to the wheels. You could suddenly find yourself coasting with no acceleration, possibly in moving traffic. The repair is a software update rather than a parts replacement.

Nissan Ariya steering wheel bolt may be loose or missing critical

Reported 2023-02-28 · Campaign 23V131000 · Model years: 2023

On some 2023 Nissan Ariya vehicles, the bolt that holds the steering wheel onto the steering column may have been tightened incorrectly or never installed. If that bolt is loose or missing, the steering wheel can come off the column while you are driving, and you would have no way to steer. Nissan says to gently rock the steering wheel forward and back with your hands to check whether it feels loose. Dealers replace the bolt free.
