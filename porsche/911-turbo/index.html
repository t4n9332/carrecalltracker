Porsche 911 rear seat belt may not hold child seat securely medium

Reported Feb 20, 2024 · Campaign 24V123000 · Model years: 2023

Porsche is recalling certain 911 vehicles. The rear seat belts have a locking feature that is meant to hold a child seat firmly in place. On these vehicles that lock can switch off too early, so the child seat may not end up properly secured. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety standard for occupant crash protection. Dealers will look at the rear three-point belts and replace them where needed.

Porsche 911 Seat-Mounted Air Bags May Deploy Unnecessarily medium

Reported Oct 25, 2023 · Campaign 23V715000 · Model years: 2022

Porsche is recalling certain 911 models that have the optional full bucket seats installed. These seats include air bags built into them. In some cases, those seat air bags may deploy unnecessarily during a crash, meaning they can go off even when they are not needed. This problem only affects vehicles equipped with the special bucket seat option, not vehicles with standard seats.

How do I check whether my Porsche 911 Turbo has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Porsche dealer, including for second owners.
