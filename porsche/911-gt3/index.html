4 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2022, 2023. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Porsche 911 Seat-Mounted Air Bags May Deploy Unnecessarily medium

Reported 2023-10-25 · Campaign 23V715000 · Model years: 2022, 2023

Porsche is recalling certain 911 models that have the optional full bucket seats installed. These seats include air bags built into them. In some cases, those seat air bags may deploy unnecessarily during a crash, meaning they can go off even when they are not needed. This problem only affects vehicles equipped with the special bucket seat option, not vehicles with standard seats.

Porsche 911 GT3 brake lights may confuse following drivers medium

Reported 2023-06-26 · Campaign 23V446000 · Model years: 2022, 2023

Porsche is recalling certain 911 GT3 Touring cars. A software error in the control unit for the rear spoiler can let two center brake lights come on at the same time — the one on the car and the one on the spoiler — when the spoiler is raised by hand at speeds below 56 mph. Federal rules on vehicle lighting do not allow this. A dealer will reprogram the spoiler control unit.

Porsche 911 GT3 Seat Belt Anchor Screw Loose high

Reported 2022-10-25 · Campaign 22V803000 · Model years: 2022

On some Porsche 911 GT3 vehicles, the screw that attaches the lower seat belt anchor to the seat frame may not have been tightened properly during assembly. Because the screw is not fully tightened, the anchor may not be as securely attached to the seat frame as it should be. This affects how firmly the seat belt anchor stays fixed to the seat frame on the affected vehicles.

Porsche manual lacks clear child seat installation instructions low

Reported 2019-12-11 · Campaign 19V875000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

The owner's manual in these Porsche cars does not give enough detail about how to properly install a child restraint system, meaning a child safety seat. Federal rules covering child seat anchorages require clear instructions, and because the manual falls short, these cars do not meet that standard. Porsche is contacting owners, and dealers will provide a supplement to the owner's manual containing proper child seat installation instructions.
