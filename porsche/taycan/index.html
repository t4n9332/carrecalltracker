Porsche Taycan recalls

25 recalls covering model years 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Porsche Cayenne, Panamera and Taycan Rearview Camera Failure medium

Reported Dec 19, 2025 · Campaign 25V896000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

In these vehicles, the rearview camera image may not show up on the display screen when the vehicle is shifted into reverse. Dealers will fix this by updating the driver assistance software, free of charge.

Porsche Taycan front passenger airbag may switch off high

Reported May 5, 2025 · Campaign 25V221000 · Model years: 2022, 2023

Porsche is recalling certain Taycan vehicles because of a fault in the system that decides whether the front passenger airbag should be switched on. This system is meant to sense whether someone is sitting in the front passenger seat. An error in it can turn the front passenger airbag off. Dealers will replace the front passenger seat cushion at no cost, and Porsche has mailed notification letters to owners.

Porsche Taycan High-Voltage Battery Short Circuit Risk critical

Reported Oct 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V731000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023

On certain Porsche Taycan vehicles, the high-voltage battery that powers the car may develop a short circuit inside one of its battery modules. This recall expands and replaces three earlier Taycan battery recalls, so even vehicles already repaired under those earlier recalls will need to have this new repair completed as well.

Porsche Taycan Battery Short Circuit Fire Risk critical

Reported Oct 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V732000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

On these Porsche Taycan vehicles, the high-voltage battery that powers the car may develop a short circuit inside one of its battery modules.

Porsche Taycan headlight software affects parking lights medium

Reported Sep 5, 2024 · Campaign 24V654000 · Model years: 2024

Porsche is recalling certain Taycan cars that may have been built with the wrong software in the module that controls the headlights. Because of that software, the parking lights may not work as intended when the turn signals are switched on. As a result, these cars do not meet the federal rule covering lamps and reflective equipment. Dealers will update the headlight control module software, and there is no charge to the owner for the update.

Porsche Taycan Front Brake Hoses May Leak high

Reported Jun 19, 2024 · Campaign 24V455000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

In certain Porsche Taycan vehicles, the front brake hoses can develop cracks and begin to leak brake fluid. Losing brake fluid can weaken how well the brakes work and make it take longer for the vehicle to stop when needed. Dealers will fix the problem by replacing the front brake hoses, free of charge to the owner.

Porsche Taycan Battery Short Circuit Fire Risk critical

Reported Mar 20, 2024 · Campaign 24V217000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

The high-voltage battery in these vehicles may develop a short circuit inside its battery modules. A short circuit like this increases the risk of the battery catching fire.

Porsche Taycan high-voltage battery may short circuit critical

Reported Mar 20, 2024 · Campaign 24V215000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023

Porsche is recalling certain Taycan models because the high-voltage battery may develop a short circuit within its battery modules.

Porsche Taycan High-Voltage Battery Fire Risk critical

Reported Dec 13, 2023 · Campaign 23V840000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023

On certain Porsche Taycan and Taycan Turbo S vehicles, along with several other Taycan variants, the high-voltage battery may develop a short circuit inside one of its battery modules. This means an unwanted electrical connection can form somewhere inside the battery pack. When a short circuit like this happens inside the battery, it increases the chances that a fire could start in the vehicle.

Porsche portable charging cable and outlet can overheat critical

Reported Dec 13, 2023 · Campaign 23V841000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024

Porsche is recalling a number of electric and plug-in hybrid models, including the Taycan, Cayenne E-Hybrid and Panamera 4S E-Hybrid. When the compact charging system is used with its industrial 220-volt or 240-volt plug at the 100 percent charge setting, some home outlets cannot handle the electrical current the system needs. The outlet or the charging cable can then overheat. Dealers will supply a new 220-volt/240-volt portable charging cable with a built-in temperature sensor, free of charge.

Porsche Taycan Battery Seal Issue Raises Fire Risk critical

Reported Sep 1, 2023 · Campaign 23V615000 · Model years: 2023

In these vehicles, the high voltage battery may not have enough sealant applied during manufacturing. Without enough sealant, liquid can get into the battery and build up inside it over time. This buildup of liquid inside the battery can cause electrical arcing. Dealers will test each vehicle's battery for leaks and replace the battery if needed, at no cost to the owner.

Porsche Taycan Brake Wear Warning Shows Wrong Color high

Reported May 16, 2023 · Campaign 23V347000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

On some Taycan vehicles, the dashboard display that is supposed to warn the driver when the brake pads are worn shows the warning in the wrong color. Because the color of this warning is incorrect, a driver may not recognize it as a signal that the brake pads need attention, since the indicator does not display the way it is meant to.

Porsche Taycan passenger seat belt warning does not work medium

Reported Mar 17, 2023 · Campaign 23V176000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023

Porsche is recalling certain Taycan vehicles. When someone is sitting in the passenger seat without the seat belt fastened, the seat belt warning light and the reminder chime do not come on the way they are supposed to. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet the federal government's safety standard covering occupant crash protection. Dealers will update the software in the instrument cluster, the display area behind the steering wheel, free of charge.

Porsche Taycan Suspension Strut Can Fail high

Reported Dec 13, 2022 · Campaign 22V921000 · Model years: 2021

Certain Porsche Taycan vehicles have a suspension strut with a retaining ring on top that can come loose over time. If the ring loosens, the strut can become dislodged and lose air, affecting the air suspension at that corner of the vehicle.

Porsche Taycan Screen And Camera Can Go Black high

Reported Jun 8, 2022 · Campaign 22V404000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

On certain 2020-2022 Taycan vehicles, a software error can cause the center screen, including the rearview camera image, and the touch control panel to stay black right after the car is started. The Real-Top-View image, including the rearview camera, can also flicker instead of showing a steady picture.

Porsche Taycan Seat Wiring May Disable Airbags high

Reported Apr 26, 2022 · Campaign 22V278000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

On certain 2020-2021 Porsche Taycan vehicles, the wire harness for the driver or passenger seat may become damaged when the seat is adjusted from side to side. A damaged wire harness in the seat may turn off the air bags for that seat. Dealers will check the seat wire harness for damage, add tape to help hold it in place, and repair any damaged wires.

Porsche Taycan Rear Seat Belt Blocks Child Seat medium

Reported Feb 22, 2022 · Campaign 22V100000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

Some Taycan vehicles with the optional rear center seat have a seat belt buckle wiring harness that can block access to the lower anchor points for the right rear seat. This can make it hard to properly attach a child seat in that seat.

Porsche Taycan Front and Rear Suspension Misaligned low

Reported Feb 2, 2022 · Campaign 22V052000 · Model years: 2021

The front and rear suspension on this Taycan may be misaligned. When the suspension is out of alignment, the tires can wear prematurely or unevenly.

Porsche Taycan Rear Suspension Bolts May Be Loose high

Reported Nov 19, 2021 · Campaign 21V911000 · Model years: 2020

Porsche is recalling certain Taycan vehicles because the screw connections that hold the front and rear upper control arms on the rear axle may be loose. These connections are part of the suspension system that keeps the wheels properly attached and controlled. If a connection isn't tight, it could work its way loose or fail over time. Dealers will replace the affected screw connections to correct the problem.

Porsche Taycan Hazard Lights May Activate Falsely medium

Reported Oct 13, 2021 · Campaign 21V794000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

The pre-collision automatic hazard lights on certain Taycan vehicles may turn on during situations that are not actual emergencies. Because the lights are not being used the way they're meant to be, the vehicle does not meet the federal requirements for this equipment.

Porsche 911 and Taycan Brake Booster Rod Detachment high

Reported Sep 8, 2021 · Campaign 21V697000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

Porsche is recalling certain 911 Carrera and Taycan vehicles because a rod connection in the brake booster may be loose. The rod could come loose and detach completely. If it detaches, the brake pedal can fail, and a failed brake pedal raises the risk of a crash.

Porsche seat belts may not lock child car seats properly high

Reported Aug 4, 2021 · Campaign 21V608000 · Model years: 2021

On some Porsche cars, including the Panamera, Taycan and 911, the seat belts have a locking feature used to hold a child seat in place. That lock can switch off too soon, so the belt may not hold the child restraint securely. Because of this, the cars do not meet a federal safety standard covering occupant protection in a crash. Dealers will check the seat belt assemblies and replace them if needed at no cost.

Porsche Taycan Software Fault Can Cut Power high

Reported Jun 29, 2021 · Campaign 21V486000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

A software problem in the monitoring system can wrongly detect a fault and shut down the powertrain, causing the car to suddenly lose the power that drives it. Dealers will reprogram the power electronics and the engine control unit to fix this, free of charge.

Porsche Panamera and Taycan: Front Suspension Arm Defect high

Reported May 17, 2021 · Campaign 21V362000 · Model years: 2021

The front lower trailing arms on these vehicles were not manufactured correctly and may break. Dealers will replace the front lower trailing arms with correctly made parts, free of charge.

Porsche Taycan Suspension Bolts May Loosen high

Reported Mar 10, 2021 · Campaign 21V157000 · Model years: 2021

Certain Porsche vehicles have suspension parts, such as nuts and bolts, that may not have been tightened correctly when the vehicle was built. These parts can loosen, and a suspension component can come loose or detach from the vehicle. A suspension component that comes loose can cause a sudden loss of vehicle stability and control, which increases the chance of a crash.

Porsche Taycan recall questions

How many recalls does the Porsche Taycan have?

NHTSA lists 25 safety recalls covering the 2020–2024 Porsche Taycan. 19 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Porsche Cayenne, Panamera and Taycan Rearview Camera Failure" (NHTSA campaign 25V896000), reported Dec 19, 2025.

Which Porsche Taycan model year has the most recalls?

The 2021 Porsche Taycan has the most, with 21 recalls. The 2024 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 6. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Porsche Taycan?

No. None of the 25 Porsche Taycan recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Porsche Taycan has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Porsche dealer, including for second owners.
