Genesis and Hyundai Seat Belt Anchors May Detach high

Reported Apr 6, 2026 · Campaign 26V218000 · Model years: 2023, 2024, 2025

The seat belt anchors for the driver and front passenger may come loose from the vehicle. If an anchor detaches, the seat belt will not properly hold the occupant in place during a crash, which raises the risk of injury. Dealers will inspect the anchors and reinforce or replace them as needed, free of charge.

Hyundai Ioniq 6 charging port door may detach medium

Reported Sep 12, 2025 · Campaign 25V606000 · Model years: 2023, 2024, 2025

This recall affects certain Hyundai Ioniq 6 electric vehicles. The panel that covers the charging port may come loose and fall off the car. Once it detaches, it can end up in the road, creating a hazard for other vehicles driving nearby.

Hyundai Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, Genesis GV60 Charging Unit Failure high

Reported Nov 18, 2024 · Campaign 24V868000 · Model years: 2023, 2024, 2025

Certain Hyundai Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, and Genesis GV60 vehicles have an Integrated Charging Control Unit that may become damaged. When this happens, the unit can stop charging the 12-volt battery. If the 12-volt battery is not being charged, the vehicle can lose drive power.

Hyundai Ioniq 6 Differential Gear Bolts May Loosen high

Reported Apr 19, 2024 · Campaign 24V283000 · Model years: 2024

The bolts in the differential gear drive unit may not have been tightened properly. If they loosen, the vehicle could lose drive power, or the differential could lock up while driving.

Hyundai and Genesis EV Charging Unit Can Fail high

Reported Mar 15, 2024 · Campaign 24V204000 · Model years: 2023, 2024

On these Hyundai and Genesis electric vehicles, the Integrated Charging Control Unit may become damaged and stop charging the 12-volt battery. This can result in a loss of drive power.

Hyundai IONIQ 5, IONIQ 6, Genesis GV60 Driveshaft May Break high

Reported Feb 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V065000 · Model years: 2023

Certain 2023 Hyundai IONIQ 5, IONIQ 6, and Genesis GV60 vehicles have a rear inner driveshaft that may not have been heat-treated correctly during manufacturing. Because of this, the driveshaft can break when it is put under load, such as while accelerating. If the driveshaft breaks, the vehicle loses the power that turns the wheels, meaning it can no longer drive forward under its own power.

Hyundai Ioniq 6 recall questions

How many recalls does the Hyundai Ioniq 6 have?

NHTSA lists 6 safety recalls covering the 2023–2025 Hyundai Ioniq 6. 5 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Genesis and Hyundai Seat Belt Anchors May Detach" (NHTSA campaign 26V218000), reported Apr 6, 2026.

Which Hyundai Ioniq 6 model year has the most recalls?

The 2023 Hyundai Ioniq 6 has the most, with 5 recalls. The 2025 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 3. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Hyundai Ioniq 6?

No. None of the 6 Hyundai Ioniq 6 recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Hyundai Ioniq 6 has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Hyundai dealer, including for second owners.
