4 recalls covering model years 2018. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Hyundai Santa Fe Sport brake part may cause engine fire critical

Reported 2022-10-27 · Campaign 22V810000 · Model years: 2018

The control box for the anti-lock brakes can go bad and cause an electrical short. That short can start a fire in the engine compartment — and it can happen while you are driving or even while the car is sitting parked. Until it is fixed, Hyundai says to park the car outside and away from buildings. A dealer will replace a fuse in the brake system at no cost to you.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Santa Fe brake module can spark engine fire critical

Reported 2022-02-03 · Campaign 22V056000 · Model years: 2018

The control box for the anti-lock brakes can fail and cause an electrical short. That short can start a fire under the hood — and it can happen while the car is parked, not just while you are driving. The fix is to replace a fuse for that brake system, and it costs you nothing. Because a parked car can catch fire, Hyundai asks owners to park outside and away from houses, garages and other buildings until the work is done.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Santa Fe Sport side curtain airbags may fail high

Reported 2017-12-13 · Campaign 17V801000 · Model years: 2018

Your Santa Fe Sport has side curtain airbags — the ones that drop down from above the side windows to protect your head in a crash. A piece inside them can come loose while the airbag is inflating. If that happens, the airbag may not fill out the way it should, so it protects you less in a crash. The loose piece could also fly into the passenger area and hit someone. Dealers will replace both curtain airbags free.

Hyundai Santa Fe Sport front springs may break apart high

Reported 2017-09-20 · Campaign 17V577000 · Model years: 2018

Some 2018 Hyundai Santa Fe Sport SUVs were built with front coil springs — the big metal springs that hold up the front of the vehicle — that were not made correctly. Under certain loads while driving, a spring can crack and break apart. A broken piece can stab into the tire and flatten it, which can make the driver lose control. All of the affected vehicles were still unsold on dealer lots, and the fix is done before sale.
