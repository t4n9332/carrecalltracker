Hyundai Kona Electric recalls

4 recalls covering model years 2019, 2020, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Hyundai Kona rear center seat belt buckle may fail high

Reported 2026-07-14 · Campaign 26V452000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

Hyundai found that the seat belt buckle for the middle rear seat may not hold a passenger properly in a crash. Someone belted into that center spot might not be restrained the way the belt is supposed to restrain them, so they could be hurt more badly in a collision. Hyundai is telling owners that nobody should sit in the rear center seat until the fix is done. A dealer will replace the buckle assembly at no charge.

Hyundai Kona and Ioniq Electric battery may short-circuit critical

Reported 2021-03-01 · Campaign 21V127000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

The big lithium-ion battery that powers these electric cars can develop an internal short circuit, which can start a fire. As a first step, Hyundai offers a battery software update you can start yourself through the car's touchscreen, or a dealer can do it for you. After that, a dealer will inspect the battery pack and replace it if there is a problem. All of it is free. Park outside, away from buildings, until the update is done.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Kona Electric and Nexo may lose braking power high

Reported 2020-12-01 · Campaign 20V748000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

The electronic brake system can misread a sensor signal and cut back how much stopping power the brakes provide. If that happens, the car takes longer to stop than the driver expects, which can lead to a crash. This affects certain 2019-2020 Nexo fuel cell cars and 2019-2021 Kona Electric cars. A dealer updates the brake system software free of charge, and the repair has been available since January 2021.

Hyundai Kona Electric battery may short out and burn critical

Reported 2020-10-13 · Campaign 20V630000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

The large battery that powers your Kona Electric may short out inside. An electrical short like that can start a fire. Because of that risk, Hyundai says to park the car outside and away from your house, garage, or any other building until the repair is done. At the dealer, the software that manages the battery will be updated, and the battery will be inspected and replaced free if it is found to be bad. Repairs began November 2020.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).
