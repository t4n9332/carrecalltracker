Hyundai Kona recalls

7 recalls covering model years 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Hyundai Kona rear center seat belt buckle may fail high

Reported 2026-07-14 · Campaign 26V452000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

Hyundai found that the seat belt buckle for the middle rear seat may not hold a passenger properly in a crash. Someone belted into that center spot might not be restrained the way the belt is supposed to restrain them, so they could be hurt more badly in a collision. Hyundai is telling owners that nobody should sit in the rear center seat until the fix is done. A dealer will replace the buckle assembly at no charge.

Hyundai Elantra, Kona and Venue can lose engine power high

Reported 2024-05-01 · Campaign 24V308000 · Model years: 2024

Your engine has a part called the EGR valve, which routes some exhaust back through the engine. On these vehicles, the wiring inside that valve can short out. If that happens, the engine can lose power, so the car may slow down or stop moving even while you are pressing the gas pedal. That could happen anywhere, including in traffic or on the highway. The dealer replaces the entire valve assembly at no cost to you.

Hyundai Kona battery cables can rub and spark fire critical

Reported 2023-12-26 · Campaign 23V901000 · Model years: 2024

Hyundai found that on some 2024 Kona vehicles the heavy 12-volt battery cables can rub against a metal bracket in the engine bay. Over time that rubbing can wear through the cable covering, and in a crash the bare wire can short out. A short like this can start a fire under the hood. Dealers slip a protective sleeve over the wiring so the cables can no longer be worn through.

Hyundai Kona rear seat belts may not work properly high

Reported 2023-09-22 · Campaign 23V650000 · Model years: 2024

The seat belts for the two outer rear seats in some 2024 Kona vehicles may not work correctly. The problem is in the retractor, the spool inside the belt that pulls it snug and locks it in a crash. If a belt does not lock or hold as it should, a passenger in the back may not be held safely during a crash. Dealers will check both belts and replace the retractor if needed.

Hyundai SUVs and sedans: oil pump controller can overheat critical

Reported 2023-07-27 · Campaign 23V526000 · Model years: 2023

These cars have a system that shuts the engine off at stoplights to save fuel. The electronic controller for that system's oil pump may have damaged parts inside, and it can overheat. An overheating pump unit can start a fire in the car. Hyundai says park outside and away from buildings until the repair is done. Dealers check the oil pump controller and replace it if needed, at no charge to you.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Elantra, Kona, Veloster engines can fail and burn critical

Reported 2021-04-28 · Campaign 21V301000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

The 2.0-liter engine in these cars has piston rings that may not have been hardened correctly when they were made. Over time the rings can wear, damage the engine, and let oil escape. Hyundai is also adding software that listens for piston ring noise so trouble can be caught early. A badly damaged engine can shut off while you are driving, and leaking oil can drip onto hot exhaust parts and start a fire. A dealer will check the engine and replace it if needed.

Hyundai Kona sticker lists wrong axle weight limit low

Reported 2020-01-17 · Campaign 20V022000 · Model years: 2020

Some 2020 Hyundai Konas left the factory with a sticker that lists the wrong weight limit for the axles. That label tells you how much weight the vehicle and its tires are built to carry. If the number is too high, an owner could load the car heavier than it should be. Hyundai says the label does not meet federal rules for certification and tire and wheel information. A dealer will place a corrected sticker over the wrong one, free.
