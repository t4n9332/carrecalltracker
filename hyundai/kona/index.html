Hyundai Kona Rear Center Seat Belt Buckle May Fail high

Reported Jul 14, 2026 · Campaign 26V452000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

Some Hyundai Kona and Kona Electric vehicles have a seat belt buckle in the rear center seat that may not properly hold a passenger in place if the vehicle is in a crash. This means that seat position may not provide the protection a seat belt is supposed to give during a collision.

Hyundai Elantra, Kona and Venue may lose drive power high

Reported May 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V308000 · Model years: 2024

Hyundai is recalling certain Elantra, Venue, and Kona vehicles because of a problem with a part of the exhaust system called the exhaust gas recirculation valve. An electrical short circuit can happen inside this valve assembly, and when that happens the vehicle can lose drive power. A dealer will replace the exhaust gas recirculation valve assembly at no cost to the owner. Hyundai mailed letters to owners about this recall, and the company has a customer service line for questions.

Hyundai Kona 12-Volt Battery Cables May Short Circuit high

Reported Dec 26, 2023 · Campaign 23V901000 · Model years: 2024

Hyundai is recalling certain 2024 Kona vehicles because the 12-volt battery cables may chafe against the engine control module bracket. This chafing could cause the cables to short circuit, including during a crash. A short circuit increases the risk of a fire in the engine compartment. Dealers will install sheathing over the wiring to prevent the chafing.

Hyundai Kona Rear Seat Belts May Fail medium

Reported Sep 22, 2023 · Campaign 23V650000 · Model years: 2024

Hyundai says the seat belt assemblies in the left and right rear seats on some Kona vehicles may not function properly. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet federal seat belt safety standards. Hyundai is recalling the affected vehicles so dealers can inspect and fix the rear seat belt retractors.

Hyundai Sonata, Tucson, Palisade Oil Pump Fire Risk critical

Reported Jul 27, 2023 · Campaign 23V526000 · Model years: 2023

Some Hyundai vehicles have an electronic controller for the engine stop-start oil pump that may contain damaged electrical components. This damage can cause the pump controller to overheat, which increases the risk of a vehicle fire.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Elantra, Kona, Veloster Engine Damage Risk high

Reported Apr 28, 2021 · Campaign 21V301000 · Model years: 2019, 2020, 2021

Hyundai is recalling certain Elantra, Kona and Veloster vehicles equipped with 2.0L engines. A part inside the engine, called the piston oil rings, may not have been properly heat-treated during manufacturing. Because of this, the engine could suffer damage over time.

Hyundai Kona Certification Label Shows Wrong Weight Rating low

Reported Jan 17, 2020 · Campaign 20V022000 · Model years: 2020

Certain 2020 Hyundai Kona vehicles have a certification label that may show the wrong Gross Axle Weight Rating, which is the maximum amount of weight each axle of the vehicle is rated to safely carry. Because the number on the label is incorrect, an owner who relies on it when loading the vehicle could unknowingly end up putting more weight on the vehicle than it was actually designed to handle.

Hyundai Kona recall questions

How many recalls does the Hyundai Kona have?

NHTSA lists 7 safety recalls covering the 2019–2025 Hyundai Kona. 5 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Hyundai Kona Rear Center Seat Belt Buckle May Fail" (NHTSA campaign 26V452000), reported Jul 14, 2026.

Which Hyundai Kona model year has the most recalls?

The 2024 Hyundai Kona has the most, with 4 recalls. The 2019 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 1. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Hyundai Kona?

Yes. 1 Hyundai Kona recall carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA: Hyundai Sonata, Tucson, Palisade Oil Pump Fire Risk (23V526000). These apply only to the affected vehicles, so check your VIN.

How do I check whether my Hyundai Kona has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Hyundai dealer, including for second owners.
