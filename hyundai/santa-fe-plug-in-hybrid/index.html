5 recalls covering model years 2022. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Hyundai Elantra and Santa Fe: rearview camera may not display medium

Reported 2024-11-21 · Campaign 24V879000 · Model years: 2022

A circuit board inside the backup camera on these Hyundai vehicles can be damaged, and when that happens the camera picture may not appear on the screen as you back up. You could be looking at a blank display instead of the area behind the car. Federal rules require a working rear view, so these vehicles fall short of the standard. The fix is a brand-new rearview camera, installed by the dealer at no cost to you.

Hyundai Santa Fe and Santa Cruz Tow Hitch Fire Risk critical

Reported 2023-03-17 · Campaign 23V181000 · Model years: 2022

Water can get into the small electrical box that controls the trailer hitch wiring. If water sits on the circuit board inside, it can short out and start a fire — while you are driving or while the vehicle is parked and turned off. Affected vehicles have tow hitch wiring that was fitted at the factory or added later by a Hyundai dealer. A dealer will install a new fuse and a wiring extension kit at no cost.

⚠ NHTSA advises: park outside and away from structures (fire risk).

Hyundai Santa Fe Plug-In Hybrid Fuel Tank May Leak critical

Reported 2023-01-25 · Campaign 23V028000 · Model years: 2022

The fuel tank on these plug-in hybrids may have been formed incorrectly at the factory. A badly made tank can leak gasoline. If leaking fuel reaches something hot enough to light it, or a spark, it can start a fire. Hyundai has not told owners to park the car outside or to stop driving it. A dealer will inspect the tank and replace it if it is one of the bad ones, at no charge.

Hyundai Santa Fe Hybrid seat belt part can explode high

Reported 2022-10-31 · Campaign 22V816000 · Model years: 2022

Seat belts have a device that yanks the belt tight the instant a crash begins, and it works by setting off a small explosive charge. In these vehicles that charge can blow the device apart instead of firing the way it should, sending metal pieces flying inside the cabin where they can hit the driver or front passenger. The fix is quick: a dealer fits a cap over the front seat belt devices so they stay together, free of charge.

Hyundai Santa Fe Hybrid gauge display may appear upside down medium

Reported 2021-12-28 · Campaign 21V00K000 · Model years: 2022

The screen in front of the driver — the one showing your speed, fuel, and warning lights — may come on flipped upside down when you start the vehicle, which makes it impossible to read. That means you cannot see how fast you are going or notice a warning light telling you something is wrong. Hyundai says this breaks a federal rule for displays. Dealers will replace the whole instrument cluster at no cost.
