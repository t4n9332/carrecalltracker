Reported 2026-07-21 · Campaign 26V470000 · Model years: 2025

Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator drive shaft can break critical

Reported 2020-11-10 · Campaign 20V693000 · Model years: 2020

Certain 2020 Explorer and Aviator all-wheel-drive models can have a drive shaft that cracks apart at a weld. The drive shaft is the spinning bar that sends power to the rear wheels. If it breaks, the vehicle can roll away even when you have put it in Park, or it can suddenly stop driving. A broken shaft can also bend, come loose, and hit the fuel tank, which can cause a fuel leak or a fire. Dealers will check the drive shaft and replace it if needed.

Ford and Lincoln backup camera image may go blank medium

Reported 2020-09-23 · Campaign 20V575000 · Model years: 2020

A poor electrical connection to the backup camera can make the screen go blank or show a fuzzy, distorted picture when you shift into reverse. It may happen on and off rather than all the time. If you cannot see what is behind you, it is easier to back into a person or an object. Federal rules require a working rear camera on these vehicles. Dealers will replace the camera at no charge.

Ford Explorer second-row center seat belt buckle may be loose high

Reported 2020-09-10 · Campaign 20V549000 · Model years: 2020

In these Explorers, the seat belt buckle for the middle seat in the second row may not have been fastened to the seat properly at the factory. If that buckle is not secured, the belt may fail to hold a passenger in place during a crash, so someone riding there could be hurt worse than they otherwise would be. Ford dealers will replace the entire second-row center seat assembly, free of charge, on affected vehicles.

Ford Explorer, Lincoln Aviator side airbag bolts may be loose high

Reported 2020-08-21 · Campaign 20V497000 · Model years: 2020

The side airbags sit inside the front seat backs. On these vehicles, the bolts holding the airbag and the seat back panel may not have been tightened enough at the factory. If they are loose, the side airbag may not work the way it should in a crash, so it may not protect the person in that seat properly. A dealer will take off the panel on the back of one or both front seats and check that everything is tight.

Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator may leak fuel critical

Reported 2019-12-04 · Campaign 19V859000 · Model years: 2020

On certain 2020 Ford Explorer and Lincoln Aviator vehicles, a protective sleeve on one fuel line can rub against the plastic line that carries liquid fuel. Over time that rubbing wears a hole through the line and gasoline can leak out. Leaking fuel near anything hot or sparking can start a fire. The fix is simple: a dealer installs a longer protective sleeve over the liquid fuel line so the two parts no longer rub.

Ford Explorer wiring can rub through and cause fire critical

Reported 2019-09-30 · Campaign 19V687000 · Model years: 2020

On certain 2020 Explorers with the 2.3L or 3.3L engine, a bundle of electrical wires may not have been tied down properly. It can rub against the spinning air conditioning pulley and wear through the wire insulation. If a bare wire then touches the main battery wire, it can short out and start a fire. A dealer will add a strap to hold the wiring safely in place, free of charge. The recall began October 21, 2019.

Ford and Lincoln seat backs may be dangerously weak high

Reported 2019-08-28 · Campaign 19V633000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020

The mechanism that lets a seat back tilt is supposed to have three metal catches holding it in place, and on these vehicles one catch may be missing. With a catch missing, the seat back is weaker than it should be and may feel loose. In a crash, a seat back like this may not hold the person in the seat properly, which can make injuries worse. Dealers will inspect the seats and replace them as needed, free of charge.

Ford Explorer, Lincoln Aviator: missing cover risks rollaway high

Reported 2019-08-05 · Campaign 19V575000 · Model years: 2020

Inside the vehicle there is a hand lever that can release the transmission from Park. It is supposed to be hidden under a cover, and on these vehicles that cover may be missing. Without it, the lever can be moved by accident and the vehicle could roll away. Some vehicles were also left in a factory setting that hides dashboard warnings and does not show which gear is selected. Dealers fix both at no cost.

Ford Explorer fuel pressure sensor may leak gasoline critical

Reported 2018-11-14 · Campaign 18V807000 · Model years: 2018

An assembly mistake means the fuel pressure sensor on these 2018 Ford Explorers can leak gasoline. Leaking fuel near anything hot enough to ignite it can start a fire under the hood. This affects Explorers built with the 2.3-liter or 3.5-liter turbocharged engines. The fix is not a patch: the dealer replaces the whole fuel line assembly, free of charge, so the leaking part is gone for good.
