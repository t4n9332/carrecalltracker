Ford E-350 recalls

13 recalls covering model years 2018, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Ford E-350 and E-450 engine cover gets too hot medium

Reported 2025-11-17 · Campaign 25V793000 · Model years: 2021

These 2021 Ford E-350 and E-450 vans got an incomplete software fix during an earlier recall, so the problem it was meant to solve is still there. The engine cover — the large housing inside the cab between the front seats — can get very hot, especially when the engine idles at a raised speed for a long time. Touching it can burn your skin. Dealers will load the correct engine software at no cost.

Ford E-350 and E-450 vans can lose power steering high

Reported 2024-02-09 · Campaign 24V097000 · Model years: 2021, 2022

On these vans with dual rear wheels, the high-pressure line that feeds the power steering is not connected well enough where it meets the brake booster unit. The connection can fail and all the power steering fluid can drain out. You then lose power steering and the extra assist that makes the brake pedal easy to press, so steering and stopping get much harder. Even if your van was fixed under the earlier recall, it needs this new repair.

Ford E-350 and F-650 trucks can roll away in Park high

Reported 2023-09-08 · Campaign 23V625000 · Model years: 2024, 2025

When you put an automatic transmission in Park, a small metal piece called the park pawl locks the transmission so the vehicle cannot roll. A flaw in how the transmission was cast means that pawl may not lock in all the way. If it does not, the truck can roll away after you think you have parked it, even with the shifter in Park. Dealers will inspect and replace the transmission or internal parts as needed.

Ford E-350, E-450 and F-59 fuel tanks may rust critical

Reported 2022-12-14 · Campaign 22V925000 · Model years: 2022, 2023

On these vans and truck chassis, the paint along a welded seam on the gas tank can peel off. Once bare metal is exposed, it can rust through, and gasoline can start leaking from the tank. If that leaking fuel reaches anything hot or a spark, it can start a fire. Dealers will inspect the fuel tank and replace it if needed, at no charge to you.

Ford trucks and vans missing tire information label low

Reported 2022-05-27 · Campaign 22V384000 · Model years: 2023

The safety label on your vehicle is missing the tire size, rim type and correct tire pressure numbers for the front and rear axles. Nothing is actually broken on the vehicle. The problem is that you, a tire shop, or a future owner could fit the wrong size tire or wheel, or inflate the tires to the wrong pressure, because the numbers are not there to check. Dealers put on a correct replacement label.

Ford E-350 and E-450 vans may lose power steering suddenly high

Reported 2022-02-16 · Campaign 22V089000 · Model years: 2021, 2022

On these vans with dual rear wheels, the hose carrying power steering fluid may not be properly connected where it meets the brake booster unit. That connection can let go and the fluid can drain out all at once. With the fluid gone, the steering wheel becomes very hard to turn and the brakes take much more foot pressure to work. A dealer will inspect the connection and replace the power steering lines if needed.

Ford E-350 and E-450 hot engine cover burn risk medium

Reported 2020-12-17 · Campaign 20V795000 · Model years: 2021

On these 2021 E-350 and E-450 vans, the insulation on the underside of the engine cover may be in the wrong place. The engine cover sits inside the cab near the driver and front passenger, and without proper insulation it can get very hot. Skin that rests against the hot spots can be burned. Dealers will check for gaps in the seal and insulation, add insulation patches where needed, and update the engine software — all free.

Ford E-350 and E-450 wiring can stall engine high

Reported 2020-03-23 · Campaign 20V175000 · Model years: 2021

A bundle of wires at the back of your van can rub against the metal frame and wear through. Those wires feed the fuel pump and the anti-lock brakes. If the fuel pump wiring is damaged, the engine can shut off suddenly while you are driving. If the brake wiring is damaged, the anti-lock brakes and the roll stability system can stop working. The dealer will check the wiring and tape it or splice in new wire.

Ford E-350, E-450 and F-550 rear axle may fail high

Reported 2020-02-21 · Campaign 20V099000 · Model years: 2021

The rear axle on these vehicles may have left the factory with too little gear oil inside. Without enough oil, the rear axle can fail and the driveshaft, the spinning shaft that sends power to the rear wheels, can come apart underneath the vehicle. If that happens, you can suddenly lose the ability to drive. On top of that, if the parking brake is not set, the vehicle can roll away on its own. Dealers will check the fluid, top it up, or replace the axle free.

Ford E-350 shuttle bus back-up camera image may flip medium

Reported 2019-06-18 · Campaign 19V466000 · Model years: 2018

These shuttle buses, built on 2017-2018 Ford E-350 and E-450 vans, use an ASA Electronics Voyager monitor to show the picture from the back-up camera. The monitor can slip back to its factory default settings, and that flips the picture left-to-right like a mirror. A driver backing up could then steer the wrong way when trying to avoid a person or object behind the bus. A free part will be added to stop the picture from reversing.

Ford and GMC ambulances: brake lights turn on by themselves medium

Reported 2019-05-03 · Campaign 19V342000 · Model years: 2018

The brake lights on these ambulances were wired in a way that can make them light up even when the driver has not touched the brake pedal. Drivers behind may think the ambulance is slowing down when it is not, and may brake hard or swerve for no reason. The lights themselves are not broken; the problem is how they are connected. Whelen will replace the brake lights at no cost to the owner.

Excellance ambulances: brake lights can turn on by themselves medium

Reported 2019-03-26 · Campaign 19V236000 · Model years: 2018

These are ambulances and emergency vehicles built on Ford, Chevrolet, Dodge, Freightliner and International chassis and fitted with Whelen brake lights. Depending on how the vehicle was wired, the brake lights can come on even when the driver is not pressing the brake pedal. That breaks the federal rule for vehicle lamps. Whelen replaces the brake lights free of charge, and owners were told to contact Whelen directly rather than a dealer. The recall began March 21, 2019.

Ford Transit and E-350 van fold-away seat backs unlock high

Reported 2019-03-18 · Campaign 19V205000 · Model years: 2018

These 2015-2018 Ford Transit and E-350 vans were modified and fitted with Freedman fold-away seats. The seat back may not stay locked in position when weight pushes against it, such as in a crash or a sudden stop. The seats do not meet the federal safety standard for seating systems. If the seat back gives way, the person sitting in it can be hurt. Quality Van Sales will have the fold-away seats inspected and repaired free of charge.
