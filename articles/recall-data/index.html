What the Recall Data Shows: Model Years Covered, Problem Areas and Brands

By the Car Recall Tracker editors · Updated Oct 10, 2026 · How we write

We track every NHTSA safety recall for model years 2018–2027. That is 2,280 recall campaigns across 32 brands. Here is what that data shows about which systems fail, which brands file the most recalls, and how often owners are told to stop driving. The figures update automatically every day.

A "campaign" is one NHTSA recall number. One campaign can cover several models and model years, and anywhere from a handful to millions of vehicles.

The categories are broad. A software error that disables a rear camera might be filed under the camera, the electrical system or the instrument cluster depending on the manufacturer. Our problem guides break this down by brand.

Recalls by brand

Large brands with many models file more recalls. That says more about the size of their line-up than about quality: a brand selling twenty models has more opportunities for a defect than one selling three. Recall counts also reflect how readily a manufacturer recalls, which is a good thing for owners.

What this means for owners

  • Recalls are routine. Almost every popular model has had several. What matters is whether your own car's recalls have been repaired — check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
  • Stop-driving advisories are rare but critical. Only about 1.1% of recalls carry one. If yours does, act the same day. See the do-not-drive list.
  • Software is a growing share. About 2.5% of recalls are remedied over the air. Confirm the update actually installed — see how OTA recall fixes work.
  • Counts alone do not rank reliability. A model with many recalls but quick, complete repairs can be a better buy than one with few recalls and many unaddressed owner complaints. The watchlist looks at that second group.

Method

Source: NHTSA recall data via its public API, collected daily for every make and model in model years 2018–2027. Each campaign is counted once, no matter how many models it covers. Brand and model counts count each campaign once per brand or model it touches, so they add up to more than the total. Problem areas come from NHTSA's component field, mapped to broad groups; recalls with components we cannot classify are left out of that table. "Do not drive", "park outside" and over-the-air flags come from NHTSA's own recall data fields.
