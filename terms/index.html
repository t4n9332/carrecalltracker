Where the data comes from

Recall notices and owner-complaint counts are retrieved from the public API of the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and refreshed daily. NHTSA data is in the public domain. We do not alter the official notice text, which is shown on every recall page. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by NHTSA or any vehicle manufacturer. Manufacturer names and model names belong to their respective owners and are used only to identify the vehicles concerned.

How the explanations are produced

The plain-English summaries, problem guides, used-car notes and severity labels are generated automatically from the official notice text by a language model, using a fixed set of rules, and are checked by sampling against the source notice. They are written to be easier to read than the legal wording, not to replace it. They can contain mistakes, may lag behind an updated notice, and never state that a vehicle is safe to keep driving. If the summary and the official notice disagree, the official notice is correct.

Accuracy and completeness

We cover model years 2018 and newer for the makes listed on the home page. A model that does not appear here may still have recalls. A recall shown here may already have been repaired on your vehicle. The only way to know whether a specific car has an unrepaired recall is the official NHTSA VIN lookup or the manufacturer.
