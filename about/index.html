About Car Recall Tracker

Car Recall Tracker republishes U.S. vehicle safety recall notices from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) public API and adds a short explanation in everyday language: what is wrong, how serious it is, and what an owner should do next.

The explanations, problem guides and used-car notes are generated automatically from the official notice text. They can contain errors and are not legal or mechanical advice. The official notice, shown on every page, is the authoritative source. If something looks wrong, please email t4n0321@gmail.com.

What the severity labels mean

Every recall is a safety defect by definition, so none of them should be ignored. The label is our own rough ranking, assigned automatically from the wording of the official notice. It is not an NHTSA rating.

LabelWhat it means here
criticalThe notice tells owners to stop driving or to park outside, or describes a fire risk or a failure that can cause a crash with no warning.
highA core safety system (brakes, steering, air bags, seat belts, engine stalling) may not work when needed.
mediumA real safety risk, but one that usually gives warning or matters only in particular conditions, such as a rear camera image that fails to display.
lowLabelling, documentation or minor compliance problems with little direct effect on how the vehicle drives.

Owner complaint figures come from NHTSA's public complaints database. We publish counts only. Complaints are unverified reports from owners, and models that sell in larger numbers naturally collect more of them, so a high count is not proof of a defect. The watchlist compares complaint areas with recall areas by broad category and can miss a recall filed under a different label.

Why this site exists

Many recalled vehicles never get their free repair. Letters go to old addresses, cars change hands, and the notices themselves are written in regulatory language that is hard to act on. The aim here is simple: make it quick to find every recall for a specific vehicle, understand what it means in a minute, and know exactly what to do next. Each make and model page also lists the manufacturer's official recall lookup and customer service number, so owners can go straight to the source.

How the data is built

  • Recalls are collected every day from NHTSA's public recall API for every make and model, model years 2018 and newer. Each record keeps the official summary, consequence and remedy text unchanged.
  • Complaint counts come from NHTSA's public complaints API. We store and publish totals by problem area and year only, never the text of individual complaints.
  • Problem guides group a make's recalls in one problem area (for example fuel system or air bags) once there are at least three of them.
  • Used-car pages combine a model year's recalls and complaint counts into a pre-purchase checklist.
  • Manufacturer contacts are checked by hand against the phone numbers printed in NHTSA owner notices.

Editorial process and use of AI

The short plain-English explanations on recall pages, the problem guides and the used-car notes are drafted by an AI language model working only from the official notice text, under fixed rules: describe only what the notice says, never tell owners a vehicle is safe to keep driving, and always point to the official remedy. They are clearly accompanied by the original notice on every page.

Because automated text can be wrong, we check it. Random samples of explanations are regularly compared line by line against the official notices, and the instructions are tightened whenever a pattern of mistakes shows up; affected pages are then rewritten. Recall pages that do not have an explanation yet, and used-car pages with too little data to say anything useful, are kept out of search engines.

The articles section — on topics like how recalls happen, reimbursement and buying a used car with an open recall — is written and reviewed by the editor, based on NHTSA's published guidance and federal regulations.

Corrections

If you find an error — a wrong model listed, an explanation that does not match the notice, an outdated phone number — email t4n0321@gmail.com with the page address. We fix confirmed errors promptly, usually in the next daily update.
