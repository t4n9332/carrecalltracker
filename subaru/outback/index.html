Subaru Outback recalls

15 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Subaru Outback and Legacy passenger airbag may not deploy high

Reported 2024-03-22 · Campaign 24V227000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

Your car has sensors in the front passenger seat that tell the airbag system whether someone is sitting there. A small electrical part inside those sensors can crack and short out. If that happens, the front passenger airbag may not go off in a crash, leaving whoever is riding in that seat without the protection the airbag is supposed to provide. Subaru dealers will replace the sensors in the front passenger seat at no charge to you.

Subaru Crosstrek, Forester, Legacy, Outback reverse lights may fail medium

Reported 2023-11-09 · Campaign 23V755000 · Model years: 2021, 2022, 2023

A weld on a switch that tells the car which gear you are in was not done well enough, so water can seep inside and break it. When that switch fails, your reversing lights may not come on and the backup camera screen may go blank. That makes it easy to miss someone or something behind you while backing up. A dealer will replace the switch free of charge.

Subaru Outback, Legacy, Ascent, Impreza driveshaft bolts loosen high

Reported 2023-09-21 · Campaign 23V647000 · Model years: 2023

On certain 2023 Subaru Outback, Legacy, Ascent and Impreza vehicles, the bolts holding the middle support of the driveshaft can work loose. The driveshaft is the spinning shaft under the car that sends engine power toward the wheels. If those bolts loosen, the front end of the shaft can come apart from the car while you are driving. A driveshaft that separates underneath a moving vehicle increases the risk of a crash.

Subaru Impreza and Outback windshields may not be bonded high

Reported 2022-09-22 · Campaign 22V712000 · Model years: 2022

The wrong clear paint coating was used on part of the body where the windshield is glued in. Because of that, the glue may not hold the windshield firmly to the car. In a crash, the windshield could come loose and fall away, which can hurt the people inside. This one cannot be fixed with a normal repair — Subaru is offering to trade your car for another one or buy it back.

Subaru Outback and Legacy transmission software may break drive chain high

Reported 2022-07-08 · Campaign 22V485000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

The computer that controls your automatic transmission has a software mistake. It can let the transmission start pulling before the internal drive chain is fully clamped in place. The chain can then slip and break, and the vehicle can suddenly lose power to the wheels while you are driving. Dealers will reload the correct software, check the stored data for signs of slipping, and look at the chain guide. If there is damage, they replace the transmission.

Subaru Outback wiring fault may cause engine stall high

Reported 2022-07-01 · Campaign 22V473000 · Model years: 2022

The wiring bundle that runs to the engine may have been damaged during building, leaving a loose connection at the engine's computer. That computer manages how the engine runs. A loose connection can make the engine cut out and stall, which could happen while you are driving and leave you without power. Subaru will replace both the engine wiring harness and the engine computer at no cost to you. Owner letters were expected to go out August 30, 2022.

Subaru Ascent, Legacy, Outback Transmission Software Can Break Chain high

Reported 2021-12-09 · Campaign 21V955000 · Model years: 2020

A programming mistake in the computer that runs the automatic transmission can let the transmission start pulling power before an internal drive chain is fully clamped. Over time that chain can slip and break. If it does, the vehicle loses drive to the wheels even though the engine is still running. Dealers will reload the software, check stored data and the chain guide for damage, and replace the transmission for free if damage is found.

Subaru, Toyota 86 fuel pump can quit while driving high

Reported 2021-07-29 · Campaign 21V587000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020

The low-pressure fuel pump sits inside the gas tank and pushes fuel toward the engine. In these vehicles that pump can fail. If it quits, the engine can stall and shut off while you are driving, and you would lose engine power with little warning. A stall in traffic, at highway speed, or in an intersection can easily lead to a crash. The dealer replaces the pump inside the tank with a new one.

Subaru Outback and Impreza shifter cable nut may loosen high

Reported 2021-01-26 · Campaign 21V024000 · Model years: 2021

The cable that connects your gear shifter to the transmission is held on by a nut, and on these 2021 Subaru Outback and Impreza cars that nut may not have been tightened enough at the factory. Over time it can work loose. If the cable comes free, the shifter may stop doing what you ask — you could pick a gear and the car not actually go into it. A dealer checks the nut and tightens it if needed.

Subaru Legacy and Outback backup camera can cut out medium

Reported 2020-12-10 · Campaign 20V766000 · Model years: 2020

A software update Subaru sent to certain 2020 Legacy and Outback models in August 2020 may have timed out before it finished installing, which scrambled the data in the system. As a result, the backup camera picture can shut off now and then while you are reversing, leaving you without a view of what is behind you. Subaru says these vehicles do not meet the federal rear-visibility rule. Dealers will reload the software and, if that is not enough, replace the control module.

Subaru fuel pump failure can stall Impreza, Outback, Legacy, Ascent high

Reported 2020-04-16 · Campaign 20V218000 · Model years: 2019

The pump that sends gasoline from the tank to the engine can stop working. If it quits, the engine shuts off while you are driving and the car will not start again. That can leave you stopped in traffic or on the side of a highway with no warning and no way to move. Subaru dealers will replace the low pressure fuel pump at no charge to you.

Subaru Outback and Legacy brake pedal bracket bolt loose high

Reported 2019-09-18 · Campaign 19V664000 · Model years: 2020

On some 2020 Subaru Outback and Legacy vehicles, a bolt holding the bracket that supports the brake pedal may be missing or not tightened enough. Over time, the area where the brake pedal is mounted can bend out of shape. If that happens, pressing the pedal does not work the brakes as well as it should, so the car may take longer to stop. Dealers will check the bracket hardware and replace the hardware or the whole brake pedal assembly.

Subaru Legacy and Outback body welds may be weak medium

Reported 2019-06-26 · Campaign 19V493000 · Model years: 2019

Some spot welds on a duct below the cowl panel — the metal panel at the base of the windshield — may not have been applied properly at the factory. Those welds are part of what gives the car's body its strength. With weak welds, the body may not hold up as well in a crash, so people inside could be hurt more badly than they otherwise would be. Subaru dealers will inspect the car and repair it, or buy it back, at no cost.

Subaru Legacy, Outback and BRZ backup camera goes blank medium

Reported 2018-12-31 · Campaign 18V935000 · Model years: 2018

A software glitch can stop the audio and display screen from starting up properly. When that happens the screen stays blank, including the backup camera picture that is supposed to appear when you shift into reverse. Without that view you cannot see a child, a pet, or an object behind the car, which makes backing into something more likely. A dealer reloads corrected software into the display system, free of charge, and it is usually a single short visit.

Subaru Legacy and Outback fuel gauge software reads wrong medium

Reported 2018-11-01 · Campaign 18V773000 · Model years: 2018

A software mistake in your Subaru's dashboard gauges means the fuel readings can mislead you. The low fuel warning light may not come on when it should, and the display showing how many miles you have left may still show fuel remaining when the tank is actually empty. Because of that, you could run out of gas with no warning and the engine could quit while you are driving. A dealer reloads corrected software into the gauge.
