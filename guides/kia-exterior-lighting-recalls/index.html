Why are Kia headlights and parking lights being recalled?

3 NHTSA recalls · Kia · Exterior Lighting · model years 2019–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-05-27

These three Kia lighting recalls involve three unrelated defects. The 2020 Telluride's high beam headlights may fail outright, reducing visibility. The 2019 Forte's LED headlights were manufactured with incorrect low-beam aiming, which can also reduce a driver's visibility. Most recently, a software error in the Power-net Domain Controller of the 2025 K5 can cause the parking lights to flicker, which the notice says reduces the vehicle's visibility to other drivers. Rather than a shared root cause, each recall traces to a different part of the lighting system — the high beam headlights, the headlight assembly's aim, and a control module's software — spanning model years from 2019 through 2025.

Warning signs to watch for

  • High beam headlights may fail to work (2020 Telluride)
  • Low beam headlights aimed incorrectly, which may show as reduced visibility (2019 Forte)
  • Parking lights flicker (2025 K5)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three Kia lighting recalls include a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the 2020 Telluride's high beam headlight recall, the 2019 Forte's headlight aiming recall, and the 2025 K5's parking light recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. Each notice does say the lighting defect can reduce visibility — either the driver's own visibility or, for the parking lights, the vehicle's visibility to other drivers — which the notices say increases the risk of a crash.

What the free repair involves

Kia dealers inspect and repair or replace the high beam headlights on the 2020 Telluride, and inspect and replace the headlight assembly on the 2019 Forte to correct its aim. For the 2025 K5, the parking light software is updated by a dealer or over-the-air. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Will a headlight recall repair cost me anything?

No. All three of these Kia lighting recalls — covering the Telluride's high beams, the Forte's headlight aim, and the K5's parking lights — are repaired by Kia dealers free of charge to the owner.

Do I have to stop driving my Kia if my headlights are recalled?

No. None of these three lighting recalls tell owners to stop driving. The notices say the defect can reduce visibility, which increases crash risk, but they do not instruct owners to park the vehicle or stop driving before the repair.

Are these three Kia lighting recalls related to each other?

No. Each involves a different defect: the 2020 Telluride's high beam headlights may fail, the 2019 Forte's LED headlights were aimed incorrectly, and the 2025 K5's parking lights flicker due to a software error.
