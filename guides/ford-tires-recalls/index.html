Why Are So Many Ford Tires Being Recalled?

3 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Tires · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-03-22

Ford's tire recalls involve three separate manufacturing problems rather than one repeated defect. A 2020 F-150 recall covered spare tires damaged during installation on the rim. A larger 2021 recall hit 2018 F-150, 2019 Escape, and 2019-2020 F-250/F-350 Super Duty trucks fitted with Continental tires that were cured too long during production, which can cause sidewall breaks or belt edge separation. Most recently, 2023 Maverick and Bronco Sport models were recalled for tires manufactured incorrectly, affecting handling. Across all three, the concern is tire failure - air loss, tread separation, or reduced control - rather than a wheel or suspension part.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Sudden loss of air pressure from a tire
  • A break or bulge in the tire sidewall
  • Tread or belt separation on a tire
  • Reduced vehicle handling or control while driving
  • The spare tire recall describes no warning sign before failure

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three Ford tire recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The 2020 F-150 spare tire recall, the 2021 recall covering the F-150, Escape, and F-250/F-350 Super Duty tires, and the 2023 Maverick and Bronco Sport tire recall do not tell owners to stop driving. The notices describe risks including rapid air loss, tire separation, and loss of handling and control, but in each case dealers are simply directed to inspect and replace tires as needed.

What the free repair involves

Across all three recalls, Ford dealers inspect and replace tires as needed, free of charge. This includes replacing damaged spare tires, replacing over-cured Continental tires found during inspection, and replacing incorrectly manufactured tires on Maverick and Bronco Sport models. No software update or over-the-air fix applies to these tire recalls.

Common questions

Which Ford models are affected by these tire recalls?

The recalls cover the 2020 F-150 spare tire, 2018 F-150, 2019 Escape, 2019-2020 F-250 and F-350 Super Duty tires, and 2023 Maverick and Bronco Sport tires. Each recall involves a different tire defect rather than one shared cause across all these models.

Do I need to stop driving because of a Ford tire recall?

No. None of these three Ford tire recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The notices direct dealers to inspect tires and replace them as necessary once the owner brings the vehicle in for the free repair.

How much does the Ford tire recall repair cost?

Nothing. Ford dealers inspect and, if needed, replace the affected tires free of charge under all three recalls. Owners should contact a Ford dealer or Ford customer service to schedule the inspection and repair.
