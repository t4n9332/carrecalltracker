Which Volkswagen seat belt recalls affect child seats and rear belts?

5 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-08-05

Three of these five Volkswagen recalls come down to the same thing: a rear seat belt that will not hold a child restraint. The 2021 ID.4 got rear outboard belts missing the automatic locking retractor, and a separate 2021 action covers middle-rear belts on the ID.4 and many Audi models. The 2018 Atlas recall pairs suspect center and left outboard buckles with an owner's manual that omitted child restraint limits for the second-row center seat. The Tiguan long wheelbase appears twice: 2018 front belt webbing that can tear in a crash, and 2020 third-row anchor bolts left untorqued — the one recall Volkswagen resolved by repurchasing the vehicles rather than repairing them.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A child seat that will not cinch tight in a rear outboard or middle-rear position because the belt does not lock
  • Frayed, cut or damaged webbing on a 2018 Tiguan front belt
  • A rear buckle on a 2018 Atlas that releases unexpectedly or will not latch securely
  • Third-row belt anchors on a 2020 Tiguan that feel loose
  • For most of these there is nothing to notice while driving — the failure shows up in a crash

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five notices tells owners to stop driving or park outside. One carries a firm restriction: on the 2020 Tiguan long wheelbase, Volkswagen says no one should occupy the third-row seats at all until the vehicle is repurchased. For the two child seat recalls, the car is safe to drive, but move the child seat to a different position — or use a locking method approved by your child seat maker — until the belts are replaced. The Tiguan webbing and Atlas buckle recalls allow normal driving while you book the inspection.

What the free repair involves

These are inspections with replacement where needed: dealers check and replace the rear outboard and middle-rear belt assemblies on the ID.4 and Audi models, the front belts on the 2018 Tiguan, and the center and left outboard buckles on the 2018 Atlas, plus an owner's manual supplement. All free of charge. The 2020 Tiguan third-row recall is different — Volkswagen repurchases the vehicle instead of repairing it.

Common questions

Can my child seat stay in the car until the belt is replaced?

The notices say an affected belt may not secure a child restraint. Move the seat to an unaffected position, or use a securing method your child seat manufacturer approves, until the dealer inspects and replaces the belt assembly free of charge.

Why is Volkswagen buying back 2020 Tiguans instead of fixing them?

That recall covers third-row seat belt anchor bolts that may not have been properly torqued, and Volkswagen's stated remedy is to repurchase the affected 2020 Tiguan long wheelbase vehicles. Until then, the notice says no one should occupy the third row.

Does the ID.4 have two separate seat belt recalls?

Yes. One covers rear outboard belts installed without an automatic locking retractor; the other covers middle-rear belt assemblies shared with many 2021 Audi models. Owner letters for both were mailed August 27, 2021, and both are inspect-and-replace at no charge.
