Why are Volkswagen seat belts recalled across so many models?

5 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-08-05

Child-seat compatibility is the clearest repeated issue in this group: the 2018 Atlas's second-row buckles can be damaged by wider child seat bases, and the 2021 ID.4 has two separate recalls for rear seat belts that may not secure a child restraint — one from a missing automatic locking retractor, the other from a retractor that deactivates too early (a problem shared with several 2021 Audi models). Separately, the 2020 Tiguan LWB's third-row seat belt anchor bolts were not properly torqued, and the 2018 Tiguan LWB's front seat belt webbing may tear during a crash. Physical hardware defects and child-seat securing are the two recurring threads, but each recall involves a different part of the belt system.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A child seat base wider than 12.6 inches may damage or release the second-row buckle (2018 Atlas)
  • A child restraint may not stay securely locked in the rear seat (2021 ID.4)
  • Front seat belt webbing may show signs of tearing or damage (2018 Tiguan)
  • None of the notices describe a warning light or driver-noticeable symptom for the seat belt anchor bolts or buckle defects

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five seat belt recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For four of them — the 2018 Atlas buckle recall, the 2018 Tiguan front belt webbing recall, and the two 2021 ID.4 child-seat recalls — the notices simply do not tell owners to stop driving. The exception is the 2020 Tiguan LWB's third-row anchor bolt recall, where Volkswagen advises that no one should occupy the third-row seat due to the safety risk, in addition to repurchasing affected vehicles.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and, as necessary, replace the affected seat belt buckles, anchor bolts, front seat belts, or rear seat belt assemblies, free of charge. For the 2020 Tiguan LWB, Volkswagen repurchases the vehicle instead of repairing the anchor bolts. All other repairs in this group are performed at no cost to the owner.

Common questions

Why does my 2020 Tiguan's third-row recall say not to use that seat?

The notice explains that the third-row seat belt anchor bolts may not have been properly torqued, which could keep the belt from performing correctly in a crash. Volkswagen advises that no one should occupy the third-row seat and is repurchasing affected vehicles rather than repairing them.

Is it safe to install a child seat in my Atlas or ID.4?

The notices describe specific limits: the 2018 Atlas's second-row buckles can be damaged by child seat bases wider than 12.6 inches, and two 2021 ID.4 recalls involve rear seat belts that may not properly secure a child restraint system. Contact your dealer for the free repair.

Why did the 2021 ID.4 have two different seat belt recalls?

They involve different defects: one recall found rear outboard seat belts without an automatic locking retractor were installed, and a separate recall found the retractors, shared with several 2021 Audi models, could deactivate too early. Both can prevent a child restraint from securing properly.
