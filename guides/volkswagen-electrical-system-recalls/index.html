Volkswagen electrical recalls: what's wrong with the ID.4's battery?

16 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-03-25

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Across these Volkswagen electrical recalls, most involve the ID.4 electric SUV's high-voltage battery, each with a different cause: worn battery cable insulation (2023 model), misaligned battery cell electrodes that raise fire risk (2023-2024 models, recalled twice), battery overheating (2023-2025 models), bad solder connections that can cause a stall (2021 models), a coolant leak into the high-voltage system (2023 models), and software that can shut down the battery management module (2021 models). Outside the ID.4, a 2021 Atlas/Atlas Cross Sport relay defect, a 2025 Jetta ground wire defect, a 2019 Beetle instrument cluster defect, an ignition switch defect on 2019-2020 Golf/GTI/Jetta, and a key-removable-from-ignition defect on 2018-2019 Beetle/GTI/Golf/Jetta round out the group.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Unexpected stall or loss of drive power while driving
  • Horn or starter that stays activated or feels hot (relay defect)
  • Key can be removed from the ignition when the shift lever isn't in Park
  • No brake warning light appears on the instrument cluster despite a brake problem
  • The battery fire-risk recalls describe no driver-noticeable warning before the risk occurs

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these recalls tell owners to stop driving, but three carry a park-outside instruction, all for the ID.4. The 2023 model battery cable recall tells owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired. The two battery cell module fire recalls (2023-2024 models, issued December 2025 and again January 2026) tell owners to park outside immediately after charging, not charge indoors overnight, avoid Level 3 DC fast chargers, and limit charging to 80% until fixed. For every other recall in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and replace faulty relays, battery cables, and ground wires; replace damaged battery cell modules or entire high-voltage batteries; update battery-management, pulse-inverter, ECU, and instrument-cluster software; replace ignition switches; and install an added switch and circuit board to fix the key-removable defect. All of these inspections, repairs, part replacements, and software updates are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Are all Volkswagen ID.4 electrical recalls about the same problem?

No. The ID.4 recalls in this group have different causes: worn battery cable insulation, misaligned battery cell electrodes, battery overheating, bad solder connections, a coolant leak into the high-voltage system, and software that can shut the battery management module down.

What does the Volkswagen relay recall affect?

It covers 2021 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport vehicles, where a damaged relay in the engine compartment fuse box could inadvertently activate the horn or starter, and an activation that lasts too long can overheat and raise the risk of fire.

Does the key-removable recall affect my Jetta or Golf?

It applies to 2018-2019 Beetle, GTI, Golf, and Golf Sportwagen, and 2018 Jetta vehicles with an automatic transmission, manual handbrake, and keyless entry, where silicate buildup could let the key come out when the shifter isn't in Park.
