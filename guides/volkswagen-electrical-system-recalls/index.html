Why do Volkswagen electrical recalls keep involving fire and power loss?

17 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 10, 2026

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Volkswagen's electrical recalls center on the ID.4. Most involve the high-voltage battery or its wiring: misaligned battery cell electrodes on 2023-2024 ID.4s, an overheating high-voltage battery on 2023-2025 ID.4s, battery circuit soldering and battery software on 2021 ID.4s, a 12-volt charging cable contacting the steering column on 2023 rear-wheel-drive ID.4s, and coolant leaking into the high-voltage system on 2023 ID.4s. Gasoline models appear too: an ignition switch that may fail on 2019 Golf, Golf SportWagen and 2019-2020 GTI and Jetta; silicate build-up on shift lever micro switch contacts that can let the key be removed when the shift lever is not in Park; fuse box relays on 2021 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport; and a 2025-2026 Jetta transmission ground wire. Fire is the most repeated consequence.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The horn or engine starter may be inadvertently activated (2021 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport relay recall).
  • A stall (2021 ID.4 high-voltage battery soldering recall) or a loss of drive power (2021 ID.4 battery software recall and 2023 ID.4 12-volt charging cable recall).
  • The electrical system can shut down and the engine can stall while driving (2019-2020 Golf, GTI and Jetta ignition switch recall).
  • Leaking coolant may affect safety-critical systems and warning lights (2023 ID.4 electric motor sealing ring recall).
  • The instrument cluster may not show a visual warning when a brake system failure is detected (2019 Beetle and Beetle Convertible recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these notices tell owners to stop driving. Three carry a park-outside instruction. The 2023 ID.4 12-volt battery charging cable recall advises owners to park outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete. Both 2023-2024 ID.4 high-voltage battery cell module recalls advise owners to park outside immediately after charging, not to leave the vehicle charging indoors overnight, not to use Level 3 DC chargers, and to limit charging to 80% until the remedy is complete. For the remaining recalls in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall and are free of charge. Dealers inspect and replace relays on the Atlas recall, inspect and repair or replace the 12-volt charging cable, replace battery cell modules on the 2021 ID.4, replace the electric motor if necessary, replace the ignition switch, install an additional switch and circuit board, inspect and repair the Jetta transmission ground wire, and update instrument cluster, battery management and high-voltage battery software.

Common questions

My ID.4 is a 2023 or 2024 — what do the notices say about charging it at home?

Both high-voltage battery cell module recalls advise owners to park outside immediately after charging, not to leave the vehicle charging indoors overnight, to avoid Level 3 DC fast chargers, and to limit the charge to 80% until the repair is complete.

Do any of these Volkswagen recalls tell me to stop driving?

None of the notices in this group carry a stop-driving instruction. Three carry park-outside instructions: the 2023 ID.4 12-volt battery charging cable recall and the two 2023-2024 ID.4 high-voltage battery cell module recalls. The other notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

Why do so many of these recalls mention fire?

Six notices name fire risk: the Atlas relay that can keep the horn or starter activated, the ID.4 12-volt cable short circuit, two ID.4 high-voltage battery cell module recalls, the overheating high-voltage battery on 2023-2025 ID.4s, and the 2025-2026 Jetta ground wire.
