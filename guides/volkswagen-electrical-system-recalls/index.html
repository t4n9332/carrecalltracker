What are the Volkswagen electrical system and ID.4 battery recalls?

16 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-03-25

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Volkswagen's 16 electrical system recalls split into two clusters. The larger one follows the ID.4: high-voltage battery cell modules with misaligned electrodes on 2023-2024 cars, a high-voltage battery that may overheat on 2023-2025 cars, incorrect soldering inside the 2021 battery's flexible printed circuit, battery management software on the 2021 ID.4, a 12-Volt charging cable contacting the steering column shaft and wearing its insulation on 2023 rear-wheel-drive cars, and coolant leaking into the high-voltage system on 2023 cars. The second cluster is conventional wiring and switches: damaged fuse box relays on the 2021 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport, an unconnected transmission ground wire on 2025-2026 Jetta, a shift lever micro switch that lets the key come out, an ignition switch that can fail, and 2019 Beetle cluster software.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The horn or engine starter activating on its own (2021 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport relay recall).
  • No visual warning shown in the instrument cluster when a brake system failure is detected (2019 Beetle and Beetle Convertible recall).
  • The key can be removed from the ignition when the shift lever is not in "Park" (Jetta, Golf, GTI, Beetle and Golf SportWagen shift lever micro switch recall).
  • A stall or loss of drive power while driving (2021 ID.4 battery circuit soldering recall, 2021 ID.4 battery management software recall, and the 2019-2020 Golf, GTI and Jetta ignition switch recall).
  • Effects on safety-critical systems and warning lights, including unintended air bag deployment (2023 ID.4 electric motor sealing ring coolant leak recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving. Three carry a park-outside instruction. The 2023 ID.4 12-Volt battery charging cable recall advises owners to park outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete. Both 2023-2024 ID.4 high-voltage battery module recalls advise owners to park outside immediately after charging, not to leave the vehicle charging indoors overnight, not to use Level 3 DC chargers, and to limit the battery's maximum charge to 80% until the repair is complete. For the remaining recalls the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

All of these remedies are free. Dealers inspect and replace the Atlas fuse box relays, inspect and repair or replace the ID.4 12-Volt charging cable, replace the 2021 ID.4 battery cell modules, inspect and replace the electric motor on the 2023 ID.4 coolant recall, replace the ignition switch, and install an additional switch and circuit board. Other recalls are software updates: battery management, pulse inverter, high-voltage battery and instrument cluster.

Common questions

Is my ID.4 affected by more than one battery recall?

Possibly. The notices cover separate ID.4 issues: battery cell modules on 2023-2024 cars, a battery that may overheat on 2023-2025 cars, soldering inside the 2021 battery, and its battery management software. Check your VIN with Volkswagen at 1-800-893-5298.

Can I still charge my ID.4 while I wait for the repair?

The two 2023-2024 high-voltage battery module notices advise owners to park outside immediately after charging, avoid charging indoors overnight, avoid Level 3 DC fast chargers, and limit charging to 80% until the repair is complete.

Why can the key come out of my Golf or Jetta ignition?

That recall covers automatic-transmission cars with a manual handbrake and keyless entry, where silicate build-up on the shift lever micro switch contacts may let the key be removed when the lever is not in Park. Dealers install an additional switch and circuit board free.
