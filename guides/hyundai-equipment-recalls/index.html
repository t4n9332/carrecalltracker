Why did my Hyundai get recalled over a label?

4 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Equipment · model years 2020–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-09-13

All four recalls in this group involve incorrect or missing labels rather than a mechanical defect. The 2020 Sonata and 2024 Tucson recalls both involve tire-related labels: the Sonata's tire pressure label states the wrong size, and the Tucson's tire and loading label misstates spare tire information. The 2020 Kona recall involves a certification label showing the wrong Gross Axle Weight Rating. The 2025 Tucson recall involves a missing glove box air bag warning label, which normally warns against placing a child in the front seat. No two recalls in this group share the same cause.

Warning signs to watch for

  • These are labeling and documentation errors — the notices describe no drivable warning sign for any of the four recalls

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the four recalls in this group carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the Sonata tire pressure label, the Tucson air bag warning label, the Kona certification label, and the Tucson tire and loading label, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. These recalls address incorrect or missing paperwork rather than a mechanical or electrical fault, and none instruct owners to park away from structures or avoid driving until the label is corrected.

What the free repair involves

Dealers or Hyundai directly install a corrected sticker over the incorrect label, or mail owners replacement labels to apply themselves, free of charge in every case. No mechanical inspection, part replacement, or software update is involved in any of these four label recalls.

Common questions

Do I need to take my Hyundai to a dealer for a label recall?

It depends on the recall. Some, like the Kona and Sonata label corrections, are done by a dealer, while others, like the Tucson air bag label, are mailed directly to owners to apply.

Why does a wrong tire size label matter?

The Sonata notice says the incorrect label could lead an owner to install the wrong size tire, which can affect vehicle handling and increase crash risk, even though the tire itself is not defective.

Are these Hyundai equipment recalls serious?

None of the four carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction, and all involve correcting labels rather than fixing a mechanical or electrical defect. Still, Hyundai and NHTSA classify them as safety recalls.
