Why do Cadillac SUVs keep getting driveline and transmission recalls?

7 NHTSA recalls · Cadillac · Power Train · model years 2019–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-02-13

These seven Cadillac powertrain recalls fall into three groups. One 2020 recall covers a transmission accumulator endcap that may be missing bolts, which can leak transmission fluid and, in the presence of an ignition source, raise fire risk; it spans many GM models including the Cadillac XT4 and XT6. Three recalls involve driveshafts or half-shafts that can separate or disengage, causing a loss of drive power or a rollaway when parked: a 2021 Escalade/Escalade ESV (with Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, and Yukon XL) driveshaft recall, a 2023 XT5/Acadia half-shaft recall, and a related 2022-2023 XT5/XT6/Blazer/Traverse/Acadia sun-gear recall. Three more, covering diesel trucks and SUVs, CT4/CT5/CT6/Camaro, and 2022 Escalade/Suburban/Tahoe/Yukon models, involve transmission failures that can lock the rear wheels.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Sudden loss of drive power while driving (driveshaft, half-shaft, and sun-gear recalls)
  • Vehicle rolling away after being placed in park (half-shaft and sun-gear recalls)
  • Rear wheels locking up while driving (diesel-truck, CT4/CT5/CT6/Camaro, and 2022 SUV recalls)
  • Transmission fluid leak linked to missing accumulator endcap bolts (2020 recall)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these seven powertrain recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. GM's notices describe serious risks - a fluid leak and possible fire from the missing accumulator bolts, a loss of drive power or rollaway from the driveshaft, half-shaft, and sun-gear recalls, and rear wheel lock-up from the diesel, CT4/CT5/CT6/Camaro, and 2022 SUV transmission recalls - but none of the seven notices tell owners to stop driving or to park outside. Owners are not instructed to stop driving under any recall in this group.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and replace the affected part depending on the recall: the transmission accumulator, driveshaft assemblies, half-shaft assemblies, or sun gears, or they install new transmission control module software for the wheel-lock-up recalls. All of these repairs are performed free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Why do so many Cadillac SUVs have half-shaft or driveshaft recalls?

Three separate recalls cover this: 2021 Escalade/Escalade ESV driveshafts that were not properly heat-treated, 2023 XT5/Acadia half-shafts improperly connected to the transmission, and a related 2022-2023 sun-gear defect that lets the half-shaft disengage. Each can cause a loss of drive power or a rollaway when parked.

What does 'rear wheel lock-up' mean in these recalls?

It means a transmission control valve or internal transmission damage can cause the rear wheels to stop turning while the vehicle is moving. GM describes this in the 2024 diesel-truck recall, the 2025 CT4/CT5/CT6/Camaro recall, and the 2026 recall covering 2022 Escalade, Suburban, Tahoe, and Yukon models.

Is the transmission bolt recall a fire risk?

Yes, GM's 2020 recall says missing bolts on the accumulator endcap can cause a transmission fluid leak, which can progress to a loss of propulsion, and a leak near an ignition source raises fire risk. Dealers inspect and replace the part free of charge.
