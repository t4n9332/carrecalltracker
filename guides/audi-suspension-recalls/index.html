Which Audi models have suspension recalls, and what can break?

3 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Suspension · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 26, 2021

Three Audi suspension recalls, each with a different fastener or component at its centre. The smallest covers 2018 A5 Sportback vehicles whose front suspension fasteners may not have been tightened enough, possibly leaving a front wheel loose. The second covers 2019 Q7, Q8, S5 Cabriolet and S5 Sportback vehicles, where the front shock absorber forks may crack and let the shock absorbers loosen. The largest by far is the 2021 rear axle recall: the lock nut on the trailing arm may break from stress corrosion, and it reaches 2019-2021 A5 Sportback, S5 Sportback, RS5 Sportback and RS5 coupe models, 2020-2021 A4, A6, A7, A8, Q5, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8 and SQ5 models, and 2021 Q7, Q8 and RS Q8.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices for these recalls do not describe any warning sign an owner would notice.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these three notices tells owners to stop driving or to park outside. Audi says loose front suspension fasteners on the 2018 A5 Sportback may leave a front wheel loose, which increases the risk of a crash; that cracked shock absorber forks on the 2019 Q7, Q8 and S5 may let the shock absorbers loosen and impair steering; and that a broken rear axle lock nut may misalign the rear axle, causing a loss of control.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect the front suspension fasteners on the 2018 A5 Sportback and replace them as necessary, replace the front shock absorber forks on the 2019 Q7, Q8 and S5, and replace the rear axle trailing arm lock nuts and, if necessary, the associated bolts on the 2021 recall. All three notices say the work is free of charge.

Common questions

How many Audi models does the rear axle lock nut recall cover?

It is the broadest of the three. Audi lists 2019-2021 A5 Sportback, S5 Sportback and RS5 models, 2020-2021 A4, A6, A7, A8, Q5, S4, S5, S6, S7, S8 and SQ5 models, and 2021 Q7, Q8 and RS Q8 vehicles.

What does Audi say goes wrong with the shock absorber forks?

The 2019 notice says the front shock absorber forks on 2019 Q7, Q8, S5 Cabriolet and S5 Sportback vehicles may develop cracks that allow the shock absorbers to loosen, and that loose shock absorbers may impair steering. Dealers replace the forks free of charge.

Should I stop driving until the suspension recall is done?

None of the three notices tells owners to stop driving. They describe the risk — a loose front wheel, impaired steering, a misaligned rear axle — and say dealers will inspect or replace the parts free of charge. Each notice gives an Audi customer service line.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
