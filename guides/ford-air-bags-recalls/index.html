Why Do So Many Ford And Lincoln Models Have Airbag Recalls?

13 NHTSA recalls · Ford · AIR Bags · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-06-27

Ford and Lincoln have issued a dozen airbag recalls across very different models, and few causes repeat — this is many separate assembly and component defects rather than one recurring problem. The Explorer and Lincoln Aviator appear twice: loose seat-back and side airbag bolts in 2020, then a side airbag weld defect in 2021. The Escape also appears twice: a curtain airbag component issue in 2018 and a diffuser problem shared with the Transit in 2020. The Bronco and Mustang each have two separate recalls for unrelated defects. One-time recalls also hit the EcoSport, Transit Connect, Expedition, Navigator, Super Duty, and Maverick.

Warning signs to watch for

  • None of these recalls describe a warning light, noise, or other sign an owner could notice beforehand
  • The defects only become apparent when the airbag deploys in a crash
  • No pre-crash symptoms are described in any of the notices in this group

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the recalls in this group tell owners to stop driving or park outside. Whether the issue is a detaching curtain airbag component, loose bolts, a missing booster propellant, a weld defect, an incorrect inflator gas fill, or a premature vent opening, each notice addresses how the airbag would perform in a future crash rather than a hazard while driving normally. Every notice in this group stops short of instructing owners to stop driving or park outside.

What the free repair involves

Across this group, dealers replace the affected component: side curtain or knee air bags, driver or passenger modules, seat-back fasteners that need tightening, impact sensors, or the propellant/inflator assembly, depending on the specific recall. None of these recalls is fixed with a software update — each requires a physical part replacement or an inspection and repair, at no cost to the owner.

Common questions

Do all these Ford airbag recalls share the same cause?

No. Each recall in this group describes a different defect — loose bolts, missing propellant, weld issues, incorrect inflator gas, and more — affecting different models and years, so a fix for one does not address another.

Why have the Explorer and Lincoln Aviator been recalled for airbags twice?

The 2020 recall covered loose seat-back and side airbag bolts; a separate 2021 recall covered a different problem, weld studs that could detach during side airbag deployment. The two defects are unrelated.

Will I notice a problem before my airbag recall is fixed?

The notices in this group describe no warning sign owners could notice beforehand — these are manufacturing defects that would only affect how the airbag performs during an actual crash.
