Why does my Tesla's backup camera image lag or go dark?

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Back Over Prevention · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-05

Across these five recalls, Tesla's most common back-over risk is a rearview camera image that displays late or fails to appear when shifting into reverse. Model 3 is affected in four of the five notices, spanning 2018 through 2023 model years across several different software releases. A 2025 recall separately covers 2024 Model 3 and 2025 Model Y, where a computer circuit board can short and blank the camera image entirely. A 2022 recall affects 2021 Model S and 2022 Model 3, where an infotainment processor that overheats during fast-charging can blank the whole center screen, not just the camera. The 2024 Cybertruck recall covers a similar reverse-camera delay unique to that model.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rearview camera image appears late after shifting into reverse
  • Backup camera screen shows no image at all
  • Center touchscreen may blank out, hiding the camera, gear selector, and warning lights
  • No warning is described before the camera fails to display

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five recall notices carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices describe the added crash risk from a delayed, blank, or missing rearview camera image when backing up, and in one case an overheating processor that can blank the entire center screen, including the gear display and warning lights, during fast-charging. In every one of the five recalls in this group, the notice stops at describing that increased risk and does not instruct owners to stop driving or to park the vehicle outside while waiting for the repair.

What the free repair involves

Four of these five recalls are fixed with a free over-the-air software update that Tesla pushes to the vehicle. The fifth, covering 2024 Model 3 and 2025 Model Y, also uses an OTA update but adds a dealer inspection and free replacement of the computer circuit board for vehicles found to have board damage or stress.

Common questions

Is Tesla's backup camera recall the same as previous rearview camera recalls?

No. Tesla has issued this fix repeatedly for different causes, including software delays, a shorted circuit board, and an overheating processor, so check your own vehicle's recall notices rather than assuming one fix covers every issue.

Do I need to bring my Tesla to a dealer for the camera recall?

For most of these recalls, no — Tesla pushes the fix as an over-the-air software update automatically. Only the 2025 Model 3/Model Y recall involving the circuit board may require Tesla to inspect and replace hardware.

Why does the rearview camera matter if I can still see out the back window?

Federal safety rules require a working rearview camera because it shows areas the rear window and mirrors can miss, so a delayed or blank image reduces what the driver can see while backing up.
