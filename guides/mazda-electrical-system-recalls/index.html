What Mazda electrical recalls hit CX-90, CX-70, CX-9 and Mazda3?

5 NHTSA recalls · Mazda · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 2, 2025

Mazda's electrical recalls here split into two eras. Three of the five cover the newest three-row SUVs, the CX-90 and CX-70, and all three are software problems: inverter software on the 2024 CX-90 and 2025 CX-70 plug-in hybrids that can cut drive power in EV mode; Dash Electrical Supply Unit software on 2024-2025 CX-90 and 2025 CX-70 models, affecting the defroster, seat belt warning, 360° view monitor and PHEV battery cooling; and body control module software behind an inaccurate fuel gauge on the 2024-2025 mild hybrids. The two older recalls differ: a wiring harness on the 2018 CX-9 that can disable the passenger frontal air bag, turn signals or engine starting, and incorrect programming of the 2019 Mazda3's air bag and seat belt indicator assembly.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Recall 7024J (CX-90 and CX-70 plug-in hybrids): the malfunction indicator lights may illuminate, and drive power may be lost in EV mode.
  • Recall 3319E (2019 Mazda3): the passenger air bag deactivation light may turn off while the air bag is deactivated, and seat belt status indicators may turn off while belts are unfastened.
  • Recall 7124J (CX-90 and CX-70): during startup the defroster, seat belt warning, 360° view monitor or PHEV high-voltage battery cooling may not function properly.
  • Recall 78251 (CX-90 and CX-70 mild hybrids): the instrument cluster fuel gauge may display an inaccurate fuel reading.
  • Recall 3419E (2018 CX-9): the turn signals or the engine starting system may not work.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these five Mazda notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside. That applies to the 2018 CX-9 wiring harness recall, the 2019 Mazda3 indicator recall, and the three CX-90 and CX-70 software recalls covering the inverter, the Dash Electrical Supply Unit and the fuel gauge display. The notices describe the risk each defect creates — loss of drive power, an unexpected stall, a passenger air bag that may not deploy — but they set out no interim instruction for owners beyond having the recall repair performed.

What the free repair involves

Three of these recalls are handled with software: dealers install improved inverter software on the plug-in hybrids, reprogram the Dash Electrical Supply Unit, and update the body control module for the fuel gauge. For the 2018 CX-9, dealers repair the wiring harness; for the 2019 Mazda3, they replace the passenger air bag and seat belt status indicator assembly. Mazda states each repair is free of charge.

Common questions

My CX-90 or CX-70 has more than one open electrical recall — why?

These SUVs appear in three separate Mazda recalls: inverter software on the plug-in hybrids, Dash Electrical Supply Unit software, and body control module software for the fuel gauge. Each is a separate recall with its own remedy and its own owner notification letter.

Does Mazda say I should stop driving my CX-9 or Mazda3?

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving or to park outside. The 2018 CX-9 notice says the wiring harness may disable the passenger frontal air bag, turn signals or the engine starting system; the 2019 Mazda3 notice says air bag and seat belt indicators may display incorrectly.

Will the CX-90 and CX-70 software recalls be done over the air?

The notices for the inverter software, the Dash Electrical Supply Unit and the fuel gauge body control module all say dealers perform the update. None of them mentions an over-the-air update. Mazda states each of these software repairs is free of charge.
