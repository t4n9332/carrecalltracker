Honda and Acura hydraulic brake recalls: what owners should know

5 NHTSA recalls · Honda · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 12, 2025

Five recalls touch hydraulic brake parts on Honda and Acura vehicles, and they split between leaking fluid and loose fasteners. On the fluid side, rear brake caliper pistons with insufficient coating affect 2016-2019 HR-V, 2018-2019 Odyssey, 2017-2019 Acura MDX and 2017-2018 MDX Sport Hybrid; a leaking ball valve in the vehicle stability assist modulator affects the 2022 Accord and 2023 Civic, Acura RDX and Integra; and an ABS modulator leak affects 2019-2020 CB500X and CBR500R motorcycles. On the fastener side, the tie rod fastener between brake booster and master cylinder affects 2020-2021 Civic, 2020-2023 Ridgeline, 2021-2023 Passport, 2021-2022 Pilot and 2020 Acura MDX, while a brake pedal pivot pin affects 2021-2025 Acura TLX and 2023-2025 MDX and Pilot.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The VSA modulator recall on the 2022 Accord and 2023 Civic, Acura RDX and Integra describes unintended vehicle movement when the brake hold feature is engaged, or an unexpected increase in brake pedal travel.
  • The brake pedal pivot pin recall on the 2021-2025 Acura TLX and 2023-2025 MDX and Pilot describes a brake pedal that shifts out of position.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these five notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside. That includes the caliper piston recall, the ABS modulator leak on CB500X and CBR500R motorcycles, the vehicle stability assist modulator leak, the brake booster tie rod fastener recall and the brake pedal pivot pin recall. Each notice states the safety risk — reduced braking, a fluid leak, unintended movement or longer pedal travel, master cylinder separation, a shifted pedal — and describes the free dealer remedy. The notices give no interim instructions beyond that.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall. For the caliper piston recall dealers bleed the brakes. For the motorcycle recall dealers inspect the ABS modulator lot number and replace the modulator as necessary. For the VSA recall dealers replace the modulator. For the booster recall dealers inspect and repair the brake booster assembly as necessary. For the pedal recall dealers inspect and replace the brake pedal assembly as necessary. All are free.

Common questions

How do I find out which of these brake recalls applies to my vehicle?

Honda lists separate recall numbers for each campaign: C31 and L30, KM6, OEJ/XEK/AEY/XEX/OEW, VEU/AEV/ZET, and FLX/XLY. Checking your VIN with Honda or Acura customer service at 1-888-234-2138, or with Honda Powersports at 1-866-784-1870, shows which one applies.

Do any of these notices tell me to stop driving?

None of the five notices carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. They describe the defect, the safety risk and the free dealer remedy, and say owners were notified by letter. Beyond that, the notices do not tell owners what to do before the repair.

What does the CB500X and CBR500R motorcycle recall cover?

It covers certain 2019-2020 CB500X and CBR500R ABS motorcycles, where excessive grease in the ABS modulator may collect debris that gets stuck in the check valve and causes a brake fluid leak. Dealers inspect the modulator lot number and replace it as necessary, free.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
