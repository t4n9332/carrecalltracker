What's Behind Honda and Acura's Repeated Brake Recalls?

5 NHTSA recalls · Honda · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-06-12

These five Honda and Acura hydraulic brake recalls involve different components rather than one repeated cause. A 2018 recall covers rear brake caliper pistons with insufficient coating on the 2018-2019 Acura MDX, MDX Sport Hybrid, HR-V and Odyssey, reducing brake performance. A 2019-2020 CB500X and CBR500R motorcycle recall involves ABS modulator grease that can lead to a brake fluid leak. A 2023 recall covers a VSA modulator ball valve leak on the Accord, Acura Integra and RDX, while a separate 2023 recall covers a brake booster-to-master-cylinder fastener issue spanning the Civic, Passport, Pilot, Ridgeline and Acura MDX. A 2025 recall covers a brake pedal pivot pin on the Acura TLX, MDX and Honda Pilot.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Reduced braking performance (2018-2019 Acura MDX, MDX Sport Hybrid, HR-V, Odyssey)
  • A brake fluid leak (2019-2020 CB500X and CBR500R motorcycles; 2022 Accord, 2023 Acura Integra and RDX)
  • Unintended vehicle movement when the brake hold feature is engaged, or increased brake pedal travel (Accord, Acura Integra, RDX)
  • A loss of brake function if the master cylinder separates from the booster (Civic, Passport, Pilot, Ridgeline, Acura MDX)
  • A brake pedal that shifts out of position and cannot be applied as intended (Acura TLX, MDX, Pilot)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five brake recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices describe an increased risk of a crash from reduced braking performance, a brake fluid leak, unintended vehicle movement or extra pedal travel, brake master cylinder separation, or a brake pedal that shifts out of position, but none tell owners to stop driving in the meantime. This applies to the Acura MDX, MDX Sport Hybrid, HR-V, Odyssey, CB500X, Accord, Acura Integra, RDX, Civic, Passport, Pilot, Ridgeline, and Acura TLX recalls.

What the free repair involves

Dealers address each cause directly: bleeding the brakes on the 2018-2019 MDX, HR-V and Odyssey; replacing the ABS modulator on the CB500X as needed; replacing the VSA modulator on the Accord, Integra and RDX; inspecting and repairing the brake booster assembly on the Civic, Passport, Pilot, Ridgeline and MDX; and inspecting and replacing the brake pedal assembly on the TLX, MDX and Pilot. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Are all these brake recalls related to the same part?

No. They involve five different components — brake caliper pistons, an ABS modulator, a VSA modulator valve, a brake booster fastener, and a brake pedal pivot pin — across different Honda and Acura models and model years.

Which recall involves a motorcycle?

The 2021 recall for the 2019-2020 CB500X and CBR500R ABS motorcycles, where excessive grease in the ABS modulator can collect debris and cause a brake fluid leak.

Does any of these recalls say to stop driving?

No. None of the five notices in this group instruct owners to stop driving or park outside, even though they describe risks like reduced braking, fluid leaks, or a brake pedal that can shift out of position.
