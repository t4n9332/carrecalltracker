Porsche electrical recalls: Taycan battery fire risk and cluster warnings

13 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Electrical System · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-10-01

Thirteen recalls, and the Taycan high-voltage battery dominates them. Five separate campaigns between December 2023 and October 2024 describe a short circuit inside a battery module, covering 2020-2024 Taycan, 4S, Turbo, Turbo S, GTS and the Cross Turismo and Sport Turismo body styles; several instruct owners to charge only to 80 percent until repaired. A 2023 campaign adds a battery sealing flaw that allows leakage. A second fire theme is charging and cooling hardware: the 220/240-volt portable charging cable on Taycan and Cayenne E-Hybrid models can overheat, and a 2017-2021 Panamera climate-control coolant pump can take on humidity and short. The rest are 2018-2020 Panamera, Cayenne and Macan control-unit and instrument-cluster faults, including missing worn brake pad warnings.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 220/240-volt portable charging cable, its plug or the outlet getting hot while charging
  • No worn brake pad warning ever appears in the instrument cluster on affected Panamera and Cayenne models
  • Missing driver display alerts, and possibly impaired drivability, on the 2018 Macan control unit recall
  • No seat belt warning chime when an occupant is unbelted on affected Taycans
  • For the Taycan high-voltage battery recalls, the notices describe no warning sign an owner would see

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these thirteen notices tells owners to stop driving or park outside, including the battery fire recalls. Porsche does impose two restrictions. In the battery short-circuit campaigns, charge only to a maximum of 80 percent until the repair is complete. In the charging cable recall, stop using the 220/240-volt portable cable and use the 110-volt home cable or public charging instead. The Panamera coolant pump and brake pad warning recalls also allow continued driving, but both involve fire or braking — get them scheduled quickly.

What the free repair involves

Taycan battery recalls involve analyzing battery data, testing for leakage and replacing high-voltage modules or the battery as needed; one 2024 campaign's final remedy is advanced diagnostic software. Porsche supplies a replacement 220/240-volt portable charging cable. Panamera gets a new external coolant pump with a connector inspection, Panamera and Macan control units are replaced, and the brake pad warning and seat belt chime faults are software updates. All free of charge.

Common questions

Why have I received several Taycan battery recall letters?

Porsche filed five separate short-circuit campaigns between December 2023 and October 2024, plus a 2023 battery sealing recall. Model years and body styles overlap, so one Taycan can fall under more than one. Check each recall number with Porsche at 1-800-767-7243.

Can I charge to 100 percent while I wait for the repair?

No. In the battery short-circuit recalls, Porsche advises charging to a maximum of 80 percent until the repair is complete. That instruction stands whether you charge at home or in public, and it applies until the dealer finishes the work.

What should I do with my 220/240-volt portable charging cable?

Stop using it. Porsche says to use the 110-volt home charging cable or public charging stations instead, because the industrial 220/240-volt plug at the 100 percent charge setting can overload some home outlets. Dealers supply a new cable free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
