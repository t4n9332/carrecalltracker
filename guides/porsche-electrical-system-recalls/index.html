Porsche Electrical Recalls Cover Battery Fires and Missing Brake Warnings

13 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Electrical System · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Oct 1, 2024

Taycan high-voltage battery problems dominate these Porsche electrical notices, with repeated module short-circuit recalls involving specified vehicles across model years 2020 through 2024. A separate 2023 Taycan notice concerns insufficient battery sealant that can allow liquid accumulation and electrical arcing. Panamera issues include humidity entering the climate-control coolant pump on certain 2017-2021 vehicles and incorrect control-unit soldering on certain 2018 variants. Two instrument-cluster notices address missing brake-pad wear warnings on specified Panamera and Cayenne models. The group also includes control-unit failure in certain 2018 Macan vehicles and portable charging equipment that can overheat an outlet or cable when used under the conditions described.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2019 Panamera Control Unit Solder Defect (AKA5): intermittent failure of various systems, possibly affecting drivability.
  • 2018 Macan Control Unit May Fail (AJ05): driver display alerts may be prevented, and drivability may be impaired.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the supplied electrical notices instructs owners to stop driving or park outside. The March 20, 2024 battery notices and October 1, 2024 affected-module replacement notice specify an 80% charging maximum until repair. The October diagnostic-software notice describes contacting owners as necessary with an 80% limit. The December 2023 charging-cable notice advises avoiding the 220V/240V compact/portable cable and using the 110V home cable or public charging stations. These instructions belong to those specific recalls; the remaining notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Free remedies include coolant-pump replacement and connection inspection for the Panamera pump recall; battery leakage testing and replacement as necessary for APB2; and battery inspection with module replacement as necessary for APB5. The charging-cable recall supplies a temperature-sensing cable free. AKA5 replaces control units free, while AKA1/AKA0 updates instrument-cluster software free.

Common questions

Does each Taycan battery recall require the same repair?

The remedies differ. APB2 calls for leakage testing and battery replacement as necessary. APB5 calls for inspection and module replacement as necessary. One October 2024 notice specifies affected-module replacement, while the other describes advanced diagnostic software as its final remedy.

Which Porsche charging recall says to stop using the portable cable?

The December 13, 2023 portable charging-cable notice advises owners not to use the 220V/240V compact/portable cable. It specifies the 110V home charging cable or public charging stations instead, pending a replacement cable incorporating a temperature sensor.

Which electrical recalls concern missing brake-pad warnings?

The February 20, 2019 AKA1/AKA0 notice and October 16, 2019 AKB8 notice concern instrument clusters that may omit brake-pad wear warnings on specified Panamera and Cayenne vehicles. Their remedies are an instrument-cluster software update and instrument-cluster reprogramming, respectively.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
