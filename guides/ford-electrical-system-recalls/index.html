Why do Ford electrical system recalls warn of fire risk?

39 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Electrical System · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-08-25

Across these Ford and Lincoln recalls, the most common cause is a power cable or wiring connection that wasn't properly secured — battery, starter, alternator or Power Distribution Box connections on the Edge, MKX, Flex, MKT, F-150 and Explorer — which can arc or short and raise fire risk. A separate recurring pattern involves plug-in hybrid high-voltage batteries: the Fusion PHEV, Explorer/Aviator PHEV, and Escape/Corsair PHEV recalls (spanning 2019-2026 models) describe manufacturing defects in battery cells that can internally short-circuit. One recall covers an overheating engine block heater on the Escape, Corsair and Nautilus. Two older recalls address FMVSS compliance issues — a Mustang instrument cluster and a Taurus ignition key — tied to rollaway risk, not fire.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Instrument cluster doesn't show PRNDL gear position when the ignition is off (some 2018 Mustangs)
  • Ignition key can be removed without shifting into Park (Taurus)
  • Most electrical and battery fire-risk recalls in this group describe no driver-noticeable warning sign before the risk occurs

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these Ford and Lincoln electrical system recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Some plug-in hybrid battery recalls add a charging instruction instead: the Fusion PHEV (2023) and Escape/Corsair PHEV (2024-12-20) notices tell owners not to charge until repaired, and the later Escape/Corsair PHEV recalls (2025-11-14, 2026-02-17) tell owners to use only "Auto EV" mode and limit maximum charge until repaired. For every other recall in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Across this group, dealers inspect and re-secure or replace power cables, fasteners and wiring harnesses; replace high-voltage battery packs and update battery/powertrain control module software on the plug-in hybrid recalls; replace the block heater element; and update instrument cluster software or replace the shifter assembly on the two compliance recalls. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why do so many Ford electrical recalls involve fire risk?

These recalls span several separate root causes — loose battery or starter cables, wiring rubbing against other parts, and defective high-voltage battery cells — that can each create an electrical short or arc. Ford's notices link each specific defect to an increased fire risk, not a single shared cause.

Do the Escape and Corsair plug-in hybrid battery recalls mean I should stop driving?

No recall in this group carries a stop-driving instruction. The 2024-12-20 notice tells owners not to charge until repaired, and the 2025-11-14 and 2026-02-17 notices say to use only "Auto EV" mode and limit maximum charge until the remedy is complete.

Will I have to pay for the repair?

No. Every recall in this group is repaired free of charge — dealers inspect and secure or replace wiring and cables, replace battery packs, or update software, and Ford covers the full cost of parts and labor.
