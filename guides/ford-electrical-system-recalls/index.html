Ford and Lincoln electrical recalls: hybrid battery fires and loose cables

39 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Electrical System · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Aug 25, 2026

Two threads run through Ford's electrical recalls. The larger is the high-voltage battery: a manufacturing defect in one or more cells is named for 2020-2024 Escape and 2021-2024 Corsair plug-in hybrids, again for 2022-2026 Escape and 2023-2026 Corsair, and for 2020-2022 Explorer and Lincoln Aviator with 3.0L PHEV engines, while 2019-2020 Fusion PHEV covers a battery energy control module damaged by excessive voltage and current flow. The second thread is power connections that can arc — starter and alternator cables on 2018 Edge and MKX and 2019 Flex and MKT, the 2020 F-150 starter nut, the 2019-2020 F-150 positive terminal fastener, and a 2020 Explorer wiring harness. Separate again: an overheating engine block heater, and rollaway compliance on the 2018 Mustang and 2017-2018 Taurus.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Instrument panel displays, braking assist or steering assist affected, or an engine stall — the 2019-2020 F-150 battery cable fastener recall.
  • The gear position display not illuminating, and no 'Key in Ignition' chime when the driver's door is opened — the 2018 Mustang instrument cluster recall (17C21).
  • The key coming out of the ignition when the transmission is not in PARK — the 2017-2018 Taurus recall (18C02).
  • A loss of drive power — the Escape, Corsair, Explorer and Aviator high-voltage battery recalls and the 2019-2020 Fusion PHEV battery energy control module recall.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving or to park outside, but several carry a charging instruction. The 2019-2020 Fusion PHEV notice advises owners not to charge their vehicles until the remedy is completed, and the 2020-2024 Escape and 2021-2024 Corsair battery notice advises owners not to charge the battery until the remedy is completed. The two plug-in hybrid software notices — 2020-2024 Escape and Corsair, and 2022-2026 Escape and 2023-2026 Corsair — advise using only 'Auto EV' mode, with owners instructed by mail to limit maximum charge until the remedy is done. The rest give no interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace high-voltage battery packs where a diagnostic test calls for it, or update battery energy control and hybrid powertrain control module software, free of charge. The Fusion PHEV notice says dealers will replace the battery and update the BECM software for free, or Ford will repurchase the vehicle. Cable recalls bring free inspection, correction, a new starter nut or a retorqued fastener; others replace a shifter assembly or block heater element free.

Common questions

Can I still charge my recalled Ford or Lincoln plug-in hybrid?

It depends which recall. The 2019-2020 Fusion PHEV notice and the 2020-2024 Escape and 2021-2024 Corsair battery notice advise owners not to charge until the remedy is completed. The two later plug-in hybrid notices advise using only 'Auto EV' mode and limiting the maximum charge.

Why is my Escape or Corsair plug-in hybrid named in more than one recall?

Ford has issued separate recalls naming a manufacturing defect in one or more high-voltage battery cells over different model-year spans — 2020-2024 Escape with 2021-2024 Corsair in one, 2022-2026 Escape with 2023-2026 Corsair in another. Each notice names its own free remedy.

What does the loose F-150 battery cable fastener actually affect?

The 2019-2020 F-150 notice says the fastener securing the power distribution box 12V cable and battery monitoring system eyelets may loosen and affect systems such as instrument panel displays and braking or steering assist, and that the engine could also stall.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
