Audi Seat Belt Recalls Affect Occupant Restraint and Child Seats

6 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 24, 2026

Audi’s seat belt notices repeatedly address crash restraint and child-seat securing, with 2025 models appearing across several distinct defects. The 2021 notice describes rear automatic locking retractors deactivating early across numerous Audi lines, plus 2021–2022 S3 Sedan vehicles. Recall 69D8 describes retractors failing to engage in specified 2025 models, including Q6 E-Tron variants. Front restraint problems include damaged retractor torsion bars under 69UM, missing buckle rivets on certain 2025 Q7, Q8, RS Q8, and SQ8 vehicles under 692K, and incorrectly manufactured belts on 2025 Q5/SQ5 variants under 69GT. The December 2016 notice separately covers pretensioner activation on specified 2017 models and the 2018 Q5.

Warning signs to watch for

  • August 2021 rear belt recall: Automatic locking retractors may deactivate early, preventing a child restraint from securing properly.
  • 69D8: The automatic locking retractor may fail to engage when a child seat is installed using the seat belt.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these six notices carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The December 2016 pretensioner notice, August 2021 rear belt notice, and recalls 69UM, 692K, 69D8, and 69GT do not tell owners to stop driving. Their stated risks concern occupants being improperly restrained during a crash or child restraints being inadequately secured. The absence of a stop-driving instruction does not establish driving safety, and these notices do not provide a common interim driving instruction for this group.

What the free repair involves

The notices specify free dealer remedies. The 2016 notice replaces affected pretensioners and air bags; the 2021 notice inspects middle-rear belt assemblies, replacing as necessary. Recall 69UM replaces one or both front belts; 692K replaces both front buckles. Recall 69D8 inspects and replaces rear assemblies as necessary; 69GT replaces driver and front passenger belts as necessary.

Common questions

Which Audi seat belt recalls concern securing a child seat?

The August 2021 notice describes rear automatic locking retractors deactivating early, while 69D8 describes failure to engage during belt-installed child-seat use. Their remedies differ: the former addresses middle-rear assemblies, and 69D8 addresses right and left rear assemblies.

Does the Q7 and Q8 buckle recall replace the front belts?

Recall 692K specifies replacing both front seat belt buckles free of charge. It covers certain 2025 Q7, Q8, RS Q8, and SQ8 vehicles whose driver or front passenger buckle may lack a rivet securing it to the stalk.

What is the repair for the 2025 Q5 and SQ5 belts?

Recall 69GT covers certain 2025 Q5, Q5 Sportback, SQ5, and SQ5 Sportback vehicles. The front belts may have been manufactured incorrectly and may fail to restrain occupants properly. Dealers replace driver and front passenger belts as necessary, free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
