Why does Audi keep recalling vehicles for seat belt defects?

6 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-04-24

Six Audi seat belt recalls fall into two recurring patterns. One is a child-seat securing problem: automatic locking retractors that deactivate or fail to engage, first affecting a broad range of 2021 models (plus the Volkswagen ID.4) and later recurring on 2025 Q6 e-tron, Q6 Sportback e-tron, S6 Sportback e-tron, and SQ6 e-tron vehicles. The other pattern is a hardware or manufacturing defect that could keep a belt from restraining an occupant in a crash: pretensioners that may not activate on the 2018 Q5, a damaged torsion bar in the 2025 S6 Sportback e-tron, a missing rivet in the buckle on 2025 Q7 and Q8 models, and incorrectly manufactured belts on 2025 Q5 and SQ5 vehicles. Most of these recalls involve the 2021 and 2025 model years.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A child seat that doesn't stay locked in place when secured with the rear seat belt (2021 recall and 2025 Q6 e-tron family)
  • Seat belt pretensioner may not activate as expected (2018 Q5) — no dashboard warning described
  • No warning sign at all for the torsion bar, missing buckle rivet, or incorrectly manufactured belt defects — manufacturing issues found only through inspection or in a crash

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six seat belt recalls include a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the Q5 pretensioner recall, the 2021 child-seat retractor recall, the S6 Sportback e-tron torsion bar recall, the Q7/Q8 buckle rivet recall, the Q6 e-tron family retractor recall, and the Q5/SQ5 belt manufacturing recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. The notices describe an increased risk of injury in a crash or of a child seat not being secured, but none of the six direct owners to stop driving or park outside while awaiting repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealer repairs vary by recall: replacing seat belt pretensioners (Q5), inspecting and replacing rear seat belt assemblies (2021 recall and Q6 e-tron family), replacing one or both front seat belts (S6 Sportback e-tron), replacing seat belt buckles (Q7/Q8), and replacing driver and front passenger seat belts as necessary (Q5/SQ5). All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why has Audi recalled so many models for seat belt problems?

The six recalls in this group involve different defects — pretensioners, automatic locking retractors for child seats, a torsion bar, a missing buckle rivet, and incorrectly manufactured belts — spread across Q5, Q6 e-tron, Q7, Q8, S6 Sportback e-tron, and SQ5 models from 2018 to 2025.

Which Audi seat belt recall affects child seats?

Two recalls involve child seats: a 2021 recall covering many Audi models plus the Volkswagen ID.4, and a 2025 recall covering the Q6 e-tron, Q6 Sportback e-tron, S6 Sportback e-tron, and SQ6 e-tron, both involving rear automatic locking retractors that fail to secure a child seat.

Does the Audi Q7 and Q8 seat belt recall affect the whole belt?

No. The 2025 Q7 and Q8 recall is limited to the driver and front passenger seat belt buckles, which may be missing a rivet that secures the buckle to the seat belt stalk; dealers replace both buckles free of charge.
