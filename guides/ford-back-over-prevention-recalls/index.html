Why do Ford and Lincoln rearview cameras keep getting recalled?

34 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Back Over Prevention · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Mar 19, 2026

Nearly every recall in this group is about the rearview camera. One cluster blames a poor electrical connection for a blank or distorted image: 2020 F-150 through F-550, Explorer, Mustang, Transit, Expedition, Escape, Ranger, Edge, Lincoln Nautilus and Corsair, and 2019 F-150 and Super Duty trucks. A second covers the 360-degree camera — video output failure on 2020-2023 Explorer, Lincoln Aviator and 2020-2022 Corsair, and a degraded anti-reflective lens coating fogging the image on 2017-2020 Super Duty and Lincoln Continental. Software explains much of the rest: the 2021-2022 Edge, Bronco images that stay on screen, the Explorer Police Interceptor display, and an overheating APIM on 2021-2026 Bronco and 2021-2024 Edge. Only the 2026-2027 E-350 and E-450 backup alarm is not a camera.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A blank or distorted rearview image — the 2020 Ford and Lincoln recall, the 2019 F-150 and Super Duty recall, the 2019 Flex, Fiesta and Lincoln MKT recall, and the 2021-2022 Edge software recall (22S14).
  • A foggy or cloudy rearview image — the 2017-2020 Super Duty and Lincoln Continental 360-degree camera recall (22S51).
  • The rearview image still displayed after a backing event has ended — the 2021-2022 Bronco recalls (22C27 and 23C05).
  • No rearview image when the display was turned off before shifting to reverse — the 2020-2022 Explorer Police Interceptor Utility recall (23C14).
  • No alarm sounding when the vehicle is backing up — the 2026-2027 E-350 and E-450 recall (26S12).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells them to park outside. What Ford describes is what the driver can see: the camera recalls say a blank, distorted, foggy or missing image reduces the driver's view behind the vehicle and increases the risk of a crash, and the two Bronco notices say an image that stays up after the vehicle is shifted out of reverse may distract the driver. The E-350 and E-450 notice says a backup alarm that fails to alert pedestrians increases the risk of crash or injury. The notices give owners no interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

Every remedy here is free of charge. Dealers replace the rearview camera on the 2020 Ford and Lincoln recall, the 2019 F-150 and Super Duty recall, and the 22S51 Super Duty and Continental recall. The rest are software: image processing module updates for the 360-degree camera recalls, SYNC and APIM updates for Bronco, Edge camera software, and Police Interceptor screen software — several available by dealer or over the air.

Common questions

My camera has never gone blank. Is my Ford still recalled?

The notices describe a condition that may occur, not one that must already be present. Check your VIN on NHTSA.gov or with Ford at 1-866-436-7332; if your vehicle is covered, the free remedy applies whether or not you have seen the problem.

Can any of these camera recalls be fixed over the air?

Some. The 2021-2022 Edge software recall (22S14), both 2021-2022 Bronco SYNC recalls (22C27 and 23C05), and the Bronco and Edge APIM recall (26S09) say the update can be done by a dealer or through an over-the-air update. The others name a dealer repair.

Why has my Bronco been recalled twice for the same screen?

The 23C05 notice says it covers 2021-2022 Broncos with 8-inch SYNC screens previously remedied under recall 22V-825, and that the rearview image may still be displayed after a backing event has ended. Both notices call for a free SYNC module software update.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
