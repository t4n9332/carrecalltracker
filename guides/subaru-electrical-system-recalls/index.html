Subaru Electrical Recalls Involving Fire Risk, Stalling and Power Loss

7 NHTSA recalls · Subaru · Electrical System · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 18, 2026

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Loss of drive power and stalling recur across these Subaru electrical recalls. The October 2019 and April 2021 notices cover certain 2017-2019 Impreza and 2018-2019 Crosstrek vehicles whose ECM may keep powering ignition coils after shutdown, causing short circuits. Separate notices cover instrument panel harness damage on certain 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza vehicles, charging terminal corrosion on certain 2019-2022 Crosstrek Hybrids, and battery ECU faults on certain 2026 Solterra vehicles. Certain 2018 Legacy and Outback vehicles have inaccurate fuel information that can lead to running out of fuel and stalling. The distinct 2019-2022 Ascent heater recall involves melting components and fire risk.

Warning signs to watch for

  • December 2022 Ascent heater recall: visible smoke or a smoke smell from the dash or driver's footwell.
  • October 2019 and April 2021 Impreza/Crosstrek ignition coil recalls: the engine may stall.
  • WTW-82: the low fuel warning light may not illuminate at the intended fuel level, and miles-to-empty may remain positive with an empty tank.
  • August 2023 Crosstrek/Impreza harness recall and February 2023 Crosstrek Hybrid charging recall: loss of drive power.
  • WRG26: unexpected loss of drive power.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One notice carries parking and conditional stop-driving instructions: the December 2022 Ascent heater recall. Owners are advised to park away from structures and avoid leaving the vehicle unattended with the engine running. Owners who notice or smell smoke from the dash or driver's footwell should immediately stop operating the vehicle and turn off the ignition. The other six notices, covering ignition coils, instrument panel wiring, Solterra battery control, fuel display software and Crosstrek Hybrid charging, do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

The 2021 ignition coil recall provides free ECM updates, coil replacement and front exhaust pipe replacement if necessary. The 2024-model harness recall provides free inspection, an additional clamp, and protective tape or harness replacement if damaged. WRG26 updates battery ECU software free; WTW-82 reprograms combination meter software free. The supplied Ascent excerpt does not specify a dealer repair.

Common questions

What does Subaru tell Ascent owners to do about smoke?

For the December 2022 recall covering certain 2019-2022 Ascents, owners noticing or smelling smoke from the dash or driver's footwell should immediately stop operating the vehicle and turn off the ignition. Owners are also advised to park away from structures.

How do the two Impreza and Crosstrek ignition coil remedies differ?

The October 2019 notice specifies an ECM update and ignition coil inspection and replacement as necessary, plus conditional front exhaust pipe replacement. The April 2021 notice specifies an ECM update, ignition coil replacement and front exhaust pipe replacement if necessary.

What is repaired under the Crosstrek Hybrid charging recall?

The February 2023 notice covers certain 2019-2022 Crosstrek Hybrid vehicles. Dealers replace the converter terminal mounting bracket. If terminal corrosion is found, they also replace the 12-Volt output harness and terminals. The notice states repairs are free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
