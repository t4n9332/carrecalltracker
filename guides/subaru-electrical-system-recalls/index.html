Subaru electrical recalls: stalling, lost power and a heater fire risk

6 NHTSA recalls · Subaru · Electrical System · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Aug 4, 2023

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Losing power is the thread through most of this group. Two notices, from 2019 and 2021, describe the same behaviour on 2017-2019 Impreza and 2018-2019 Crosstrek vehicles: the Engine Control Module continuing to power the ignition coil after the engine is shut off, causing a short circuit and a possible stall. A 2023 recall covers 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza vehicles where the instrument panel harness may contact the steering beam bracket and short, and another covers 2019-2022 Crosstrek Hybrid vehicles with a corroding converter harness terminal that can stop the 12-Volt battery charging. The outliers are the 2019-2022 Ascent PTC cabin heater ground bolt, the only fire risk here, and a fuel gauge software error on 2018 Legacy and Outback.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Smoke seen or smelled from the dash or driver's footwell area — the 2019-2022 Ascent PTC heater recall tells an owner who notices this to immediately stop operating the vehicle and turn the ignition off.
  • The low fuel warning light may not illuminate at the intended remaining fuel level, and the miles-to-empty display may show a positive number with an empty tank (2018 Legacy and Outback fuel gauge recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall carries an interim instruction. For the 2019-2022 Ascent PTC heater recall, Subaru advises owners to park away from structures and to avoid leaving the vehicle unattended while the engine is running, and says an owner who notices or smells smoke from the dash or driver's footwell should immediately stop operating the vehicle and turn the ignition off. The other five notices do not tell owners to stop driving. They describe risks of stalling, loss of drive power or unexpectedly running out of fuel, with no instruction to park or limit driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies vary. Dealers update the ECM software and inspect or replace ignition coils, and the front exhaust pipe in some cases; add a clamp, tape or a new instrument panel harness on the 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza; reprogram the combination meter software on the 2018 Legacy and Outback; and replace the converter terminal mounting bracket, plus corroded harness and terminals, on the Crosstrek Hybrid. Repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

Is the Ascent recall the one where I have to park away from the house?

Yes. The 2019-2022 Ascent PTC heater notice advises owners to park away from structures and not to leave the vehicle unattended while the engine is running, because an improperly fastened ground bolt may melt the ground terminal and surrounding components, increasing the risk of a fire.

Why do two recalls describe the same ignition coil problem on my Impreza?

Both the 2019 and 2021 notices cover 2017-2019 Impreza and 2018-2019 Crosstrek vehicles, and both describe the ECM continuing to power the ignition coil after the engine is shut off. The notices do not say how the two relate; a VIN check shows which applies.

My 2024 Crosstrek is nearly new — what is the wiring recall about?

The notice says insufficient clearance may let the instrument panel harness contact the steering beam bracket, damaging the wire insulation and causing a short circuit, which may cause a loss of drive power. Dealers inspect the harness and install an additional clamp, free of charge.
