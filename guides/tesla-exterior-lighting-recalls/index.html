Why are Tesla headlights, taillights and light bars under recall?

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-08-05

Lighting recalls for Tesla split between lamps that do not come on and lamps that put out too much light. SB-22-00-016 covers 2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles on which one or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate, and SB-20-00-002 covers 2020 Model Y vehicles with a global rear lamp and tow package configuration whose trailer brake lights do not illuminate. On the brightness side, SB-24-17-003 covers 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y low beams that may exceed the maximum light output, and SB-25-00-008 covers 2024-2026 Cybertruck front parking lights. A separate October 2025 recall covers service-installed off-road light bars on 2024 Cybertrucks.

Warning signs to watch for

  • One or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate (recall SB-22-00-016).
  • Trailer brake lights do not illuminate during braking (recall SB-20-00-002).
  • The off-road light bar may loosen and detach from the windshield (the October 2025 Cybertruck light bar recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these five notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside. The notices describe the consequences: a taillight that fails to illuminate may reduce the visibility of the vehicle (SB-22-00-016); trailer brake lights that fail to illuminate during braking increase the risk of a crash (SB-20-00-002); low beams (SB-24-17-003) and parking lights (SB-25-00-008) that are too bright can reduce visibility for oncoming drivers; and a detached light bar may fall and create a road hazard. The notices give owners no interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

SB-22-00-016 and SB-25-00-008 are addressed by an over-the-air software update, free of charge. SB-20-00-002 was addressed by a firmware update released over the air as of September 23, 2020. For the October 2025 light bar recall, Tesla Service inspects the light bar and installs an additional mechanical attachment or replaces the light bar as necessary, free of charge. The remedy for SB-24-17-003 is currently under development.

Common questions

Is there a fix yet for the headlights that are too bright?

The notice for SB-24-17-003 says the remedy is currently under development and that owner notification letters are expected to be mailed October 3, 2026. It covers certain 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles whose low beams may exceed the maximum light output.

Does the Cybertruck parking light recall need a service visit?

The notice for SB-25-00-008 says Tesla released an over-the-air software update, free of charge, for certain 2024-2026 Cybertruck vehicles operating software prior to 2025.38.3. Owner notification letters were mailed December 12, 2025, and owners may contact Tesla customer service at 1-877-798-3752.

My Model Y taillight goes out sometimes — is that covered?

SB-22-00-016 covers certain 2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles on which one or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate. Tesla has released an over-the-air software update, free of charge, and owner notification letters were mailed January 14, 2023.
