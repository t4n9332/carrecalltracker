Tesla Exterior Lighting Recalls Cover Failed Lamps and Excessive Brightness

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Aug 5, 2026

Tesla’s exterior lighting recalls involve lamps that fail to illuminate, lamps that exceed brightness limits, and an accessory light bar that may detach. Model 3 and Model Y recur: the taillight notice covers certain 2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles, while the headlight notice covers certain 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles. A separate trailer brake-light software problem affects certain 2020 Model Y vehicles with the specified rear lamp and tow package configuration. Cybertruck notices address overly bright front parking lights on certain 2024-2026 vehicles running software before 2025.38.3 and light-bar attachment problems on certain 2024 vehicles.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Model 3 and Model Y Taillight Failure: one or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate.
  • Model 3 and Model Y Headlights Too Bright: headlight low beams may be too bright.
  • Model Y Trailer Brake Lights Won't Work: trailer brake lights fail to illuminate during braking.
  • Cybertruck Front Parking Lights Too Bright: front parking lights may be too bright.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these five notices instructs owners to stop driving or park outside. The Model 3 and Model Y taillight and headlight notices describe crash risks from reduced vehicle visibility or excessive brightness affecting oncoming drivers. The Model Y trailer brake-light notice describes a crash risk when trailer brake lights fail during braking. The Cybertruck notices describe excessive parking-light brightness and a detached light bar becoming a road hazard. These notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Tesla provides free OTA updates for the taillight and Cybertruck parking-light recalls. The trailer brake-light notice specifies an OTA firmware update but does not state a charge. The headlight remedy remains under development. For the Cybertruck light bar, Tesla Service inspects and adds a mechanical attachment, or replaces the bar using tape and an additional mechanical attachment as necessary, free of charge.

Common questions

Which Model 3 and Model Y years appear in the lighting notices?

The taillight notice covers certain 2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles. The headlight notice covers certain 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles. The trailer brake-light notice concerns certain 2020 Model Y vehicles with the specified configuration.

Does the headlight brightness recall have a repair available?

The supplied headlight notice says the remedy is currently under development. It identifies excessive low-beam output on certain 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles. It does not describe a completed repair or state its cost.

Are both Cybertruck lighting problems fixed through software?

The front parking-light recall has a free OTA software update. The off-road light-bar recall requires Tesla Service to inspect the accessory and add a mechanical attachment, or replace it with tape and an additional mechanical attachment as necessary, free of charge.
