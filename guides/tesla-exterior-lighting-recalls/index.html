Why is my Tesla being recalled for its headlights or taillights?

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-08-05

These five recalls cover Tesla exterior lighting that either fails to work or shines brighter than federal rules allow. A 2022 recall covers 2020 Model Y and 2023 Model 3 taillights that may intermittently fail to illuminate. A larger 2026 recall covers low-beam headlights on 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y that can exceed the maximum brightness allowed, and a 2025 recall covers Cybertruck front parking lights with the same brightness problem. A 2020 recall covers 2020 Model Y trailer brake lights that fail to illuminate when towing with the factory tow package. A separate 2025 Cybertruck recall covers a service-installed off-road light bar that can loosen and detach from the windshield.

Warning signs to watch for

  • One or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate
  • Low-beam headlights or front parking lights may shine brighter than allowed, per the notices
  • Trailer brake lights may not illuminate when towing with the tow package
  • A service-installed off-road light bar may loosen and detach from the windshield

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five recall notices carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices describe reduced visibility to other drivers as the concern, whether from a taillight that doesn't illuminate, headlights or parking lights that shine too bright for oncoming traffic, or trailer brake lights that don't come on when towing. The light-bar recall instead describes a detached part becoming a road hazard for other vehicles. In every one of these five cases, the notice does not tell owners to stop driving while waiting for the repair.

What the free repair involves

Three of these recalls are corrected with a free over-the-air software update: the taillight, trailer-brake-light, and parking-light brightness fixes. The light-bar recall gets a free dealer inspection and reinforced or replaced attachment. The headlight-brightness recall's remedy was still under development as of the notice, with no fix released yet.

Common questions

Is there already a fix for the Tesla headlight brightness recall?

As of the notice, no — Tesla listed the remedy for the low-beam headlight brightness recall as still under development, with owner notification letters expected in October 2026 for affected owners.

Will Tesla fix my Cybertruck's detached light bar for free?

Yes. The notice says Tesla Service will inspect the light bar and add reinforcement or replace it, using an additional mechanical attachment as necessary, at no cost to the owner.

Why do brighter headlights count as a safety recall?

The notices say low beams or parking lights that exceed the federal maximum brightness can reduce visibility for oncoming drivers, which is why NHTSA required both brightness issues to be recalled.
