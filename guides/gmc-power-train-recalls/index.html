Why do GMC trucks and SUVs lose drive power or lock up?

10 NHTSA recalls · GMC · Power Train · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-07

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

Ten recalls hit GMC driveline hardware in three repeating patterns. Shafts lead: right front intermediate driveshafts on 2017-2018 Acadia and 2018 Terrain, an improperly welded driveshaft joint on 2019-2020 Sierra 1500, rear driveshafts on 2021 Yukon and Yukon XL, and 2023 Acadia half-shafts twice over — once improperly connected to the transmission, once from an incorrect sun gear. Transmission control valves that can lock the rear wheels form the second pattern, covering diesel 2020-2022 Sierra 1500, 2500 and 3500 and 2021 Yukon and Yukon XL, then 2022 Yukon and Yukon XL, then trucks repaired incorrectly. A start/stop accumulator with missing bolts covers 2018-2020 Terrain and 2019-2020 Acadia, and a transfer case recall reaches Yukon and Yukon XL.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Loss of propulsion — the start/stop accumulator recall covering 2018-2020 Terrain and 2019-2020 Acadia says missing endcap bolts could result in a transmission oil leak and may progress to a loss of propulsion.
  • Sudden loss of drive power — named in the 2021 Yukon and Yukon XL rear driveshaft recall.
  • Vehicle rollaway when the vehicle is placed in park — named in both 2023 Acadia half-shaft recalls.
  • Rear wheel lock-up — named in the diesel recall covering 2020-2022 Sierra 1500, 2500 and 3500 and 2021 Yukon and Yukon XL, and in the 2022 Yukon and Yukon XL transmission recall.
  • Front and/or rear wheels locking up without warning to the driver — named in the transfer case recall covering Yukon and Yukon XL.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall carries a stop-driving instruction: the transfer case recall covering certain 2026 Sierra 1500, Yukon and Yukon XL with four-wheel or all-wheel drive and certain 2015-2020 Yukon, Yukon XL and other GM SUVs. Its notice advises owners not to drive their vehicle until the remedy is completed. For the other nine recalls here — the driveshaft, half-shaft, sun gear, start/stop accumulator and transmission control valve recalls — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. None of the ten asks owners to park outside.

What the free repair involves

Remedies vary by recall. Dealers replace driveshafts, half-shafts or sun gears, and inspect and if necessary replace the transfer case assembly. On the start/stop recall they inspect the transmission accumulator and replace it if any bolts are missing. On the transmission control valve recalls they install new control module software. Every notice states the work is free of charge, and none is done over the air.

Common questions

My Yukon is on the do-not-drive recall — what does the notice say?

The transfer case notice advises owners not to drive their vehicle until the remedy is completed. Dealers will inspect and, if necessary, replace the transfer case assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 18, 2026.

What if my Sierra's transmission was already repaired under an earlier recall?

One recall covers 2020 Sierra 2500 and 3500 and 2020-2022 Sierra 1500 vehicles that may have been repaired incorrectly under recall 24V797. Dealers will inspect the vehicles and update the transmission control software, free of charge.

Does software fix a control valve that has already failed?

On the diesel recall, the notice says dealers install new transmission control module software free of charge, and that GM will provide a special coverage program to cover repair of transmissions identified by the remedy software as containing a defective control valve.
