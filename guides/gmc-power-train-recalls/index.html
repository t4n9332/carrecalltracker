Why do GMC trucks and SUVs keep losing power on the road?

10 NHTSA recalls · GMC · Power Train · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-07

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

These 10 recalls fall into three recurring patterns. Right-front driveshafts have fractured on 2018 Equinox/Terrain and 2017-2018 Acadia, and separated on 2019-2020 Silverado 1500/Sierra 1500 and 2021 Escalade/Suburban/Tahoe/Yukon. Half-shafts have separated from the transmission on 2023 XT5/Acadia and, from a sun-gear defect, on 2022-2023 XT5 and 2023 XT6/Blazer/Traverse/Acadia. A transmission control valve has triggered rear-wheel lockup across three recalls on 2020-2022 diesel and gas Silverado, Sierra, Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, and Escalade, including a 2026 repeat repair. Separately, missing accumulator bolts caused transmission leaks on many 2018-2020 GM crossovers, and a missing transfer case part can lock wheels on 2019-2020 Tahoe and Yukon.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A transmission fluid leak or fluid pooling under the vehicle
  • Sudden loss of drive power or propulsion while driving
  • A wheel locking up without warning while driving
  • The vehicle rolling away on its own after being placed in park

Is it safe to keep driving?

Of these 10 recalls, one carries a stop-driving instruction: the 2026 recall for 2019 Chevrolet Tahoe and 2019-2020 GMC Yukon vehicles with a missing transfer-case component, which tells owners not to drive the vehicle until it is inspected and repaired. None of the other nine recalls in this group carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction; for the driveshaft, half-shaft, transmission accumulator, and rear-wheel-lockup recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Across these recalls, dealers inspect and replace driveshaft or half-shaft assemblies, replace the transmission accumulator or transfer case assembly when parts are missing or damaged, and install updated transmission control module software to address rear-wheel lockup. All repairs are performed free of charge, with owners notified by mail once a fix is ready.

Common questions

Why do so many different GM models share the same transmission recall?

GM builds Chevrolet, GMC, Buick, and Cadillac models on shared driveline and transmission platforms, so a defective part like a control valve or driveshaft used across several brands triggers one recall covering multiple makes and models at once.

Are the wheel-lockup recalls all the same problem?

No. They involve different causes on different vehicles: a transmission control valve on 2020-2022 diesel and gas trucks and SUVs (three recalls), and a separate missing transfer-case component on 2019-2020 Tahoe and Yukon that carries a stop-driving instruction.

How do I know if my GMC has an open powertrain recall?

Check the vehicle's 17-character VIN through NHTSA's recall lookup or contact GMC customer service directly; GM also mails owner notification letters once a remedy is available for each specific recall.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
