Why Subaru control arms and suspension bolts keep getting recalled

3 NHTSA recalls · Subaru · Suspension · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 1, 2023

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

Subaru's suspension recalls are few, and each comes down to a fastener or a weld rather than a worn-out part. Two involve the front lower control arm: a left-front control arm weld that may fail and let the arm separate from the crossmember on the 2021 Impreza, and an improperly tightened ball joint castle nut on the 2023 Ascent that can result in retention pin failure and separation of the ball joint. The third covers rear stabilizer bracket bolts that may loosen and detach on the 2018-2019 Crosstrek, the 2019 Crosstrek Hybrid and the 2019 Forester. The Impreza is the model whose notice tells owners not to drive until the repair is complete.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices for these recalls do not describe any warning sign an owner would notice.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One of the three notices carries a stop-driving instruction: the 2021 Impreza left-front lower control arm recall, which advises owners not to drive their vehicle until the repair is complete. For the 2018-2019 Crosstrek, 2019 Crosstrek Hybrid and 2019 Forester rear stabilizer bolt recall, and for the 2023 Ascent ball joint recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. No notice in this group carries a park-outside instruction.

What the free repair involves

For the 2021 Impreza, dealers inspect the left-front lower control arm and replace it as necessary. For the 2023 Ascent, dealers inspect and, if necessary, replace the lower control arm. For the 2018-2019 Crosstrek, 2019 Crosstrek Hybrid and 2019 Forester, dealers retorque the rear stabilizer bracket bolts and replace any that are missing. All three notices say the work is free of charge.

Common questions

Can I drive my 2021 Impreza while waiting for the control arm repair?

That notice advises owners not to drive the vehicle until the repair is complete. Dealers inspect the left-front lower control arm and replace it as necessary, free of charge. Contact a Subaru dealer to arrange the repair.

Which models are in the rear stabilizer bolt recall?

It covers certain 2018-2019 Crosstrek, 2019 Crosstrek Hybrid and 2019 Forester vehicles, where the rear stabilizer bracket bolts may loosen and detach. A VIN lookup with Subaru or NHTSA confirms whether a particular vehicle is included.

What does the 2023 Ascent ball joint recall involve?

The notice says the left or right front lower control arm may have an improperly tightened ball joint castle nut, which can result in retention pin failure and separation of the ball joint. Dealers inspect and, if necessary, replace the control arm free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
