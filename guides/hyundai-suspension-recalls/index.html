Hyundai suspension recalls: coil springs, ball joints and loose bolts

4 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Suspension · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest May 19, 2026

Fasteners that were not tightened enough are the thread running through three of these four Hyundai suspension recalls. The 2020 Elantra recall covers lower control arm ball joint fasteners that may have been insufficiently tightened, allowing the ball joint to detach. Both Ioniq 5 recalls concern the rear suspension: one covers 2025 Ioniq 5 rear suspension alignment adjustment bolts that may have been improperly tightened, and the other covers rear suspension fasteners on 2025 Ioniq 5 and 2026 Ioniq 9 vehicles that may loosen and detach. The oldest recall is different: 2018 Santa Fe Sport front coil springs that may have been improperly manufactured and may fracture under certain loads while driving. The Ioniq 5 appears in two separate recalls.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices for these recalls do not describe any warning sign an owner would notice.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tell owners to stop driving, and none tell owners to park outside. The 2018 Santa Fe Sport notice states that the affected vehicles were all unsold on dealer lots, with the springs to be replaced before sale. The 2020 Elantra, the 2025 Ioniq 5 adjustment bolt recall and the 2025 Ioniq 5 and 2026 Ioniq 9 fastener recall each give owners no interim driving instruction beyond having the repair performed. Beyond that, the notices say nothing about continuing to drive.

What the free repair involves

For the 2018 Santa Fe Sport, dealers replace both front coil springs. For the 2020 Elantra, dealers inspect and, as necessary, tighten the ball joint fasteners. For the 2025 Ioniq 5 bolt recall, dealers replace the adjustment bolts, align the wheels and replace tires if necessary. For the rear suspension fastener recall, dealers inspect and replace the fasteners and perform a rear alignment as necessary. All are free of charge.

Common questions

Does the Santa Fe Sport coil spring recall affect cars already sold?

The notice for the 2018 Santa Fe Sport recall states that all of the affected vehicles were unsold on dealer lots, and that dealers will replace both front coil springs free of charge before the vehicle is sold.

Why is the Hyundai Ioniq 5 in two suspension recalls?

The notices describe two different rear suspension problems. One covers 2025 Ioniq 5 rear suspension alignment adjustment bolts that may have been improperly tightened. The other covers rear suspension fasteners on 2025 Ioniq 5 and 2026 Ioniq 9 vehicles that may loosen and detach.

Should I keep driving my Elantra until the ball joint fasteners are checked?

The 2020 Elantra notice does not tell owners to stop driving. It says a detached ball joint can cause a loss of vehicle control, increasing the risk of a crash, and that dealers will inspect and, as necessary, tighten the fasteners free of charge.
