Loose bolts and broken springs: Hyundai suspension recalls explained

4 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Suspension · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-19

Fasteners are the story in three of Hyundai's four suspension recalls. The 2025 IONIQ 5 appears twice: once for rear suspension alignment adjustment bolts that were improperly tightened, and again — alongside the 2026 IONIQ 9 — for rear suspension fasteners that can loosen and detach entirely, the most serious of the group. The 2020 Elantra was recalled for lower control arm ball joint fasteners that were insufficiently tightened, allowing the ball joint to separate from the arm. The odd one out is the 2018 Santa Fe Sport, where improperly manufactured front coil springs could fracture under load; every affected vehicle was still unsold, so dealers replaced the springs before delivery.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices list no specific warning sign for the loose or detaching fasteners — the first sign may be the loss of control itself.
  • Rear-end handling that feels unstable, since the IONIQ 5 defect is described as a loss of vehicle stability control.
  • Uneven or accelerated rear tire wear on the 2025 IONIQ 5, whose remedy includes a wheel alignment and, if needed, new tires.
  • On the 2018 Santa Fe Sport, a fractured front coil spring could cut a tire, so a sudden front tire failure is the associated risk.

Is it safe to keep driving?

No suspension recall here tells owners to stop driving or park outside. The 2025 IONIQ 5 and 2026 IONIQ 9 fastener recall is the one to move fastest on — the fasteners can loosen and detach, and the resulting loss of control would come without warning — so take the earliest inspection slot your dealer has. The IONIQ 5 adjustment bolt and 2020 Elantra ball joint recalls also allow normal driving while you schedule the repair. The 2018 Santa Fe Sport spring recall was completed before sale, so no owner should be driving an unrepaired one.

What the free repair involves

Mostly inspection and re-fastening. For the IONIQ 5 and IONIQ 9 rear fasteners, dealers inspect and replace them and perform a rear alignment as necessary. For the IONIQ 5 adjustment bolts, they replace the bolts, align the wheels and replace tires if needed. Elantra ball joint fasteners are inspected and tightened, and Santa Fe Sport front coil springs were replaced in pairs. All free.

Common questions

My 2025 IONIQ 5 shows two suspension recalls — is that a duplicate?

No. One covers rear alignment adjustment bolts that were improperly tightened; the other covers rear suspension fasteners that can loosen and detach, and it also includes the 2026 IONIQ 9. They are separate campaigns with separate remedies, so both need to be closed.

Will I need to pay for an alignment or tires afterward?

No. On the IONIQ 5 campaigns the dealer handles it: the remedy includes a rear or full wheel alignment, and on the adjustment bolt recall tires are replaced if necessary — all part of the free-of-charge repair.

I bought a used 2018 Santa Fe Sport — could the spring recall still be open?

Hyundai stated that every affected vehicle was unsold on dealer lots and that the front coil springs were replaced before sale. It should already be closed, but check your VIN on NHTSA's recall site or with a Hyundai dealer to confirm.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
