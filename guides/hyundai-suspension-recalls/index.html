What suspension problems have led to Hyundai recalls?

4 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Suspension · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-19

These four Hyundai suspension recalls span very different vehicles and parts. In 2018, unsold Santa Fe Sport vehicles on dealer lots were recalled because improperly manufactured front coil springs could fracture under load and puncture a tire. In 2020, the Elantra was recalled because lower control arm ball joint fasteners may not have been tightened enough, letting the ball joint detach. More recently, the Ioniq 5 has been recalled twice: in 2025 for rear suspension alignment bolts that may have been improperly tightened, and in 2026 for rear suspension fasteners that may loosen and detach. Both Ioniq 5 issues can cause a loss of vehicle control or stability.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A fractured front coil spring puncturing a tire (2018 Santa Fe Sport, unsold vehicles only)
  • No described warning before a ball joint detaches (2020 Elantra)
  • Loose rear suspension bolts causing a loss of stability control (2025 Ioniq 5)
  • Rear suspension fasteners loosening and detaching, leading to a loss of vehicle control (2025 Ioniq 5, 2026 recall)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four suspension recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The 2018 coil spring recall applied only to unsold vehicles on dealer lots, which were repaired before sale. The 2020 Elantra ball joint recall, and the 2025 and 2026 Ioniq 5 suspension bolt recalls, all say the defect can cause a loss of vehicle control or stability, increasing the risk of a crash — but none of the notices tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replaced both front coil springs on unsold Santa Fe Sport vehicles before sale, and inspect and tighten the Elantra's ball joint fasteners as needed. For the Ioniq 5, dealers replace the suspension alignment bolts and align the wheels, or inspect and replace rear suspension fasteners and realign the vehicle. All repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

Was the 2018 Hyundai Santa Fe Sport coil spring recall a risk to owners who already bought the car?

No. The notice says all affected vehicles were still unsold on dealer lots at the time of the recall, and dealers replaced both front coil springs free of charge before any of those vehicles were sold to a customer.

Why were two Ioniq 5 suspension recalls issued so close together?

The 2025 recall covered rear suspension alignment bolts that may have been improperly tightened; the 2026 recall covers rear suspension fasteners that may loosen and detach. The notices describe them as separate defects, both risking a loss of vehicle control.

What warning signs are there for the Elantra ball joint recall?

The notice for the 2020 Elantra doesn't describe a warning sign owners can notice — it just says loosely tightened fasteners can let the ball joint detach from the lower control arm, causing a loss of vehicle control.
