Why do Lincoln Aviator and Navigator seats move or detach?

8 NHTSA recalls · Lincoln · Seats · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-21

Seat recalls for Lincoln vehicles cover several unrelated defects. The Aviator and Explorer share the most repeats: a missing recliner pawl on 2020 models, a second-row seat switch that can bind and let the seat unlatch, fold, or slide on 2020-2025 models (two separate recalls), and an unoccupied driver's seat that can recline unexpectedly during remote unlock or start on 2025 models. The Navigator and Expedition share a 2018 model-year recall for a center seat missing reinforcement brackets and a 2020 model-year recall for a headrest bracket weld. The 2018 MKX and 2019-2020 Nautilus have a recall where seat wiring can affect airbag deployment, and the 2022 Aviator has a separate recall for a detaching head restraint cushion.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Seat back feels loose or reclines unexpectedly
  • Second-row seat unlatches, folds, or slides on its own
  • Seat appears to shift or move in the seat track
  • Head restraint cushion detaches when adjusted fully forward

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these eight recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices describe an increased risk of injury if a seat, seat back, headrest, or head restraint cushion fails to properly restrain an occupant in a crash. For the MKX and Nautilus, damaged seat wiring can cause the airbag to deploy inadvertently, raising the risk of a crash, or fail to deploy, raising the risk of injury. The notices do not tell owners to stop driving in any case.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect seat structures, recliner mechanisms, and seat tracks, replacing components as necessary; inspect and repair the second-row seat switch bezel; replace front-row head restraints on the Aviator; and inspect the seat wire harness on the MKX and Nautilus, adding protective tape or repairing damaged wires. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why do Lincoln Aviator and Explorer second-row seats move while driving?

Two recalls say the easy-entry second-row outer seat switch can bind or stick, causing the seat to unlatch, fold, tip, or slide unexpectedly. Dealers inspect the switch and replace the surrounding bezel as necessary, free of charge.

Is the Lincoln Navigator seat recall about the same problem as the seat belt recall?

No. The seat recalls involve missing center-seat brackets, a headrest weld, and unrelated structural issues, while separate recalls cover the seat belt pretensioner. Check your Navigator's VIN against each recall separately, since they address different components.

Does the Lincoln MKX seat wiring recall affect the airbag?

Yes. On the 2018 MKX and 2019-2020 Nautilus, the seat cushion frame can damage the wire harness during the seat's tilt function, which can cause the airbag to deploy inadvertently or fail to deploy in a crash.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
