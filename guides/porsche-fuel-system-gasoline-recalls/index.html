Why are Porsche fuel systems leaking and risking fire?

3 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Fuel System, Gasoline · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-09-23

All three recalls in this group involve a fuel system component that can leak fuel near a potential ignition source, and each notice identifies fire as the risk. A 2015-2018 Macan recall covers a fuel pump service cover that can crack after contacting the fuel pump's flow nozzle. A 2024-2026 Cayenne and Panamera recall (plus the 2025 Panamera E-Hybrid) covers high-pressure fuel pump fasteners that may not be tightened properly. A 2019-2020 Cayenne Turbo recall covers a fuel line quick connector in the engine compartment that may leak. Each recall targets a different fuel-system part, but the underlying concern — a fuel leak near an ignition source — is the same across all three.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices do not describe a smell, warning light, or other sign a driver would notice before a fuel pump cover, fuel pump fastener, or fuel line connector begins to leak.

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the three recall notices in this group tell owners to stop driving or to park outside. For the Macan, the notices say a fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source can increase the risk of a fire; the same risk is described for the Cayenne/Panamera fasteners and the Cayenne Turbo fuel line connector. The notices do not instruct owners to stop driving in any of these three cases.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace or rework the affected fuel system part depending on the recall: the Macan gets a reworked or replaced fuel pump flange with a protective film, the Cayenne/Panamera fuel pump screw connections are inspected and tightened (with the pump and fuel lines replaced if needed), and the Cayenne Turbo gets a new fuel line and quick connector. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Do I need to stop driving my Porsche because of these fuel system recalls?

No. None of the three recall notices tell owners to stop driving or park outside. They identify a fire risk from a fuel leak and direct owners to schedule a free repair at a dealer.

Is my Porsche's fuel pump or fuel line actually leaking?

The notices describe a defect that may cause a leak — a cracked fuel pump cover, loose fuel pump fasteners, or a leaking quick connector — but they don't say every affected vehicle is currently leaking, and they don't describe an owner-noticeable warning sign.

Which Porsche models are covered by these fuel system recalls?

The Macan (2015-2018), the Cayenne and Panamera (2024-2026, including the 2025 Panamera E-Hybrid), and the Cayenne Turbo (2019-2020) are each covered by a separate fuel system recall in this group.
