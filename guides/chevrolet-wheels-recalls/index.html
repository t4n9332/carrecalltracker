Why Are Some Chevrolet Wheel Lug Nuts and Bolts Loose?

3 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Wheels · model years 2019–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-14

Three Chevrolet recalls in this group involve wheel fasteners that were not correctly installed at the factory. On 2019 Chevrolet 5500HD trucks (shared with Isuzu-badged siblings), rear outer wheel lug nuts may not have been tightened properly. On 2023 Chevrolet Colorado trucks (shared with the GMC Canyon), front wheel hub bolts may have been over-tightened and damaged during installation. On 2025 Chevrolet Tahoe SUVs with 24-inch wheels (shared with Cadillac Escalade and GMC Yukon models), the wrong bolts were installed on the front wheel hubs, which can loosen or deform over time. In each case the concern is the same: fasteners that do not hold the wheel securely, spanning pickup trucks and full-size SUVs across three separate model years.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The notices do not describe any warning sign an owner would notice before a fastener fails.

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three Chevrolet wheel recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices for the 2019 Chevrolet 5500HD lug nut recall, the 2023 Chevrolet Colorado hub bolt recall, and the 2025 Chevrolet Tahoe wheel bolt recall do not tell owners to stop driving. Each notice instead describes the risk if the fasteners fail: a wheel separating from the vehicle or a loss of vehicle control, either of which increases the risk of a crash.

What the free repair involves

Across all three recalls, dealers inspect the affected wheel fasteners and tighten or replace them as necessary: lug nuts on the 2019 Chevrolet 5500HD, hub bolts on the 2023 Chevrolet Colorado, and hub bolts on the 2025 Chevrolet Tahoe. In every case the notice states the repair is performed free of charge.

Common questions

Do I need to stop driving my Chevrolet because of a wheel recall?

No. None of these three recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The notices describe the risk of a wheel separating or a loss of vehicle control if the fasteners fail, but do not instruct owners to stop using the vehicle.

What is actually wrong with the wheels on these Chevrolet vehicles?

The problems differ by recall: rear lug nuts that may not have been tightened on the 2019 5500HD, front hub bolts over-tightened and damaged on the 2023 Colorado, and incorrect bolts installed on 2025 Tahoe models with 24-inch wheels.

Will I have to pay to get my wheel fasteners fixed?

No. Each notice states that dealers will inspect and tighten or replace the affected lug nuts or bolts free of charge. Owners are notified by mail and should contact a dealer to schedule the repair.
