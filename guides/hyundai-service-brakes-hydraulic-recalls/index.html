Which Hyundai models were recalled for brake fire or failure risks?

9 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-04-11

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

These nine Hyundai brake recalls fall into two groups. Three are engine-compartment fire risks from a corroding or short-circuiting ABS module, affecting the Tucson (2018-2021), Santa Fe, Santa Fe Sport and Santa Fe XL (2018-2019) — all three carry a park-outside instruction. The other six involve reduced braking performance rather than fire: contaminated brake fluid swelling seals in the Palisade, a sharp-edged brake master cylinder in the Accent, a misaligned brake booster diaphragm in the Palisade, faulty sensor signals in the Kona Electric and Nexo, and software problems in the Ioniq 5 N that can weaken braking or cause unintended acceleration.

Warning signs to watch for

  • No warning is described for the ABS module fire risk — the notices say it can corrode or short internally without mentioning a sign to notice
  • Longer brake pedal travel or a longer distance needed to stop (Accent)
  • Reduced brake feel when using the Ioniq 5 N's left-foot braking feature
  • Vehicle continuing to accelerate after you release the pedal with N e-shift engaged (Ioniq 5 N)

Is it safe to keep driving?

Three of these nine recalls — the 2020 Tucson HECU fire recall, the 2022 Santa Fe/Santa Fe Sport/Santa Fe XL ABS recall, and the 2022 Santa Fe Sport ABS recall — tell owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired, because of an engine-compartment fire risk; none tell owners to stop driving. The two Ioniq 5 N software recalls instead tell owners not to use the left-foot braking or N e-shift features until updated. For the remaining recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Repairs vary by recall: dealers replace a fuse or the ABS multi-fuse for the fire-risk recalls, update the anti-lock brake or vehicle control software, replace the brake master cylinder and flush the fluid, or inspect and replace the brake booster. The Ioniq 5 N software update can be applied over-the-air or by a dealer. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Which Hyundai brake recalls tell owners to park outside?

The 2020 Tucson HECU recall, the 2022 Santa Fe/Santa Fe Sport/Santa Fe XL ABS recall, and the 2022 Santa Fe Sport ABS recall all tell owners to park outside and away from structures because a corroded or shorting ABS module can cause an engine fire.

Are the Ioniq 5 N brake problems fixed with a software update?

Yes. Hyundai recalled the 2025 Ioniq 5 N twice for integrated electronic brake and vehicle control software that can reduce braking or cause unintended acceleration; the fix can be applied over-the-air or by a dealer, free of charge.

What causes reduced braking in the Accent and Palisade recalls?

The Accent's brake master cylinder had sharp edges that could damage an oil seal, and the Palisade was recalled separately for contaminated brake fluid swelling seals and for a brake booster vacuum leak — each can reduce braking performance.
