Hyundai brake recalls: ABS fire risk and reduced braking performance

9 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 11, 2025

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Nine Hyundai hydraulic brake recalls fall into two kinds. Three are fire recalls tied to anti-lock brake electronics, and all three carry a park-outside instruction: the September 2020 Tucson HECU corrosion recall covering 2019-2021 Tucson, later expanded to add 2016-2018 Tucson; the February 2022 recall covering 2016-2018 Santa Fe, 2017-2018 Santa Fe Sport, 2019 Santa Fe XL and 2014-2015 Tucson; and an October 2022 recall for the 2018 Santa Fe Sport. The other six concern reduced braking performance: master cylinder problems on the 2021 Palisade and 2021 Accent, a brake booster vacuum leak on the 2023 Palisade, IEB software on 2019-2020 Nexo and 2019-2021 Kona Electric, and two 2025 IONIQ 5 N software recalls.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Longer brake pedal travel, or a longer distance required to stop (2021 Accent brake master cylinder recall).
  • Significantly reduced braking performance (2019-2020 Nexo and 2019-2021 Kona Electric integrated electronic brake software recall).
  • Reduced braking performance when the left-foot braking feature is activated (both 2025 IONIQ 5 N recalls).
  • The vehicle continuing to accelerate after the pedal is released with the N e-shift feature engaged (April 2025 IONIQ 5 N recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

Three of these nine recalls carry a park-outside instruction: the September 2020 Tucson HECU recall, the February 2022 Santa Fe, Santa Fe Sport, Santa Fe XL and Tucson ABS module recall, and the October 2022 Santa Fe Sport ABS module recall each advise parking outside and away from structures until the repair is complete. No notice in this group tells owners to stop driving. The February 2025 IONIQ 5 N notice advises not using the left-foot braking feature until the remedy is performed, and the April 2025 notice advises not using left-foot braking or N e-Shift until the remedy is performed.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the HECU fuse in the Tucson recall and update Electronic Stability Control software; the two ABS module recalls replace the ABS multi-fuse. Others update IEB software, replace the brake master cylinder and flush the fluid on the 2021 Palisade, replace the master cylinder on the Accent, and inspect and replace the 2023 Palisade brake booster. The IONIQ 5 N software update can be over-the-air. All free of charge.

Common questions

Why do the Hyundai ABS recalls tell me to park outside?

The 2020 Tucson HECU recall and the two ABS module recalls state that corrosion or a module malfunction can cause an electrical short and an engine compartment fire, and each advises parking outside and away from structures until the recall repair is complete.

What do the IONIQ 5 N recalls say about left-foot braking?

Both 2025 notices say the integrated electronic brake and vehicle control unit software may cause reduced braking performance when left-foot braking is activated, and advise owners not to use the feature until the remedy is performed. The April notice also covers continued acceleration with N e-shift engaged.

Do I have to pay for these brake repairs?

No. Each of these notices states the remedy is performed free of charge, whether that is a fuse, a master cylinder, a brake booster or a software update. Owners may contact Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460 to check their VIN.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
