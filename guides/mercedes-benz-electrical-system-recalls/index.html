Why does Mercedes-Benz keep recalling vehicles for electrical fire risks?

22 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Electrical System · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-01

⚠ 4 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Electrical system recalls at Mercedes-Benz center on two repeating fire risks: high-voltage battery faults in the EQB, recalled four times between February 2025 and February 2026 for internal battery failure and a bus-bar screw issue causing sudden loss of drive power, and wiring faults in AMG models — the CLS53 and E53 recalled three times for transmission wiring harness corrosion or an overheated 48-volt ground cable. Sprinter vans face a separate cluster: multimedia/camera software, seat wiring affecting the side air bag, a park-lock software error letting the van move after being shifted into Park, and an eCall failure. A 2016-2019 Metris recall involves an exposed battery terminal.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Exposed battery compartment under the front passenger seat when the seat is moved forward (2016-2019 Metris)
  • Multimedia or rearview display going black or rebooting (2019-2021 Sprinter)
  • Vehicle moving after being shifted into Park without the parking brake applied (2019-2020 Sprinter)
  • Fire-risk wiring, cable, and battery recalls (AMG, EQB) describe no warning sign before a fire
  • eCall emergency system failure (2019-2021 Sprinter/Metris) is not something an owner would notice until it's needed

Is it safe to keep driving?

Four recalls in this group tell owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired: the February 2025 EQB battery fire recall, the July 2025 EQB loss-of-drive-power recall, the December 2025 EQB battery fire recall, and the February 2026 EQB battery fire recall. Three of those also tell owners to charge no higher than 80% capacity in the meantime. None of the other recalls here — including the Metris battery cover, the AMG wiring and cable recalls, and the Sprinter recalls — tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Fixes vary widely: dealers install a battery compartment cover (Metris), inspect and repair or reroute wiring with an added bracket (AMG), replace a wiring harness (AMG), update battery management software or replace the high-voltage battery (EQB), update multimedia, park-lock, or communication module software — sometimes over the air (Sprinter), and repair seat wiring with added clips (Sprinter). All repairs are free.

Common questions

Which Mercedes-Benz electrical recall requires parking outside?

Four EQB high-voltage battery recalls, issued between February 2025 and February 2026, tell owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired, and most also limit charging to 80% capacity in the meantime.

Are Mercedes-Benz AMG models prone to electrical fires?

The CLS53 and E53 have been recalled three times for wiring or cable issues — corroded transmission wiring harness connectors and an overheated 48-volt ground cable — that the notices say can increase fire risk.

What electrical problems affect Mercedes-Benz Sprinter vans?

Sprinter recalls in this group cover multimedia and camera software, seat wiring that can affect the side air bag, a park-lock software error, and an eCall communication failure, each fixed free by dealers.
