Mercedes-Benz electrical recalls: EQB battery fires, AMG wiring, Sprinter software

22 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Electrical System · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-01

⚠ 4 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Two clusters dominate. Four recalls cover the EQB electric SUV: three say the high voltage battery may fail internally and lead to a fire while parked or driving, and one says bus bar screws may cause a sudden loss of drive power. Three more concern fire risk in the AMG E53, CLS53, GT43 and GT53 range — a misrouted transmission wiring harness exposed to corrosion, a transmission harness connection that may not have been correctly wired, and a 48-volt ground cable connection that may not be tightened properly. Others cover van electronics: Sprinter MBUX displays, swivel seat wiring, a park lock software error, an eCall SIM card fault and a missing Metris battery compartment cover.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Recall VS3PA27 ERI/ERO (2019-2020 Sprinter): the vehicle may move after the gear selector has been placed in Park without the parking brake applied.
  • Recall VS3UXPOBOX (2019-2021 Sprinter): the MBUX display may remain black or reboot after startup, so the rearview camera image may not display properly.
  • The July 2025 EQB recall: the notice says the system can have a sudden loss of drive power without warning.
  • Recall VS2SIMKOM/VS3SIMKOM (Sprinter, Metris): a mobile network connection failure can disable the emergency call (eCall) system.
  • The notices for the EQB high voltage battery fire recalls and for the AMG wiring and 48-volt ground cable recalls do not describe a warning sign an owner would notice.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

Four EQB recalls carry a park-outside instruction. The February 2025, December 2025 and February 2026 high voltage battery recalls tell owners to park outside and away from structures and to charge to no more than 80% of battery capacity until the repair is complete. The July 2025 bus bar recall tells owners to park outside until the recall repair is complete. None of these notices tells owners to stop driving. For the AMG wiring, 48-volt cable, Sprinter and Metris recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

For the EQB, dealers update the battery management system software in two recalls, replace the high voltage battery in another, and inspect and replace it as necessary in the fourth. For the AMG models, dealers repair and reroute the transmission harness, replace the two-part harness and connector, or inspect the 48-volt ground cable. Van recalls bring software updates, wiring clips or a battery cover. These repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

I own an EQB — what do the notices say I should do right now?

The February 2025, December 2025 and February 2026 battery recalls tell owners to park outside away from structures and charge to no more than 80% until the repair is done. The July 2025 recall tells owners to park outside until the repair is complete.

My AMG E53 already had a wiring harness recall repair — is it finished?

The June 2024 notice says the transmission wiring harness connection might not have been correctly wired during a prior recall repair and during production, and that dealers will replace the two-part wiring harness and connector free of charge.

Why do so many Sprinter and Metris vans show up under electrical recalls?

The van notices cover separate systems: MBUX multimedia software, front seat swivel wiring, park lock software, emergency call SIM card software, and a Metris backup battery compartment that may have no cover. Each is its own recall with its own dealer remedy.
