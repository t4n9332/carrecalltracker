Why do Lincoln transmission and drivetrain recalls keep repeating?

12 NHTSA recalls · Lincoln · Power Train · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 24, 2026

Across these twelve powertrain recalls, Lincoln's Aviator, Navigator, Corsair and Nautilus keep returning for the same two themes: vehicles that may not stay in Park, and drivetrain parts that give out. Rollaway recalls cover the 2018 Navigator (missing transmission roll pin), the 2023 Aviator (loose transmission bolt), the 2018-2021 Navigator and 2020-2021 Aviator (park pawl damage), and the 2020 Aviator (missing Manual Park Release cover). Drive-power recalls hit the 2020 Nautilus and 2021 Corsair (rear drive unit lubricant), the 2020 Aviator (drive shaft fracture), the 2023 Navigator (front axle pinion), the 2021-2022 Corsair (HPCM software) and the 2025 Aviator (loose cross member bolts). A 2020 recall covers the 2019-2020 Nautilus and 2020 Corsair for a transmission fluid leak with fire risk.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2019 Manual Park Release recall (2020 Aviator) says vehicles in Factory Mode do not display warning alerts or the transmission gear selected on the instrument cluster.
  • The 2024 and 2025 HPCM software recalls (2021-2022 Corsair) say the vehicle may shift into neutral unexpectedly, causing a loss of drive power.
  • The 2025 valve body recall (2025 Aviator) names reverse gear failure, or unexpected forward movement in reverse or neutral gear.
  • The 2023 front axle pinion recall (2023 Navigator) says a fractured pinion may cause the front wheels to lock up and a loss of four-wheel drive.
  • The 2018 roll pin recall (2018 Navigator) and the 2023 loose bolt recall (2023 Aviator) say the shifter position and instrument panel may indicate Park when the vehicle is not secured in park.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these twelve notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. For the park pawl recall covering the 2018-2021 Navigator and 2020-2021 Aviator, Ford mailed interim letters notifying owners of the safety risk, with additional letters to follow once the remedy is available. Every other notice states only the risk — rollaway, loss of drive power, loss of vehicle control, or, for the start/stop accumulator recall, an increased fire risk — without any instruction to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall. Dealers replace the start/stop accumulator, the drive shaft as necessary, the front axle pinion gear set, the transmission main control valve body, and transmissions or cross member bolts found loose; install a missing roll pin or Manual Park Release cover; top up or replace rear drive axles; and update HPCM or powertrain control module software. Every notice states the repair is free of charge.

Common questions

My Lincoln's display shows Park — does that mean it is in Park?

Three notices describe park faults. The 2018 Navigator may be missing a transmission roll pin; it may roll away if the parking brake is not applied. The 2023 Aviator may have a loose transmission bolt, and the 2018-2021 Navigator and 2020-2021 Aviator may suffer park pawl damage.

Why was my Corsair recalled twice for shifting into neutral?

The 2024 recall updates the Hybrid Powertrain Control Module software on 2021-2022 Corsairs. A 2025 recall covers vehicles previously repaired incorrectly under that recall and updates the same software again. Both notices say the update is free; check your VIN to see which applies.

Which of these recalls involve a fire risk?

Two. The 2020 start/stop accumulator recall (2019-2020 Nautilus, 2020 Corsair) says a transmission fluid leak near an ignition source may increase fire risk. The 2020 Aviator drive shaft recall says a fractured shaft can lead to a fuel leak or fire.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
