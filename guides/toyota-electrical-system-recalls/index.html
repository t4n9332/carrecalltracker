Why do Toyota electrical recalls keep warning about fires?

17 NHTSA recalls · Toyota · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-27

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Toyota's electrical-system recalls in this group split into two patterns: short circuits that raise fire risk, and software or wiring faults that disable safety features. Fire-risk cases include the 2018 Prius engine wire harness, 2021-2022 RAV4 Prime DC-DC converter, 2013-2018 RAV4 replacement batteries, and the Toyota Supra's shared BMW starter relay. Software faults include a Tundra instrument display going blank, a Corolla Cross passenger air bag that may not deploy, Prius rear doors that can open on their own, and a wider instrument panel warning-light failure across RAV4, Highlander and other models. A 2018-2019 Tundra, Sequoia and Avalon air bag ECU fault and an aftermarket Highlander windshield wiring issue round out the group.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Instrument panel or display going blank, or not showing speed, brake and tire pressure warnings
  • A rear door opening unexpectedly, especially if water has entered the door switch
  • Engine stalling, including during cold-weather acceleration or after an aftermarket windshield replacement
  • Most fire-risk and air bag recalls in this group describe no warning sign before the fault

Is it safe to keep driving?

One recall in this group carries a park-outside instruction: the 2025 Toyota Supra and BMW starter relay recall (which also covers 2019-2022 BMW models) tells owners to park outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete, because a corroded relay may short circuit and increase fire risk. The other recalls in this group — including the RAV4 Prime, Tundra, Corolla Cross, Prius, Highlander, Sequoia, Avalon, and a second Toyota Supra starter recall — do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Across this group, dealers inspect and replace wiring, connectors, batteries, converters or starters found to be damaged, and update software in the air bag, engine, instrument panel and hybrid control computers — sometimes delivered over-the-air. Toyota also replaces door switches and instrument panels where needed. All repairs, parts and software updates are performed free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Which Toyota models are most affected by these electrical recalls?

The Prius, RAV4 and RAV4 Prime, Tundra, and Toyota Supra appear most often in this group, with issues ranging from wiring shorts and battery fitment to software errors affecting displays, air bags and doors.

Do any of these recalls say to stop driving?

Only the 2025 Toyota Supra and BMW starter relay recall tells owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired. The other recalls in this group do not instruct owners to stop driving.

Will the repair cost anything?

No. Every recall in this group states that dealers will inspect, repair, replace parts, or update software free of charge, and owners are notified by mail when the remedy is available.
