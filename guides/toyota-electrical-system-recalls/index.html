Why do so many Toyota electrical recalls involve fire risk?

17 NHTSA recalls · Toyota · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest May 27, 2026

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Toyota's electrical recalls here fall into three repeating patterns. Fire risk from short circuits and overheating is the most common: the engine wire harness at the hybrid Power Control Unit on 2016-2018 Prius, the DC-DC converter on 2021-2022 RAV4 Prime, replacement 12-volt batteries on 2013-2018 RAV4, and the engine starter shared with BMW on 2020-2022 and 2021-2023 Supra. A second pattern is display software: 2022-2023 Tundra and Tundra Hybrid combination meters going blank, and a software error that can leave the instrument panel on 2023-2025 RAV4, Highlander and GR Corolla and 2024-2025 Tacoma and Grand Highlander without speed, brake and tire pressure warnings. A third involves air bags, on 2018-2019 Tundra and Sequoia and 2022-2023 Corolla Cross.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A blank instrument panel LCD display, in the 2022-2023 Tundra and Tundra Hybrid combination meter software recall.
  • An instrument panel that does not display vehicle speed, brake system or tire pressure warning lights, in the 2023-2025 instrument panel software recall.
  • An engine stall, in the 2021 RAV4 Prime recall covering rapid acceleration in EV Mode during cold weather driving.
  • An engine stall, in the Wanda aftermarket replacement windshield recall for 2014-2018 Highlander.
  • An unlocked rear door opening unexpectedly, in the 2023-2024 Prius and Prius Prime door switch recall.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall in this group carries a park-outside instruction: the BMW starter relay recall covering 2020-2022 Toyota Supra tells owners to park outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete. In the 2021-2022 RAV4 Prime DC-DC converter recall, owners are advised not to charge the vehicle when the ambient air temperature is below or expected to drop below 41 degrees Fahrenheit. None of the notices in this group tells owners to stop driving, including the 2021-2023 Supra engine starter recall.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall. Most are hardware: dealers replace the engine starter on Supra, the DC-DC converter on RAV4 Prime, both rear door switches on Prius, and the battery clamp, tray and terminal cover on RAV4. Others are software updates, for the air bag ECU, the Tundra combination meter and the RAV4 Prime HEV ECU. The 2023-2025 instrument panel update is over-the-air for non-plug-in models. All of this work is free of charge.

Common questions

My Supra is in the starter relay recall — what does the notice tell me to do meanwhile?

The recall covering 2020-2022 Supra advises owners to park their vehicles outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete. It does not tell owners to stop driving. Dealers replace the engine starter free of charge.

Can I still charge my 2021-2022 RAV4 Prime?

The DC-DC converter notice advises owners not to charge the vehicle when the ambient air temperature is below or expected to drop below 41 degrees Fahrenheit. It does not tell owners to stop driving. Dealers replace the DC-DC converter free of charge.

Will the instrument panel recall be fixed over the air?

In the 2023-2025 instrument panel software recall, dealers update the software over-the-air for non-plug-in hybrid vehicles, free of charge. For plug-in hybrids, dealers inspect the instrument panel assembly and either replace it or update the software, also free of charge.
