Why Do Chevrolet Bolt EVs Keep Getting Battery Fire Recalls?

14 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-12-12

⚠ 5 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Twelve GM electrical-system recalls affecting Chevrolet vehicles include five separate recalls, filed between 2020 and 2024, for the same underlying issue: the Bolt EV and Bolt EUV's high-voltage battery can catch fire when charged to full or near-full capacity. Other recalls are one-off: a Silverado/Sierra battery cable connection that can cause stalling and arcing (2019), a Malibu software error that disables fuel injectors (2019), a Corvette hood that can open while driving (2020), medium-duty trucks whose ABS warning light may not illuminate (2020), an Express Cutaway Van wiring corrosion issue (2023), a blank instrument panel on newer Buick/Chevrolet crossovers (2023), and insufficiently insulated rear drive unit wiring on 2024 EVs including the Chevrolet Blazer EV and Silverado EV.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Vehicle stalling or failing to start unexpectedly (2019-2020 Silverado/Sierra battery cable issue, 2018 Malibu fuel-injector software error).
  • Instrument panel display going blank at startup or while driving (2024 Buick Encore GX, Envista, and Chevrolet Trax).
  • Hood or front trunk lid popping open while driving above a certain speed (2020 Corvette).
  • ABS malfunction warning light failing to turn on even when there's a problem (2019-2020 Chevrolet medium-duty trucks).
  • Sudden loss of drive power (2024 EVs including the Chevrolet Blazer EV and Silverado EV).

Is it safe to keep driving?

Five Bolt EV/EUV battery-fire recalls, filed in 2020, twice in 2021, and twice more in 2024, tell owners to limit charging to 90%, charge more often, avoid letting the range drop below 70 miles, park outside after charging, and not charge indoors overnight. None of these recalls, or the other seven electrical recalls in this group covering the Silverado, Malibu, Corvette, medium-duty trucks, Express Cutaway Van, Buick/Chevrolet crossovers, and 2024 EVs, carry a stop-driving instruction.

What the free repair involves

For the Bolt EV/EUV recalls, dealers ultimately replace defective battery modules or update diagnostic software after the interim charging steps. Elsewhere, dealers clean or replace battery cable connections, reprogram ECM/BCM/VCU software (some via over-the-air update), reseal HVAC wiring, or replace rear drive units — all free of charge.

Common questions

Why does my Chevrolet Bolt EV keep getting battery recalls?

GM issued five separate recalls between 2020 and 2024 for the same high-voltage battery fire risk on the Bolt EV/EUV — later ones were needed because the software fix or diagnostic update from an earlier recall didn't fully catch defective battery modules.

Can I still charge my recalled Chevrolet Bolt EV?

The notices tell owners to limit charging to 90%, charge more often, avoid letting the range drop below 70 miles, park outside after charging, and not charge indoors overnight, as interim steps until the dealer repair is completed.

What electrical problem affects the 2024 Chevrolet Blazer EV and Silverado EV?

GM recalled certain 2024 Blazer EV and Silverado EV models, along with the GMC Hummer EV and Sierra EV and Cadillac Lyriq, because insufficiently insulated wires in the rear drive unit's electric motor can contact each other and cause a loss of drive power.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
