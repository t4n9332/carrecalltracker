Which Ford exterior lighting recalls affect headlights, brake lights and tail lights?

27 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 22, 2026

Two patterns dominate this Ford group, and both are Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 108 issues. The larger one is false brake lights: six separate recalls cover 2017-2019 ambulances, fire trucks and first-responder vehicles built on Ford F-550, F-450, E-350 and E-450 chassis and fitted with Whelen brake lights that can illuminate when the brake pedal has not been pressed. The second is daytime running lights that will not dim — 2018-2020 F-150 trucks with high-series LED headlamps, and 2018-2019 F-250, F-350 and F-450 Super Duty. The rest are one-offs: flickering parking lights on 2022-2023 Bronco Sport, burnt LED driver diodes on 2025 Mustang and Lincoln Nautilus, a stop light switch on 2025 F-53, and loose tail light connectors on 2019 Ranger.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Daytime running lights stay on instead of dimming to parking lamps when the master lighting switch is turned from Autolamps to Headlamps On (2018-2020 F-150, recall 20C03).
  • Daytime running lights may not dim when the headlight switch is moved to the "On" position (2018-2019 F-250, F-350 and F-450 Super Duty, recall 19C10).
  • Parking lights may flicker when the headlights are activated (2022-2023 Bronco Sport, recall 22C22).
  • Brake lights may illuminate when the brake pedal has not been pressed (the Whelen brake light recalls on Ford-chassis ambulances, fire trucks and first-responder vehicles).
  • Rear lights may function intermittently or not at all (2019 Ranger inline connector recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the notices here carries a stop-driving instruction, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside. Each states the consequence and then says only that owners are not told to stop driving. The 2025 F-53 notice adds that the vehicle may be shifted out of park without the brake pedal applied, increasing the risk of a rollaway and crash, and the 2025 Mustang and Lincoln Nautilus notice says a headlight failure can reduce visibility for the driver and other road users. Neither notice gives owners an interim step.

What the free repair involves

Dealers reprogram the body control module on the F-150 (20C03) and Super Duty (19C10), replace the LED driver module on Bronco Sport (22C22), inspect and replace LED driver modules on 2025 Mustang and Nautilus (25C39), replace the stop light switch on F-53 (26C01), and inspect and replace the Ranger inline connectors if needed. On the emergency vehicles, Whelen or the body builder replaces the brake lights. All free of charge.

Common questions

My ambulance or fire truck is on a Ford chassis — who handles the brake light recall?

These recalls were filed by the body builders, not Ford. The notices direct owners to Whelen or to the company that built the vehicle — Watts, Excellance, Fire Equipment Sales & Service, Blanchat, Taylor Made or Unruh Fire — to have the brake lights replaced.

Do the daytime running light recalls mean my headlights won't work?

The notices describe a dimming problem, not a failure. The F-150 notice (20C03) says the daytime running lights may remain activated instead of dimming to parking lamps; the Super Duty notice (19C10) says they may not dim when the headlight switch is moved to "On".

Which of these recalls affect the rear lights?

Several do. The 2019 Ranger notice covers stop, turn, rear position and reverse lamp functions; the 2025 F-53 notice covers brake lights; the six Whelen recalls cover brake lights on Ford-chassis emergency vehicles; and the 2025 Nautilus notice names the rear tail light, rear turn signal and rear position light.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
