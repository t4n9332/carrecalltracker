What steering and driver-assist defects has Tesla recalled?

4 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Steering · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-02-14

These four Tesla recalls cover different parts of the steering and driver-assist systems rather than one repeating defect. Two involve loss of power steering assist: a 2022 recall for Model S and Model X electronic power steering that can lose assist on rough roads, and a 2025 recall for Model 3 where a circuit board overstress can cause a loss of assist when accelerating from a stop. A 2023 recall covers Full Self-Driving Beta software on Model S, Model X, Model 3, and Model Y acting unsafely at intersections. A separate 2023 recall covers Model S and Model X airbags installed incorrectly after a steering yoke or wheel swap.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Steering may suddenly require much greater effort, especially at low speeds or after hitting a pothole (power steering recalls)
  • Vehicle with FSD Beta may travel through an intersection without stopping, use a turn-only lane to go straight, or proceed during a steady yellow light
  • No driver-noticeable symptom for the airbag/steering yoke recall — found only by inspection of past service work

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The power steering recalls describe increased steering effort, particularly at low speed, without telling owners to stop driving. The FSD Beta recall describes the software's unsafe intersection behavior without a stop-driving instruction, and Tesla addressed it with a remote software update. The airbag recall, affecting vehicles where the steering yoke or wheel was previously replaced, also carries no stop-driving instruction. Across this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Three of these four recalls are fixed with an over-the-air software update: the EPAS recalibration, the FSD Beta update, and the 2025 power steering fix. For the airbag recall, Tesla Service inspects the vehicle and replaces the driver air bag if it is the wrong type for the installed steering yoke or wheel. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Do I need to visit a Tesla service center for these steering recalls?

Only for the airbag recall, which requires an in-person inspection and possible air bag replacement. The two power steering recalls and the FSD Beta recall are fixed through an over-the-air software update instead.

What does the Full Self-Driving Beta recall actually fix?

It addresses FSD Beta software that could let the car travel through an intersection without stopping, use a turn-only lane to go straight, or proceed during a steady yellow light. Tesla fixed it with a software update.

Why would a steering wheel replacement affect my airbag?

If a steering yoke or wheel was swapped for a different style, the air bag originally installed may not be designed for the replacement, so Tesla inspects and replaces it if needed.
