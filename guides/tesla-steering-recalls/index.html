Tesla steering recalls: power steering assist loss, Full Self-Driving and air bags

4 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Steering · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-02-14

Two of these four Tesla recalls are about losing power steering assist, but for different reasons and different cars. The 2022 recall covers 2017-2021 Model S and Model X, where the electronic power assist steering system may lose assist when driving on rough roads or after hitting a pothole. The 2025 recall covers 2023 Model 3 and Model Y running software prior to 2023.38.4, where the steering assist circuit board may experience an overstress condition and lose assist when the vehicle reaches a stop and then accelerates again. The other two are unrelated: Full Self-Driving Beta behavior at intersections on 2016-2023 Model S and Model X, 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y, and an incorrect driver air bag installed when a steering yoke or wheel was replaced on 2021-2024 Model S and Model X.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Greater steering effort, especially at low speeds, if power steering assist is lost (2022 Model S and Model X recall SB-22-00-014).
  • Loss of power steering assist when the vehicle reaches a stop and then accelerates again (2025 Model 3 and Model Y recall SB-25-00-004).
  • FSD Beta traveling straight through an intersection while in a turn-only lane, entering a stop sign-controlled intersection without a complete stop, or proceeding on a steady yellow (2023 recall SB-23-00-001).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. The 2022 Model S and Model X power steering recall, the 2023 Full Self-Driving Beta recall, the 2023 driver air bag recall for replaced steering yokes and wheels, and the 2025 Model 3 and Model Y power steering recall each state the risk — greater steering effort, unlawful or unpredictable movement through intersections, or an air bag that may deploy incorrectly — without any interim instruction to owners about continuing to drive.

What the free repair involves

Three of the four are over-the-air software updates: recalibrating the EPAS system on 2017-2021 Model S and Model X, an update for Full Self-Driving Beta, and an update for the 2023 Model 3 and Model Y recall. For the air bag recall, Tesla Service inspects and replaces the driver air bag as necessary. All four notices say the remedy is free of charge.

Common questions

Will the power steering recalls be fixed without a service visit?

Both power steering notices describe an over-the-air software update, free of charge — the 2022 one recalibrates the EPAS system on 2017-2021 Model S and Model X, and the 2025 one covers 2023 Model 3 and Model Y running software prior to 2023.38.4.

Why is an air bag recall grouped under steering?

Because it follows a steering part. The notice covers 2021-2024 Model S and Model X where an incorrect air bag may have been installed when the steering yoke or round steering wheel was replaced with a different style. Service inspects and replaces the driver air bag as necessary.

Do the notices say to stop using Full Self-Driving Beta?

They do not give that instruction. The 2023 notice says FSD Beta may allow the vehicle to act unsafe around intersections and to exceed speed limits, and that Tesla will release an over-the-air software update, free of charge, for the listed vehicles.
