Why do so many Kia electrical recalls warn of fire risk?

19 NHTSA recalls · Kia · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-02

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Fire risk is the most common thread among Kia's electrical recalls reviewed here: a wiring harness damaged by a fender burr in the 2018 Stinger, a loosening alternator terminal nut in the 2020 Sedona and 2023 Sportage, a high-voltage battery short-circuit in the 2018-2019 Soul EV, inadequate HVAC blower wiring in the 2021-2023 Sorento, and misaligned battery electrodes in the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 — the only recall here with a park-outside instruction. A second pattern involves a loss of drive power or steering assist: the 2022-2024 EV6's charging unit, the 2024 Sorento's power steering wiring, and the 2025 EV9's rear drive motor. A third group involves software errors affecting the 2024-2025 Sorento's headlights and taillights and the 2025 K4 and Sorento's instrument panel display.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Loss of low beam headlights and taillights (2024-2025 Sorento)
  • Instrument panel display failure, including loss of the speedometer or tire pressure display (2025 K4 and Sorento)
  • Loss of drive power while driving (2022-2024 EV6, 2025 EV9)
  • Loss of power steering assist, making the vehicle harder to control (2024 Sorento)
  • The wiring, alternator, and battery fire-risk recalls describe no warning sign before a fire occurs

Is it safe to keep driving?

Only one of these recalls, the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 battery fire recall, carries an interim instruction: Kia tells owners to park outside and away from structures and to limit charging to 80% until the repair is done. None of these recalls tell owners to stop driving entirely. For the rest — the Stinger, Sedona, Sportage, Soul EV, Sorento (wiring, steering, headlight, and display recalls), and the other EV6 and EV9 recalls — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Kia dealers address these recalls by inspecting and replacing damaged wiring harnesses and battery components, tightening loose alternator terminal nuts, and replacing the EV6 charging unit or EV9 drive motor. Software fixes include an updated battery management system for the Soul EV and, for the Sorento headlight and instrument panel issues, a control-unit software update delivered by a dealer or, in one case, over-the-air. All repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

My Kia EV6 or EV9 battery recall says park outside — what does that mean?

For the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 battery fire recall, Kia tells owners to park outside and away from structures and to limit charging to 80% until the dealer replaces the high voltage battery system, because of the fire risk described in the notice.

Why do so many Kia recalls mention a fire risk?

The notices reviewed here point to different causes — a chafed wiring harness, a loosening alternator nut, a damaged HVAC wiring harness, and battery defects — each of which the notices say can lead to a short circuit or fire, not one shared root cause.

Is the repair for these Kia electrical recalls free?

Yes. Every recall listed here — whether it involves a wiring harness, alternator nut, battery, steering wiring, or a software update — is repaired by a Kia dealer free of charge to the owner.
