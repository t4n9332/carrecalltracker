Why do Kia electrical recalls involve fires, EV batteries and software?

19 NHTSA recalls · Kia · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 2, 2026

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Nineteen recalls sit under Kia's electrical heading, and the listed ones fall into three currents. Fire risk from wiring and connections runs oldest to newest: a front wiring harness on 2018 Stinger, alternator terminal nuts on 2020 Sedona and 2023 Sportage, and an HVAC blower motor wiring harness on 2021-2023 Sorento under SC351. The EV side is its own story — high voltage battery cells on 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9, an Integrated Charging Control Unit on 2022-2024 EV6 that is the subject of two of these recalls, a rear gear drive unit on 2025 EV9 under SC337, and an E400 battery pack on 2015-2019 Soul EV. Software errors make up the rest, including SC331 headlights and SC361 instrument panel displays.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2015-2019 Soul EV E400 battery recall: the notice says the new software will illuminate the EV warning light, limit charging capacity and reduce vehicle power if abnormal voltage is detected.
  • Recall SC331: the notice says a Body Domain Control Unit software error may cause a loss of low beam headlights and taillights on 2024-2025 Sorento, 2025 Sorento Hybrid and Plug-In Hybrid.
  • Recall SC361: the notice says the instrument panel display may fail to show information such as the speedometer, tire pressure or warning lights.
  • Recall SC248: the notice says a loose alternator battery positive terminal nut on 2023 Sportage can result in an engine stall or an electrical surge.
  • The two 2022-2024 EV6 ICCU recalls: the notices say a damaged Integrated Charging Control Unit can stop charging the 12-volt battery, which can result in a loss of drive power.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall in this group carries a parking instruction. For the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 high voltage battery recall, owners are advised to park outside and away from structures and to limit charging to a maximum of 80% until the recall repair is complete. That instruction belongs to that recall only. For every other recall listed here — the Stinger, Sedona, Sportage, Soul EV, Sorento, EV6 ICCU, EV9 gear drive unit, headlight and instrument panel recalls — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Work varies by recall. Dealers replace the high voltage battery system assembly on the EV6 and EV9 battery recall, and replace the wiring harness and blower motor resistor under SC351. The EV6 ICCU recalls inspect and replace the ICCU and its fuse and update its software. SC248 and the Sedona recall tighten the alternator terminal nut. SC331 updates BDC software; SC361 goes over-the-air or through a dealer. Free of charge.

Common questions

Should I still charge my EV6 to 100%?

For the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 high voltage battery recall, owners are advised to limit their charge to a maximum of 80% and park outside away from structures until the recall repair is complete.

My EV6 already had an ICCU recall done. Why is there another?

Two recalls in this group cover the Integrated Charging Control Unit on certain 2022-2024 EV6 vehicles, one with owner letters expected April 2024 and one with letters mailed December 2024. The notices do not explain how the two relate, so check your VIN against both.

Which Kia electrical recalls involve a fire risk?

Six listed here name fire risk: the 2018 Stinger front wiring harness, the 2020 Sedona alternator terminal nut, SC248 on 2023 Sportage, SC351 on 2021-2023 Sorento, the 2015-2019 Soul EV E400 battery short-circuit, and the 2022-2024 EV6 and 2024 EV9 battery recall.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
