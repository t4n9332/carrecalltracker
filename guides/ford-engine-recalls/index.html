Why do Ford engine recalls warn about fire and stalling?

13 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Engine · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-20

⚠ 2 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Two 2018-2019 recalls address engine block heater cables on F-150 and Super Duty trucks that corrode and risk overheated or melted wiring. A separate, repeated pattern covers 2.5L hybrid and plug-in hybrid engines in the Escape, Maverick and Corsair: after an internal engine failure, oil and fuel vapor can reach hot components and start an engine compartment fire — this appears in three recalls from 2023 to 2025, plus a related 2024 recall for Police Interceptor Explorers. Two more recalls, both for the Expedition and Lincoln Navigator, describe battery junction box short circuits that can start an engine fire while parked or driving. Other recalls cover stalling (poor cylinder lubrication, loose motor mounts), oil leaks, wiring, and an overheating engine cover.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Unexpected engine noise, a sudden drop in power, or smoke from the engine compartment (Escape, Maverick, Corsair HEV/PHEV and Police Interceptor Explorer recalls)
  • Engine cover surface in the cabin feels excessively hot to the touch (E-350/E-450)
  • Sudden loss of drive power or an engine stall (F-150 lubrication and Explorer/Aviator motor mount recalls)
  • Most other recalls in this group — including the block heater, battery junction box and wiring harness recalls — describe no driver-noticeable warning sign before the risk occurs

Is it safe to keep driving?

Two recalls in this group — the 2022 and 2025 Ford Expedition/Lincoln Navigator battery junction box recalls — instruct owners to park outside and away from structures until repaired. Three other recalls (the 2023, 2024 and 2025 Escape/Maverick/Corsair and Police Interceptor Explorer engine-fire recalls) tell owners to park and shut off the engine as soon as possible if they notice unexpected noise, reduced power or smoke. No recall in this group carries a stop-driving instruction. For every other recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers disable or replace block heater cables, replace battery junction boxes and remove a ground wire, update powertrain control module software, replace engine long-blocks or cylinder heads, retighten motor mount fasteners, and add wiring sheathing or cover insulation, depending on the recall. All of this work, including parts and labor, is performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why does Ford tell owners to park Expedition and Navigator SUVs outside?

The 2022 and 2025 Expedition/Navigator recalls describe a short circuit in the battery junction box that can start an engine fire while parked or driving. Because the risk exists even with the vehicle off, Ford tells owners to park outside and away from structures until the junction box is replaced.

What should I do if I hear strange noises or smell smoke from my Escape or Maverick engine?

The Escape, Maverick and Corsair HEV/PHEV recalls tell owners to park and shut off the engine as quickly as possible if they hear unexpected engine noise, notice reduced power, or see smoke, since these can signal the engine failure that leads to a compartment fire.

Is the repair free for these Ford engine recalls?

Yes. Every recall in this group is repaired at no cost — dealers replace or repair the affected parts, from block heater cables to engine long-blocks and battery junction boxes, and update software where needed, all free of charge to the owner.
