Why are Nissan hoods and doors under latch recalls?

3 NHTSA recalls · Nissan · Latches/locks/linkages · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-01-21

Nissan's latch recalls split between hoods and doors. Two are hood recalls, and both involve the secondary latch, the backup that holds the hood if the primary latch lets go. On certain 2013-2018 Altima vehicles, the notice says that if the primary hood latch is inadvertently released, the secondary latch may corrode over time, bind and remain unlatched when the hood is closed. On certain 2022 Pathfinder vehicles, insufficient welds may allow the secondary hood latch retainer bracket to detach from the front bumper assembly. The third recall is newer and covers doors on certain 2025 Altima and Sentra, 2025-2026 Frontier and 2026 Kicks vehicles, where door strikers may have been improperly welded and break.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Hood opening while driving: the 2013-2018 Altima recall says the hood could unexpectedly open if the primary latch is inadvertently released again and the secondary latch is not engaged.
  • Hood opening and a blocked view: the 2022 Pathfinder recall says a detached secondary hood latch may allow the hood to open if the primary latch is not engaged, obstructing the driver's view.
  • A door opening while moving: the door striker recall says a door may open unexpectedly while the vehicle is in motion.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these three notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. The 2013-2018 Altima notice says the hood could unexpectedly open while driving if the primary latch is inadvertently released again and the secondary latch is not engaged. The 2022 Pathfinder notice says a detached secondary latch may allow the hood to open if the primary latch is not engaged, obstructing the driver's view. The door striker notice says a door that opens while the vehicle is in motion increases the risk of injury or a crash. None gives an interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

All three notices say the work is free of charge. For the 2013-2018 Altima, dealers install a stronger release spring in the hood release lever, a warning label near the secondary hood latch, and an Owner's Manual addendum card with instructions on lubricating and maintaining the secondary hood latch mechanism. For the 2022 Pathfinder, dealers inspect and replace the secondary hood latch bracket as necessary. For the door recall, dealers replace the door strikers.

Common questions

Why doesn't the Altima hood recall replace the latch itself?

The notice describes the remedy as a stronger release spring in the hood release lever, a warning label near the secondary hood latch, and an Owner's Manual addendum card with instructions on how to lubricate and maintain the secondary hood latch mechanism, free of charge.

Which Nissan models does the door striker recall cover?

The notice covers certain 2025 Altima and Sentra, 2025-2026 Frontier and 2026 Kicks vehicles whose door strikers may have been improperly welded and break. Nissan's numbers for the recall are PD185 and PMA61, and owner notification letters were mailed March 17, 2026.

What should I do while I wait for the latch repair?

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving, and none gives an interim instruction. They describe the hood or door opening when the latch or striker fails. Dealers perform the listed repairs free of charge, so contact a dealer with your VIN.
