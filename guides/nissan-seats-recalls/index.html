Why Do Nissan Pathfinder and Rogue Seats Keep Getting Recalled?

6 NHTSA recalls · Nissan · Seats · model years 2021–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-03-11

The 2022 Pathfinder is the most recalled model in this group, appearing in three separate seat recalls: detaching third-row headrests, a missing second-row seat track bolt (shared with the 2022 Infiniti QX60), and second-row seat back frames with insufficient welds. The 2023 Rogue, Pathfinder, and Infiniti QX60 share a driver's seat weld failure recall, while the 2021 Rogue has a separate recall for child seat tether wires that may be improperly welded. The 2025 Sentra's driver's seat frame may not be fully secured to the seat track. Across the group, the recurring pattern is welding and fastening defects - missing bolts, insufficient welds, and detached brackets - in seat frames, tracks, and headrests.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Third-row headrest that moves, wobbles, or detaches (2022 Pathfinder)
  • Driver's or second-row seat that feels loose or shifts (2022-2023 Pathfinder, QX60, Rogue, 2025 Sentra)
  • Child seat that doesn't anchor securely to the rear tether (2021 Rogue)
  • Most of these recalls describe no specific warning sign beyond the seat, track, or headrest not being fully secured

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six seat recall notices tell owners to stop driving or park outside. Each says only that a headrest, seat track, seat frame weld, or child seat tether that isn't properly secured may fail to protect an occupant in a crash, increasing the risk of injury. That covers the 2022 Pathfinder headrest and seat back recalls, the 2022 Pathfinder/QX60 seat track bolt recall, the 2023 Rogue/Pathfinder/QX60 seat weld recall, the 2025 Sentra seat frame recall, and the 2021 Rogue child seat anchor recall. No notice directs owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Repairs across this group are all inspection-and-replacement jobs: dealers replace third-row headrests, install a missing seat track bolt, replace a driver's seat cushion frame or lower seat frame assembly, or replace rear seatbacks as needed. All of these repairs are performed free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Why does the Nissan Pathfinder have so many seat-related recalls?

The 2022 Pathfinder has three separate seat recalls: detaching third-row headrests, a missing second-row seat track bolt (shared with the Infiniti QX60), and insufficiently welded second-row seat backs. Each involves a different seat component.

What's the seat weld recall on the 2023 Rogue, Pathfinder, and QX60 about?

Nissan says the driver's powered seat may not be fully secured to the seat frame, which can cause the seat frame welds to detach from the rear inboard seat bracket.

Is the 2021 Nissan Rogue recall about the seat belt or the seat itself?

It's about the seat, not the seat belt. Nissan says the second-row upper tether wires may be improperly welded to the seatback frame, preventing a child seat from being securely anchored to the vehicle.
