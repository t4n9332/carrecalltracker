Why has Audi recalled so many models for brake defects?

8 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-04-15

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

Eight Audi brake recalls span very different root causes rather than one shared defect. The Q5 and SQ5 were recalled twice: in 2018 for brake caliper pistons with insufficient coating (shared with several 2018 Volkswagen models), then in 2019 for a brake master cylinder casting that could be too short on 2018-2019 models. Audi's E-Tron electric SUV shows up four times for unrelated brake booster and pedal problems: a 2019-2024 pedal-to-booster fastener issue, a 2020-2021 welded rotor and shaft that can separate, 2021 brake booster corrosion, and a 2026 recall covering additional 2020-2021 E-Tron Sportback and related models for the same pedal fastener defect. The 2020 Q3 and 2019-2021 A6/A7/S7 recalls each cover a separate, unrelated brake defect.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Loss of ABS, stability control, brake assist, and the speedometer display, described only in the 2021 E-Tron brake booster corrosion recall
  • A brake pedal plate that is visibly bent or has separated from the pedal, described in the 2020 Q3 recall
  • Reduced braking performance from an under-coated brake caliper piston, described in the 2018 recall
  • Most of these recalls describe no warning sign before a dealer inspection finds the defect

Is it safe to keep driving?

One of these eight recalls — the June 2018 VW and Audi brake caliper recall covering the 2018 Q5 and SQ5 — told owners not to drive their vehicle until the brake system had been checked. The other seven recalls in this group, covering the Q5/SQ5 master cylinder, E-Tron brake pedal and booster issues, the Q3 pedal plate, and the A6/A7/S7 brake fluid cap, do not tell owners to stop driving, according to the notices.

What the free repair involves

Dealers address each defect on its own terms: bleeding brake calipers, inspecting and replacing brake master cylinders, brake boosters, or brake pedal plates, replacing rear axle-related brake components, or swapping an incorrectly labeled brake fluid reservoir cap, depending on the recall. All of these repairs are performed free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Why has Audi recalled the Q5 and SQ5 for brakes twice?

The two recalls have separate causes: a 2018 recall addressed brake caliper pistons with insufficient coating on 2018 Q5/SQ5 models, while a 2019 recall addressed a brake master cylinder casting that could be too short on 2018-2019 Q5/SQ5 models.

Does the E-Tron have a brake pedal recall?

Yes. Audi recalled certain E-Tron models because a fastener connecting the brake pedal to the brake booster may have been improperly assembled, letting the pedal detach; a 2026 recall covers additional E-Tron and E-Tron Sportback models for the same issue.

Do any of these Audi brake recalls tell owners to stop driving?

Only one does: the 2018 recall covering the 2018 Q5 and SQ5 (also covering several Volkswagen models) told owners not to drive until the brake system had been checked. The other seven recalls in this group do not include that instruction.
