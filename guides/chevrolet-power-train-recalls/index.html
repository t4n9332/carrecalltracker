Why do Chevrolet driveshafts and axle shafts keep separating while driving?

15 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Power Train · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-07

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

Across this group, the most common failure is a driveshaft, half-shaft, or axle-shaft that can separate from the vehicle because of a manufacturing defect - missing retaining rings on the Camaro and Blazer/Traverse, a bad weld on the Silverado 1500 and Sierra 1500, incomplete heat-treating on the Escalade, Suburban, Tahoe, and Yukon, an incorrect sun gear on the XT5, XT6, Acadia, Blazer, and Traverse, and missing ball bearings on the Corvette. Separately, missing accumulator bolts caused transmission fluid leaks on a wide range of 2018-2020 Buick, Cadillac, Chevrolet, and GMC crossovers and sedans, while diesel trucks and SUVs and one Tahoe/Yukon transfer-case recall involve wheels locking up unexpectedly.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A visible transmission fluid leak or fluid smell under the vehicle
  • A sudden loss of drive power while underway
  • The vehicle rolling away on its own after being shifted into park
  • A wheel locking up unexpectedly while driving (2019-2020 Tahoe, Yukon)
  • Harsh shifting, reduced power, or unintended deceleration (2022-2023 Express, Savana)

Is it safe to keep driving?

One recall carries a stop-driving instruction: the 2026 notice covering the 2019 Chevrolet Tahoe and 2019-2020 GMC Yukon tells owners not to drive the vehicle until the transfer case assembly is inspected and, if necessary, replaced, because a front or rear wheel can lock up without warning. For every other recall in this group - the driveshaft, half-shaft, axle-shaft, transmission-bolt, and diesel wheel-lockup recalls - the notices do not tell owners to stop driving, only to have the dealer make the repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect the affected part - transmission accumulator, driveshaft, half-shaft, axle shaft, sun gear, transfer case, or transmission control valve - and replace it if it's found to be defective. On the Express and Savana vans and the diesel trucks and SUVs, the fix is a transmission control module software update instead of a part swap. All of these repairs are free.

Common questions

Why do so many different Chevrolet models share driveshaft or half-shaft problems?

Each recall in this group has its own cause - missing retaining rings, bad welds, incomplete heat-treating, an incorrect sun gear, or missing ball bearings - found in a specific model and model year, not one shared design flaw across every Chevrolet.

Should I stop driving my 2019 Chevrolet Tahoe because of the wheel-lockup recall?

Yes for that specific recall - GM's May 2026 notice for the 2019 Tahoe and 2019-2020 Yukon tells owners not to drive until the transfer case is inspected and repaired, because a wheel can lock up without warning.

Is the repair for these transmission and driveshaft recalls free?

Yes. GM says dealers will inspect and, if needed, replace the affected part - or update transmission control module software on the Express, Savana, and diesel trucks and SUVs - at no cost to the owner.
