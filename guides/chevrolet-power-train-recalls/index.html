Why Chevrolet driveshafts, half-shafts and transmissions are being recalled

15 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Power Train · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest May 7, 2026

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory.

Shafts that carry power to the wheels are the common thread. Several recalls involve parts that can come apart: the right front intermediate driveshaft on the 2018 Equinox, an unwelded driveshaft joint on 2019-2020 Silverado 1500 and 2020 Silverado 2500, a missing axle shaft retaining ring on the 2021 Camaro, missing ball bearings in 2021-2022 Corvette rear half-shafts, and a missing retaining ring on 2023 Blazer and Traverse half-shafts. Transmissions make up much of the rest: missing start-stop accumulator endcap bolts across 2018-2020 Equinox and Traverse, a failing control valve on 2020-2022 diesel Silverados, and control module software on the 2022-2023 Express. A 2026 recall covers a missing transfer case component on 2026 Silverado 1500, Suburban and Tahoe and certain 2015-2020 Suburban and Tahoe.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2026 transfer case recall: the notice says the front and/or rear wheels may lock up without warning to the driver.
  • 2022-2023 Express transmission software recall: the notice names harsh shifting, reduced power, unintended deceleration, rear wheel lockup, and movement in an unintended direction.
  • Diesel transmission control valve recall (2020-2022 Silverado 1500, 2500 and 3500, 2021 Tahoe and Suburban): the notice says the rear wheels may lock up.
  • 2023 Blazer and Traverse half-shaft recall: the notice says the vehicle may roll away when it is placed in park.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall here carries a stop-driving instruction: the transfer case recall covering 2026 Silverado 1500, Suburban and Tahoe and certain 2015-2020 Suburban and Tahoe, where owners are advised not to drive their vehicle until the remedy is completed. That instruction applies only to that recall. None of these notices tells owners to park outside. For the other recalls listed here, including the driveshaft, half-shaft, axle shaft, start-stop accumulator and transmission software recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Most of these repairs are replacements: dealers replace driveshafts, half-shafts, axle shafts, sun gears, and the transfer case assembly if inspection finds it necessary, and replace the start-stop transmission accumulator if bolts are missing. Two are software: new transmission control module software on the diesel trucks and a control module calibration on the Express. Each notice says the repair is free of charge.

Common questions

My Tahoe is on the do-not-drive recall list. What does the notice say to do?

The transfer case notice says owners are advised not to drive their vehicle until the remedy is completed, and that dealers will inspect and, if necessary, replace the transfer case assembly free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 18, 2026.

Does the diesel transmission recall cover a transmission that has already failed?

The notice says dealers will install new transmission control module software free of charge, and that GM will provide a special coverage program to cover repair of transmissions the remedy software identifies as containing a defective control valve.

What do the notices say can happen if a half-shaft or driveshaft comes apart?

They describe a loss of drive power or propulsion, which increases the risk of a crash. The 2023 Blazer and Traverse notice and the sun gear notice also describe vehicle rollaway when the vehicle is placed in park.
