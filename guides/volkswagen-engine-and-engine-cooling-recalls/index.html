Volkswagen engine and cooling recalls: bearing failures, coolant leaks, stalling

5 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2022–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2023-08-23

Two patterns dominate this group. The first is connecting rod bearing damage that can destroy the engine: it covers 2022-2023 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport alongside 2021-2023 Audi Q5 and Q5 Sportback, then repeats a year later for 2024 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport. Both notices warn the engine can fail, with a fire risk on top of a stall. The second is engine control unit software on the 2022 Taos with all-wheel drive, which can stall the car as it comes to a stop — recalled once in 2021 and again in 2022. A separate 2022-2023 Golf R and GTI notice covers a radiator not attached correctly to the intercooler, which can damage a coolant hose.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The engine stalling as you slow to a stop, on all-wheel-drive 2022 Taos models
  • Temperature gauge climbing, overheating, or coolant pooling under a Golf R or GTI
  • Sudden loss of engine power or a stall in traffic on Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport or Q5
  • A burning smell from leaking oil reaching hot engine parts
  • The 2024 Atlas notice says the engine can quit without warning, so expect no advance dashboard alert

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the five notices orders owners to stop driving or park outside, so all of these vehicles may be driven until the free repair. Priorities differ, though. The two connecting rod bearing recalls on Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport and Audi Q5 combine a stall in traffic with a fire risk, so book the inspection now. On the 2022-2023 Golf R and GTI, watch the temperature gauge and pull over safely if the engine runs hot. The Taos stall recalls are software fixes; keep driving, but schedule promptly.

What the free repair involves

For the bearing recalls, dealers inspect the engine and replace it if necessary. On the Golf R and GTI, they inspect the radiator and replace any damaged parts. Both Taos recalls are an update of the engine control unit software, done at the dealer rather than over the air. Everything is free of charge.

Common questions

My Taos was already recalled in 2021 — why is there a second one?

Volkswagen recalled 2022 Taos all-wheel-drive models for an engine control unit stall in August 2021 and again in September 2022. The second notice covers the same stall-when-stopping behavior, so have the dealer confirm which ECU update your VIN still needs.

Will I get a whole new engine under the bearing recall?

Only if the inspection finds a problem. Both bearing notices say dealers inspect and, if necessary, replace the affected engine at no cost. The inspection itself is free whether or not your engine turns out to need replacing.

Why is an Audi Q5 listed in a Volkswagen recall?

Volkswagen Group of America files recalls for both brands, and the October 2022 notice lists 2021-2023 Audi Q5 and Q5 Sportback together with 2022-2023 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport. Owners of either brand get the same free inspection and repair.
