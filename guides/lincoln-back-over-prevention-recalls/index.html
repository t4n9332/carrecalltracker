Why does my Lincoln backup camera screen go blank?

19 NHTSA recalls · Lincoln · Back Over Prevention · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-03-19

Nearly every recall in this group ends the same way: the rear image goes blank, distorted or foggy when you shift into reverse. Aviator is the most repeatedly named nameplate, with 2020-2024 model years appearing across software, camera-replacement and cell-phone-interference campaigns, followed by Corsair (2020-2023) and Navigator (2018-2021). Two root causes dominate: image processing module software on vehicles with the 360-degree camera system, and hardware faults such as poor electrical connections, failed video output, or a degraded anti-reflective lens coating on Continental cameras. Ford has re-issued the problem more than once — a 2025 recall covers Aviator and Corsair vehicles repaired incorrectly under campaign 23V-342, and a separate 2025 Aviator recall involves the camera dropping out above 10 mph.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Blank, black or scrambled display where the rear view should appear when you select reverse
  • A foggy or cloudy-looking rearview image on Continental models with the 360-degree camera
  • Image flickers or becomes distorted when a cell phone is in the cabin (2020-2024 Aviator)
  • On the 2025 Aviator, the camera image fails once vehicle speed reaches 10 mph or more
  • No warning light or chime is described in these notices — you may only discover it when backing up

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these Lincoln camera recalls tells owners to stop driving or to park outside. Ford treats every one as safe to keep driving, provided you do not rely on the screen: back up slowly, use your mirrors, and turn and look over your shoulder each time you reverse. One extra instruction applies to the 2020-2024 Aviator interference recall, where Ford advises owners not to use a cell phone while reversing until the shielding is installed. Schedule the repair promptly anyway, since a blank camera raises the risk of backing into someone.

What the free repair involves

Repairs split between software and hardware. Dealers reflash the image processing module or rearview camera software on 360-degree systems, replace the camera itself where video output, wiring or the lens coating is at fault, and install shielding on the accessory protocol image module for the Aviator interference recall. The 2025 Aviator can be updated over the air. All work is free.

Common questions

My Aviator already had the camera recall done — why another letter?

Ford found that some 2020-2023 Aviator and 2020-2022 Corsair vehicles were repaired incorrectly under recall 23V-342, so a 2025 campaign reprograms the image processing module again. Earlier Aviator recalls also moved from software updates to full camera replacement.

Does a blank camera mean my Lincoln fails a federal safety standard?

Yes. Several of these notices state the vehicles fail Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111, "Rear Visibility," because the image does not display when you shift into reverse. That non-compliance is why the repair is mandatory and free.

Can I get the fix over the air instead of visiting a dealer?

Only the 2025 Aviator recall lists an over-the-air option for the accessory protocol interface module software, and a dealer can also install it. Every other Lincoln camera recall here requires a dealer visit for reprogramming or camera replacement.
