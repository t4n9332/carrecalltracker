Subaru recalls affecting windshields, rear cameras and defrosters explained

4 NHTSA recalls · Subaru · Visibility · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 4, 2025

Subaru's visibility recalls have no single mechanical cause; they group around what the driver can see. Two are display and defogging failures: a software issue that may leave the audio display uninitialized and the backup camera screen blank on the 2018 Legacy, Outback and BRZ, and an HVAC fault that may leave the defroster and defogger inoperative on the 2023-2025 Solterra, part of a Toyota recall that also covers the bZ4X and Lexus RZ. The other two are glass: incorrect paint clearcoat that may leave the windshield insufficiently bonded on the 2022 Impreza and Outback, and improperly tempered driver's side rear-door glass on the 2019 Crosstrek. Outback and Impreza appear more than once.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2018 Legacy, Outback and BRZ recall says the camera display may remain blank and the rearview image will not display while backing up.
  • The 2023-2025 Solterra recall says the defroster and defogger may become inoperative during certain HVAC system faults, such as electrical compressor failure.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tells owners to stop driving, and none carries a park-outside instruction. They state the risks instead: the 2018 Legacy, Outback and BRZ notice says failing to display the rearview image while backing up increases the risk of a crash; the Solterra defroster notice says decreased visibility under certain driving conditions increases that risk; the 2022 Impreza and Outback windshield notice says an insufficiently bonded windshield can detach during a crash; and the 2019 Crosstrek notice says glass shattering into large pieces increases the risk of injury.

What the free repair involves

The remedies vary. For the 2018 Legacy, Outback and BRZ, dealers reprogram the audio system display software. For the 2023-2025 Solterra, dealers update the HVAC control ECU software and inspect the electrical compressor, replacing it if necessary. For the 2019 Crosstrek, dealers replace the driver's side rear-door window glass. For the 2022 Impreza and Outback, Subaru offers a vehicle exchange or repurchase. Each notice says free of charge.

Common questions

Does the windshield recall mean Subaru will buy back my vehicle?

The notice for the 2022 Impreza and Outback says Subaru will offer owners a vehicle exchange or repurchase, free of charge, because the windshield may be insufficiently bonded. Subaru's customer service line handles questions about how that offer works.

Is the Solterra defroster recall just a software update?

Partly. The notice says dealers update the HVAC control ECU software, and also inspect the electrical compressor and replace it if necessary, free of charge. The same recall covers the 2023-2025 Toyota bZ4X and Lexus RZ.

My backup camera screen is blank — is that the 2018 recall?

The 2018 Legacy, Outback and BRZ notice describes a software issue in which the audio display may not initialize, leaving the camera display blank. Dealers reprogram the audio system display software free of charge; a VIN check confirms coverage.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
