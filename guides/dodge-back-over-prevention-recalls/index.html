Dodge Backup Camera and Pedestrian Warning Recalls Explained for Owners

9 NHTSA recalls · Dodge · Back Over Prevention · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Aug 13, 2026

Rearview images that fail to display dominate these nine Dodge back over prevention recalls, alongside an image that remains displayed and missing pedestrian sounds. Journey appears in the 2018 mirror remedy notice and the 2019-2020 camera microprocessor notice. Durango appears for a lingering image on certain 2019-2020 vehicles and radio software problems on certain 2021-2022 vehicles. Charger notices address stealth mode on certain 2018-2020 Pursuit vehicles, exterior warning sound on certain 2024-2025 EVs, and rearview display failure on certain 2026-2027 vehicles. Hornet recurs in the 2023-2025 display notice and the 2023-2024 plug-in hybrid pedestrian siren notice. These notices identify distinct software, display, camera, and sound-system problems.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2018 Journey backup camera recall, U56: The rearview image may disappear while backing up; the 2025 Journey camera recall describes an image that may not display properly.
  • 2020 Charger Pursuit stealth-mode recall: The backup camera image will not display in reverse when stealth mode is selected.
  • 2024 Durango radio software recall, 66B/79B; 2025 Hornet display recall, 13C/38C; and 2026 rearview camera recall, 78D: The rearview image may fail to display.
  • September 2020 lingering-image recall: The rearview camera image may remain displayed after shifting out of reverse.
  • 2023 Hornet pedestrian siren recall: The siren may fail to alert pedestrians in reverse; 2025 Charger recall, 44C: Exterior sound, including pedestrian warnings, may be absent.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the nine listed notices tells owners to stop driving or park outside. This applies to the Journey image-loss and camera microprocessor notices, Charger Pursuit stealth-mode notice, Durango radio software notice, Hornet display notice, lingering-image notice, Hornet pedestrian siren notice, Charger amplifier notice, and 78D rearview camera notice. For each of these recalls, the supplied notice does not give a stop-driving instruction. The OTA options described in certain notices concern the repair method; they do not provide a separate driving instruction.

What the free repair involves

Specified remedies are free. U56 replaces the mirror; 66B/79B updates radio software through dealers or OTA; 13C/38C updates software or replaces the display as necessary. The Hornet siren recall inspects its connection and secures or replaces components if needed. 44C inspects and updates amplifier software as necessary. The 2025 Journey notice replaces the camera; 78D updates radio software through dealers or OTA.

Common questions

Which Dodge camera recalls offer an over-the-air repair?

The 66B/79B notice offers dealer or OTA radio software updates, as does 78D. The September 2020 lingering-image notice offers a free dealer radio display software update or an optional remote OTA update. These options belong to those specific notices.

Why does my Charger Pursuit lose its camera image in stealth mode?

The August 2020 notice covers certain 2018-2020 Charger Pursuit vehicles equipped with stealth mode. It says the rearview image will not display in reverse when that mode is selected. Dealers update body control module software to disable stealth mode in Reverse, free.

What pedestrian warning problems affect Hornet and Charger vehicles?

The 2023 Hornet notice concerns certain 2023-2024 plug-in hybrids with a missing or disconnected pedestrian siren. Recall 44C concerns certain 2024-2025 Charger EVs whose amplifier may lack software, preventing exterior sound. Their remedies address the siren connection and amplifier software, respectively.
