Porsche seat belt recalls: loose rear anchors and child seat problems

6 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-02-20

Two patterns run through Porsche's six seat-belt recalls. The first is an anchor screw that was never tightened properly: it affects the 2022 911 GT3's lower belt anchor and the rear outboard belts on 2016-2020 Macan and Macan S, 2017-2020 Macan Turbo and 2017-2020 Panamera models — the broadest recall in the group. The second is child-seat security: 2023 911 Carrera variants may have rear three-point belts that will not properly secure a child seat, 2021 911, Panamera and Taycan belt assemblies raise the same concern, and 2020-2021 Taycans with the optional rear center seat can have buckle wiring blocking the right rear lower child seat anchors. A 2019 Cayenne recall stands apart: the passenger side rear buckle may break under load.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A rear seat belt anchor that feels loose where it bolts to the seat frame
  • A child seat that will not pull tight or sit securely with the rear belt
  • Wiring from the center seat belt buckle blocking the right rear lower child seat anchors on a Taycan
  • A rear buckle that feels damaged or does not latch cleanly (2019 Cayenne, Taycan center seat)
  • In most of these recalls there is no warning at all — the defect only shows in a crash

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the six recalls restricts driving, and Porsche has issued no stop-driving or park-outside instruction. These are crash-protection defects: the car drives normally and the risk only appears in a collision. The 2019 Cayenne buckle and the untightened anchor screws leave rear-seat occupants at greater injury risk, and three notices involve belts that may not secure a child seat properly. Until the free repair is done, it is sensible to move a child seat, and where practical rear passengers, out of the affected seating position.

What the free repair involves

It varies by recall. Dealers replace the passenger rear buckle on the 2019 Cayenne; replace and tighten the lower belt anchor screws on the 911 GT3 and on Macan and Panamera rear outboard positions, replacing the seat frame if the threads are damaged; inspect and replace rear three-point belts or belt assemblies as necessary; and reroute the Taycan center buckle wiring harness. All free of charge.

Common questions

Can I use a child seat in the back before the recall is done?

Three of these recalls involve belts or anchors that may not secure a child seat properly — 2023 911 Carrera models, 2021 911, Panamera and Taycan belts, and Taycans with the rear center seat. Until the belts are inspected or replaced, use an unaffected seating position.

Can I just tighten the seat belt anchor screw myself?

No. Porsche's remedy is to replace and tighten the anchor screws, and to replace the seat frame if the threads are damaged. That inspection and torque work is done by a dealer free of charge, and a reused screw may not hold in a crash.

How do I check whether my Macan or Panamera is included?

The anchor screw recall spans 2016-2020 Macan and Macan S, 2017-2020 Macan Turbo and several 2017-2020 Panamera variants, so model year alone is not enough. Call Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243 with your VIN to confirm.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
