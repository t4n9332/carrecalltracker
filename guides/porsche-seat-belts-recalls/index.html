Why do so many Porsche seat belt recalls involve child seats?

6 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-02-20

Six Porsche seat belt recalls fall into two groups. Three involve loose hardware: a 2022 911 GT3 recall and a 2016-2020 Macan/2017-2020 Panamera recall both cover a lower seat belt anchor screw that wasn't tightened properly, and a 2019 Cayenne recall covers a rear seat belt buckle that may break under load. The other three involve child restraints: a 2021 Panamera and Taycan recall and a 2023 911 Carrera recall both cover a seat belt automatic locking retractor that deactivates too early to secure a child seat, while a 2020-2021 Taycan recall covers a wiring harness that blocks access to the rear child seat anchors.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A child seat that won't lock securely or feels loose when secured with the seat belt (2021 Panamera/Taycan and 2023 911 Carrera automatic locking retractor recalls)
  • Trouble reaching the rear child-seat anchors because the center seat belt buckle wiring is in the way (2020-2021 Taycan)
  • The notices do not describe a noticeable warning sign for the loose seat belt anchor screws (2022 911 GT3; 2016-2020 Macan/2017-2020 Panamera) or the rear seat belt buckle that may break under load (2019 Cayenne).

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six recall notices carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The three anchor and buckle recalls (911 GT3, Macan/Panamera, and Cayenne) describe an increased risk of injury if the seat belt detaches or breaks during a crash, but do not tell owners to stop driving. The three child-restraint recalls (Panamera/Taycan, 911 Carrera, and the second Taycan recall) describe an increased risk of injury if a child seat isn't secured properly, and likewise do not instruct owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace and tighten the loose seat belt anchor screws (replacing the seat frame if the threads are damaged), replace the broken rear seat belt buckle, and replace the seat belt assemblies affected by the early-deactivating retractors. For the Taycan wiring issue, dealers reroute and secure the wiring harness and replace the buckle if damaged. All repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

Do I need to stop driving my Porsche because of a seat belt recall?

No. None of the six recall notices in this group tell owners to stop driving or park outside. Each describes an increased risk of injury during a crash and directs owners to a free dealer repair.

Why do several of these recalls specifically affect child seats?

Three recalls — the 2021 Panamera/Taycan, 2023 911 Carrera, and 2020-2021 Taycan — involve the automatic locking retractor deactivating too early or a wiring harness blocking anchor access, both of which can prevent a child restraint from being secured properly.

Which Porsche models have a loose seat belt anchor recall?

The 2022 911 GT3 has a loose lower seat belt anchor screw recall, and a separate recall covers 2016-2020 Macan and 2017-2020 Panamera models with the same issue on the outboard rear seats.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
