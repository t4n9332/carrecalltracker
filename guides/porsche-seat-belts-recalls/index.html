Porsche seat belt and child seat anchor recalls: what owners should know

6 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Feb 20, 2024

Six Porsche seat belt recalls fall into two clusters. The largest involves child restraints: the automatic locking retractor may deactivate early on 2021 911 Carrera, Panamera and Taycan models in one recall and on 2023 911 models in another, while 2020-2021 Taycans with the optional rear center seat have a buckle wiring harness that blocks the right rear lower child seat anchors. The second cluster is hardware: the screw holding the lower seat belt anchor to the seat frame may not have been tightened properly on the 2022 911 GT3, and on rear outboard seats in Macan and Panamera models covered by the 2023 recall. Separately, a 2019 Cayenne passenger rear buckle may break under load.

Warning signs to watch for

  • In the 2020-2021 Taycan recall (ANA), the center rear seat belt buckle wiring harness obstructs access to the right rear lower child seat anchors, preventing a child seat from being properly secured.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these six notices tells owners to stop driving or to park outside. Each describes what could happen in a crash: an unsecured child restraint, an anchor that detaches, or a buckle that breaks. The notices for the 2021 and 2023 automatic locking retractor recalls, the 2022 911 GT3 and 2023 Macan and Panamera anchor screw recalls, the 2019 Cayenne buckle recall and the 2020-2021 Taycan harness recall all state only the crash risk and the free repair; none gives any interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

Work varies by recall and is free. For the retractor recalls dealers inspect and replace seat belt assemblies or the rear three-point belts as necessary. For the anchor screw recalls they replace and tighten the screws, replacing the seat frame on the GT3 if the threads are damaged. The Cayenne gets a new rear buckle; the Taycan harness is rerouted and secured, with a damaged buckle replaced.

Common questions

My child seat will not sit tight in my Porsche — is that covered?

Two recalls describe child restraint problems: the automatic locking retractor deactivating early on 2021 911, Panamera and Taycan models and on 2023 911s, and a wiring harness blocking the right rear anchors on 2020-2021 Taycans. Check your VIN with Porsche.

Do I have to pay for the seat belt work?

These notices all state the repair is performed free of charge — inspecting and replacing seat belt assemblies, replacing and tightening anchor screws, replacing a seat frame with damaged threads on the 911 GT3, and rerouting the Taycan buckle wiring harness.

Can I tell by looking whether my belt anchor screw is loose?

The notices do not describe anything an owner would see or feel; they state only that the screw attaching the lower anchor to the seat frame may not have been tightened properly. The remedy in those recalls is dealer work on the screws themselves.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
