Why Do BMW X5 And X7 Seat Recalls Keep Repeating?

9 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Seats · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2023-04-06

Nine seat recalls in this group split into three recurring failure types. Welding problems account for three recalls: seatback frames on the 2018-2019 X3/X4 and 2019-2021 seat frames on the M340i, X3, and X4, plus one rear headrest weld on a single 2020 M340i. Loose or missing attachment bolts account for another three: 2019 X5 driver seat and seat belt bolts, 2019 X7 lounge seat adapter bolts (caught before any were sold), and a 2023 X5 driver seat backrest bolt. Head restraints make up the rest: incorrect second-row restraints on the 2021 X5/X7 and missing locking tabs on 2023 X5 restraints, plus 2022 X7 third-row backrest springs. The X5 recurs across four separate years.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A front seatback that could suddenly fold forward or backward on its own (2019 X3/X4 recall)
  • The other eight recalls in this group describe no symptom an owner could notice before a dealer inspection — the bolt, weld, spring, and head restraint defects aren't something you'd feel while driving

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these nine recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices do not tell owners to stop driving, including the ones describing loose seat or seat belt bolts, improperly welded seat frames or headrests, incorrect or detachable head restraints, and loose backrest springs. Most of these issues are described as increasing the risk of injury during a crash rather than as a hazard during normal driving. Owners are directed to have the dealer complete the free inspection or repair described in the applicable notice.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect the affected seat, bolt, weld, or head restraint and repair or replace it as needed: tightening or replacing bolts, replacing seat frames or backrests, reinstalling or replacing springs, or replacing head restraints and guide sleeves. In one case the vehicles were still on dealer lots and were repaired before sale. All of this work is free of charge.

Common questions

Which BMW model has had the most seat-related recalls?

The X5 appears in seat or head restraint recalls in 2019, 2021, 2022, and 2023, more than any other model in this group, though each recall involves a different bolt, weld, or restraint defect.

Do these seat recalls affect the airbags?

No. This group covers seat frames, mounting bolts, welds, and head restraints. Seat belt sensor and buckle issues are covered under BMW's separate seat belt recalls, not these seat structure recalls.

Can I tell if my BMW's seat has one of these defects?

Mostly no. Except for a 2019 X3/X4 recall describing a seatback that could unexpectedly fold, these defects — loose bolts, improper welds, incorrect head restraints — are not described as noticeable before a dealer inspects the vehicle.
