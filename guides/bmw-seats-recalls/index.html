Why BMW seats get recalled: weak welds, loose bolts, head restraints

9 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Seats · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2023-04-06

Nine recalls, and the X3, X5 and X7 carry most of them. Bad welds come up repeatedly: front seatback frames on 2018-2019 X3 and X4 that could fold forward or rearward, seat frames not welded properly to the rails on 2019-2021 330i, M340i, X3 and X4, and a rear center headrest on a 2020 M340i. Loose or missing fasteners are next — driver seat and belt bolts on the 2019 X5, business lounge seat adapter bolts on the 2019 X7, and a backrest bolt on the 2023 X5. Head restraints fill out the group: wrong type on 2021 X5 and X7 second rows, missing locking tabs on the 2023 X5, plus third-row backrest springs on 2019-2022 X7s.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A front seatback that rocks, shifts or folds forward or rearward unexpectedly
  • Noticeable play in the driver's seat or where the backrest meets the seat frame
  • A second-row head restraint that lifts out or will not lock at a set height
  • An X7 third-row backrest that does not stay locked upright
  • Most of these faults show nothing during normal driving — they only matter in a crash

Is it safe to keep driving?

No seat recall in this group restricts driving, and none asks owners to park outside. Every one is a crash-protection problem: the seat behaves normally day to day, but a folding seatback, a loose bolt or a head restraint that can detach leaves an occupant less protected in an impact. The 2018-2019 X3 and X4 seatbacks that can fold forward or rearward are the ones not to postpone. The 2019 X7 business lounge seats were never sold — they were repaired while quarantined on dealer lots.

What the free repair involves

All repairs are free. Depending on the recall, dealers replace the front seats outright, or the seat frame and back rest; inspect and torque the driver's seat and seat belt attachment bolts, or install a missing backrest bolt; replace seat adapter bolts; replace the rear center headrest; inspect and replace second-row head restraints or their guide sleeves; and refit or replace X7 third-row compression springs.

Common questions

Nothing feels loose in my seat. Is the recall still worth doing?

Yes. Weld and bolt defects in this group are internal to the seat frame and usually give no day-to-day sign. The dealer inspects and only replaces or tightens what is actually wrong, at no cost, and that closes the recall on your VIN.

Is it safe to carry passengers in the back seats meanwhile?

The notices place no restriction on using the vehicle. The head restraint and third-row recalls mean those specific occupants have less crash protection until inspected, so if you can seat people elsewhere while you wait for the appointment, that is the cautious choice.

How do I know if my X5 has the wrong head restraints?

You should not judge this yourself. The 2021 X5 and X7 recall involves incorrect second-row restraints, and the 2023 X5 recall involves guide sleeves missing locking tabs. Dealers inspect and replace the parts as necessary, free of charge.
