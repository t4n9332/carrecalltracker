Why do BMW seat recalls keep involving loose bolts and welds?

9 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Seats · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 6, 2023

BMW's seat recalls repeat two faults. Bolts that were not correctly tightened or installed show up on the 2019 X5, on 2019 X7 models with the business lounge seat option, and on the 2023 X5, where one of four bolts holding the driver's backrest to the seat frame may not have been correctly installed. Welding is the other pattern: front seatback frames on 2018-2019 X3 and 2019 X4 models, seat frames not welded to the rails on 2019-2021 330i, M340i and 2020-2021 X3 and X4 models, and a rear center headrest on two 2020 M340i cars. Head restraints add a third strand, with second-row parts recalled on the 2021 X5 and X7 and the 2023 X5.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The seatback folding forward or rearward, named in the front seatback frame weld recall covering 2018-2019 X3 and 2019 X4 models.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these nine seat notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. Each describes a risk that applies in a crash, a seat, backrest, headrest or head restraint that may not restrain an occupant, and directs owners to a dealer for the remedy. One notice adds that the vehicles were not with owners: the 2019 X7 business lounge seat recall says they were quarantined on dealer lots and had not been sold.

What the free repair involves

Dealers torque or install seat attachment bolts on the 2019 and 2023 X5, replace the seat adapter bolts on the X7 business lounge seat, replace whole front seats on the 2018-2019 X3 and X4, and replace the seat frame and back rest on the 330i, M340i, X3 and X4 weld recall. Head restraints and third-row springs are inspected and replaced or reinstalled. All free of charge.

Common questions

Which BMW models appear most often in these seat recalls?

The X5 appears in four notices: 2019 driver's seat bolts, 2021 second-row head restraints, a 2023 backrest bolt and 2023 head restraint guide sleeves. The X7 appears in three: seat adapter bolts, second-row head restraints and third-row backrest springs. The X3 appears in two weld recalls.

Are the two head restraint recalls the same problem?

They are separate recalls. The 2021 X5 and X7 notice says the second-row outer head restraints may be the incorrect type for the vehicle and could detach. The 2023 X5 notice says the second-row outboard head restraints were made without locking tabs in the guide sleeves.

What do these notices say the actual risk is?

Every seat notice in this group frames the risk as injury in a crash, from a seat, backrest, headrest or head restraint that may not restrain the occupant as intended. Only the 2018-2019 X3 and 2019 X4 notice describes the seatback folding forward or rearward.
