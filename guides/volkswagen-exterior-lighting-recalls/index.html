Volkswagen headlight aim recalls: are my lights pointed wrong?

5 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-06-02

Four of these five recalls are about headlight aim, and all five are Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard compliance issues rather than parts that break. The 2019 Jetta with LED headlights has a passenger-side unit positioned incorrectly; 2020 Atlas Cross Sport and 2021 Atlas have a driver's-side headlight aimed too low during production; 2018-2019 Atlas and Passat models — that notice's text reaches back to 2012 Passats — are missing the blocking cap that locks horizontal aim; and the 2021 ID.4 had those caps fitted to the vertical adjuster instead of the horizontal one. The odd one out is the 2018 Tiguan long wheelbase, whose back-up lights may not be bright enough.

Warning signs to watch for

  • One headlight clearly aimed lower, higher or differently than the other
  • Reduced visibility on the passenger side after dark (2019 Jetta with LED headlights)
  • Oncoming drivers flashing you because of headlight glare (2021 ID.4)
  • Back-up lights that look faint when reversing in the dark (2018 Tiguan LWB)
  • No dashboard warning accompanies any of these — you have to look for yourself

Is it safe to keep driving?

No notice in this group tells owners to stop driving or park outside, and every one of these vehicles can be driven. The concern is night driving: on the 2019 Jetta the notice describes reduced visibility on the passenger side, so limiting night trips until the headlight is replaced is sensible. Misaimed or unlocked aim on Atlas, Passat and ID.4 can dim your view or throw glare at oncoming drivers. The 2018 Tiguan's dim back-up lights mainly raise the risk of a low-speed backing crash — take extra care reversing.

What the free repair involves

The repairs vary by recall: dealers replace the passenger-side headlight on the 2019 Jetta, adjust the driver's-side headlight on Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport, inspect and block the horizontal adjustment on Passat and Atlas, move the caps from the vertical to the horizontal aim on the ID.4, and replace the inner taillight assemblies on the 2018 Tiguan. All of it is free.

Common questions

Can't I just re-aim the headlights myself?

Several of these recalls exist because the aim adjusters were left unlocked or capped on the wrong axis, which is why the vehicles failed to comply with the federal lighting standard. Let the dealer set and block the aim properly — the work is free.

My Atlas shows up in two lighting recalls. Is that right?

It can be. 2018-2019 Atlas models appear in the 2019 missing blocking cap recall, while the 2021 Atlas and 2020 Atlas Cross Sport appear in the 2020 driver's-side aim recall. Check your VIN; each open recall gets its own free repair.

Would I even notice the dim back-up lights on my 2018 Tiguan?

Maybe not. The notice says the back-up lights in the taillight assemblies may not be bright enough to meet the federal standard, not that they stop working. Dealers replace the inner taillight assemblies free; that recall began December 15, 2017.
