Why do so many Audi models have passenger air bag recalls?

14 NHTSA recalls · Audi · AIR Bags · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-04-03

Audi's air bag recalls fall into two recurring patterns. Four separate recalls (2018-2021) involve the Passenger Occupant Detection System (PODS) falsely detecting a fault and switching off the front passenger air bag, hitting the A3, S3 and RS3 family repeatedly as well as A4, A5, S4, S5, A6, A7, S6 and S7 models from roughly 2012-2020. A second pattern is manufacturing and assembly defects - a loose mounting screw, a cracked weld, an incorrectly molded tear seam or score line, or mistimed second-stage deployment - that can keep the passenger air bag or side curtain air bag from working properly in a crash, spread across A4 Allroad, A5, Q3, Q5, SQ5, Q7 and A6/A7 models from 2018-2021.

Warning signs to watch for

  • None of these notices describe a warning sign an owner could notice before a crash - the air bag and PODS defects are hidden until deployment
  • In a few recalls (2019 Q3, 2020 A6/A7), the notices say a defective air bag may also throw small plastic pieces into the cabin during deployment
  • The notices don't mention any dashboard light or alert for these air bag issues

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these Audi air bag recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. Each notice instead describes a risk that shows up only if the vehicle is in a crash - a side curtain air bag that doesn't inflate, a passenger air bag disabled by a false PODS fault, or an air bag that deploys improperly due to a manufacturing defect. For every recall in this group, covering models from the A3 and A4/A5 lines to the Q3, Q5, Q7 and A6/A7/S6/S7 lines, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Repairs vary by defect but are always free. Dealers replace or modify air bag assemblies (head curtain air bags, instrument panels, air bag score lines), install PODS repair kits or new sensor mats and connectors, tighten or replace mounting screws, or update the air bag control module software, depending on which recall applies to the vehicle.

Common questions

What is PODS and why does it keep showing up in Audi air bag recalls?

PODS is the Passenger Occupant Detection System that senses whether the front passenger seat is occupied. Several recalls describe PODS falsely detecting a malfunction and switching off the passenger air bag even when someone is sitting there.

Do any of these Audi air bag recalls affect the driver's air bag?

No. Every recall in this group involves either a side curtain air bag or the front passenger air bag - the notices describe disabling, improper deployment, or damage to those systems, not the driver's air bag.

Is the fix for a PODS-related recall the same as for a manufacturing-defect recall?

No. PODS recalls are fixed with new sensor mats, connectors, or wiring, while manufacturing-defect recalls involve replacing or repairing physical parts like mounting screws, instrument panels, or air bag score lines.
