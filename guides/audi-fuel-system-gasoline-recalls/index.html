Why do so many Audi models have fuel leak recalls?

5 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Fuel System, Gasoline · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-09-24

Five Audi fuel-system recalls span different causes and vehicles. Three of them describe a fuel leak that can catch fire near an ignition source: 2018 A6, A7, and Q7 models with leaking fuel rails; 2018-2020 A3 (and shared Volkswagen Golf, GTI, and Jetta models) with a suction jet pump seal that lets fuel leak into the evaporative emissions system and out of the charcoal canister; and the 2025 SQ7, where fuel pump fasteners may not be tightened. Separately, 2019-2021 Q7 and Q8 vehicles have a fuel pump component that can break and stall the engine, and 2021-2022 Q3 vehicles have a damaged vent pipe that can spill fuel from the filler neck when the cap is opened.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Fuel spilling out when the fuel filler cap is opened (2021-2022 Q3)
  • Engine stalling caused by fuel pump failure (2019-2021 Q7/Q8)
  • No specific warning sign is described for the fuel rail, tank seal, or SQ7 fuel pump fastener leaks — these are found through inspection, not something a driver would notice beforehand

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five fuel-system recalls include a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the A6/A7/Q7 fuel rail recall, the A3/Golf/GTI/Jetta fuel tank seal recall, the SQ7 fuel pump fastener recall, the Q7/Q8 fuel pump recall, and the Q3 filler neck recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. The notices describe risks including fire near an ignition source, engine stall, and injury from spilled fuel, but none of the five direct owners to stop driving or park outside while awaiting repair.

What the free repair involves

Repairs vary by defect: dealers replace the fuel rails (A6/A7/Q7), replace the suction pump inside the fuel tank (A3/Golf/GTI/Jetta), tighten or replace the fuel pump and lines (SQ7), replace the fuel pump (Q7/Q8), and replace the vent pipe, charcoal canister, or fuel tank as needed (Q3). All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Do any of these Audi fuel-system recalls tell owners to stop driving?

No. None of the five recalls in this group — covering the A6/A7/Q7 fuel rails, the A3/Golf/GTI/Jetta tank seal, the SQ7 fuel pump fasteners, the Q7/Q8 fuel pump, and the Q3 filler neck — instruct owners to stop driving or park outside.

What causes the Audi fuel leak recalls?

The causes differ: leaking fuel rails on 2018 A6/A7/Q7, a damaged suction jet pump seal on 2018-2020 A3 and related Volkswagen models, and loose fuel pump fasteners on the 2025 SQ7. Each can let fuel leak near a potential ignition source.

Is the Audi Q7/Q8 fuel pump recall the same defect as the fuel leak recalls?

No. The 2019-2021 Q7/Q8 fuel pump recall involves an internal component that can break and stall the engine, a different defect from the fuel-leak recalls affecting other Audi and Volkswagen models in this group.
