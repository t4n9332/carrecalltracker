Why do BMW batteries and starters keep catching fire?

28 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-23

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory. 2 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Three failure families dominate BMW's electrical recalls. First, high-voltage batteries in the i4, iX and i7 from 2022-2025: internal damage, insufficient weld seams, improperly assembled cell modules and faulty battery control software, causing fire or sudden loss of drive power. Second, starters and starter-generators — 2019-2020 X5, X6, X7 and 3, 7 and 8 Series starter motors that overheat after repeated cranking, corroded starter relays on 2019-2022 X3, X4, Z4, 330i and the Toyota Supra, and loose 48-volt or positive battery cable connections on 2021 and 2025 models. Third, plug-in hybrid battery cells on 2020-2021 530e, 330e, X3 xDrive30e, i8 and the MINI Countryman. Fire is the recurring theme.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Engine cranks repeatedly but will not start, then heat or a burning smell from the starter
  • Sudden loss of drive power while moving in an i4, iX or i7
  • Engine stalls while driving, reported on the 2018 M5 fuel pump software fault
  • Smoke or hot smell near the battery cable or 48-volt starter-generator connection
  • Many of these short circuits and battery faults give no warning before a fire

Is it safe to keep driving?

One recall is a stop-driving order: the 2022-2023 i4 and iX high-voltage battery campaign tells owners not to drive or charge, and to park outside away from structures until the battery is replaced. The 2019-2022 starter relay recall covering X3, X4, Z4, 330i, 530i and the Supra says park outside and away from buildings. The 2020-2021 plug-in hybrids may be driven but must not be charged, and sport mode, manual mode and the shift paddles are off limits. Remaining notices allow normal driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies split by cause. Fire-risk battery recalls bring module or full high-voltage battery replacement; the i4, iX and i7 power-loss campaign is a battery ECU software update. Starter recalls replace the starter motor, starter relay, or starter-generator and positive battery cable, or update vehicle software; some cars repaired under recall 24V576 need the starter replaced again. Others tighten the 48-volt connection or replace a control unit. Every repair is free.

Common questions

Which BMW electrical recall means I should stop driving?

Only the 2022-2023 i4 and iX high-voltage battery recall. BMW told those owners not to drive or charge the vehicle and to park outside away from structures until the battery was replaced. The other notices permit continued driving.

My starter was already replaced — am I finished?

Not necessarily. BMW's September 2025 starter recall explicitly includes vehicles already repaired under recall 24V576, and those owners must have the vehicle repaired again. Check your VIN rather than assuming the earlier dealer visit closed it out.

Can I still charge my 530e or X3 plug-in hybrid?

No. Until the battery module is inspected and replaced if necessary, BMW instructs owners of these plug-in hybrids not to charge the vehicle, and to avoid sport mode, manual mode and the shift paddles while driving.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
