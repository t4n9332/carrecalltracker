Why do BMW electrical recalls keep pointing to fire risk?

28 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 23, 2026

⚠ 1 of these recalls carries a do-not-drive advisory. 2 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Fire is the recurring word in these BMW electrical notices. One thread runs through the batteries: welding beads between hybrid cells on 2021 330e and X5 xDrive45e, 2020-2021 X3 xDrive30e and 2020 MINI Countryman SE ALL4; debris in cells on 2020-2021 530e, X3 xDrive30e, i8 and Countryman All4 SE; internal damage on 2022-2023 iX and 2022 i4; weld seams on 2024 i4 and i5; and module assembly on 2022-2025 iX, 2023-2024 i7 and 2022-2023 i4. A second thread is the battery-to-starter-generator connection: the 48-volt assembly on 2021 models, and loose or overheating positive cable connections on 2025 X3, 3, 4 and 5 Series cars. The i4, iX, X5 and X3 recur most often.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2025 X3 30 xDrive battery cable recall: an overheated connection can cause an engine stall.
  • 2025 330i, 4 Series and X3 starter-generator recall: a loose connection can result in an engine stall.
  • 2022-2025 iX, i7 and i4 battery module recall: module failure may shut down the high-voltage system and cause a loss of drive power.
  • 2023 iX M60 recall: a broken gear weld seam could result in a vehicle rollaway if the parking brake is not applied.
  • 2019-2020 X5 and X7 starter motor recall: the notice describes a starter motor failure and repeated attempts to start the vehicle.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall here carries a stop-driving instruction: for 2022-2023 iX and 2022 i4 vehicles, owners are advised not to drive or charge them, and to park outside and away from structures until the remedy is complete. The hybrid battery-debris recall covering 2020-2021 530e, X3 xDrive30e, i8, 330e, 745Le xDrive, X5 xDrive45e and MINI Countryman instructs drivers not to charge, not to drive in manual or sport mode, and not to use the shift paddles until the remedy is completed. The other notices listed do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies vary by cause. Dealers replace high-voltage battery modules or complete batteries on the i4, iX, i5 and i7 recalls, and inspect and if necessary replace hybrid battery modules on the plug-in recalls. On the starter-generator and battery-cable recalls they inspect, tighten or replace the connection or cable. The 2019-2020 starter motor recall is a software update. The notices listed state the work is free of charge.

Common questions

My i4 or iX is under a battery recall — what do the notices say?

For 2022-2023 iX and 2022 i4 vehicles with possible internal high-voltage battery damage, owners are advised not to drive or charge them and to park outside, away from structures, until the remedy is complete. Dealers replace the high-voltage battery free of charge.

Why do several BMW recalls mention the starter-generator?

Three notices describe the electrical connection between the battery and the starter-generator: the 48-volt assembly on 2021 models, an improperly tightened positive cable on 2025 X3 30 xDrive, and a connection that may come loose on 2025 330i, 4 Series and X3 vehicles.

Will my repair be new parts or a software update?

It depends on the recall. Battery recalls call for module or battery replacement, the connection recalls for inspection, tightening or replacement, and the 2019-2020 X5 and X7 starter motor recall for a vehicle software update. These notices state the work is free.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
