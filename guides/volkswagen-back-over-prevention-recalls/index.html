Why does the Volkswagen rearview camera keep getting recalled?

12 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Back Over Prevention · model years 2021–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 22, 2026

Every recall in this group is a rear visibility problem, and most are software. The ID.4 appears repeatedly: 2022 models for a delayed or deactivated image, 2022 models given a replacement infotainment control unit with incorrect software, 2023 models for an image that may not display, and 2021-2023 models for displays that do not boot or sporadically reset. The Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport appear for a delayed image, a distorted image on 2024-2025 models, and a low-voltage electrical event on 2024-2026 models. Tiguan, Jetta, Taos and Golf R models are covered by infotainment memory, camera control unit and camera hardware notices. Several cite Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A blank or unresponsive rearview camera image when the vehicle is placed in reverse (2022 Golf R and 2022-2023 ID.4 rearview camera recall).
  • A black screen or infotainment system freeze (2021 Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport, Golf, Jetta and Tiguan rearview camera recall).
  • A delayed or deactivated rearview image after shifting into reverse (2022-2024 Golf R and 2024 Atlas rearview camera recall).
  • A distorted rearview camera image when the vehicle is in reverse (2024-2025 Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport recall).
  • Center and instrument panel displays that do not boot or sporadically reset, including loss of speedometer information (2021-2023 ID.4 software recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these twelve notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells them to park the vehicle outside. That covers the ID.4, Atlas, Atlas Cross Sport, Golf R, Golf GTI, Jetta, Taos, Tiguan and Arteon notices listed here. What each one says is that a rearview camera image that is blank, delayed, distorted or deactivated reduces the driver's view behind the vehicle and increases the risk of a crash, and that the 2021-2023 ID.4 display fault can also mean loss of speedometer information.

What the free repair involves

Most are software: dealers update the infotainment software, the camera control unit software or the vehicle software, depending on the recall. Two are hardware: the rearview camera itself is replaced on the 2022 Golf R and 2022-2023 ID.4 recall, and the peripheral camera control module is replaced on the 2023 ID.4 recall. All of this work is free of charge.

Common questions

My Volkswagen already had this fixed — why another recall?

Two notices describe vehicles previously repaired: the 2026 Tiguan recall covers 2024 Tiguans previously repaired under recall 25V082, and a 2023 notice covers 2022 ID.4s whose replacement infotainment central control unit contained incorrect software. Both remedies are a free software update.

Is a Volkswagen rearview camera recall repair free?

Yes. Every notice in this group says the dealer work — an infotainment or camera control unit software update, a rearview camera replacement, or a peripheral camera control module replacement — is free of charge, and Volkswagen mails owner notification letters.

Which Volkswagen models appear most often in these camera recalls?

The ID.4 and the Atlas family appear most. ID.4 model years 2021 through 2023 are named across several notices, and the Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport appear in notices covering 2021 and 2024-2026 model years. Tiguan, Jetta, Golf R, Golf GTI and Taos also appear.
