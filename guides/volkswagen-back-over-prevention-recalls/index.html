Why do so many Volkswagens have rearview camera recalls?

12 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Back Over Prevention · model years 2021–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-22

These twelve Volkswagen recalls all involve the rearview camera failing to work as required when the vehicle is in reverse, across nearly the entire lineup — ID.4, Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport, Golf R and Golf GTI, Jetta and Jetta GLI, Tiguan, and Taos, plus a shared Audi Q3 campaign. The causes differ: a defective eMMC memory module, software that delays or blanks the image after an update or repair, a camera control unit error at startup, a low-voltage electrical event, and hardware camera faults. Several models were recalled more than once for new instances of the same underlying symptom, including a 2026 Tiguan recall for vehicles previously repaired under an earlier 2025 recall.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rearview camera screen staying black or blank when shifting into reverse
  • Camera image freezing, or the infotainment system freezing, during an ignition cycle
  • Rearview camera image appearing delayed by a few seconds after shifting into reverse
  • Distorted rearview camera image while backing up

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these twelve recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Even for the recalls where the rearview camera goes completely blank, freezes, or is delayed after shifting into reverse, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. That holds across every model in this group — ID.4, Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport, Golf R and GTI, Jetta, Tiguan, Taos, and the shared Audi Q3 campaign — regardless of whether the fix is a software update or a hardware replacement.

What the free repair involves

Most of these recalls are fixed with a software or camera control unit update through the dealer. Two recalls require a hardware fix instead: dealers replace the rearview camera itself on affected 2022 Golf R and ID.4 vehicles, and replace the peripheral camera control module on affected 2023 ID.4 vehicles. All of this work is free of charge.

Common questions

Is this the same rearview camera defect every time?

No. Different recalls trace to different causes — a memory module defect, various software bugs, a camera control unit startup error, or a low-voltage electrical fault — even though the visible symptom, a missing or delayed camera image, often looks the same.

Does VW replace the whole camera or just update software?

Most of these recalls are fixed with a software or camera control unit update. Two of them — covering 2022 Golf R and ID.4, and 2023 ID.4 — require dealers to replace camera hardware instead, all free of charge.

Why did my Tiguan or Atlas get this recall again after an earlier fix?

Volkswagen issued new 2025 and 2026 recalls for Tiguan and Atlas rearview camera startup errors, including a 2026 Tiguan recall specifically for vehicles already repaired under an earlier 2025 recall, meaning the first fix did not fully resolve the issue.
