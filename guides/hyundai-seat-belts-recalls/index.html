Why does Hyundai keep recalling seat belts across so many models?

12 NHTSA recalls · Hyundai · Seat Belts · model years 2020–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-07-14

Across these twelve Hyundai and Genesis recalls, the most common problem is a front seat belt pretensioner that can explode when it deploys in a crash, sending metal fragments into the cabin. That defect repeats across the Elantra, Accent, Venue, Santa Fe Hybrid, Genesis GV80 and GV70 through several expanding recalls between 2021 and 2022, with the fix changing from replacing the pretensioner to capping it. Separately, newer models face different seat belt hardware problems: the Palisade's buckles may fail to latch, the Ioniq 5's retractors may not be secured, the Genesis G90, Ioniq 6 and Santa Fe's anchors may detach, and the Kona's rear center buckle may not restrain an occupant properly.

Warning signs to watch for

  • No warning before a crash — the pretensioner recalls describe no symptom you'd notice while driving
  • Seat belt buckle that won't latch or click when you try to buckle in (Palisade)
  • Seat belt retractor that feels loose or not fully secured (Ioniq 5)
  • Rear center seat belt buckle that doesn't properly hold you in place (Kona)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these 12 recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the Palisade buckle recall, owners are told to insert the belt firmly with a quick, direct motion and pull to confirm it's secured until the buckle is replaced. For the Kona rear center seat belt recall, owners are advised not to use the rear center seat until the buckle assembly is replaced. For the remaining recalls in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Depending on the recall, dealers replace the seat belt pretensioner or secure it with a cap, replace the buckle assembly, inspect and secure loose retractors, or inspect and reinforce or replace detached anchors. All of this work is performed free of charge under the recall.

Common questions

Which Hyundai models have had seat belt pretensioner recalls?

The pretensioner explosion recalls cover the Elantra, Accent, Venue and Santa Fe Hybrid, plus the Genesis GV80 and GV70, in a series of recalls issued between 2021 and 2022 that expanded the affected vehicles over time.

Why did Hyundai switch from replacing the pretensioner to capping it?

The notices don't explain the reason for the change in remedy. Later recalls in this group note that dealers will secure the seat belt pretensioner with a cap instead of replacing it, free of charge.

Do any of these Hyundai seat belt recalls tell owners to stop driving?

No. None of the 12 recalls in this group carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Two recalls give interim steps — firmly inserting the Palisade buckle, and not using the Kona's rear center seat — until parts are replaced.
