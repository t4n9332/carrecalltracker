Why do Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, Metris and GLB tires need recalls?

3 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Tires · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-10-18

These three tire recalls have separate causes across separate models. The 2019 Sprinter recall covers winter tires that may not have been installed in the correct rolling direction, reducing traction in wet or snowy conditions. The 2018 Metris recall covers an incorrect label listing the wrong maximum combined tire load capacity, failing federal labeling standards. The 2025 GLB 250 recall covers right-side front and rear tires with sidewall damage that can cause a sudden loss of tire pressure. No model or cause repeats between these three recalls.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Reduced traction in wet or snowy conditions (Sprinter winter tire installation)
  • No warning sign described for the Metris incorrect tire load label — it is a labeling defect, not something a driver would feel
  • Sudden loss of tire pressure on the right-side tires (GLB sidewall damage)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The 2019 Sprinter winter tire installation recall, the 2018 Metris tire load label recall, and the 2025 GLB sidewall damage recall each describe an increased risk of a crash — from reduced traction, an overloaded vehicle, or a sudden loss of tire pressure — but none of the notices instructs owners to stop driving or to park the vehicle outside while waiting for repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealers check the winter tires' rolling direction on Sprinter vans, correcting it or replacing tires with uneven wear; install corrected load-capacity labels on Metris vans and inspect and replace incorrect tires as needed; and inspect and replace the sidewall-damaged tires on affected GLB vehicles. All of these repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

What's wrong with the winter tires on recalled Sprinter vans?

They may not have been installed in the correct rolling direction, which can reduce traction in wet or snowy conditions; dealers check and correct the direction or replace tires that show uneven wear.

Why were Metris vans recalled over tire labels, not the tires themselves?

The label listing the maximum combined tire load capacity was incorrect, which could let an owner overload the van without realizing it; dealers install corrected labels and inspect and replace tires as necessary, free of charge.

What's the risk with the 2025 GLB tire recall?

The right-side front and rear tires on some 2025 GLB vehicles may have sidewall damage that can cause a sudden loss of tire pressure, which increases the risk of losing control of the vehicle.
