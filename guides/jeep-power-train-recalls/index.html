Jeep power train recalls: clutch, transmission and driveline problems

17 NHTSA recalls · Jeep · Power Train · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 30, 2026

The recalls listed here cluster around a few repeating failures. The most persistent is the clutch pressure plate overheating and fracturing on manual-transmission Wranglers and Gladiators, which appears in three separate recalls covering 2018-2020, 2018-2021 and 2018-2023 Wranglers alongside 2020-2023 Gladiators. A second cluster is losing the PARK function: an incorrect park lock rod across 2018 Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep and Ram models, a parking wheel that may not engage on the 2022 Gladiator, worn front differential pinion gears on the 2019 Grand Cherokee and Durango, and a 2018 Cherokee halfshaft bearing cage. A third is loss of drive or stalling: a 2019 Cherokee transmission calibration, a 2019 Cherokee and 2018 Pacifica transmission weld, a 2020 Gladiator driveshaft assembled without grease, and 2021 Wrangler and Gladiator control module software.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2018 park lock rod recall says the transmission may not shift into 'PARK.'
  • The 2022 Gladiator recall says the vehicle may lose the 'park' position, including vehicle rollaway.
  • The 2019 Cherokee and 2018 Pacifica transmission weld recall says the vehicle will stop moving.
  • The 2019 Cherokee transmission calibration recall and the 2021 Wrangler and Gladiator software recall each say the engine may stall.
  • The clutch recalls say damage to nearby components can result in debris falling onto the road or a loss of drive.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the notices listed here tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. Two say something about sales rather than driving: the 2018-2021 Wrangler and Gladiator clutch notice says FCA ordered a stop sale as of February 25, 2021, and the 2022 Gladiator transmission notice says the entire US population is unsold units that will be repaired before they can be sold. The remaining notices describe the crash or fire risk and direct owners to a dealer.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall: dealers install the correct park lock rod, replace the transmission, the front halfshaft, the front differential, the driveshaft or the transmission oil cooler hose, re-flash the transmission controller, or update powertrain control module software. On the clutch recalls they reroute a wire harness, add torque-limiting software, or replace the clutch assembly with a software update. Where the notices state it, the work is free of charge.

Common questions

Which Wranglers and Gladiators are covered by the clutch recalls?

The clutch recalls cover vehicles with manual transmissions only. The three notices name 2018-2020, 2018-2021 and 2018-2023 Wranglers, and 2020, 2020-2021 and 2020-2023 Gladiators. A dealer or a VIN lookup can tell you which of the three, if any, lists your vehicle.

What do the notices say about a Jeep that will not hold park?

Several describe losing park: the incorrect park lock rod on 2018 models, the 2022 Gladiator parking wheel, worn front differential pinion gears on the 2019 Grand Cherokee and Durango, and the 2018 Cherokee halfshaft, which the notice says can allow movement while in Park.

Is a full transmission replacement really part of these recalls?

Two notices say dealers will replace the transmission: the 2019 Cherokee and 2018 Pacifica welding recall, and the 2022 Gladiator parking mechanism recall. Both state the work is free of charge, and the Gladiator notice says the affected US vehicles were unsold.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
