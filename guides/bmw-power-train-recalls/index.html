Why Do BMW's Transmission And Driveshaft Recalls Keep Happening?

12 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Power Train · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-08-12

BMW's power train recalls in this group split fairly evenly between motorcycles and cars. On two wheels, reverse gear control units on the R18 and K1600 lines each got separate recalls for the same underlying problem: a seal that lets moisture in and overheats the unit. Other motorcycle campaigns cover gearbox and driveshaft hardware — a transmission gear hardness defect on the K1600 that could lock the rear wheel, a gearbox input shaft that can break on the R1250GS/RT, a slipping cardan shaft on the new R1300RT, and unlubricated clutch cover bolts on the S1000 series. On four wheels, the X3, X4, X5 and X7 SUVs recur across separate transmission mechatronics and driveshaft recalls, alongside one-off issues on the M5/M8 wiring harness and X5/X7 starter bolt.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Oil leaking from around a motorcycle's clutch cover
  • An engine that stalls unexpectedly (X5, X7 starter bolt recall)
  • A vehicle rolling away on its own, even when parked (X3, X4, and M550i-related recalls)
  • A sudden loss of drive power while riding or driving (M5, M8, K1600, R1300 RT, and mechatronics-related recalls)
  • A rear wheel that locks up while riding (K1600 transmission gear recall)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these 12 recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For every one of them, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving, whether the notice describes an oil leak at a clutch cover, an overheating reverse gear control unit, an engine stall, a rear wheel lockup, a loss of drive power, or a vehicle rollaway risk. Owners are simply directed to schedule the free dealer repair described in the relevant notice.

What the free repair involves

Across these recalls, dealers inspect and replace hardware specific to each defect: clutch cover bolts, reverse gear control units, shift forks and gears, transmission wiring harnesses, final drive pivot pins, mechatronics units or modules, cardan shafts, and driveshaft-to-differential connections. One recall updates engine control unit software instead of replacing parts. Every repair listed in these notices is performed by a dealer free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Are BMW motorcycles and BMW cars affected by the same power train recall?

No. The R18 and K1600 reverse gear overheating recalls are separate campaigns for different motorcycle lines, and no car recall in this group shares a cause with a motorcycle recall. Check your specific VIN on NHTSA.gov to see which recall applies to your vehicle.

Does the X3/X4 transmission recall affect the Toyota Supra?

Yes. A 2023 recall for a leaking transmission mechatronics module covers the BMW X3, X4, X5, and X6 together with the Toyota GR Supra, since BMW issued the recall for all of them in a single notice.

What should I do if my BMW motorcycle recall involves reverse gear overheating?

Contact BMW customer service or your dealer for the free repair described in your notice. The R18 recall used a temporary fix that disconnects the control unit, while the K1600 version goes straight to a control unit replacement.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
