Why are GMC and Chevrolet seat bolts and welds being recalled?

4 NHTSA recalls · GMC · Seats · model years 2019–2022 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2022-09-29

These four recalls involve seat frames that were not properly bolted or welded during assembly, not a design defect in the seats themselves. On 2021 Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, and Yukon XL, front seat attachment bolts were left loose or missing after a seat was removed for an earlier repair. On 2022 Colorado and Canyon, a front passenger seat hook was not properly welded to the seat track. On 2022-2023 Equinox and 2022 Terrain, the driver's seat height-adjust pivot bolt was stripped or loose. On 2019 Equinox and Terrain, the rear-right seat's head-restraint bracket had insufficient welds. Each defect could let the seat move or fail to hold its occupant in a crash.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A front or driver's seat that feels loose, wobbly, or shifts at its base or height-adjust lever
  • A vehicle that had a front seat removed for a prior repair (2021 Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, Yukon XL) is the type affected by the loose-bolt recall
  • The notices describe no warning sign for the weld defects on the Colorado/Canyon and Equinox/Terrain recalls; owners cannot detect these by inspection

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four seat recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the loose front-seat bolts on 2021 Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, and Yukon XL, the unwelded seat hook on 2022 Colorado and Canyon, the stripped driver's seat bolt on 2022-2023 Equinox and 2022 Terrain, and the insufficient rear-seat welds on 2019 Equinox and Terrain, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. Each notice instead warns that the affected seat may not properly restrain its occupant if a crash occurs before the repair is made.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect the affected seat and, depending on the recall, install missing attachment bolts, replace the seat cushion frame when a weld or hook has failed, or replace the seatback frame on the rear-right seat. All repairs are performed free of charge, with owners notified by mail once parts are available.

Common questions

Why did GM recall seats that were just reinstalled after a repair?

On 2021 Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon, and Yukon XL, GM says a repair that required removing one or both front seats wasn't always followed by properly reinstalling the attachment bolts, leaving the seat loose.

Are these seat recalls related to seat belt recalls?

No. These four recalls involve the seat frame, bolts, or welds that anchor the seat itself, not the seat belt webbing or buckle, which GM has recalled separately on other GMC models.

How would I know if my seat has a bad weld or bolt?

The notices don't describe a way to tell by looking or feeling; GM identifies affected vehicles by production date and build records, so owners should check for a recall notice rather than inspect the seat themselves.
