Why do Porsche hazard lights and headlights keep triggering recalls?

6 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 5, 2024

Software is the common thread. Five of these six recalls are Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 108 noncompliances tied to control unit programming: 2024 Cayenne, Cayenne S, Cayenne Coupe and Turbo GT models whose rear hazard and parking lights may not illuminate after shutdown; 2020 911 Carrera coupes and cabriolets whose hazard warning lights may not function when activated; 2020-2021 Taycan models whose pre-collision automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations; 2022-2023 911 GT3 Touring models that can show two center high-mounted brake lights at once; and 2024 Taycan models with incorrect headlight control module software. The sixth is hardware: missing caps over the low-beam adjustment screws on a long list of older Macan, Cayenne, Panamera, Cayman and Boxster models.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2024 Cayenne recall states the rear hazard warning lights and parking lights may not illuminate if activated within 10 seconds after the gateway control unit shuts off.
  • The 2020 911 Carrera recall states the hazard warning lights may not function when activated by the operator.
  • The 2020-2021 Taycan recall states the pre-collision automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations.
  • The 2022-2023 911 GT3 Touring recall states both center high-mounted brake lights may activate simultaneously when the rear spoiler is manually deployed below 56 MPH.
  • The 2024 Taycan recall states the parking lights may not operate as intended when the turn signals are activated.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these six notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside or away from structures. That applies to the 2024 Cayenne rear hazard and parking light recall, the 2020 911 Carrera hazard light recall, the 2020-2021 Taycan automatic hazard light recall, the missing headlight adjustment screw cap recall, the 2022-2023 911 GT3 Touring brake light recall and the 2024 Taycan headlight control module recall. None of them gives owners an interim instruction to follow before the dealer visit.

What the free repair involves

Five recalls are handled with programming: updating the gateway control unit software on the 2024 Cayenne, reprogramming the control unit on the 2020 911 Carrera, reprogramming the hazard lights on the 2020-2021 Taycan, reprogramming the rear spoiler control unit on the 911 GT3 Touring, and updating the headlight control module software on the 2024 Taycan. The sixth: dealers inspect and install missing headlight sealing caps. All are free of charge.

Common questions

Do I need to do anything before the dealer fixes the lights?

The notices describe the defects and the dealer remedies, and they do not give owners any interim step to take. None of the six tells owners to stop driving. Owners are notified by mail and the work is done at a Porsche dealer.

Which Porsche models are affected by the hazard light recalls?

Three recalls involve hazard lights: 2024 Cayenne, Cayenne S, Cayenne Coupe, Turbo GT and S Coupe; 2020 911 Carrera coupes and cabriolets; and 2020-2021 Taycan models, where the pre-collision automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations.

Is the missing headlight cap recall really a safety issue?

The notice states the missing caps over the low-beam horizontal adjustment screws can allow the headlights to be improperly adjusted, which may fail to illuminate the road properly or glare oncoming drivers, and that the vehicles do not comply with FMVSS 108.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
