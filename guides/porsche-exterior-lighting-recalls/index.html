Why Are Porsche Hazard, Parking, and Brake Lights Recalled?

6 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Exterior Lighting · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Sep 5, 2024

Software-related lighting behavior repeats across Porsche's exterior-lighting notices, especially hazard-light operation. Certain 2020 911 Carrera variants may have hazard lights that fail to activate, while certain 2020-2021 Taycan vehicles may activate automatic hazard lights in non-emergency situations. Certain 2024 Cayenne variants have rear hazard and parking lights that may fail under a specific shutdown timing condition. Separate notices concern parking-light operation with turn signals in certain 2024 Taycan vehicles and simultaneous center brake-light activation in certain 2022-2023 911 GT3 Touring vehicles. The hardware-focused exception concerns missing headlight adjustment-screw caps, with listed examples including the 2018 Macan S and 2020 Macan.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2024 Cayenne rear-light recall (APB1): rear hazard and parking lights may not illuminate when activated within 10 seconds after the gateway control unit shuts off, after vehicle shutdown.
  • 2020 911 Carrera hazard-light recall (AKC1): hazard warning lights may not function when activated.
  • 2020-2021 Taycan hazard-light recall (AMC1): automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations.
  • 2022-2023 911 GT3 Touring brake-light recall (APA8): both center brake lights may activate when the rear spoiler is manually deployed below 56 MPH.
  • 2024 Taycan headlight-software recall (ARB4): parking lights may not operate as intended when turn signals are activated.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the six supplied exterior-lighting notices tells owners to stop driving or park outside. This applies to APB1 for Cayenne rear hazard and parking lights, AKC1 for 911 Carrera hazard lights, and AMC1 for Taycan automatic hazard lights. The ANB4 headlight-cap notice, APA8 911 GT3 Touring center brake-light notice, and ARB4 Taycan headlight-software notice likewise do not tell owners to stop driving. These notices provide no stop-driving or park-outside instruction for the lighting defects they describe.

What the free repair involves

Free software remedies update the Cayenne gateway control unit for APB1, reprogram the 911 control unit for AKC1, reprogram Taycan hazard lights for AMC1, reprogram the rear spoiler control unit for APA8, and update the headlight control module for ARB4. ANB4 provides free headlight-cap inspection and installation of missing caps as necessary.

Common questions

Why would my Taycan hazard lights activate without an emergency?

AMC1 covers certain 2020-2021 Taycan vehicles whose pre-collision automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations. The notice says this could confuse other drivers. Dealers reprogram the hazard lights free of charge under that recall.

What triggers the recalled Cayenne rear-light problem?

APB1 describes certain 2024 Cayenne variants after vehicle shutdown: rear hazard and parking lights may not illuminate if activated within 10 seconds after the gateway control unit shuts off. Dealers update that control unit's software free of charge.

Does the headlight-cap recall require new headlights?

ANB4 specifies inspecting the headlights for sealing caps and installing missing caps as necessary, free of charge. The notice explains that missing caps over the low-beam horizontal adjustment screws can allow improper adjustment; it does not specify headlight replacement.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
