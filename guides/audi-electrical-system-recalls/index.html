Audi electrical recalls: high-voltage battery fire risk and power loss

15 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Mar 31, 2025

High-voltage batteries dominate this group. Five recalls describe batteries that may overheat or short circuit: 2019-2022 e-tron Quattro and 2020-2022 e-tron Sportback, the 2022-2024 e-tron GT and RS e-tron GT in two separate recalls, and Q5 plug-in hybrids covering 2022-2023 and 2021-2024 model years, each listed with the 2022 A7 PHEV. Moisture is a second thread: a charging socket seal on the 2019 e-tron and a starter generator on 2019-2020 A6 mild hybrids with 2.0TFSI engines. Liquid reaching the gateway control module appears twice, on 2018-2022 Q5 and SQ5 and on 2019-2022 A6 and A7. Portable charging cables, a fuel level sender and a front door crash sensor connector complete the list.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Warning lights together with the message "Electrical system: fault" (2019 e-tron charging socket seal recall).
  • An inaccurate fuel level reading displayed on the instrument cluster (March 2022 A6 and A7 fuel level sender recall).
  • A sudden reduction in engine power (the Q5 and SQ5 gateway control module recall and the A6 and A7 gateway control module recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

The 2019 e-tron charging socket seal recall is the one carrying a driving instruction: its notice says that if warning lights appear along with the message "Electrical system: fault," owners should immediately stop driving the vehicle and park it outdoors away from structures. The other notices in this group do not tell owners to stop driving. Some give charging instructions instead: the March 2024 e-tron GT recall says to charge only to a maximum of 80 percent until the repair is complete, and the charging cable recall says not to use the 220V/240V portable cable.

What the free repair involves

Remedies split by cause, and each notice says the work is free of charge. For the battery recalls dealers install onboard diagnostic or advanced diagnostic software and replace high-voltage battery modules where necessary. Elsewhere dealers install a new charging socket seal, replace the starter generator, supply a 220V/240V charging cable with a temperature sensor, fit a protective cover on the gateway control module, install a new sensor in the fuel tank, or inspect and replace front door crash sensors.

Common questions

Can I keep charging my Audi while I wait for a battery recall repair?

It depends on the recall. The March 2024 e-tron GT notice says to charge only to a maximum of 80 percent until the repair is done. The charging cable notice says to use the 110V home cable or public charging stations instead of the 220V/240V cable.

What does the notice tell 2019 e-tron owners to do if a fault message appears?

That notice says if warning lights appear with the message "Electrical system: fault," owners should immediately stop driving and park outdoors away from structures. Dealers install a new seal and, as necessary, replace affected system components, free of charge.

Why does a spilled drink turn up in an Audi recall?

Two notices say liquid spilled in the rear seat, and on Q5 and SQ5 models water entering through an insufficient body seam, may penetrate the gateway control module and cause it to shut down, which will suddenly reduce engine power. Dealers install a protective cover.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
