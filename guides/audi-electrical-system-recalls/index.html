Why are Audi's high-voltage batteries recalled for fire risk?

15 NHTSA recalls · Audi · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-03-31

Most of these Audi electrical recalls involve high-voltage battery or charging fire risk in e-tron, e-tron GT, and Q5 plug-in hybrid models from 2019-2024: moisture entering the battery pack or starter generator, a short circuit inside a battery module, or an overheating portable charging cable. Separately, four recalls describe non-fire electrical faults - a door crash-sensor connector that can detach on the 2021 A6 and Q7, liquid that can penetrate the gateway control module and cut engine power on 2018-2021 Q5/SQ5 and 2019-2022 A6/A7 models, and a sticking fuel level sensor giving inaccurate readings on 2019-2021 A6 and A7 models.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2019 e-tron battery recall says warning lights and an "Electrical system: fault" message can appear on the dash
  • The portable charging cable recall describes an outlet or charging cable that can overheat during use
  • A gateway control module shutdown causes a sudden, noticeable drop in engine power (Q5/SQ5 and A6/A7 liquid-intrusion recalls)
  • An inaccurate fuel level reading on the instrument cluster is the warning sign in the A6/A7 fuel sensor recall
  • The battery-overheating and starter-generator recalls describe no warning sign an owner would notice before a short circuit occurs

Is it safe to keep driving?

Only the 2019 Audi e-tron high-voltage battery recall carries a driving instruction: if warning lights appear along with an "Electrical system: fault" message, the notice says to immediately stop driving and park outside, away from structures. None of the other Audi electrical system recalls in this group - covering the A6 hybrid starter generator, e-tron GT and Q5 battery overheating, portable charging cables, the door sensor connector, gateway control module water intrusion, and the fuel level sensor - tell owners to stop driving or park outside.

What the free repair involves

Fixes are free and vary by defect: dealers replace a faulty charging-socket seal or starter generator, install diagnostic software to monitor battery modules and replace them if needed, supply a new charging cable with a temperature sensor, replace door crash sensors or fuel level senders, or add a protective cover and seal to keep liquid out of the gateway control module.

Common questions

Which Audi models are affected by the high-voltage battery fire recalls?

The e-tron, e-tron GT and RS e-tron GT, and the Q5 plug-in hybrid (2019-2024 model years, depending on the recall) are each covered by separate battery overheating or short-circuit recalls in this group.

Should I stop using my Audi's portable charging cable?

The 2023 recall notice advises owners not to use the 220V/240V portable charging cable and to use only the 110V home cable or public charging stations until dealers supply a replacement cable with a temperature sensor.

Are the door sensor and fuel sensor recalls related to the battery fire recalls?

No. The door crash-sensor connector and fuel level sensor recalls involve separate electrical faults - a detaching connector and a sticking sensor - unrelated to the high-voltage battery fire recalls in this group.
