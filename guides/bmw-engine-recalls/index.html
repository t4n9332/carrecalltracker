What's behind BMW's engine recalls for leaks, shafts and brake assist?

3 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Engine · model years 2019–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-08-02

These three BMW engine recalls involve three unrelated problems. A 2019 recall covers S 1000 RR motorcycles whose oil cooler hoses may not be properly attached to the oil pipes, risking an oil leak. A separate 2019 recall covers 2019-2020 330i, 330i xDrive, and 2019 Z4 models, where the engine's counterbalance shaft needle roller bearings may have been installed improperly, letting the shaft loosen and potentially damage the engine severely. A 2021 recall covers 2019-2021 M340i, X3, X4, Z4 and Toyota Supra models, where engine management software during startup can cause a loss of brake assist, though full mechanical braking stays available.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Visible oil leaking or dripping near the rear tire on S 1000 RR motorcycles
  • Severe engine damage if the counterbalance shaft loosens in affected 330i and Z4 models
  • Reduced brake assist during engine start in affected M340i, X3, X4, Z4 and Supra models, though mechanical braking still works

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three engine recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The oil cooler hose recall and the counterbalance shaft recall describe increased crash risk from an oil leak or potential engine damage, and the brake assist software recall notes that loss of power assist increases crash risk, but full mechanical braking remains available. In each case, the notices describe the risk without instructing owners to stop driving while awaiting repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the oil cooler assembly and hoses on affected S 1000 RR motorcycles, replace the entire engine on affected 330i and Z4 vehicles with the counterbalance shaft defect, and update the engine management software on the M340i, X3, X4, Z4 and Supra brake-assist recall — all free of charge.

Common questions

Are these three BMW engine recalls related?

No. They involve three separate defects: a motorcycle oil cooler hose, a car engine's counterbalance shaft bearings, and engine management software that affects brake assist. They only share the engine component category.

What happens if the counterbalance shaft loosens?

According to the recall, it can cause severe engine damage in affected 2019-2020 330i and 2019 Z4 vehicles, increasing the risk of a crash. The remedy is a full engine replacement at no cost to the owner.

Will I still be able to brake if my BMW is affected by the software recall?

Yes. The notice for the 2019-2021 M340i, X3, X4, Z4 and Toyota Supra recall states that full mechanical braking without power assist remains available, even though brake assist can be lost during engine start.
