Why do Chevrolet trucks and SUVs keep getting seat belt bolt recalls?

15 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-01-16

Most of the seat belt recalls in this group involve loose, missing, or incorrect hardware - fasteners, bolts, or brackets - that keep a seat belt from being properly secured to the seat or vehicle frame, spread across full-size trucks and SUVs like the Silverado, Sierra, Suburban, Tahoe, and Yukon, plus the Malibu, Camaro, Blazer, Colorado, Equinox, and Bolt EV. Several full-size SUVs (Escalade, Suburban, Tahoe, Yukon) also had third-row seat belts that were entrapped, damaged, or attached with a deformed rivet. The new Silverado EV, Hummer EV, and Sierra EV pickups have had their own separate seat belt bolt and bezel issues in 2023-2025. One 2019-2020 Silverado/Sierra recall involved a pretensioner fire risk instead of a hardware fastening problem.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A second-row center seat belt buckle that's hard to find or reach, hidden beneath interior trim (2021 Chevrolet Tahoe)
  • The pretensioner fire-risk recall (2019-2020 Silverado 1500, Sierra 1500) only becomes a hazard if the pretensioner deploys in a crash - there's no everyday warning sign
  • The notices describe no other driver-noticeable warning sign before a crash - these are hardware defects found by inspection

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The 2024 Chevrolet Silverado EV recall for a second-row center seat belt bezel does tell owners not to let anyone sit in that specific seat until the bezel is replaced, because the belt could tear in a crash. For every other recall in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving; they simply describe the seat belt or bracket defect and the fix.

What the free repair involves

Across these recalls, dealers tighten or replace incorrect bolts, replace seat belt retractors or buckle assemblies, reroute or replace damaged third-row belts, reposition a trapped buckle latch, close off a pretensioner vent opening, or replace a seat belt bezel - all free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Do any of these Chevrolet seat belt recalls mean I should stop driving?

No - none of them carry a stop-driving order. The only driving restriction in this group is on the 2024 Silverado EV bezel recall, which says not to seat anyone in the second-row center seat until it's repaired.

Why did my Chevrolet get recalled for a seat belt bolt instead of the belt itself?

Several recalls found the belt hardware - the bolts, brackets, or rivets that hold the belt to the seat or frame - loose, missing, or incorrectly formed, rather than a problem with the webbing or buckle mechanism itself.

Is the seat belt recall repair free?

Yes. GM says dealers will tighten, replace, or reroute the affected seat belt hardware at no cost to the owner, and owners are notified by mail when a fix is ready.
