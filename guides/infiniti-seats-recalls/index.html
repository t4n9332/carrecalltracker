Infiniti QX60 seat recalls: missing bolts, detached welds, stuck seats

3 NHTSA recalls · Infiniti · Seats · model years 2022–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 13, 2023

All three Infiniti seat recalls in this group come from Nissan North America and all three land on the QX60, two of them alongside the Nissan Pathfinder. Two concern the second row. On 2022 QX60 and Pathfinder vehicles, the second-row seat track may be missing a mounting bolt, a noncompliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 210. On 2023 QX60 vehicles, the second-row adjustable seat may extend past its intended rear stopper point and may be difficult to move, become stuck, or fail to lock in the correct position. The third covers the driver's powered seat on 2023 QX60, Pathfinder and Rogue vehicles, where the seat may not be fully secured to the seat frame and welds can detach from the rear inboard seat bracket.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A second-row seat that is difficult to move, becomes stuck, or fails to lock in the correct position — the 2023 Infiniti QX60 second-row seat recall.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the three notices tell owners to stop driving, and none tell owners to park outside. The 2022 QX60 and Pathfinder seat track notice, the 2023 QX60 second-row seat notice and the 2023 QX60, Pathfinder and Rogue driver's seat notice each describe a seat that may not properly restrain an occupant in a crash, and each points owners to a dealer inspection or replacement. None of them gives owners an interim instruction or a temporary measure before that repair.

What the free repair involves

For the 2022 QX60 and Pathfinder, dealers inspect the second-row seat track and install a new mounting bolt as necessary. For the 2023 QX60 second-row seat, dealers remove and replace the seat. For the 2023 QX60, Pathfinder and Rogue, dealers inspect the driver's seat rail clearance and, if necessary, replace the driver's seat cushion frame. All three notices state the work is free of charge.

Common questions

My 2023 QX60 second-row seat sticks — is that the recall?

The 2023 QX60 notice describes a second-row adjustable seat that may extend past the intended rear stopper point and may be difficult to move, become stuck, or fail to lock in position. Dealers remove and replace that seat free of charge.

Does the driver's seat recall also cover the Nissan Pathfinder and Rogue?

Yes. The April 2023 recall covers 2023 Infiniti QX60, Nissan Pathfinder and Nissan Rogue vehicles, where the driver's powered seat may not be fully secured to the seat frame. Dealers inspect the seat rail clearance and replace the seat cushion frame if necessary.

Do the notices say anything about carrying passengers in the second row?

They do not. The 2022 seat track and 2023 second-row seat notices describe an increased risk of injury to an occupant in a crash, but neither tells owners to keep anyone out of the second row or to stop driving.
