Why Are Infiniti QX60 Seats Being Recalled for Safety Defects?

3 NHTSA recalls · Infiniti · Seats · model years 2022–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2023-04-13

Three separate recalls have hit Infiniti QX60 seats in as many years. In 2022, certain QX60 and Nissan Pathfinder models left the factory with a second-row seat track missing a mounting bolt. In 2023, a different QX60 batch shipped with a second-row seat that could extend past its rear stopper, becoming hard to move, stuck, or unable to lock in place. Later in 2023, Nissan found that the driver's power seat on some QX60, Pathfinder, and Rogue models wasn't fully secured to its frame, letting the seat-frame welds detach from the rear inboard bracket. Every recall in this group centers on the QX60, with the second-row seat track and mounting hardware the recurring weak point.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Second-row seat that's hard to move, gets stuck, or won't lock into place (2023 QX60)
  • Driver's seat that feels loose, with the seat frame no longer secured to its rails (2023 QX60, Pathfinder, Rogue)
  • No symptom is described for the missing seat-track bolt recall (2022 QX60, Pathfinder) — it's found by dealer inspection, not by how the seat feels

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three recalls carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notice for the missing second-row seat-track bolt (2022 QX60, Pathfinder) does not tell owners to stop driving. The notice for the second-row seat that can stick or fail to lock (2023 QX60) does not tell owners to stop driving. The notice for the driver's seat weld failure (2023 QX60, Pathfinder, Rogue) does not tell owners to stop driving either. Each notice ties the defect to reduced crash protection for the seat occupant, but none directs owners to park the vehicle or avoid driving it.

What the free repair involves

Dealers address each recall differently: installing a new mounting bolt in the second-row seat track, removing and replacing the entire second-row seat assembly, or inspecting the driver's seat rail clearance and replacing the seat cushion frame if needed. All repairs are performed free of charge, and owners are contacted directly by mail once parts are available.

Common questions

Which Infiniti QX60 model years are covered by these seat recalls?

The QX60 appears in all three recalls: 2022 models for the missing seat-track bolt, and 2023 models for both the second-row seat lock issue and the driver's seat weld failure. Related Nissan Pathfinder and Rogue models are also covered.

Is the Nissan Pathfinder affected by these Infiniti seat recalls too?

Yes. The Pathfinder shares platforms with the QX60 and is named in two of the three recalls: the 2022 missing seat-track bolt and the 2023 driver's seat weld failure. The Nissan Rogue is also named in the weld recall.

How do I know if my seat has been repaired under one of these recalls?

Nissan mailed owner notification letters for each recall once parts were available, and dealers complete the repair free of charge. Contact Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669 with your VIN to confirm whether your vehicle needs the fix.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
