What's behind Mercedes-Benz exterior lighting recalls on Sprinter vans and GLC?

4 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Exterior Lighting · model years 2019–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-02-02

Mercedes-Benz exterior lighting recalls split cleanly between Sprinter vans and the GLC. Both Sprinter campaigns are control unit software problems: 2019 Sprinter VS30 vans equipped with LED rear fog lights that may not illuminate despite the rear fog light switch being turned on, and 2019 Mercedes-Benz and Freightliner Sprinter 1500, 2500, 3500 and 4500 vans whose taillight brightness can be significantly reduced or switch off completely if the manual turn-switch is moved quickly from one lighting position to another. The two GLC campaigns are hardware and aim instead: 2021 GLC 300 and AMG GLC 43 and 63 models whose headlight electrical connection may not be watertight, which can result in headlight failure, and 2019-2022 GLC 300, 2020 GLC 350e and 2020-2022 AMG GLC 43 models with headlights adjusted too high.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rear fog lights that do not illuminate with the rear fog light switch turned on (2019 Sprinter VS30 rear fog light recall).
  • Taillight brightness significantly reduced, or taillights switching off completely, after the manual light switch is turned quickly between positions (2019 Sprinter taillight recall).
  • Headlight failure (2021 GLC headlight electrical connection recall).
  • Headlight glare for oncoming traffic (2019-2022 GLC headlight adjustment recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the four notices tells owners to stop driving, and none carries a park-outside instruction. Each states only what the defect affects: the lack of functioning rear fog lamps prevents the enhanced visibility of the vehicle to other drivers; reduced taillight visibility increases crash risk; headlight failure can decrease visibility for both the driver and surrounding traffic; and incorrectly aimed headlights can reduce the visibility of oncoming traffic. Beyond those consequence statements, the notices say nothing about whether owners should continue to drive.

What the free repair involves

The two Sprinter recalls are software. Dealers update the software settings of the control unit body controller for the rear fog lights, and update the body control unit software for the taillights. For 2021 GLC models, dealers inspect and replace the headlight sealing plugs as necessary. For the headlight aim recall, dealers inspect the headlight adjustment and correct it if necessary. Each notice states the work is free of charge.

Common questions

Why are both Sprinter lighting recalls fixed with software?

Both notices describe control unit problems. For 2019 VS30 vans with LED rear fog lights, dealers update the software settings of the control unit body controller. For the 2019 Sprinter taillights, dealers update the body control unit software. Both notices state the work is free of charge.

My GLC headlights look fine — could it still be recalled?

Two GLC recalls apply. One covers 2021 models whose headlight electrical connection may not be watertight, which can result in headlight failure; dealers inspect and replace the sealing plugs as necessary. The other covers headlights adjusted too high; dealers inspect the adjustment and correct it if necessary.

Do any of these lighting recalls tell owners to stop driving?

None of the four does, and none carries a park-outside instruction. Each notice says only that the lighting defect reduces visibility — of the vehicle to other drivers, of the road for the driver, or for oncoming traffic — and increases the risk of a crash.
