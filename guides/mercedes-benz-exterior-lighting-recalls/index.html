Why do Mercedes-Benz Sprinter and GLC lights fail or misalign?

4 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Exterior Lighting · model years 2019–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-02-02

This group splits into two patterns. On 2019 Sprinter vans, the problems are electrical: LED rear fog lights may not illuminate even with the switch on, and quickly turning the manual light switch can dim or shut off the taillights on 2019 Sprinter 1500 and 4500 models — both fixed with a body control unit software update. On 2021 AMG GLC43 and GLC63 vehicles, the headlight electrical connection may not be watertight, causing headlight failure. A separate recall on 2021 AMG GLC43 vehicles covers headlights adjusted too high, creating glare for oncoming traffic. No mechanical bulb failures are described.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rear fog lights don't turn on even though the switch is on
  • Taillights dim or switch off completely when the manual light switch is turned quickly from one position to another
  • Headlights fail entirely if the electrical connection lets in water
  • The notices don't describe a symptom the driver would notice before headlights are aimed too high — it's identified by the glare it creates for oncoming drivers

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Mercedes-Benz says non-working rear fog lights and dimmed or failed taillights reduce how visible the Sprinter is to other drivers, and that headlight failure or glare from misaimed headlights on the GLC reduces visibility for the driver or oncoming traffic — each notice says this increases the risk of a crash. For all four recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers address these free of charge: Sprinter rear fog light and taillight problems are fixed with a body control unit software update, while GLC headlight issues are handled by inspecting and replacing the sealing plugs where the connection isn't watertight, or by inspecting and correcting the headlight aim.

Common questions

Is the GLC headlight recall the same defect as the Sprinter taillight recall?

No. The Sprinter recalls involve fog and taillights fixed with a software update; the GLC recalls involve a headlight seal that isn't watertight and headlights aimed too high, both fixed by hand at the dealer.

Will the taillight fix require replacing any bulbs on my Sprinter?

The notice describes a body control unit software update, not a bulb or wiring replacement, to stop the taillights from dimming or switching off when the light switch is turned quickly.

Which GLC model years are affected by the headlight aim recall?

This specific recall lists 2021 AMG GLC43 vehicles with headlights adjusted too high, which the notice says creates glare for oncoming drivers and reduces their visibility, increasing the risk of a crash.
