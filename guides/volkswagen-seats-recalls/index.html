Why do Volkswagen seats keep having weld and bolt recalls?

3 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · Seats · model years 2018–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-02-01

Seat frame welds and missing hardware are the recurring issues in this group. The 2018 e-Golf and Golf R share a rear seat problem: the head restraint guide sleeves may be incorrectly welded to the seat frame, reducing head restraint stability in a crash. The 2020 Audi Q5, SQ5, Atlas Cross Sport, Jetta, and Tiguan, plus the 2021 Atlas, share a different weld defect in the front seat frame and backrest adjuster that could let the backrest break in a rear-end crash. Separately, the 2021 Jetta and Tiguan had front seat track bolts missing entirely, letting the seat move without restriction. Each recall involves a distinct weld or fastener, not a single shared part.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rear head restraints that feel loose or unstable (2018 e-Golf, Golf R)
  • A front seat that moves forward or backward more than expected (2021 Jetta, Tiguan)
  • None of the notices describe a driver-noticeable symptom for the front seat frame or backrest adjuster weld defect (2020-2021 models)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these three seat recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the 2018 e-Golf and Golf R head restraint recall, the 2020-2021 Audi and Volkswagen backrest weld recall, and the 2021 Jetta and Tiguan seat track bolt recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. Instead, each notice describes how a crash could reveal the defect — reduced head restraint stability, a broken backrest, or unrestricted seat movement — without instructing owners to park the vehicle or avoid driving it.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect the affected seats and, as necessary, replace the rear seat frame, replace the seatback frame, or install missing front seat track bolts, free of charge. The specific repair depends on which recall applies — head restraint welds, backrest adjuster welds, or missing seat track hardware.

Common questions

Why were bolts missing from my Jetta or Tiguan's front seats?

The notice for the 2021 Jetta and Tiguan does not give a cause beyond stating that bolts may be missing from the front seat tracks, which lets the seat move front and back without restriction. Dealers inspect the tracks and install the missing bolts free of charge.

Does the seat weld recall affect Audi models too?

Yes. The 2020 front seat frame and backrest weld recall also covers the Audi Q5 and SQ5, alongside the Volkswagen Atlas Cross Sport, Jetta, and Tiguan, and the 2021 Atlas. All affected owners get the same free inspection and, if needed, seatback frame replacement.

Is my e-Golf or Golf R's rear head restraint unsafe to use?

The notice says the head restraint guide sleeves may be incorrectly welded, which can reduce rear head restraint stability in a crash. It does not tell owners to stop driving. Dealers inspect the welds and replace the seat frame if necessary, free of charge.
