Why do so many Ford models have seat belt recalls?

18 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-27

Seat belt recalls across this group fall into a few repeating problems: hardware that isn't properly attached (loose or improperly secured buckles and anchor bolts on the 2020 Explorer, 2020-2021 Explorer and Aviator, and 2022 Maverick; misassembled or misrouted belts on the 2019 and 2019-2020 Ranger SuperCab), pretensioners that don't work as intended (the 2019 Edge anchor crimp, the 2021 Edge retractor, and the 2018-2020 Expedition and Navigator pretensioner that can lock the belt), and one sensor that can misclassify a child occupant as an adult on the 2020 Expedition. The 2015-2018 F-150 recall is different: its pretensioner sparks could ignite nearby interior materials. Edge and Ranger SuperCab each appear in two separate seat belt recalls.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A seat belt that won't retract or extend normally (Expedition, Navigator)
  • A seat belt or buckle that feels loose, misrouted, or not fully attached (Explorer, Aviator, Maverick, Ranger)
  • The notices describe no advance warning sign for most of these defects — they typically only show up in a crash

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the seat belt recalls in this group carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. That includes the 2015-2018 F-150 pretensioner fire recall, the 2019 and 2021 Edge recalls, the 2019 and 2019-2020 Ranger SuperCab recalls, the 2020 Explorer and 2020-2021 Explorer/Aviator recalls, the 2020 Expedition sensor recall, the 2018-2020 Expedition/Navigator recall, the 2021 Transit Connect recall, the Transit/Promaster recall, and the 2022 Maverick recall. For all of these, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealer repairs across this group mostly involve inspecting and, where necessary, replacing the affected seat belt hardware: buckles, retractor pretensioners, anchor bolts, or full belt assemblies. The F-150 fire recall instead adds heat-resistant tape and insulation near the pretensioner. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Does my seat belt recall mean I should stop driving?

No. None of the seat belt recalls in this group tell owners to stop driving or park outside; the notices describe defects that could affect protection in a future crash, not an immediate driving hazard.

Why did my Ford F-150 seat belt recall mention fire?

On 2015-2018 F-150 trucks, sparks from a deployed seat belt pretensioner could ignite carpeting or insulation inside the B-pillar; dealers add heat-resistant tape and remove the flammable materials, free of charge.

My Expedition or Navigator seat belt won't move — is that the recall?

Possibly. On 2018-2020 Expedition and Lincoln Navigator vehicles, the seat belt pretensioner can inadvertently deploy and lock the belt so it will not retract or extend; dealers replace the affected seat belt retractors free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
