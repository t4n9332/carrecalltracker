Ford seat belt recalls: anchors, pretensioners and buckles explained

18 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-27

Nearly every Ford seat belt recall here traces to hardware that was mis-assembled or left unsecured at the factory. Anchor and buckle bolts are the biggest repeat: 2020-2021 Explorer and Aviator buckle anchor bolts, 2020 Explorer second-row center buckles, and 2022 Maverick and 2021-2022 Mustang Mach-E rear buckles not properly attached to the floor. Pretensioners and retractors form a second cluster — 2015-2018 F-150 pretensioner sparks igniting B-pillar material, a mis-crimped 2019 Edge anchor, a 2021 Edge retractor pretensioner, and 2018-2020 Expedition and Navigator belts that deploy and lock. Ranger Super Cab passenger belts were misrouted, 2020 Expedition tension sensors misclassify occupants, and upfitted Transit and ProMaster vans have mis-set Freedman retractors.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A belt that won't retract or extend, as though it has locked itself (Expedition, Navigator).
  • Webbing that is misrouted — twisted, outside its guide, or not lying flat across the shoulder (Ranger Super Cab).
  • A child seat that can't be cinched tight because the belt's locking retractor releases early.
  • The passenger airbag status doesn't match who is actually in the front seat (2020 Expedition).
  • A buckle stalk that feels loose, rattles, or moves where it anchors to the seat or floor.

Is it safe to keep driving?

One recall comes with a conditional do-not-drive. For 2019-2021 Ranger Super Cab, Ford gives owners a preliminary seat belt inspection and says that if you find a problem, are unsure of the result, or would rather not check, do not drive the vehicle and contact a dealer. No other recall here tells owners to stop driving or park outside. Keep driving and keep everyone belted, but where a notice names one seating position — the Explorer second-row center, the Maverick rear seats, the Expedition front passenger seat — leave it empty until repaired.

What the free repair involves

Dealers do all of this free. Depending on the recall they replace pretensioner or retractor assemblies, seat belt buckles, or an entire second-row seat assembly; inspect and secure buckle, retractor and belt anchor bolts; remove B-pillar insulation and wiring harness tape on the F-150 and install heat-resistant tape; and add a missing HVAC drain tube elbow on Expedition and Navigator.

Common questions

My 2020 Expedition has two seat belt recalls — are they the same thing?

They're related but separate. One covers a belt tension sensor that misclassifies the occupant; the other covers a sensor that classifies a child as an adult. Both are fixed by replacing the front passenger seat belt buckle at no charge.

My Transit or ProMaster was converted by an upfitter — who handles the recall?

The converter does. TransitWorks and National Van Builders recalled vans built on 2019-2020 Ford Transit and 2019 Ram ProMaster with Freedman foldaway seats; their dealers replace the retractors free, with parts and reimbursement handled through Freedman Seating.

Should I stop using the affected seat belt until it's fixed?

Keep wearing your belt — none of these notices says otherwise. Where the defect is tied to one position, such as the Explorer second-row center seat or Maverick rear buckles, the notices suggest not seating anyone there until the repair is complete.
