Why do Chevrolet hydraulic brake system recalls keep appearing?

17 NHTSA recalls · Chevrolet · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Nov 6, 2025

⚠ 3 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Chevrolet's hydraulic brake recalls split into two threads. Electronic Brake Control Module software drives several: ESC and ABS disabled on 2019 Silverado 1500 and Cadillac CT6, electronic brake assist disabled on 2019 Silverado 1500, and unintended braking that pulls the vehicle to one side on 2015-2020 Tahoe and Suburban and 2014-2018 Silverado 1500 with the 5.3-liter engine, 3.08 rear axle and four-wheel drive. Hardware drives the rest: caliper faults on the 2020 Silverado 1500, 2020 Bolt EV and 2020 Equinox, rear caliper piston coating on 2018-2019 Equinox, Cruze, Impala, Volt and Bolt EV, and brake fluid leaks creating a fire risk on Silverado Medium Duty trucks and 2021-2022 Silverado 2500 and 3500.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Increased braking effort and a longer distance required to stop — the vacuum pump recall covering Silverado, Suburban, Tahoe and Escalade.
  • The vehicle pulling to one side unexpectedly when the driveline-protection system brakes a wheel on its own — the Tahoe, Suburban and Silverado 1500 wheel-speed sensor recall.
  • In the 2019 Silverado 1500 and Cadillac CT6 EBCM software recall, the notice states the ESC and ABS malfunction warning lights will not illuminate when those systems are disabled.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

Three notices carry an interim instruction. The 2021-2022 Silverado 2500 and 3500 brake module recall tells owners to park outside and away from other structures until the repair is completed. The 2019-2023 Chevrolet 4500HD, 5500HD and 6500HD brake pressure sensor recall and the 2019-2024 Silverado Medium Duty 4500HD, 5500HD and 6500HD recall both tell owners to park outside and away from structures until the repair is complete. None of those three tells owners to stop driving. For every other recall here, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies differ by recall. Dealers reprogram or recalibrate the brake control module on the software and calibration recalls, bleed the brake system on the rear caliper piston recall, replace calipers, caliper bolts or the brake pressure modulator valve assembly on the hardware recalls, and replace brake pressure sensor or switch parts on the medium-duty trucks. Each notice says the work is free of charge.

Common questions

Why do so many of these Chevrolet brake recalls end in a software update?

Four trace to the Electronic Brake Control Module: ESC and ABS disabled on 2019 Silverado 1500 and CT6; electronic brake assist disabled on 2019 Silverado 1500; unintended braking on Tahoe, Suburban and Silverado 1500; and reduced vacuum brake assist. Dealers reprogram the module free of charge.

Which of these recalls tell owners to park outside?

Three do: the 2021-2022 Silverado 2500 and 3500 brake module recall, the 2019-2023 Chevrolet 4500HD, 5500HD and 6500HD brake pressure sensor recall, and the 2019-2024 Silverado Medium Duty recall. Each says to park outside and away from structures until the repair is complete.

Will I notice anything before a brake problem shows up?

The notices describe only a few things an owner would notice: increased braking effort and longer stopping distances in the vacuum pump recall, and the vehicle pulling to one side in the driveline-protection recall. The 2019 EBCM software notice says the ESC and ABS warning lights will not illuminate.
