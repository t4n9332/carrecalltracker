Jeep seat and head restraint recalls: what owners should check

4 NHTSA recalls · Jeep · Seats · model years 2019–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-07-17

These four Jeep recalls involve the seats themselves rather than the belts, and three are recorded as failures to meet a Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard. Head restraints account for two: front head restraints that were not welded properly on the 2022 Compass, and second-row head restraints that may not lock upright on 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L models. The other two involve seat structure — front passenger seat mounting bolts that may be loose on the 2019 Cherokee, and a second-row center-seat recliner pull strap that may bind on the seat trim of 2022 Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer models, keeping the seat back from locking upright. Each recall names a single model line.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L recall says the second-row head restraints may not lock in the upright position.
  • The 2022 Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer recall says the center-seat recliner pull strap may bind on the seat trim, preventing the seat back from locking into the upright position.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. All four describe risk in the event of a crash: a seat that moves, a seat back that is not locked into position, or head restraints that do not lock or were not welded properly can increase the risk of injury to the occupants of those seats. The notices give owners no interim instruction and no limit on which seats may be used before the repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealers tighten the front passenger seat mounting bolts on the 2019 Cherokee recall and replace both front seat head restraints on the Compass recall. On the Grand Cherokee recall they inspect and replace both second-row head restraints as necessary, and on the Wagoneer recall they inspect and replace the rear center-seat recliner strap as necessary. Each notice says the work is free of charge.

Common questions

What does a head restraint recall mean for my Jeep?

Two notices cover head restraints: the 2022 Compass, where the front restraints were not welded properly, and 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L, where second-row restraints may not lock upright. Both are recorded as failures to meet Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 202.

Can the affected seat still be used before the repair?

The notices do not address using or avoiding the affected seat. They state the risk of injury in a crash and say dealers will tighten, inspect or replace the parts. Owners wanting guidance for a specific vehicle can call FCA US at 1-800-853-1403.

When were owners notified about these seat recalls?

The 2019 Cherokee bolt recall began April 11, 2019. Wagoneer letters were mailed December 23, 2021, Compass letters June 3, 2022, and Grand Cherokee head restraint letters September 5, 2025. A dealer can confirm whether the repair is still outstanding on your VIN.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
