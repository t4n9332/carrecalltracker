Why are Jeep seats and head restraints being recalled?

4 NHTSA recalls · Jeep · Seats · model years 2019–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-07-17

These four recalls cover separate seat hardware defects. The 2019 Jeep Cherokee was recalled because the front passenger seat's mounting bolts may be loose, letting the seat move in a crash. Two recalls involve head restraints that fail to lock upright: the 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L for second-row restraints, and the 2022 Compass for front restraints that were improperly welded. Separately, the 2022 Jeep Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer were recalled because a second-row center-seat recliner pull strap can bind, preventing that seat back from locking in the upright position. Each recall stems from a different Jeep model and a distinct hardware fault, but all involve seating components failing to comply with federal seat or head restraint standards.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A loose front passenger seat that can move during a crash (2019 Cherokee)
  • A second-row head restraint that will not lock in the upright position (2023-2024 Grand Cherokee/Grand Cherokee L)
  • A second-row center seat back that will not lock upright because the recliner strap binds (2022 Wagoneer/Grand Wagoneer)
  • The notice describes no driver-noticeable warning sign for the Compass head restraint weld defect

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four seat recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. That includes the Cherokee loose seat mounting bolt recall, the Grand Cherokee/Grand Cherokee L head restraint recall, the Wagoneer/Grand Wagoneer recliner strap recall, and the Compass head restraint weld recall. Each notice states that the seating defect can increase the risk of injury to an occupant during a crash, but none of the notices tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers correct each seat defect at no cost: tightening the Cherokee's passenger seat mounting bolts, inspecting and replacing the Grand Cherokee/Grand Cherokee L second-row head restraints, replacing the Wagoneer/Grand Wagoneer recliner strap, and replacing the Compass's front head restraints. All repairs described in these notices are free to the owner.

Common questions

Why was my Jeep Grand Cherokee's head restraint recalled?

The 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L were recalled because the second-row head restraints may not lock in the upright position, which can increase injury risk in a crash and fails federal head restraint standards.

Is a loose seat bolt recall dangerous to drive with?

The 2019 Cherokee notice says loose front passenger seat mounting bolts could let the seat move in a crash, increasing injury risk, but it does not instruct owners to stop driving.

What's wrong with the Jeep Wagoneer second-row seat?

On 2022 Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer models, a second-row center-seat recliner pull strap can bind on the seat trim, preventing that seat back from locking upright.
