Jeep seat and head restraint recalls: what owners should know

4 NHTSA recalls · Jeep · Seats · model years 2019–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-07-17

All four are crash-protection compliance problems rather than driving faults, and head restraints are the recurring theme. The 2022 Compass front head restraints were not welded properly, and on 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L the second-row head restraints may not lock in the upright position — both failures of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 202, with the Grand Cherokee recall the broadest here by model year. The other two are seat hardware: front passenger seat mounting bolts left loose on the 2019 Cherokee, and a second-row center recliner pull strap on 2022 Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer that can bind on the seat trim so the seat back never locks upright.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A head restraint that will not stay locked in the upright position
  • The second-row center seat back does not lock upright, or its recliner strap catches on the trim
  • A front passenger seat that feels loose on its mounts
  • The improperly welded Compass head restraints may look and feel completely normal

Is it safe to keep driving?

All four are lower-urgency compliance recalls, and none tells owners to stop driving or park outside. The notices say the vehicles drive normally; the risk falls on whoever occupies the affected seat in a crash. Where you have the choice, avoid using that position until it is fixed — the 2022 Wagoneer second-row center seat whose back may not lock, the second row of 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L, and the front seats of a 2022 Compass. The 2019 Cherokee bolts simply need tightening.

What the free repair involves

All four repairs are free at a Chrysler dealer, and none involves software or an over-the-air update. Dealers tighten the front passenger seat mounting bolts on the 2019 Cherokee, inspect and replace the rear center-seat recliner strap on the Wagoneer, inspect and replace both second-row head restraints on the Grand Cherokee, and replace both front head restraints on the 2022 Compass.

Common questions

Is my Jeep unsafe to drive with a seat recall open?

The notices rate these below mechanical failures — the vehicle drives normally and none instructs owners to stop. The danger appears in a crash, for whoever is sitting in the affected seat or row, so schedule the free repair.

What exactly is wrong with the head restraints?

Two separate defects. On the 2022 Compass, the front head restraints were not welded properly. On 2023-2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L, the second-row restraints may not lock upright. Both fail Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 202.

Should anyone sit in the affected seat before the repair?

The notices say occupants of those positions face a higher injury risk in a crash. Seat passengers elsewhere if you can, especially in the Wagoneer's second-row center seat, whose back may not lock upright, until the dealer completes the free fix.
