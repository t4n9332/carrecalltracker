Why do Mercedes-Benz seat belts fail to protect occupants and children?

4 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Seat Belts · model years 2019–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-12-12

These four seat belt recalls split into two patterns. Two affect adult occupant protection: on 2018-2019 E-Class, CLS, GLC, and G-Class models, the front seat belt buckle may incorrectly register as unbuckled, disabling PRE-SAFE and the belt pretensioners, while on 2022-2025 C 300 and 2023-2024 AMG C 43 vehicles, a poorly installed seat belt cover can let the driver-side anchor come loose. The other two affect child seat installation: on 2025-2026 GLE 350 4MATIC vehicles, the center rear seat belt was built without the child seat locking function, and on 2021 G-Class, E-Class, CLS, and AMG GT models, the rear seat belt automatic locking retractors can deactivate too early to hold a child restraint.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Front seat belt warning light or chime may indicate the belt is unbuckled even when it is properly fastened (2018-2019 models).
  • The driver-side seat belt cover may look loose or improperly installed near the anchor point (2022-2025 C 300, 2023-2024 AMG C 43).
  • A child seat installed in the affected center rear or rear seating position may not lock securely or may feel loose.
  • The notices describe no other warning signs owners would notice before a crash.

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these four seat belt recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The 2018-2019 buckle-detection recall, the 2022-2025 C 300 and 2023-2024 AMG C 43 seat belt anchor recall, the 2025-2026 GLE 350 rear seat belt recall, and the 2021 rear seat belt retractor recall all describe an increased risk of injury to an occupant or child in a crash, but none of the notices tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and repair or replace the affected part in each recall: replacing the front seat belt buckle as needed, inspecting and repairing the seat belt cover, replacing the center rear seat belt assembly on the GLE 350, and inspecting and replacing rear seat belt assemblies as necessary on the 2021 models. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Will my seat belt recall affect airbag deployment too?

Only the 2018-2019 buckle-detection recall mentions another system: if the buckle is misread as unbuckled, the PRE-SAFE feature and belt pretensioners may not activate in a crash. The other three seat belt recalls do not mention airbags.

Is my child seat affected in a recalled Mercedes-Benz rear seat?

In the 2025-2026 GLE 350 and the 2021 G-Class, E-Class, CLS, and AMG GT recalls, the rear seat belt may not lock a child restraint properly, raising the risk of injury to a child in a crash. Dealers repair the belt assembly free of charge.

Do I need to stop driving for a Mercedes-Benz seat belt recall?

No recall in this group tells owners to stop driving or park outside. Each notice instead describes an increased risk of injury to an occupant or child if a crash occurs before the dealer completes the free repair.
