Honda Transmission and Driveshaft Recalls: What Owners Should Know

6 NHTSA recalls · Honda · Power Train · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-10-23

Across these six Honda power train recalls, two patterns stand out. Three motorcycle recalls — the 2018 CBR300R, CRF250L and 2019 CB300R; the 2024 CBR650R; and the 2025 CB1000SP — involve shifting hardware: a transmission main-shaft circlip, gear shift arm fasteners, or a gearshift pedal pivot bolt that can loosen or detach, leaving the rider unable to shift or the transmission misaligned. Separately, the 2021 Acura RDX and 2022 CR-V Hybrid share a driveshaft that may have been improperly heat-treated, while the 2023 Accord Hybrid and CR-V Hybrid share an improperly manufactured electronic transmission, both causing a loss of drive power. The 2018-2019 Odyssey recall is unrelated: a battery issue can make the transmission control unit shift to Park unexpectedly.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Motorcycle transmission shifting into gear unexpectedly at engine start, or the transmission and rear wheel seizing while riding (CB300R, CBR300R, CRF250L)
  • Inability to shift gears while riding (CBR650R, CB1000SP)
  • Sudden loss of drive power while driving (Acura RDX and CR-V Hybrid driveshaft; Accord Hybrid and CR-V Hybrid transmission)
  • A vehicle rolling away after being parked (Acura RDX/CR-V Hybrid if the parking brake isn't applied; Odyssey if the parking rod is damaged)
  • The notices do not describe a dashboard warning or other sign before these failures occur

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six Honda power train recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For the three motorcycle recalls, the notices do not tell riders to stop riding, despite describing a risk of losing the ability to shift or unexpected transmission engagement. For the Acura RDX, CR-V Hybrid driveshaft, Accord Hybrid/CR-V Hybrid transmission, and Odyssey recalls, the notices likewise do not tell owners to stop driving, even though they describe a loss of drive power or a vehicle rolling away when parked.

What the free repair involves

Honda dealers address each recall differently: replacing the transmission main shaft, gear shift arm, or gearshift pedal pivot bolt on the affected motorcycles; replacing the front driveshaft on the Acura RDX and CR-V Hybrid; replacing the electronic CVT on the Accord Hybrid and CR-V Hybrid; and securing battery connections, updating transmission control software, and checking the parking rod on the Odyssey. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Which Honda models are affected by these transmission and driveshaft recalls?

The group covers the 2018 CBR300R and CRF250L, 2019 CB300R motorcycles; the 2024 CBR650R; the 2025 CB1000SP; the 2021 Acura RDX and 2022 CR-V Hybrid; the 2023 Accord Hybrid and CR-V Hybrid; and the 2018-2019 Odyssey.

Can I still ride my Honda motorcycle under these recalls?

The notices for the CB300R/CBR300R/CRF250L, CBR650R, and CB1000SP recalls do not instruct riders to stop riding, though they describe a risk of losing the ability to shift gears or unexpected transmission movement until the dealer repair is completed.

Is the repair free for these recalls?

Yes. Honda dealers perform the inspection, part replacement, or software update at no cost to the owner for all six recalls in this group, including the motorcycle shifting repairs and the driveshaft and transmission replacements.
