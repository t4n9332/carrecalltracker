Why do GMC trucks keep recalling seat belt bolts and buckles?

9 NHTSA recalls · GMC · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-01-16

Nine GMC seat belt recalls point to assembly errors more than a shared design flaw. Several found improperly tightened or missing bolts: front seats on 2023-2024 Colorado and 2023 Canyon, 2023-2024 Hummer EV and 2024 Silverado EV, and a rear center seat on 2024 Silverado EV and 2024-2025 Sierra EV, plus front-row center and other positions across 2019-2021 Silverado 1500/2500/3500, Sierra 1500/2500/3500, Suburban, Tahoe, and Yukon XL, and a wide 2020-2021 bolt mix-up spanning the Bolt EV to the Yukon. Third-row seat belts on 2021-2022 Escalade, Suburban, Tahoe, and Yukon were separately misrouted or had a deformed buckle rivet. One 2019-2020 Silverado/Sierra recall involves a pretensioner that can vent hot gas into carpet.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Most of these bolt, bracket, and pretensioner defects produce no warning sign an owner can notice before a crash
  • Visible fraying, cuts, or wear on a third-row seat belt that was pinched behind the seat-folding mechanism
  • A seat belt buckle that feels loose or wobbly at its mounting point

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these nine recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction, including the recall covering a seat belt pretensioner that can vent hot gas into carpet. For every recall in this group, from loose seat belt bolts and misrouted third-row belts to bracket and buckle-rivet defects, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving. The concern in each case is that the belt may not properly restrain an occupant, or that carpet could ignite, if a crash or pretensioner deployment occurs before the repair is made.

What the free repair involves

Dealers tighten or replace the affected seat belt bolts, inspect and reattach or replace seat belt brackets, inspect third-row belts and replace or reroute any that are damaged, and replace buckle assemblies with deformed rivets. On the pretensioner fire recall, dealers close off the bracket opening that lets gas escape. All repairs are free.

Common questions

Why does GMC have so many separate seat belt bolt recalls?

Most stem from assembly-line errors — the wrong bolt, an under-tightened bolt, or a misrouted belt — rather than one shared design defect, so GM issued a new recall each time inspectors found a different bolt or model affected.

Does a loose seat belt bolt mean the belt won't work at all?

The notices say an improperly secured bolt or bracket may not let the belt restrain an occupant as intended in a crash, increasing injury risk; they don't say the belt will fail completely.

Is the seat belt fire recall the same as the bolt recalls?

No. The 2019-2020 Silverado and Sierra fire recall involves a pretensioner venting hot gas into carpet, a separate defect from the loose-bolt and bracket recalls affecting other model years and components.
