Ford engine and cooling recalls: oil leaks, stalls and fire risk

13 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest May 27, 2026

Oil is the common thread. Ford cites a cracked oil separator housing on 2020-2022 Escape and 2021-2022 Bronco Sport with 1.5L engines, a damaged turbocharger oil supply line on 2023 Explorer 2.3L GTDI, and a misaligned engine cup plug on 3.5L GTDI F-150, Expedition and Lincoln Navigator, named in two recalls. A second thread is engine failure and lost power: the 2021 Escape Hybrid crankshaft, intake valves breaking on 2021-2022 Bronco, F-150, Edge, Explorer, Nautilus and Aviator with 2.7L or 3.0L Nano EcoBoost engines, connecting rods on 2025 Transit, F-150 and Explorer, the Focus and EcoSport oil pump belt, and 2025 EGR valves. One recall is Roush's, not Ford's: a supercharger kit sold for 2018 Mustangs.

Warning signs to watch for

  • An oil pressure warning light, or smoke from the engine compartment — the 2023 Explorer 2.3L turbocharger oil supply line recall.
  • Unexpected engine noises, a reduction in vehicle power, or smoke — the 2020-2022 Escape, 2021-2022 Corsair and 2022 Maverick engine fire recall.
  • An engine stall and a loss of power braking assist — the 2016-2018 Focus and 2018-2022 EcoSport oil pump belt recall.
  • A loss of drive power — the 2021-2022 Nano EcoBoost intake valve recall (24S55) and the 2025 EGR valve recall (26S10).
  • An engine stall — the 2025 Transit, F-150 and Explorer connecting rod recall (25S55) and the engine cup plug recalls (24S70 and 25S30).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

No notice in this group tells owners to stop driving, and none tells them to park outside. Two carry an instruction about what to do if something happens: the 2023 Explorer turbocharger oil line notice advises owners to park and shut off the engine as soon as possible if they see an oil pressure warning light or smoke coming from the engine compartment, and the 2020-2022 Escape, 2021-2022 Corsair and 2022 Maverick fire notice advises the same if owners hear unexpected engine noises, notice a reduction in power, or see smoke. The other notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

All remedies are free. Dealers replace the oil separator, replace the turbo oil supply line, or inspect the engine cup plug and replace it if needed. For power loss they inspect the crankshaft, connecting rods or valves and replace the engine as necessary, or replace the oil pump belt and tensioner, or the EGR valves. Transit Connect gets a PCM software update; Roush replaces the supercharger fuel rail subassemblies.

Common questions

What does the engine cup plug recall cover on the F-150 and Expedition?

Two notices name a misaligned engine cup plug on 3.5L GTDI engines: 24S70 covers 2024-2025 F-150, 2024 Expedition and 2024 Lincoln Navigator, and 25S30 covers 2024-2025 F-150 and Expedition and 2024 Navigator. Dealers inspect the alignment and replace the plug if necessary, free.

Is the Roush supercharger recall handled by Ford?

The notice says Roush Performance Products, not Ford, is recalling these aftermarket supercharger kits sold for installation on 2018 Mustangs. Roush will notify known owners and replace the defective fuel rail subassemblies free of charge; owners may contact Roush at 1-800-597-6874.

What should I do if my 2023 Explorer shows an oil pressure warning light?

The 2023 Explorer 2.3L GTDI notice advises owners to park and shut off the engine as soon as possible if they see an oil pressure warning light or smoke coming from the engine compartment. Its remedy is a free turbocharger oil supply line replacement.
