Why do Ford engine and cooling system recalls list oil leaks?

13 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-27

The most common thread in this group is an oil leak that can reach hot engine or exhaust components and raise fire risk — from a cracked oil separator housing (Escape, Bronco Sport), a damaged turbo oil supply line (Explorer), and a misaligned engine cup plug (Expedition, Navigator, repeated in 2024 and 2025). A separate pattern covers engine failure itself: a manufacturing defect in the Escape Hybrid crankshaft, a failing oil pump belt on the Focus and EcoSport, broken intake valves across several 2021-2022 SUVs, and defective connecting rods on the 2025 Explorer. The Escape, Maverick and Corsair HEV/PHEV recall covers engine oil and fuel vapor reaching hot parts after an internal failure. Two older, unrelated issues involve an aftermarket Mustang supercharger fuel leak and Transit Connect cooling software.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Oil pressure warning light illuminates, or smoke comes from the engine compartment (Explorer turbo oil leak recall)
  • Unexpected engine noise, a drop in power, or smoke from the engine (Escape, Maverick, Corsair HEV/PHEV recall)
  • Sudden loss of drive power or an engine stall (crankshaft, oil pump belt, intake valve, connecting rod and EGR valve recalls)
  • The remaining recalls in this group — including the oil separator, cup plug, supercharger and cooling software recalls — describe no driver-noticeable warning sign

Is it safe to keep driving?

No recall in this group carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Two notices add an interim instruction instead: the 2022 Escape/Maverick/Corsair recall tells owners to park and shut off the engine as quickly as possible if they hear unexpected noise, notice reduced power, or see smoke, and the 2023 Explorer turbo recall tells owners to do the same if they see an oil pressure warning light or smoke from the engine compartment. For every other recall in this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the affected part — oil separator housings, turbo oil supply lines, engine cup plugs, crankshafts, oil pump belts and tensioners, intake valves via engine replacement, connecting rods, EGR valves, or supercharger fuel rails — and update powertrain control module software on some recalls. All of this repair work is done free of charge.

Common questions

Why do so many Ford recalls in this group mention oil leaks?

Several separate defects can each let engine oil reach a hot exhaust or engine part and increase fire risk — a cracked oil separator housing, a damaged turbo line, and a misaligned cup plug are three distinct causes named in different recalls, not one shared defect.

What should I do if I see an oil pressure warning light?

The 2023 Explorer turbo oil leak recall tells owners to park and shut off the engine as soon as possible if the oil pressure warning light comes on or they see smoke from the engine compartment. That instruction applies specifically to that recall's notice.

Are these engine repairs free?

Yes. Every recall in this group is repaired at no cost to the owner, whether the fix is replacing an oil separator housing, a turbo line, an engine, or updating control module software.
