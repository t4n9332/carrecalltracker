Mercedes-Benz Door Latch and Hood Lock Problems Owners Should Know

7 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Latches/locks/linkages · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 24, 2026

Door opening and latching failures recur across these Mercedes-Benz notices, with Sprinter vans and G-Class vehicles appearing repeatedly. The 2018 Sprinter door recall concerns right-side doors that may remain inaccessible after electronic unlocking. Separate Sprinter notices cover hood catches on 2018-2019 vehicles and incorrect auxiliary locks on 2019-2020 vans with optional electric sliding doors. G-Class rear-door problems appear in the May 2021 and September 2024 notices; the latter covers specified vehicles that received replacement latches. Other notices address an incorrectly installed bowden cable in certain 2026 GLA 250 vehicles and driver-door lock micro-switch corrosion that can interfere with automatic parking functions across several model lines.

Warning signs to watch for

  • October 2018 Sprinter door recall: right-side doors may not open from inside or outside after electronic unlocking.
  • May 2021 G-Class rear-door recall: a rear door may appear closed without fully latching, open while driving, or fail to open from inside.
  • September 2024 G-Class rear-door recall: affected rear doors may appear closed without fully latching, open while driving, or fail to open from inside.
  • March 2026 GLA 250 recall: a door may not open from inside.
  • September 2019 Sprinter hood recall: the hood may open while driving.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the listed notices carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The October 2018 Sprinter right-side door, May 2021 G-Class rear-door, September 2024 G-Class right-rear-door, and March 2026 GLA 250 bowden-cable notices do not tell owners to stop driving. The July 2026 driver-door lock micro-switch notice likewise does not tell owners to stop driving. Neither the September 2019 Sprinter hood-catch notice nor the March 2021 Sprinter electric sliding-door auxiliary-lock notice tells owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Remedies are free: October 2018 Sprinter locks are inspected and replaced if necessary; May 2021 G-Class rear latches and September 2024 right-rear latches are replaced. March 2026 GLA cables are inspected and repaired as necessary; July 2026 driver-door locks are replaced. September 2019 Sprinter hood screws are inspected and tightened as necessary; March 2021 sliding doors are converted from electric to manual function.

Common questions

Does an earlier G-Class latch repair satisfy the September 2024 recall?

The September 2024 notice says vehicles in that recall previously repaired under recall 21V-353 need the new remedy. Its specified population includes certain G 550 and G63 AMG vehicles that received replacement door latches during service or a prior recall repair.

Will the Sprinter sliding-door recall preserve electric operation?

The March 2021 notice specifies converting the affected sliding door from electric to manual function, free of charge. It concerns certain 2019-2020 Sprinter vehicles with an optional electric sliding door whose auxiliary lock may be the incorrect type.

How can a driver-door lock problem lead to rollaway?

The July 2026 notice says corrosion can affect the micro-switch in the driver's door lock. If it fails to detect an open door, the electronic parking brake or Auto-Park feature may not engage automatically, potentially resulting in rollaway.
