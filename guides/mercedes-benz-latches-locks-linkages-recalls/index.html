Which Mercedes-Benz door latch and lock recalls affect your vehicle?

7 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Latches/locks/linkages · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 24, 2026

Mercedes-Benz recalls in this group split between Sprinter vans and passenger cars. Three cover Sprinters: right-side door latches built with incorrect components on 2018 Sprinter 2500 and 3500, an improperly tightened upper hood catch screw on 2018-2019 Sprinters, and an incorrect auxiliary lock on the optional electric sliding door of 2019-2020 Sprinters. Rear door latches on the G-Class appear twice, covering 2019-2021 G550 and G63 AMG, then 2019-2023 G 550 and 2019-2022 G63 AMG vehicles that received a replacement latch. The newest entries move elsewhere: a bowden cable on 2026 GLA 250 doors, and a corroding driver's door lock micro-switch covering 2019-2022 A-Class, 2020-2021 CLA, 2022-2023 C-Class, 2024 CLE, 2020-2026 GLA and GLB, and 2023-2024 GLC models.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Recall for 2018 Sprinter 2500 and 3500: when the doors are electronically unlocked, the right-side doors may not be able to be opened from inside or outside the vehicle.
  • G-Class rear door latch recalls: the rear door may appear closed even when the latch is not fully engaged, and damaged latch components may prevent the rear door from being opened from inside.
  • Recall for 2026 GLA 250: the door may not open from inside the vehicle.
  • Recall for 2018-2019 Sprinter hood catch: the hood may open while the vehicle is being driven.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the seven notices in this group tells owners to stop driving, and none tells them to park the vehicle outside. That includes the Sprinter right-side door latch recall, the Sprinter hood catch recall, the Sprinter electric sliding door recall, both G-Class rear door latch recalls, the 2026 GLA 250 bowden cable recall and the driver's door lock micro-switch recall. The notices describe the safety risks they carry, including doors that may not open, a hood that may open while driving and a vehicle rollaway, and direct owners to have the dealer remedy performed.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the rear door latches on the 2021 G-Class recall and the right-side rear door latches on the 2024 one. Elsewhere they inspect and replace the 2018 Sprinter locks if necessary, repair the GLA 250 bowden cable assembly as necessary, replace the driver's door lock for the micro-switch recall, tighten the hood catch screw connection, and convert the electric sliding door to manual function. All free of charge.

Common questions

My G-Class already had a door latch replaced. Do I still need this recall?

The 2024 notice covers 2019-2023 G 550 and 2019-2022 G63 AMG vehicles that received a replacement door latch during a service visit or prior recall repair, and it says vehicles previously repaired under recall 21V-353 need the new remedy performed.

What does a door lock have to do with my car rolling away?

The notice says the micro-switch in the driver's door lock may corrode and fail to detect an open door, which prevents the electronic parking brake or Auto-Park feature from engaging automatically and may result in a vehicle rollaway.

Will my Sprinter's electric sliding door still work after the repair?

The notice says dealers will convert the sliding door from electric to manual function, free of charge, on 2019-2020 Sprinters equipped with the optional electric sliding door. It does not describe any other remedy for this recall.
