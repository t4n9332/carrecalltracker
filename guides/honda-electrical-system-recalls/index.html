Which Honda electrical recalls involve power loss or wiring failures?

12 NHTSA recalls · Honda · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Dec 23, 2025

These twelve Honda recalls run from wiring damage to control software, and the hybrids recur. Loss of drive power covers the 2020 CR-V Hybrid, Accord Hybrid and 2020-2021 Insight through the DC-DC converter, the 2023-2025 Accord Hybrid through an integrated control module reset, and the 2017-2018 Clarity Fuel Cell through fuel cell control software. CR-V Hybrid appears twice more: a 2023 high-voltage battery module that can spark, and a 2020-2022 12-volt battery cable missing a fuse. Motorcycles make up a third of the group, with harness and switch faults on the CRF250L, CRF450L, CRF1100 Africa Twin and CBR650R. Software faults also disrupt displays and body systems on the 2018-2020 Accord, 2019-2020 Insight, 2019-2021 Pilot, and 2019-2020 Odyssey and Passport.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Malfunctions of the windshield wipers, defroster, rearview camera, exterior lights, audible warning of a stopped vehicle and power windows (Accord and Insight body control module recall, X95).
  • Exterior lighting, horn and instrument panel affected, with the panel not displaying speed or fuel level (2018 CRF250L wiring harness recall, KJ7).
  • Loss of horn function, or the headlight cannot be switched from low beam to high beam (CRF1100 Africa Twin handlebar switch recall, KS8).
  • A blown fuse causing loss of turn signal, brake light, speedometer and horn function (2024-2025 CBR650R recall, KT7).
  • A rearview camera image that is delayed or does not display (Pilot, Odyssey and Passport central network software recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these twelve notices tells owners to stop driving or to park the vehicle outside, and none gives an interim instruction to follow before the repair. The notices describe the risks instead: the Odyssey third-row power outlet harness recall cites a fire risk and the CR-V Hybrid battery cable recall cites a fire or injury risk in a crash; the 2023 CR-V Hybrid battery module recall cites sparking, injury, fire and a loss of drive power; and the Clarity Fuel Cell, DC-DC converter and Accord Hybrid ICM recalls each cite a loss of drive power that increases crash risk.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the 2023 CR-V Hybrid battery module, the CR-V Hybrid 12-volt battery cable, the CRF450L horn and mount and the CRF1100 handlebar switch assembly; they inspect and repair the Odyssey outlet harness, CRF250L harness and CBR650R harness. Others are dealer software updates to the BCM, power converter, fuel cell control unit or ICM. One rearview camera recall lets owners download the update themselves. All are free of charge.

Common questions

Which Honda hybrids are covered by these electrical recalls?

The 2020 CR-V Hybrid, 2020 Accord Hybrid and 2020-2021 Insight are in the DC-DC converter recall, the 2023 CR-V Hybrid in the battery module recall, the 2020-2022 CR-V Hybrid in the battery cable recall, and the 2023-2025 Accord Hybrid in the ICM software recall.

Are Honda motorcycles included?

Yes. Four recalls cover motorcycles: the 2018 CRF250L wiring harness, the 2019 CRF450L horn mount, the 2020-2022 and 2024-2025 CRF1100 Africa Twin handlebar switch harness, and the 2024-2025 CBR650R turn signal switch harness. Honda's motorcycle customer service line for these recalls is 1-866-784-1870.

Can the rearview camera software update be done without a dealer visit?

For the central network software recall on 2019-2021 Pilot, 2019-2020 Odyssey and 2019-2020 Passport, the notice says owners may either follow mailed instructions to download and update the software themselves for free, or have a dealer perform the update free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
