Why does Tesla keep recalling Model 3, Y, S, X seat belts?

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-05-28

Across these five Tesla recalls, the recurring issue is seat belt hardware that was not properly attached during assembly or service, not one shared design flaw. Two recalls cover Model 3 and Model Y front or rear seat belt fasteners that were not properly attached at the factory. A third covers Model 3 second-row seat belt buckles and anchors reassembled incorrectly during service. A fourth covers Model S front-row seat belts that may not be connected to their pretensioner anchors. The most recent recall is different: a 2024 software defect that can keep the seat belt warning chime and light from activating across most of Tesla's lineup, including Model S, Model 3, Model Y, and Model X.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Seat belt warning chime and light may not sound when a driver is unbelted (2024 recall)
  • No noticeable symptom for the seat belt fastener and anchor recalls — the defect is found only by inspection
  • Front-row seat belt may not be securely connected to its pretensioner anchor (Model S/X recall)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The seat belt fastener, anchor, and pretensioner-connection recalls do not include a stop-driving or park-outside instruction — the notices only describe inspecting and repairing the belts. The 2024 seat belt warning chime recall, fixed through a software update, likewise carries no stop-driving instruction. Across this group, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving; they describe increased risk of injury in a crash rather than an immediate operating restriction.

What the free repair involves

For the fastener, anchor, and pretensioner-connection recalls, Tesla Service inspects the seat belt hardware and tightens, reassembles, or replaces it as needed. For the 2024 warning chime defect, Tesla pushes an over-the-air software update. All repairs are performed free of charge to the owner.

Common questions

Do I need to bring my Tesla in for these seat belt recalls?

Yes — most of these recalls require a Tesla Service inspection and repair of the belt fasteners or anchors. The seat belt warning chime recall is fixed remotely with an over-the-air software update instead.

Will I notice if my Tesla is affected by one of these recalls?

Not usually. The notices describe defects found through inspection, such as a loose fastener or disconnected anchor, rather than a symptom you would feel while driving.

Is it safe to keep driving while I wait for the repair?

The notices do not instruct owners to stop driving for any of these five recalls. They describe an increased risk of injury in a crash, not an immediate driving restriction.
