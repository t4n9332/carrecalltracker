Why do Tesla seat belt anchors keep coming loose?

5 NHTSA recalls · Tesla · Seat Belts · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-05-28

Every Tesla seat belt recall here traces back to an anchor point that was never fastened correctly. Three of the five involve assembly-line fasteners: front shoulder belts at the B-pillar on 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2019-2021 Model Y, second-row retractor fasteners on 2019-2021 Model Y, and front-row belts not connected to their pretensioner anchors on 2021-2023 Model S and Model X. A fourth covers 2017-2022 Model 3 cars where the second-row left buckle and center anchor were reassembled incorrectly during service. Model 3 and Model Y appear most often, with 2018-2021 build years repeating. The fifth is different: a compliance recall where the unbelted warning light and chime may not activate, spanning 2012-2024 Model S, 2015-2024 Model X, 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A shoulder belt anchor at the B-pillar, or a rear belt retractor, that feels loose when you tug on it
  • A front seat belt that pulls away from or detaches at its pretensioner anchor
  • A rear buckle or center belt anchor on a Model 3 that was worked on during a past service visit
  • No seat belt warning light or chime when you drive off unbelted
  • Otherwise these defects give no warning — a belt can look normal and latch normally and still be poorly attached

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of the five recalls carries a stop-driving or park-outside order, and Tesla does not restrict use of the car in any of them. The two fastener recalls and the Model S and X pretensioner-anchor recall are still rated serious, because a belt that is poorly attached or detaches may not hold you in a crash — the notices say to have it inspected promptly. The Model 3 rear-belt reassembly recall matters mainly to back-seat riders. The warning light recall is the mildest; just buckle up every time until the update installs.

What the free repair involves

Tesla Service inspects the belt hardware and repairs what it finds: fixing or replacing the B-pillar and second-row retractor fasteners, connecting the front-row belts to their pretensioner anchors or replacing the belt assemblies, and reassembling the Model 3 rear buckle and center anchor. The warning light and chime recall is handled by an over-the-air software update. All of it is free of charge.

Common questions

My seat belts latch and retract fine — do I still need the inspection?

Yes. A fastener that was never properly attached at the B-pillar or at a rear retractor can hold in everyday use and still fail in a crash. The notices call for an inspection, not for you to judge the belt by feel.

Which of these recalls is fixed over the air?

Only the 2024 recall for the unbelted warning light and chime. Tesla released an over-the-air software update covering 2012-2024 Model S, 2015-2024 Model X, 2017-2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y. The other four need a service visit.

Why does one recall involve work done at a Tesla service center?

The 2022 Model 3 recall covers second-row left seat belt buckles and center belt anchors that were incorrectly reassembled during vehicle service, not on the assembly line. That is why it spans 2017-2022 cars rather than one narrow build period.
