Why are Mercedes-Benz seat and headrest defects raising airbag injury risks?

5 NHTSA recalls · Mercedes-Benz · Seats · model years 2019–2021 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2021-05-28

These five seat recalls cluster around two issues: seat position relative to the airbag, and seat hardware defects. Several 2019-2021 E-Class and AMG E53/E63 vehicles are affected by seat positioning problems, including a missing mechanical stop that lets the front passenger seat move too close to the airbag, a separate seat limiter recall with the same result, and a faulty seat position switch on a wide range of 2021 AMG models that can prevent air bags and seat belt force limiters from activating in a crash. Separately, 2019 Sprinter vans have a front seat height adjuster weld that may be insufficiently welded, and 2021 C-Class and E-Class cabriolet models have rear headrests that may be incorrectly mounted to the seatback.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Rear headrests on affected cabriolet models may look or feel loose, wobbly, or improperly seated (2021 C-Class and E-Class convertibles).
  • The front passenger seat may be able to move or adjust closer to the airbag than it should on affected E-Class and AMG E53 models.
  • The notices describe no noticeable warning sign for the Sprinter seat weld or the AMG seat position switch defects before a crash.

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these five seat recalls tell owners to stop driving or park outside. The Sprinter front seat weld recall, the front passenger seat adjustment stop recall, the seat position switch recall affecting a range of 2021 AMG models, the E53 passenger seat airbag distance recall, and the convertible rear headrest recall all describe an increased risk of injury in a crash, but none of the notices instruct owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace or repair the affected seat hardware: replacing the front seat tilt adjuster on the Sprinter, installing or inspecting the seat adjustment stop, replacing the seat position switch, recalibrating the passenger seat limiter, and inspecting or replacing the rear seatback guide bushings for the headrests. All repairs are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why do so many Mercedes-Benz seat recalls involve airbags?

Three of these five recalls involve seat position relative to the airbag: a missing adjustment stop, a faulty seat position switch, and a seat limiter that allowed the seat too close to the airbag. Each can affect how the airbag and seat belt protect an occupant in a crash.

Is the Sprinter seat recall about the seat belt or the seat frame?

It's about the seat frame. A weld seam on the front seat height adjuster may be insufficiently welded, which could let the seat cushion shift forward and increase injury risk in a crash.

Which Mercedes-Benz models have the rear headrest recall?

The 2021 C-Class and E-Class cabriolet models, including the C 300, AMG C 43, AMG C 63, AMG C 63 S, E 450, and AMG E 53 cabriolets, may have rear headrests incorrectly mounted to the seatback.
