Dodge Electrical Recalls Cover Dashboard Warnings, Stalling, and Fire Risks

6 NHTSA recalls · Dodge · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 23, 2026

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Dashboard information failures recur across these Dodge electrical recalls. The 2019 Challenger and Charger notices concern instrument clusters that may not properly illuminate warnings; the 2021 Challenger notice concerns insufficiently illuminated safety system indicators. For 2024-2025 Charger electric vehicles, a software error may cause the instrument panel display to fail. Challenger and Charger appear most often among the listed Dodge models. Separate notices address cruise control that may not disengage in 2014-2018 Journey, Charger, and Durango vehicles and 2015-2018 Challengers; voltage regulator chip failures affecting several Dodge models; and improperly tightened battery or high voltage cable connections in 2023-2024 Hornet plug-in hybrids.

Warning signs to watch for

  • May 2018 cruise control recall: The vehicle may maintain speed or accelerate despite attempts to deactivate cruise control.
  • August 2018 stalling recall, U87: The vehicle may stall or fail to start.
  • April 2026 Charger EV display recall, 39D: The instrument panel may fail to display gear selection and warning lights.
  • March 2019 cluster recall, V32: Instrument cluster warnings may not illuminate properly.
  • August 2021 Challenger recall, Y58: Safety system status indicators may be insufficiently illuminated in daylight and nighttime conditions.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

The September 2023 Hornet loose cable recall carries the group's one park-outside instruction: owners are advised to park outside and away from structures until the recall repair is complete. None of the listed notices instructs owners to stop driving. The May 2018 cruise control notice separately advises owners to stop using cruise control. The August 2018 stalling, April 2026 Charger EV display, March 2019 cluster warning, and August 2021 Challenger indicator notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Repairs are free of charge in these notices. Hornet dealers inspect and tighten cable connections as necessary. The May 2018 cruise recall calls for software inspection and an engine or powertrain module flash; U87 requires PCM replacement. Dealers update instrument panel software for 39D, update cluster software for V32, and reprogram clusters for Y58.

Common questions

Which Dodge electrical recall tells owners to park outside?

The September 2023 loose cable recall covers certain 2023-2024 Dodge Hornet plug-in hybrids. Its notice advises parking outside and away from structures until the recall repair is complete. Dealers inspect and tighten cable connections as necessary, free of charge.

Do the Challenger dashboard recalls involve the same warning problem?

The 2019 Challenger notice describes instrument cluster warnings that may not illuminate properly. The 2021 Challenger notice describes safety system status indicators that are insufficiently illuminated in daylight and nighttime conditions. Each notice specifies a free software remedy.

What does the Dodge cruise control recall tell owners to do?

The May 2018 notice advises owners to stop using cruise control. It describes vehicles maintaining speed or accelerating despite attempts to deactivate the system. Dealers inspect the software and flash the engine or powertrain control module, free of charge.
