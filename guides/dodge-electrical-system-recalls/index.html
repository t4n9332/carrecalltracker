Dodge electrical recalls: stalling, cruise control and blank instrument displays

6 NHTSA recalls · Dodge · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Apr 23, 2026

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Displays dominate this group: three recalls concern instrument panel software — the 2019 Challenger, Charger and Chrysler 300 cluster that may not illuminate driver warnings (V32), the 2021 Challenger whose safety system indicators are not illuminated sufficiently day or night (Y58), and the 2024-2025 Charger EV and Jeep Wagoneer S display that may fail from a software error (39D). Two larger 2018 recalls reach across Charger, Challenger, Durango and Journey: a cruise control that may not disengage, remedied by a control module software flash, and a powertrain control module voltage regulator chip that may fail, causing a stall or no start (U87). The 2023-2024 Hornet plug-in hybrid stands apart: cable connections that may not be tightened properly can overheat and result in a fire while parked or driving.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A stall or a no-start condition (2018 powertrain control module recall, U87).
  • Cruise control that cannot be shut off by the brake pedal or the switch, with the vehicle maintaining its speed or accelerating (2018 cruise control recall).
  • Instrument cluster driver warnings that do not illuminate properly (2019 Challenger, Charger and Chrysler 300 recall, V32).
  • Safety system status indicators not sufficiently illuminated in daylight and nighttime driving (2021 Challenger recall, Y58).
  • An instrument panel display that fails to show gear selection and warning lights (2024-2025 Charger EV and Wagoneer S recall, 39D).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

One recall carries a park-outside instruction: the 2023-2024 Hornet plug-in hybrid cable recall, where owners are advised to park outside and away from structures until the recall repair is complete. No notice in this group tells owners to stop driving. The 2018 cruise control notice advises owners to stop using cruise control. For the rest — the 2018 powertrain control module recall (U87) and the cluster and display recalls V32, Y58 and 39D — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Software carries most of these: a flash of the engine or powertrain control module for the 2018 cruise control recall, and cluster or instrument panel reprogramming for V32, Y58 and 39D. Dealers replace the powertrain control module outright for the 2018 stall recall (U87), and inspect and tighten the Hornet's cable connections as necessary. Every notice says free of charge.

Common questions

Why is my Hornet supposed to be parked outside?

The notice for certain 2023-2024 Hornet plug-in hybrids says the 12-volt battery positive cable and/or high voltage connector cable may not be tightened properly, which can overheat connections and result in a fire while parked or driving. Owners are advised to park outside and away from structures until repaired.

Can I still use cruise control in my 2018 Dodge?

The 2018 recall addresses a defect that could prevent the cruise control system from disengaging, and the notice advises owners to stop using cruise control. Dealers inspect the software and perform a software flash on the engine or powertrain control module, free of charge.

What does the Charger EV instrument display recall cover?

Recall 39D covers certain 2024-2025 Dodge Charger and Jeep Wagoneer S electric vehicles, where a software error may cause the instrument panel display to fail, so it does not show gear selection and warning lights. Dealers update the software free; letters were mailed May 14, 2026.
