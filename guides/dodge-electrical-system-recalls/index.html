What electrical problems have caused Dodge recalls: fire, stalling, dashboards?

6 NHTSA recalls · Dodge · Electrical System · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-04-23

⚠ 1 carries a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Six Dodge electrical-system recalls span very different failure points. The most serious, from September 2023, involves the Dodge Hornet plug-in hybrid: a loose 12-volt or high-voltage cable connection that can overheat and start a fire while parked or driving. Two recalls address engine control problems — a 2014-2019 defect across many Dodge, Jeep, Chrysler, and Ram models where a short circuit can prevent cruise control from disengaging, and a separate 2018 defect where a faulty voltage regulator chip in the powertrain control module can cause a stall or no-start condition. The remaining recalls cover dashboard visibility: 2019 Chrysler/Dodge cluster illumination failures, a 2021 Challenger dash-light recall, and a 2024-2025 Wagoneer S/Charger EV instrument display software error.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Warm smell or visible damage near the 12-volt or high-voltage cable connections (2023-2024 Hornet)
  • Cruise control that won't disengage when the brake pedal is pressed or the system is switched off (2014-2019 models)
  • Engine stalling or failing to start (2018 models)
  • Instrument cluster or dashboard warning lights that don't illuminate or aren't visible (2019 Chrysler/Dodge, 2021 Challenger)
  • Instrument panel display that fails to show gear position or warning lights (2024-2025 Wagoneer S/Charger EV)

Is it safe to keep driving?

One recall, the September 2023 Dodge Hornet and Alfa Romeo Tonale loose-cable fire risk, tells owners to park outside and away from structures until the repair is complete. The cruise-control recall separately advises owners to stop using cruise control until dealers perform the fix. For the stalling recall and the three dashboard/display recalls, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and tighten loose cable connections, perform a software flash or replace the powertrain control module, replace the PCM or update software to fix cruise control, and reprogram or update instrument cluster and display software. All repairs are done free of charge.

Common questions

Is the Dodge Hornet fire recall serious?

Yes. It carries a park-outside-and-away-from-structures instruction because a loose 12-volt or high-voltage cable connection can overheat and cause a fire while the vehicle is parked or being driven, until the dealer repair is completed.

Which Dodge models are covered by the cruise control recall?

The 2014-2019 recall spans many Dodge, Jeep, Chrysler, and Ram vehicles, including Journey, Charger, Durango, and Challenger. Owners are advised to stop using cruise control until the dealer performs the software fix or PCM replacement.

Are the dashboard warning light recalls linked to the fire recall?

No. The 2019 Chrysler/Dodge cluster recall, the 2021 Challenger dash-light recall, and the 2024-2025 Wagoneer S/Charger EV display recall are separate software and visibility defects, unrelated to the Hornet's cable-fire recall.
