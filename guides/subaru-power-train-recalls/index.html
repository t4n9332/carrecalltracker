What Subaru transmission and driveshaft recalls should owners know about?

8 NHTSA recalls · Subaru · Power Train · model years 2019–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Nov 9, 2023

Two patterns dominate Subaru's power train recalls. Driveshafts account for three: center support bolts that may loosen on the 2019 Ascent and on the 2023 Ascent, Impreza, Legacy and Outback, plus front driveshafts whose outer race may crack on the 2023-2024 Forester, 2023 WRX and 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza. The rest involve the continuously variable transmission — a hydraulic sensor that may misread pressure on the 2019 Ascent, and Transmission Control Unit programming that may let the clutch engage before the drive chain is clamped on the 2019-2020 Ascent, 2020 Legacy and Outback, and the 2020-2021 Legacy and Outback. A select lever cable nut on the 2021 Impreza and Outback and an inhibitor switch on the 2021 Crosstrek, 2022 Forester and 2021-2023 Legacy and Outback round out the group.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Reverse lights may not illuminate and the rearview camera image may not display — the inhibitor switch recall covering the 2021 Crosstrek, 2022 Forester and 2021-2023 Legacy and Outback.
  • Loss of drive power — named in the 2019 Ascent transmission hydraulic sensor recall and in both Transmission Control Unit drive chain recalls.
  • The gear selector may not function properly — the 2021 Impreza and Outback select lever cable nut recall.
  • Vehicle rollaway when parked without the parking brake engaged — the front driveshaft recall for the 2023-2024 Forester, 2023 WRX and 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the eight notices in this group tells owners to stop driving, and none carries a park-outside instruction. What they do describe is the risk each defect creates: separation of the front end of the driveshaft, a broken front driveshaft that can cause a loss of drive power or a rollaway when parked without the parking brake engaged, a drive chain that may slip and break, a gear selector that may not work properly, and reverse lights and a rearview camera image that may not work. Each notice says these increase the risk of a crash.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the 2019 Ascent's driveshaft assembly and center support brace; on the 2023 models they clean the bolt mounting surfaces and install new bolts. The drive chain recalls involve reprogramming the Transmission Control Unit, inspecting for chain slip and replacing the transmission where damage is found. Dealers also tighten the select lever cable nut, replace front driveshafts, or replace the inhibitor switch. All free of charge.

Common questions

Is my Subaru covered by the drive chain recalls?

The drive chain recalls list the 2019-2020 Ascent, 2020 Legacy and Outback, and the 2020-2021 Legacy and Outback. Checking your VIN with Subaru or NHTSA is the only way to confirm whether your specific vehicle is included.

How do I know if my driveshaft was recalled?

Two recalls cover center support bolts that may loosen: the 2019 Ascent, and the 2023 Ascent, Impreza, Legacy and Outback. A separate recall covers front driveshafts that may crack on the 2023-2024 Forester, 2023 WRX and 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza.

My reverse lights and backup camera stopped working — is that recalled?

The inhibitor switch recall for the 2021 Crosstrek, 2022 Forester and 2021-2023 Legacy and Outback says a failed switch may prevent reverse lights illuminating and the rearview camera image displaying. Dealers replace the inhibitor switch free of charge.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
