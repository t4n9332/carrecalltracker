Why do Ford suspension parts keep coming loose or breaking?

18 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Suspension · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest May 27, 2026

⚠ 2 of these recalls carry a do-not-drive advisory.

Fasteners dominate this group: Ford says front lower control arm fasteners were not tightened properly on 2024-2025 Broncos, rear suspension subframe bolts were loose or missing on 2021 Bronco Sports, rear toe link fasteners were not properly tightened on 2019 Expedition and Lincoln Navigator, and U-bolts holding the rear axle were insufficiently tightened on 2018 E-450 transit bus chassis. Control arms are the other theme — incorrectly installed or repaired ball joints on 2021-2026 Bronco Sport and 2022-2026 Maverick, incorrect front lower control arms on 2021 Bronco Sport, and rear lower control arm bolts that were not heat-treated on 2020 Explorer and Aviator. Bronco Sport, Explorer and Aviator, and F-53 stripped chassis each appear more than once.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A disconnected rear toe link can cause a sudden change in vehicle handling (2019 Expedition and Lincoln Navigator rear toe link fastener recall).
  • A fractured rear toe link will cause a sudden change in vehicle handling (2013-2018 Lincoln MKT, Ford Flex and Taurus toe link recall).
  • Rear axle or driveshaft failure may cause loss of motive power, failure of the parking brake, and the inability to shift into PARK (2020-2021 F-53 and F-59 stripped chassis rear axle lubricant recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

Two of the notices here tell owners not to drive. Ford advises owners of 2024-2025 Bronco vehicles not to drive until the lower control arm fastener repair is done, and owners of 2021-2026 Bronco Sport and 2022-2026 Maverick vehicles not to drive until the front lower control arm ball joint remedy is completed. For the other recalls in this group — including the Expedition and Navigator toe link fasteners, the Flex, Taurus and MKT toe links, the Explorer and Aviator bolts, and the F-53 and F-59 chassis recalls — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Most of these remedies are inspections: dealers check the fastener, bolt, ball joint or U-bolt and tighten, secure or replace it as needed. Some are straight replacements — both rear lower control arm bolts on 2020 Explorer and Aviator, and the rear suspension toe links on Flex, Taurus and MKT. Dealers also replace incorrect parts found in the 2021 Explorer and Aviator rear suspension module. All repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

My Bronco Sport is in one of these recalls — can I keep driving it?

For the 2021-2026 Bronco Sport and 2022-2026 Maverick front lower control arm ball joint recall, Ford advises owners not to drive until the remedy is completed. The 2021 Bronco Sport control arm and rear subframe bolt notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

Which Ford models show up most often in these suspension recalls?

Bronco Sport appears three times: incorrect front lower control arms, loose or missing rear suspension subframe bolts, and the front lower control arm ball joints. Explorer and Lincoln Aviator appear twice, and the F-53 stripped chassis appears in two separate recalls.

What happens if the replacement parts are not available yet?

For the 2013-2018 Lincoln MKT, Ford Flex and Taurus toe link recall, Ford said remedy parts were not available when owners were first notified and that a second notice would be mailed when parts became available. The replacement is free of charge.
