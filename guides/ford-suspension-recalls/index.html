Why do Ford Bronco and Maverick suspensions keep detaching?

18 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Suspension · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-05-27

⚠ 2 of these recalls carry a do-not-drive advisory.

Front suspension detachment is the most serious repeating pattern in this group, spanning Ford's Bronco-based platform: the 2024 Bronco (front lower control arm fasteners) and the 2021-2024 Bronco Sport and 2022-2024 Maverick (front lower control arm ball joints) both carry stop-driving orders. Elsewhere, bolts and fasteners loosen or break on rear suspension components — toe links on the 2018 Flex, Taurus and MKT, rear suspension bolts on the 2021 Bronco Sport and 2020 Explorer/Aviator, and stabilizer brackets on the 2020-2021 F-53. Wrong parts were installed on the 2021 Bronco Sport and 2021 Explorer/Aviator, and axle lubricant or U-bolt problems affect the 2020-2021 F-53/F-59 and 2018 E-450 bus chassis.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A sudden change in how the vehicle handles or steers, from a fractured rear toe link or loosened rear suspension fasteners
  • Reduced braking, or being unable to shift into Park, from inadequate rear axle lubricant (F-53/F-59)
  • A ball joint stud that breaks and affects front wheel control (aftermarket F-150 recall)
  • Most recalls in this group, including both front control arm detachment recalls, describe no noticeable warning before the part separates

Is it safe to keep driving?

Two recalls tell owners not to drive until repaired: the 2024 Bronco front control arm recall, and the 2021-2024 Bronco Sport/2022-2024 Maverick front suspension recall, both involving parts that can detach and cause loss of vehicle control. None of these recalls carry a park-outside instruction. For the other recalls in this group — covering the E-450, Expedition, Navigator, Flex, Taurus, MKT, 2021 Bronco Sport, Explorer, Aviator, F-53, F-59, and the F-150 aftermarket ball joint — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and, as necessary, tighten or replace the affected hardware free of charge: control arm fasteners, ball joints, rear toe links, suspension bolts, stabilizer brackets, and axle U-bolts. Some recalls also replace incorrectly installed control arms or rear suspension module parts, or correct rear axle lubricant and wheel bearings.

Common questions

Why are the Bronco, Bronco Sport, and Maverick all recalled for suspension detachment?

Ford issued two separate recalls: the 2024 Bronco for front lower control arm fasteners that may not be tightened properly, and the 2021-2024 Bronco Sport and 2022-2024 Maverick for front lower control arm ball joints that may be installed incorrectly. Both can let the control arm disconnect.

What does it mean if Ford tells me not to drive my Bronco Sport or Maverick?

It means the front lower control arm ball joint may already be disconnecting, or about to disconnect, from the wheel knuckle, which can cause loss of vehicle control. Ford advises against driving until a dealer inspects and repairs it, free of charge.

Are any of these suspension recalls for aftermarket parts?

Yes, two. Tenneco/DRiV recalled its MOOG ball joint sold for aftermarket installation on 2015-2018 Ford F-150 trucks, and MORryde recalled U-bolts on 2018 Ford E-450 bus chassis conversions — both aftermarket parts, not original Ford components.
