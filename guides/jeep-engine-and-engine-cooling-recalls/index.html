Why Do Some Jeep Engines Stall, Overheat, or Lose Power?

6 NHTSA recalls · Jeep · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2024-12-12

Six NHTSA recalls affecting Jeep engine and cooling systems point to two recurring failure modes: stalling and power loss. The Grand Cherokee and Cherokee both have separate engine defects — a cracked EGR cooler that can let coolant combust inside the intake manifold on 2018-2019 Grand Cherokee diesels, and an EGR valve that can stick open on 2021 Grand Cherokee and Dodge Durango models with 3.6L engines, cutting drive power unexpectedly. Cherokee and Compass share a 2018-model-year oil pump defect that can stall the engine, while a separate 2019 Cherokee recall covers missing valve stem keepers and a reversed camshaft cap. Diesel Grand Cherokees and Ram 1500 trucks from 2018-2020 share a crankshaft position sensor defect, and 2024 Grand Cherokees have a separate radiator crossmember bolt issue affecting crash safety.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Sudden loss of engine power while driving, in vehicles with the EGR valve defect
  • Engine stalling caused by oil pump, camshaft, or crankshaft sensor failures
  • The notices do not describe any warning sign before the EGR cooler crack or loose radiator crossmember bolts occur

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices for the EGR cooler crack, the EGR valve sticking open, the loose radiator crossmember bolts, the oil pump and camshaft/valve defects, and the crankshaft position sensor issue all describe risks — fire, power loss, air bag or fuel-system failure in a crash, and stalling — but none tell owners to stop driving or park outside while waiting for repair. The notices simply state the defect and the dealer remedy.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace or inspect the affected part free of charge: the EGR cooler and intake manifold, the EGR valve assembly, the radiator crossmember bolts, the oil pump, or the valve and camshaft components. The crankshaft position sensor recall is instead addressed with a free powertrain control module software update rather than a part replacement.

Common questions

Which Jeep Grand Cherokee years are affected by the EGR cooler recall?

The EGR cooler cracking recall covers 2018 and 2019 Jeep Grand Cherokee vehicles built with the 3.0L EcoDiesel engine. The remedy replaces the EGR cooler and, if necessary, the intake manifold, free of charge.

Does the EGR valve recall affect the Dodge Durango too?

Yes. The 2021 EGR valve recall covers both the 2021 Dodge Durango and 2021 Jeep Grand Cherokee with 3.6L engines, where the valve can stick open and cause a sudden loss of drive power.

What caused the 2024 Grand Cherokee radiator crossmember recall?

FCA found the radiator crossmember bolts on some 2024 Grand Cherokee and Grand Cherokee L vehicles may not be properly secured, which can affect air bag deployment and cause a fuel leak in a crash.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
