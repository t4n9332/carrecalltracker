Why do Nissan and Infiniti backup cameras keep getting recalled?

9 NHTSA recalls · Nissan · Back Over Prevention · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-11-26

All nine recalls here concern the rear image a driver sees when backing up, and most cite Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 111, "Rear Visibility." Three patterns repeat. Infotainment or display software faults leave the screen blank or the camera inoperative on 2021-2022 Rogue, 2022 Frontier, Titan, Titan XD and Pathfinder, on 2024-2025 Rogue and 2025 Infiniti QX80, on 2025 Frontier and Kicks, and on 2025 Murano with 2025-2026 Armada and Infiniti QX80. Camera harness damage distorts or removes the image on 2019-2021 Altima, 2020-2021 Sentra and 2018-2022 LEAF. The oldest recall, covering many 2018-2019 Nissan and Infiniti models, involves display settings that can hide the rear image, and a separate 2020 LEAF recall stands alone.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2020 LEAF recall states the back-up camera images can disappear while the vehicle is in reverse.
  • The 2024 Rogue and Infiniti QX80 recall and the 2025 Frontier and Kicks recall both state the display may be blank when the vehicle is shifted into reverse.
  • The 2023 Altima and Sentra recall and the 2024 LEAF recall state that harness damage can cause distortion or loss of the rearview camera image.
  • The July 2022 Rogue, Frontier, Pathfinder and Titan recall states the infotainment head unit may reboot; the October 2022 recall states the infotainment system may continuously reboot when the vehicle is restarted.
  • The 2019 Nissan and Infiniti recall states the settings can be adjusted so the rear view image is no longer visible, and the system retains that setting the next time the vehicle is placed in reverse.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of the nine notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park the vehicle outside. Each states the same consequence: without a rear image, the driver's view behind the vehicle is reduced and the risk of a crash increases. No notice gives an interim instruction, such as relying on mirrors or avoiding reverse, while waiting for the remedy. Four notices describe a remedy that can be delivered by a dealer or through an over-the-air update, and all direct owners to Nissan or Infiniti customer service.

What the free repair involves

Most of these recalls are software work: dealers update the back-up camera, center display or infotainment software, and in four notices that update can also arrive over the air. Two are hardware: dealers inspect the rearview camera and harness for damage and replace them as necessary, and if no damage is found they apply protective tape and reroute the harness. Every notice states the work is free of charge.

Common questions

My camera has always worked. Is my Nissan still included?

Recalls apply by VIN, not by whether an owner has seen the problem. The notices say dealers will perform the remedy free of charge and describe no condition under which an owner should skip it. Nissan customer service is 800-647-7261; Infiniti's is 1-800-662-6200.

Can the fix be done over the air instead of at a dealer?

Four notices say the software will be updated by a dealer or through an over-the-air update, free of charge: the two 2022 infotainment recalls, the 2024-2025 Rogue and Infiniti QX80 recall, and the 2025 Murano, Armada and Infiniti QX80 recall.

Why does the LEAF show up in more than one of these recalls?

LEAF appears in three notices for different reasons: the 2019 display-settings recall lists 2018-2019 models including LEAF, the 2020 recall covers 2020 LEAF vehicles where the image can disappear in reverse, and the 2024 recall covers 2018-2022 LEAF with camera harness damage.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
