Which Ford seat recalls involve seats that move or unlock?

21 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Seats · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jul 21, 2026

Two threads run through Ford's seat recalls. The larger one involves vans: converters including Quality Van, National Van, Waldoch, Royale and Mobility Transportation recalled 2014-2018 Transit, E-350 and Econoline vehicles fitted with Freedman GO-ES foldaway seats, whose back cushion may not stay locked when loaded. The rest come from Ford and cluster on trucks and SUVs: 2018 Expedition and Lincoln Navigator second-row seats, manual recliner mechanisms missing a third pawl on 2018-2020 F-150, 2019-2020 Super Duty, 2018-2019 Explorer, 2019-2020 Expedition, 2020 Escape and 2020 Aviator, the easy-entry second-row switch on Explorer and Aviator in both 2025 and 2026, loose front seat pivot bolts on 2021-2023 Bronco, and child seat anchorage on 2020-2021 Ranger.

Warning signs to watch for

  • 2020-2025 Explorer and Aviator (25S67): the easy-entry second-row outer seat switch may bind or stick, and the seats may unlatch, fold or slide unexpectedly while driving.
  • 2020-2026 Explorer and 2020-2027 Aviator (the 2026 recall): the same switch may bind or stick, with the seats unlatching, tipping or sliding unexpectedly.
  • 2018-2020 F-150, 2019-2020 Super Duty, 2018-2019 Explorer, 2019-2020 Expedition, 2020 Escape and 2020 Aviator (19C07): a recliner mechanism missing the third pawl results in a loose seat back.
  • 2020-2021 Ranger Super Cab: the fixed rear head restraints may interfere with installing certain child seats, and the child seat may not attach properly to the lower-rear anchors.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. Several describe letters sent before a repair was ready: interim letters for the 2020-2021 Ranger were mailed November 5, 2021, an interim notification for the 2018 Expedition and Navigator center seat went out December 21, 2018, and interim letters for the Explorer and Aviator second-row switch were mailed August 19, 2026, with that remedy arriving in phases by model and model year. For the converted vans, the notices direct owners to the company that modified the vehicle.

What the free repair involves

For the Freedman foldaway seats, dealers inspect and repair the seat. Ford dealers inspect recliner mechanisms and replace seats as needed, replace missing J-channel brackets or the center seat frame, replace Bronco pivot links and bolts, replace the second-row switch bezel on Explorer and Aviator, and fit Ranger removable head restraints. Every notice here that mentions cost says the work is free of charge.

Common questions

My Transit was converted by another company — who repairs the seats?

The foldaway seat recalls were filed by the converters — Quality Van, National Van, Waldoch, Royale and Mobility Transportation — and each notice lists that company's own customer service number for owners to arrange the seat inspection and repair.

Is the Explorer and Aviator second-row seat repair available now?

The 2026 notice says this is a phased campaign, with the remedy becoming available in different phases based on model and model year. Interim letters explaining the safety risk were mailed August 19, 2026, and additional letters will follow.

Can I still fit a child seat in a recalled Ranger?

The notice gives owners no interim instruction. It says the fixed head restraints may interfere with installing certain child seats and the child seat may not attach properly to the lower-rear anchors; dealers adjust the anchorages and fit removable head restraints.
