BMW engine and cooling recalls: what four notices tell owners

4 NHTSA recalls · BMW · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Aug 13, 2024

Across these four BMW recalls there is no single repeating defect, but two themes appear twice each: fire risk and lost power. Fire risk comes from the 2023 X7 M60i and XM, whose exhaust-to-catalytic-converter fasteners may not have been tightened correctly, and from the water pump recall, where an improperly sealed electrical connector may be exposed to water and short circuit on models including 2012-2015 X1, 2012-2016 Z4, 528i and 328i, 2013-2017 X3, 2015-2018 X4 and 2016-2018 X5 xDrive40e. Lost power covers the crankshaft sensor firmware recall, which began with 2018 540d xDrive and was expanded to 2018-2019 BMW models, and a single 2019 F850GS motorcycle with improperly installed connecting rods.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A vehicle stall (2018-2019 crankshaft sensor recall).
  • A loss of engine power (2019 F850GS connecting rod recall).

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. The exhaust fastener, crankshaft sensor and water pump notices each state the risk — fire, a stall, an electrical short — without any instruction to stop driving or to limit use in the meantime. The F850GS recall covers one motorcycle that the notice says has not yet been sold, with the dealer replacing the engine before sale. Beyond that, the notices say nothing about what owners should do while waiting for the repair.

What the free repair involves

Repairs differ by recall and each notice says the work is free. Dealers replace the gasket between the exhaust system and the catalytic converter and tighten the fasteners on the X7 M60i and XM; replace the crankshaft sensor; inspect and replace the water pump and plug connector as necessary and install a protective shield; and, on the F850GS, replace the engine. None of these notices describes an over-the-air update.

Common questions

Which BMW models does the water pump short circuit recall cover?

The notice lists 2012-2015 X1 sDrive28i and xDrive28i, 2012-2016 Z4 sDrive28i, 528i, 528i xDrive, 328i and 328xi, 2016-2018 X5 xDrive40e, 2014-2016 228i, 228xi, 428i, 428i xDrive and 328xi Gran Turismo, 2013-2017 X3 sDrive28i and xDrive28i, 2015-2018 X4 xDrive28i, and 2015-2016 428xi.

The crankshaft sensor recall mentions stalling — what does the notice actually say?

It says the crankshaft sensor may have incorrect firmware, preventing it from properly processing input from the crankshaft reluctor ring, possibly resulting in a stall, and that a stall increases crash risk. It does not tell owners to stop driving.

Will I pay anything for these engine repairs?

Each of these four notices states the repair is performed free of charge, including the gasket and fastener work, the crankshaft sensor replacement, the water pump inspection and shield, and the F850GS engine replacement. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417.
