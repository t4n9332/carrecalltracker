Why do Ford equipment recalls cover heaters, cameras and labels?

15 NHTSA recalls · Ford · Equipment · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2026-01-15

The largest recurring issue in this group is an engine block heater that cracks, leaks coolant, and can short-circuit when plugged in — this spans three 2025-2026 recalls covering a wide range of Ford and Lincoln models. A separate pattern covers five 2019 recalls for ASA Electronics Voyager back-up camera monitors on Ford-based shuttle buses, ambulances, and cargo vans (E-350, E-450, F-450, F-550, Econoline) that can revert to factory default settings and show a reversed image. The remaining recalls involve incorrect or missing labels rather than a mechanical defect: an EcoSport brake fluid cap with the wrong pictogram, a Bronco/Ranger tire label overstating load capacity, incorrect F-350 weight labels, and Super Duty/E-Series labels missing tire size and pressure information.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Back-up camera shows a mirror-reversed image when backing up (five 2019 camera monitor recalls)
  • Tire, weight or brake fluid label on the vehicle shows information that doesn't match the vehicle (EcoSport, Bronco, Ranger, F-350, Super Duty and E-Series label recalls)
  • The block heater recalls describe no driver-noticeable warning sign before a short circuit occurs when the heater is plugged in

Is it safe to keep driving?

No recall in this group carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The three engine block heater recalls (2025-10-10, and two dated 2026-01-15) add an interim instruction of their own: owners are advised not to plug in the block heater until the vehicle is repaired, with an alternative option to remove the cord and install a blanking plug. For every other recall in this group — the back-up camera and labeling recalls — the notices do not tell owners to stop driving.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the block heater (or install a blanking plug and remove the cord as an alternative), replace or reprogram the back-up camera monitor or add a harness relay capacitor to stop the reversed image, and replace mislabeled brake fluid caps, tire labels, and weight or safety certification labels. All of this is done free of charge.

Common questions

Why is my block heater cord being recalled instead of the whole vehicle?

The defect is specific to the engine block heater: it can crack, leak coolant, and short-circuit when plugged in, raising fire risk. Because the cause is isolated to that part, dealers replace or disable just the heater rather than a broader vehicle system.

Can I still use my block heater while I wait for the repair?

No. The notices for the block heater recalls advise owners not to plug in the block heater until the vehicle has been repaired, because plugging it in is what can cause the short circuit and fire risk described.

Is fixing a reversed back-up camera image free?

Yes. In each of the five camera monitor recalls in this group, the manufacturer or upfitter replaces or corrects the monitor at no cost to the owner, whether by adding a relay capacitor module or replacing the display.
