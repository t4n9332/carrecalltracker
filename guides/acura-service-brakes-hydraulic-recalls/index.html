Why does Acura keep recalling vehicles for brake problems?

6 NHTSA recalls · Acura · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-12-11

Acura has had six separate hydraulic brake recalls since 2018, but each targets a different part of the brake system rather than one repeating defect. The MDX is the most frequently named model, appearing in the 2018 caliper-coating recall, the 2023 brake booster tie-rod recall, and the 2025 brake pedal pivot pin recall. The TLX and Integra were added in the pedal pin and VSA modulator recalls, respectively, while the ILX (2018-2020) had its own 2025 recall for contaminated brake fluid, and the ZDX (2024) had a software-related ABS recall fixed over the air. Several of these recalls, including the 2018, 2023, and 2025 pedal pin recalls, also covered shared Honda models like the HR-V, Odyssey, Accord, Civic, Passport, Ridgeline, and Pilot.

Warning signs to watch for

  • Reduced braking performance or longer stopping distances (2018 caliper and 2025 ILX brake fluid recalls)
  • Unintended vehicle movement or a brake pedal that travels farther than normal (2023 VSA modulator recall)
  • Loss of brake function if the master cylinder separates from the booster (2023 tie-rod recall)
  • Unexpected activation of the anti-lock brakes that releases brake pressure (2024 ZDX recall)
  • A brake pedal that shifts out of position and may not respond as expected (2025 pivot pin recall)

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The notices for the 2018 caliper, 2023 VSA modulator, 2023 brake booster tie-rod, 2024 ZDX ABS software, 2025 brake pedal pivot pin, and 2025 ILX brake fluid recalls all describe increased crash risk but do not tell owners to stop driving or to park away from structures. Owners are directed to schedule the free repair through their dealer, including an over-the-air software option for the ZDX.

What the free repair involves

Dealers address each defect differently: bleeding the brakes (2018), replacing the VSA modulator (2023), inspecting and repairing the brake booster assembly (2023), updating ABS software over the air or at a dealer (2024 ZDX), inspecting and replacing the brake pedal assembly (2025), and replacing the brake master cylinder (2025 ILX). All repairs are free of charge.

Common questions

Does one part keep failing across these Acura brake recalls?

No. Each of the six recalls involves a different component: calipers, the VSA modulator, the brake booster tie rod, ABS software, the brake pedal pivot pin, and the master cylinder, spanning 2018 through 2025.

Is my Acura MDX covered by all of these recalls?

No. The MDX appears in three of the six recalls (2018, 2023 tie-rod, and 2025 pedal pin); the other three affect different models like the TLX, ILX, RDX, Integra, and ZDX.

Can the ZDX brake recall be fixed without a dealer visit?

Yes. Honda says the 2024 Acura ZDX's electronic brake control module software can be updated over the air, or by a dealer, free of charge.
