Kia brake recalls: fire risk, master cylinders and lost power assist

4 NHTSA recalls · Kia · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 7, 2023

⚠ 2 carry a park-outside (fire risk) advisory.

Two of these four recalls are fire recalls tied to the Anti-lock Brake Hydraulic Electronic Control Unit. The August 2020 Stinger recall began with certain 2019 Stinger vehicles with 3.3L T-GDI engines and, on December 30, 2020, added certain 2018-2021 Stinger vehicles, for an engine compartment fire in the area where the HECU is located. The March 2021 recall covers certain 2017-2021 Sportage and Cadenza vehicles, where the HECU electrical circuit may short-circuit and cause an engine compartment fire. The other two concern braking performance: 2020-2021 Rio, where the brake master cylinder may have sharp edges that damage the inner oil seal, and 2023 Sportage, where the brake booster diaphragm may become misaligned. Sportage appears twice.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The 2020-2021 Rio recall (SC223) says reduced braking performance can cause longer brake pedal travel or extend the distance required to stop.
  • The 2023 Sportage recall (SC272) says an internal vacuum leak in the brake booster can result in a loss of power brake assist, extending the distance required to stop.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

Two recalls here carry an interim instruction. The Stinger HECU fire recall advises owners to park outside and away from structures as a precaution until the recall repair is complete, and the Sportage and Cadenza HECU fire recall advises the same. Neither tells owners to stop driving. For the 2020-2021 Rio brake master cylinder recall and the 2023 Sportage brake booster recall, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving; both describe a longer stopping distance as the risk the defect creates.

What the free repair involves

Each remedy is free of charge. For the Stinger, dealers install a new fuse kit in the electrical junction box. For the Sportage and Cadenza recall, dealers replace certain fuses in the electrical junction box, and vehicles with an electronic parking brake also receive HECU software work. For the 2020-2021 Rio, dealers replace the brake master cylinder, and for the 2023 Sportage, dealers inspect and replace the brake booster as necessary.

Common questions

Why would a brake control unit cause an engine compartment fire?

The Stinger notice says that while driving, an engine compartment fire may occur in the area where the Anti-lock Brake Hydraulic Electronic Control Unit is located. The Sportage and Cadenza notice says the HECU electrical circuit may short-circuit, which can cause an engine compartment fire.

My Stinger is a 2018. Was it in the original recall?

The notice says Kia recalled certain 2019 Stinger vehicles with 3.3L T-GDI engines on August 27, 2020, then on December 30, 2020 expanded the population to add certain 2018-2021 Stinger vehicles. Check your VIN with Kia at 1-800-333-4542.

What do the Rio and Sportage notices say about how the brakes behave?

The Rio notice says reduced braking performance can cause longer brake pedal travel or extend the distance required to stop. The 2023 Sportage notice says a loss of power brake assist can extend the distance required to stop. Neither notice tells owners to stop driving.
