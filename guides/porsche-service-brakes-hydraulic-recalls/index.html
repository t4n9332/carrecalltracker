Porsche brake recalls: Taycan hoses, brake booster rods and corroded lines

4 NHTSA recalls · Porsche · Service Brakes, Hydraulic · model years 2018–2024 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Jun 19, 2024

These four Porsche brake recalls center on the Taycan, which appears in three of them. Front brake hoses on 2020-2025 Taycans can crack and leak brake fluid; 2020-2022 Taycans display a brake pad wear warning in the wrong color in the instrument cluster; and on 2020-2021 Taycans and 911 Carreras the pressure input rod connection at the brake booster may be loose and could detach. The fourth recall covers 2018 Panamera hybrid models, including Sport Turismo and Executive versions: the front axle brake lines may corrode over time, which the notice says can affect front braking performance.

Warning signs to watch for

  • In the 2020-2022 Taycan recall (APA5), the instrument cluster displays the worn brake pad warning indicator in an incorrect color.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these four notices tells owners to stop driving or to park outside. Each states a crash risk instead: reduced front braking from a corroded brake line on the 2018 Panamera hybrids, brake pedal failure if the booster pressure input rod detaches on 2020-2021 911 Carrera and Taycan models, misreading the wrong-colored pad wear indicator on 2020-2022 Taycans, and longer stopping distances if a front brake hose leaks on 2020-2025 Taycans. No notice here gives any interim instruction.

What the free repair involves

All four repairs are free of charge. Dealers replace the front brake line and any damaged brake components on the 2018 Panamera hybrids, repair or replace the brake booster as necessary on the 911 Carrera and Taycan, update the instrument cluster software on 2020-2022 Taycans, and replace the front brake hoses on the 2020-2025 Taycan.

Common questions

Why does my Taycan show an odd brake pad warning?

One recall covers 2020-2022 Taycans where the instrument cluster displays the worn brake pad warning indicator in an incorrect color, which the notice says could be misinterpreted. The remedy is an instrument cluster software update performed free of charge.

Are all Porsche models affected by these brake recalls?

These four recalls name specific models: 2018 Panamera hybrids, 2020-2021 911 Carrera, and Taycans in the 2020-2021, 2020-2022 and 2020-2025 ranges. Porsche's owner notification letters and a VIN check identify whether a particular vehicle is included.

What do the notices say about driving before the repair?

None of the four tells owners to stop driving or park outside. They describe the consequences each defect can have on braking and the crash risk that follows, and they direct owners to have the free dealer repair performed.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
