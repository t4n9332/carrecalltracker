Why does the VW Atlas have so many separate air bag recalls?

16 NHTSA recalls · Volkswagen · AIR Bags · model years 2018–2025 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2025-12-10

Across these Volkswagen air bag recalls, the Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport appear more often than any other model, with separate 2018-2023 recalls for a passenger air bag that could tear, water intrusion into the air bag control module, an incorrect side air bag, corroded door wiring that could delay side air bag deployment, a passenger occupant sensor that could deactivate the air bag, and a missing weld on a side air bag housing. Tiguan, Passat, Jetta, and ID.4 each had one or two separate air bag recalls of their own, for issues ranging from incorrect parts installed at the factory to software and folding defects. A 2024 recall also covers Beetle and older Passat driver air bag inflators that could explode from long-term propellant degradation.

Warning signs to watch for

  • No warning sign is described for most of these recalls — the notices say the defects would only become apparent in a crash, when the air bag fails to deploy, deploys incorrectly, or deploys with too much force
  • The 2018 Atlas air bag control module water damage recall warns the air bag could deploy unintentionally, without a crash
  • The 2021 Jetta air bag module recall also warns of possible unintentional deployment

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these Volkswagen air bag recalls carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction, including the two that warn an air bag could deploy unintentionally without a crash — the 2018 Atlas control module recall and the 2021 Jetta module recall. For the rest, covering incorrect or incorrectly folded air bags, tearing, delayed deployment, deactivation, and a driver inflator that could explode, the notices likewise do not tell owners to stop driving. Each notice instead directs owners to schedule a dealer repair.

What the free repair involves

Dealers replace the specific defective part for each recall — the air bag module, control module, sensor, side air bag, wiring harness, or driver inflator — or in some cases replace the steering wheel and air bag together. All of these repairs, across every Volkswagen air bag recall in this group, are performed free of charge.

Common questions

Why does the VW Atlas keep coming up in air bag recalls?

Volkswagen issued several separate Atlas and Atlas Cross Sport air bag recalls between 2018 and 2023, each for a different defect — a tearing passenger bag, water damage to the control module, wiring corrosion, a faulty sensor, and more — rather than one repeated problem.

Could my VW's air bag go off on its own while I'm driving?

Only two recalls in this group describe that risk: the 2018 Atlas control module recall and the 2021 Jetta module recall, both linked to unintentional deployment. The other recalls involve air bags that may not work correctly during an actual crash.

Is the Beetle inflator recall the same as older Takata recalls?

The 2024 notice attributes the Beetle and older Passat driver inflator recall to propellant degradation from long-term heat and humidity exposure, the same general mechanism as earlier Takata-related campaigns, and covers a separate set of vehicles from the other air bag recalls here.
