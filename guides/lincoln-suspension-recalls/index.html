Why does Lincoln keep recalling rear suspension components on SUVs?

6 NHTSA recalls · Lincoln · Suspension · model years 2018–2023 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest 2023-06-23

This group covers six separate rear-suspension recalls, each with its own cause. The 2019 Navigator (shared with the Expedition) had loosely tightened rear toe link fasteners. The 2018 MKT (shared with the Flex and Taurus Police Interceptor/SHO models) had toe links that could fracture under stress. The 2020 Corsair had rear coil springs that could break from contact with a toe link bracket. The 2020 Aviator and Explorer had rear control arm bolts that were not heat-treated and could break. The 2021 Aviator and Explorer instead received mislabeled suspension module parts. The 2023 Nautilus had incorrectly built rear shock absorbers that could damage nearby brake and axle components.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A sudden change in how the vehicle handles
  • A loss of steering control
  • Changes to ride height, braking, or handling from mismatched suspension parts
  • Longer stopping distances from a damaged brake hose
  • The notices do not describe any warning signs before these parts fail

Is it safe to keep driving?

None of these six recalls carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. For all of them, the notices do not tell owners to stop driving in the meantime. That covers the toe link fastener recall on the 2019 Navigator, the toe link fracture recall on the 2018 MKT, the coil spring recall on the 2020 Corsair, the control arm bolt recall on the 2020 Aviator, the mislabeled suspension parts recall on the 2021 Aviator, and the shock absorber recall on the 2023 Nautilus.

What the free repair involves

Dealers inspect and repair the specific rear suspension hardware for each recall: tightening or replacing toe link fasteners and toe links, checking clearance and installing new coil springs, replacing control arm bolts, swapping mislabeled suspension parts, and replacing shock absorbers along with any damaged brake hoses or axle components. All of this work is done free of charge.

Common questions

Why did my Lincoln get a rear suspension recall?

This group covers six different hardware defects, not one repeated problem: loose or fracturing toe links, breakable coil springs, un-heat-treated control arm bolts, mislabeled parts, or defective shock absorbers, depending on the model and year.

Is it safe to keep driving with an open suspension recall?

None of these six recalls tell owners to stop driving, so the notices don't require parking the vehicle. Contact your dealer to schedule the free repair for your specific recall as soon as possible.

How do I know which suspension recall applies to my Lincoln?

Each recall applies to specific model years, such as the 2019 Navigator, 2020 Corsair, or 2023 Nautilus. Check your VIN with NHTSA or your dealer to confirm which recall, if any, applies to your vehicle.

A recall applies only to specific vehicles. Check your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
