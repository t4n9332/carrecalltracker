Which Toyota engines are recalled for stalling and power loss?

7 NHTSA recalls · Toyota · Engine And Engine Cooling · model years 2018–2026 (we track 2018 and newer) · newest Oct 7, 2026

Seven Toyota engine recalls cluster around three causes. Three cover the V35A engine in the Tundra, where debris from the manufacturing process may contaminate the engine and cause a main bearing to fail: 2022-2023 Tundra with the Lexus LX600, 2022-2024 Tundra with Lexus LX and the 2024 Lexus GX, and 2024 Tundra on its own. Two involve the 2.5L four-cylinder: porosity in the engine castings on 2020 Camry, Camry Hybrid, Avalon Hybrid, Lexus ES300h and 2019-2020 RAV4 and RAV4 Hybrid, and oversized pistons on the 2018 Camry. The last two are control-unit software: the 2022 Prius and Prius Prime ECU may falsely detect an error and shut down the hybrid system, and the 2026 RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid motor generator ECU may reduce power or shut down at higher speeds.

Warning signs to watch for

  • The coolant leak recall (20TA04) says a coolant leak can cause the engine to overheat and cause a vehicle stall in conventional gasoline vehicles, and that the engine may be damaged mechanically.
  • The 2018 Camry recall says pistons larger than specified may cause the engine to stall.
  • The 2026 RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid recall (26TA22) says the plug-in hybrid system may reduce power or shut down when driven at higher speeds.
  • The Tundra V35A recalls say main bearing failure can result in an engine stall and loss of drive power.
  • The 2022 Prius and Prius Prime recall (21TB07/21TA07) says the ECU software may shut down the hybrid system, causing a loss of drive power.

Can you keep driving? What the notices say

None of these seven notices tells owners to stop driving, and none tells owners to park outside. For the 2024 Tundra main bearing recall announced in May 2026, Toyota mailed interim letters notifying owners of the safety risk, with additional letters to follow. The remaining notices describe the risk and the repair only. The coolant leak notice covering 2020 Camry and 2019-2020 RAV4 models says a leak can cause the engine to overheat and stall, and that engine damage may increase the risk of fire.

What the free repair involves

For the coolant leak recall, dealers inspect and replace the engine, including the engine block, as necessary. For the 2018 Camry, dealers check piston date codes and replace the engine assembly if affected. The 2022-2023 Tundra recall replaces the engine assembly; the later Tundra recalls inspect the #1 main bearing and replace the engine if necessary. The Prius and RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid recalls update ECU software. All free of charge.

Common questions

My Tundra appears in more than one engine recall — what is the repair?

Three notices cover V35A main bearing failure. The 2022-2023 Tundra recall says dealers replace the engine assembly. The 2022-2024 and 2024 Tundra notices say dealers inspect the #1 main bearing using vehicle inspection software and drive data, then replace the engine if necessary, free of charge.

Are the Prius and RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid software fixes done over the air?

The 2022 Prius and Prius Prime notice says dealers update the ECU software, free of charge, and letters were mailed November 18, 2021. The 2026 RAV4 Plug-In Hybrid notice also calls for a dealer ECU software update, free of charge.

Which models and engines does the coolant leak recall cover?

The notice covers 2020 Avalon Hybrid, Camry, Camry Hybrid and Lexus ES300h, plus 2019-2020 RAV4 and RAV4 Hybrid with 2.5L four-cylinder engines. It says porosity in the engine castings may result in cracks that let coolant leak internally and/or externally.
