Acura Integra recalls

4 recalls covering model years 2023, 2024, 2025. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Honda and Acura models: driver's seat may be loose high

Reported 2026-01-29 · Campaign 26V054000 · Model years: 2024

The fasteners holding the driver's seat cushion frame may not have been tightened enough at the factory, so the driver's seat can be loose. A seat that is not solidly attached may not hold the driver in place in a crash, which means a higher chance of injury. The vehicles also do not meet the federal safety rule for seating systems. Dealers will replace the driver's seat cushion frame at no charge.

Honda and Acura Driver's Seat May Be Loose high

Reported 2024-11-14 · Campaign 24V859000 · Model years: 2024

On certain Honda Accord, Accord Hybrid, Civic Sedan, Civic Hatchback, Pilot and HR-V vehicles and Acura Integra models, the driver's seat cushion frame may not have been tightened properly when the car was built. That can leave the driver's seat not firmly secured to the floor. These vehicles do not meet the federal safety standard for seating systems. Dealers will replace the driver's seat cushion frame at no cost to the owner.

Honda and Acura steering gearbox may make steering difficult high

Reported 2024-10-03 · Campaign 24V744000 · Model years: 2023, 2024, 2025

The steering gearbox — the part that turns your steering wheel motion into the wheels turning — may have been built wrong on these Honda and Acura models. Parts inside can rub too hard against each other, which can make the vehicle difficult to steer. Difficulty steering raises the chance of a crash. The dealer will replace a spring inside the gear and add or move grease around as needed, all free of charge.

Acura RDX and Integra brake unit can leak fluid high

Reported 2023-06-15 · Campaign 23V430000 · Model years: 2023

These cars have a part that helps control braking and stability. A small valve inside it can leak brake fluid. When that happens, the car may creep or roll on its own while the automatic brake hold feature is holding it still, or the brake pedal may sink farther down than normal before the brakes bite. Either one makes a crash or injury more likely. Dealers will replace the whole unit at no cost.
