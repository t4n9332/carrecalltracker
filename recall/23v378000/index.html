Lincoln MKC battery sensor can catch fire under hood

NHTSA campaign 23V378000 · Reported 2023-05-26 · Ford Motor Company · critical severity what's this?

⚠ Park outside, away from buildings — fire risk. See all such recalls.

Affected vehicles

  • 2018 Lincoln MKC
  • 2019 Lincoln MKC

What's wrong, in plain English

A small sensor that watches your battery can short out and get very hot. When that happens, it can start a fire in the engine compartment — while you are driving or while the vehicle is parked and switched off. Ford is telling owners to park outside and away from buildings until the repair is done. A dealer will add a fuse to that sensor's power wiring. Owner letters were mailed August 8, 2023.

How serious is it?

Serious — the notice does not say to stop driving, but a fire can start even when parked, so park outside and away from structures until it is fixed.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and park outside away from your house or garage in the meantime.
2. Contact your Lincoln dealer or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332 and mention recall 23S28.
3. The dealer installs an in-line fuse on the battery sensor circuit free of charge.

Official notice

Component
ENGINE
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2015-2019 Lincoln MKC vehicles. The battery monitor sensor may short-circuit and overheat, causing a fire in the engine compartment while parked or driving.
Safety risk
An engine compartment fire while parked or driving can increase the risk of injury.
Remedy
Owners are advised to park outside and away from structures until the recall repair is complete. Dealers will add an in-line fuse to the battery monitor sensor power circuit, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed August 8, 2023. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 23S28.
