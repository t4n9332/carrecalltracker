Audi A6 and A7 may have mislabeled brake fluid cap

NHTSA campaign 23V601000 · Reported 2023-08-25 · Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2019 Audi A6
  • 2019 Audi A7
  • 2020 Audi A6
  • 2020 Audi A7
  • 2021 Audi A6
  • 2021 Audi A6 Allroad
  • 2021 Audi A7
  • 2021 Audi S7

What's wrong, in plain English

Some of these cars left the factory with the wrong label on the brake fluid reservoir cap. That cap tells whoever tops up the brakes which fluid to use, so a wrong label can lead a repair shop — or you — to pour in the wrong type. The wrong brake fluid can weaken how well the car stops. A dealer will look at the cap and replace it free of charge if it is the wrong one. Letters went out in October 2023.

How serious is it?

Moderate: the brakes work normally unless the wrong fluid has actually been put in, but because it affects stopping ability, have the cap checked and leave brake fluid top-ups to the dealer until then.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether campaign 23V601000 covers your car.
2. Book an inspection with an Audi dealer, or call Audi customer service at 1-800-253-2834 (recall 47T9).
3. Have the cap replaced if it is mislabeled — the inspection and new cap are free.

Official notice

Component
SERVICE BRAKES, HYDRAULIC:FLUID
Summary
Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (Audi) is recalling certain 2021 Audi S7, S6 Sedan, RS7, RS6 Avant, A6 Allroad, 2019-2021 A7, and A6 Sedan vehicles. An incorrectly labeled brake fluid cap may have been installed, which can result in the use of the wrong type of brake fluid.
Safety risk
Incorrect brake fluid can reduce braking ability and increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect the brake fluid reservoir cap and, if necessary, replace it, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed October 17, 2023. Owners may contact Audi's customer service at 1-800-253-2834. Volkswagen's number for this recall is 47T9.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
