BMW i4 and iX may lose pedestrian warning sound

NHTSA campaign 23V026000 · Reported 2023-01-25 · BMW of North America, LLC · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 BMW I4
  • 2022 BMW IX
  • 2023 BMW IX

What's wrong, in plain English

Electric cars are nearly silent at low speed, so federal rules require them to make an artificial sound that warns people nearby. On these i4 and iX models, the control unit that creates that sound can fail when the car starts up, leaving the vehicle silent. People walking through a parking lot or crossing in front of you may not hear you coming. A dealer will update the sound system software at no charge.

How serious is it?

This is a moderate risk to people outside the car rather than to you, and the vehicle is safe to keep driving — just be extra watchful around pedestrians in parking lots and crosswalks until it is fixed.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your i4 or iX is included.
2. Contact your BMW dealer, or BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417, to schedule the repair.
3. Have the dealer update the external sound generator software — it is free.

Official notice

Component
BACK OVER PREVENTION:WARNINGS:EXTERNAL/PEDESTRIAN ALERT
Summary
BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2022-2023 i4 eDrive40 and iX xDrive50 electric vehicles. During vehicle start-up, the artificial sound generator control unit may experience a fault and fail to generate the external pedestrian warning sound. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 141, "Minimum Sound Requirements for Hybrid and Electric Vehicles."
Safety risk
Without external warning sounds, pedestrians may be unaware of an approaching vehicle, increasing the risk of injury.
Remedy
Dealers will update the external artificial sound generator software, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed March 9, 2023. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
