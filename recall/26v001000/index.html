Volvo EX30 high-voltage battery can overheat and catch fire

NHTSA campaign 26V001000 · Reported 2026-01-02 · Volvo Car USA, LLC · critical severity what's this?

⚠ Park outside, away from buildings — fire risk. See all such recalls.

Affected vehicles

  • 2025 Volvo EX30

What's wrong, in plain English

The large battery that powers your EX30 can short out inside and get too hot, which Volvo says raises the risk of a fire. Until the vehicle is fixed, Volvo wants owners to park outside and away from buildings, and to charge the battery no higher than 70 percent. A dealer will inspect the battery and replace modules if needed, free of charge. Volvo says this recall has been replaced by a newer one, number 26V103.

How serious is it?

Serious — the notice does not tell you to stop driving, but it does say to park outside, away from structures, and to keep the charge under 70% because of fire risk.

What you should do

1. Park outside away from buildings and keep charging to a maximum of 70% until the repair is done.
2. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — VINs for this campaign became searchable January 26, 2026, and this recall was replaced by recall 26V103.
3. Contact your Volvo dealer or Volvo customer service at 1-800-458-1552 (recall R10355); inspection and any module replacement are free.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY
Summary
Volvo Car USA, LLC (Volvo Car) is recalling certain 2025 EX30 vehicles. The high-voltage battery may experience a short circuit and overheat.
Safety risk
A high-voltage battery that overheats increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Owners are advised to park outside and away from structures and limit their charge to a maximum of 70% until the recall repair is complete. Dealers will inspect and replace the battery modules as necessary, free of charge. Owners may contact Volvo Car’s customer service at 1-800-458-1552. Volvo Car's number for this recall is R10355. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning January 26, 2026. This recall was replaced by recall number 26V103.
