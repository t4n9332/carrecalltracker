Ford Super Duty trucks' daytime lights stay too bright

NHTSA campaign 19V808000 · Reported 2019-11-12 · Ford Motor Company · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2019 Ford F-250 SD
  • 2019 Ford F-350 SD
  • 2019 Ford F-450 SD

What's wrong, in plain English

Your truck's daytime running lights — the lights that stay on whenever the truck is running — are supposed to dim when you turn the headlight switch to "On." On these trucks, they may stay at full brightness instead. That breaks a federal lighting rule. Bright, undimmed lights can glare into the eyes of drivers coming toward you or ahead of you, making it harder for them to see. A dealer fixes it with a free software update.

How serious is it?

Moderate — the notice says the glare can make it harder for other drivers to see and raises crash risk, but there is no instruction to stop driving, so you can keep using the truck while you schedule the free fix.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included.
2. Call a Ford dealer and ask for recall 19C10 (Ford customer service: 1-866-436-7332).
3. Have the dealer reprogram the body control module — the repair is free.

Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING:HEADLIGHTS
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2018-2019 F-250, F-350 and F-450 Super Duty trucks. The Daytime Running Lights (DRL) may not dim when the headlight switch is moved into the "On" position. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."
Safety risk
DRL that cannot dim may reduce the visibility of other drivers, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Ford has notified owners, and dealers will reprogram the Body Control Module (BCM) software, free of charge. The recall began November 25, 2019. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 19C10.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
