Chrysler 300, Charger, Challenger tire pressure sensors quit early

NHTSA campaign 22V504000 · Reported 2022-07-14 · Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) · medium severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Chrysler 300
  • 2022 Dodge Challenger
  • 2022 Dodge Charger

What's wrong, in plain English

These cars have sensors in the wheels that watch your tire pressure. The battery inside a sensor can die too early, so the sensor stops working. When that happens, the system will not warn you that a tire is going low. Worse, if the warning light is already on because of a dead sensor battery, it can hide a real low-tire problem. A low tire can fail while you drive. Dealers will check the sensors and replace any bad ones.

How serious is it?

This is a moderate concern rather than an emergency, and the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but a hidden low tire can fail and cause a crash, so check your tire pressure by hand until the sensors are fixed.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your car is covered by recall 22V504000 (FCA number Z68).
2. Schedule a dealer visit to have the tire pressure sensors inspected and replaced as needed, free of charge.
3. In the meantime check your tire pressures with a gauge; call FCA at 1-800-853-1403 with questions.

Official notice

Component
TIRES:PRESSURE MONITORING AND REGULATING SYSTEMS
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US LLC) is recalling certain 2022 Dodge Challenger, Dodge Charger, and Chrysler 300 vehicles. The Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) sensor battery may fail prematurely and cause the sensor to become inoperative. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 138, "Tire Pressure Monitoring Systems."
Safety risk
Inoperative TPMS sensors will not alert the driver to low tire pressure. In addition, a TPMS warning light that is already illuminated from a failed battery sensor will mask an actual tire pressure failure issue. Either of these scenarios can result in tire failure and increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the TPMS sensors, as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed August 2, 2022. Owners may contact FCA US, LLC at 1-800-853-1403. FCA US, LLC's number for this recall is Z68.
