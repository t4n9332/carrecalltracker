Volkswagen ID.4 high-voltage battery can overheat and catch fire

NHTSA campaign 26V030000 · Reported 2026-01-21 · Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. · critical severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2023 Volkswagen ID.4
  • 2024 Volkswagen ID.4
  • 2025 Volkswagen ID.4

What's wrong, in plain English

The large battery pack that powers your ID.4 can get too hot. A battery that overheats can start a fire. Volkswagen will have dealers load updated battery software, and if an inspection shows it is needed, replace the battery pack itself, all at no cost to you. Owner letters were mailed in March 2026, and vehicle numbers could be looked up on the government recall site starting in January 2026. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving or to park outdoors.

How serious is it?

This is a fire risk, which is serious, but Volkswagen has not issued a do-not-drive or park-outside warning, so you may keep driving while you get the free repair scheduled promptly.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your ID.4 is covered by recall 93EA.
2. Call your Volkswagen dealer now and schedule the free battery software update and inspection.
3. If you have questions or did not get a letter, contact Volkswagen customer service at 1-800-893-5298.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY
Summary
Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (Volkswagen) is recalling certain 2023-2025 ID.4 vehicles. The high-voltage battery may overheat.
Safety risk
A high-voltage battery that overheats increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Dealers will update the high-voltage battery software, and replace the high-voltage battery, as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed March 18, 2026. Owners may contact Volkswagen customer service at 1-800-893-5298. Volkswagen's number for this recall is 93EA. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov on January 23, 2026.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
