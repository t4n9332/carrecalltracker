Nissan Sentra driveshaft may slip, causing rollaway risk

NHTSA campaign 26V410000 · Reported 2026-06-26 · Nissan North America, Inc. · high severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2025 Nissan Sentra

What's wrong, in plain English

The left front driveshaft, the rod that sends engine power to the front wheel, may not be pushed all the way into the transmission. It can work its way loose, leak transmission fluid, and leave the car without power to the wheels. Because the shaft is not fully locked in, the car may also roll away on its own when parked if you have not set the parking brake. Dealers will inspect and replace parts if needed.

How serious is it?

Serious, since you could lose power while driving or have the car roll away when parked, so always set the parking brake and get the free inspection done soon, though the notice does not say to stop driving.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your Sentra is covered by recall PMA68.
2. Contact your Nissan dealer for an inspection appointment, or call Nissan at 1-800-867-7669.
3. Until it is fixed, always use the parking brake; the inspection and any driveshaft or transmission work is free.

Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:DRIVELINE:DRIVESHAFT
Summary
Nissan North America, Inc. (Nissan) is recalling certain 2025 Nissan Sentra vehicles. The front left-side driveshaft may not be fully seated in the continuously variable transmission (CVT) assembly, which can cause a transmission fluid leak and loss of drive power.
Safety risk
An unexpected loss of drive power can increase the risk of a crash. In addition, a driveshaft that is not fully engaged can result in a vehicle rollaway if the parking brake is not applied, increasing the risk of a crash or injury.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect the front left driveshaft, and if necessary, replace the driveshaft and CVT assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed August 12, 2026. Owners may contact Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669. Nissan's number for this recall is PMA68. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov on June 30, 2026.
