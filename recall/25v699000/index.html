Tesla Cybertruck parking lights are too bright for others

NHTSA campaign 25V699000 · Reported 2025-10-15 · Tesla, Inc. · low severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2024 Tesla Cybertruck

What's wrong, in plain English

The software that runs your truck's lights can make the front parking lights shine brighter than the law allows. Those lights are meant to be dim markers, not headlights, so when they are too bright they can dazzle drivers coming the other way and make it hard for them to see. Tesla has already sent out a free over-the-air software update that fixes it. You do not need to visit a service center — just make sure your truck installs the update.

How serious is it?

Low for you — the truck is safe to drive, but the glare is a hazard to oncoming drivers until the software update is installed.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Cybertruck is covered.
2. Connect the truck to Wi-Fi and install the free over-the-air update (software version 2025.38.3 or later).
3. If the update will not install, contact Tesla customer service at 1-877-798-3752 (recall SB-25-00-008).

Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING
Summary
Tesla, Inc. (Tesla) is recalling certain 2024-2026 Cybertruck vehicles operating software prior to 2025.38.3. The vehicle controller software may cause the front parking lights to be too bright and exceed the maximum light output, adversely affecting the vision of other drivers. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."
Safety risk
Parking lights that are too bright can reduce visibility of oncoming drivers, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Tesla released an over-the-air (OTA) software update, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 12, 2025. Owners may contact Tesla customer service at 1-877-798-3752. Tesla's number for this recall is SB-25-00-008.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
