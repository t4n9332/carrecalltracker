Acura ILX brakes can weaken from an internal leak

NHTSA campaign 25V859000 · Reported 2025-12-11 · Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2018 Acura ILX
  • 2019 Acura ILX
  • 2020 Acura ILX

What's wrong, in plain English

Contaminated brake fluid can make a seal inside the part that pushes fluid to your brakes change shape. Once that seal is deformed, fluid leaks inside the part instead of doing its job, and the brakes get weaker. You could need more distance to stop than you expect, especially in an emergency. Honda dealers will replace that part for free. VIN lookup opens December 17, 2025, and owner letters were mailed May 4, 2026.

How serious is it?

Reduced braking and longer stopping distances raise the risk of a crash; the notice does not say to stop driving, but do not put this repair off.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your ILX is covered.
2. Contact your Acura dealer or Honda customer service at 1-888-234-2138 and reference recall RN5.
3. Have the brake part replaced — the repair is free.

Official notice

Component
SERVICE BRAKES, HYDRAULIC:FOUNDATION COMPONENTS:MASTER CYLINDER
Summary
Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2016-2020 Acura ILX vehicles. Contaminated brake fluid may cause a seal inside the brake master cylinder to deform, which can cause an internal brake fluid leak and reduce brake function.
Safety risk
Reduced brake function can extend the distance required to stop, increasing the risk of a crash or injury.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the brake master cylinder, free of charge. Owner letters were mailed May 4, 2026. Owners may contact Honda's customer service at 1-888-234-2138. Honda's number for this recall is RN5. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning December 17, 2025. The information in your report suggests that Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) may have been aware of this issue more than five business days before filing a report with NHTSA. Please be reminded that under Federal law, this agency is to be notified of all safety defect and/or noncompliance decisions within five business days. 49 CFR 573.6 Significant civil penalties can be assessed for this violation.
