Toyota Tundra Fuel Line Can Rub Through and Leak

NHTSA campaign 23V566000 · Reported 2023-08-10 · Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing · critical severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Toyota Tundra
  • 2022 Toyota Tundra Hybrid
  • 2023 Toyota Tundra
  • 2023 Toyota Tundra Hybrid

What's wrong, in plain English

A plastic fuel line runs close to the metal brake lines underneath the truck. The two can rub against each other, wearing through the plastic line and letting gasoline leak out. Leaking fuel near anything hot or anything that sparks can start a fire. The dealer's repair adds protective material and a clamp so the fuel line can no longer rub on the brake lines, at no cost to you. Owner letters were mailed November 6, 2024.

How serious is it?

A fuel leak means a fire risk, which is serious; the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but if you smell gasoline or see fuel under the truck, stop driving and call a dealer immediately.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls under recall 23V566000 (Toyota's numbers 23TB09/23TA09).
2. Call your Toyota dealer, or Toyota customer service at 1-800-331-4331, and schedule the repair promptly.
3. Have the protective material and clamp installed on the fuel tube — the repair is free.

Official notice

Component
FUEL SYSTEM, GASOLINE:DELIVERY:HOSES, LINES/PIPING, AND FITTINGS
Summary
Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing (Toyota) is recalling certain 2022-2023 Tundra and Tundra Hybrid vehicles. The plastic fuel tube routed near metallic brake lines may chafe against the brake lines and become damaged, possibly resulting in a fuel leak.
Safety risk
A fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source can increase the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Dealers will install protective materials and a clamp on the fuel tube, free of charge. Owner letters were mailed November 6, 2024. Owners may contact Toyota's customer service at 1-800-331-4331. Toyota's numbers for this recall are 23TB09/23TA09.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
