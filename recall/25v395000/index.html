BMW i4, i5, iX electric motor software can cut power

NHTSA campaign 25V395000 · Reported 2025-07-23 · BMW of North America, LLC · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 BMW I4
  • 2022 BMW IX
  • 2023 BMW IX
  • 2024 BMW I5
  • 2024 BMW IX
  • 2025 BMW I4 Edrive40

What's wrong, in plain English

The software that runs the electric drive motor can shut down the high-voltage system. If that happens, the vehicle loses drive power and will not pull, even though you are still steering and moving. BMW is fixing this with a software update. For many owners it arrives over the air, downloaded by the car itself, and a dealer can also install it. Either way there is no cost. Notification letters went out in August 2025.

How serious is it?

Serious — losing drive power in traffic increases the risk of a crash, so install the update as soon as you can, though the notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is included.
2. Accept the over-the-air software update when your vehicle offers it, and keep the car connected so it can download.
3. If you have not received the update, call BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417 or a BMW dealer — the update is free either way.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION MOTOR:CONTROLLER:SOFTWARE
Summary
BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2022-2025 i4, 2022-2024 IX, 2023-2024 I7, and 2024 I5 vehicles. The electric drive motor software may shut down the high-voltage system, causing a loss of drive power.
Safety risk
A loss of drive power increases the risk of a crash.
Remedy
The electric drive motor software will be updated over-the-air (OTA) or by a dealer, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed August 5, 2025. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
