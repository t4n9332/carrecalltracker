Ford Explorer and Lincoln Windshields May Have Air Bubbles

NHTSA campaign 25V730000 · Reported 2025-10-24 · Ford Motor Company · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2025 Ford Explorer
  • 2025 Lincoln Aviator

What's wrong, in plain English

The windshield on some of these vehicles was made with air bubbles you can see in the glass. Those bubbles can distort or block part of the driver's view of the road, which makes a crash more likely. Glass like this does not meet the federal safety standard for auto glass. The dealer will look at your windshield and replace it for free if it has the problem. Ford mailed owner letters on November 6, 2025.

How serious is it?

Anything that blocks your view of the road raises the chance of a crash, though the notice does not tell owners to stop driving while they wait for the inspection.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is in recall 25V730000.
2. Contact your Ford or Lincoln dealer, or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332 (recall number 25C60).
3. Have the dealer inspect the windshield and replace it if needed, at no charge to you.

Official notice

Component
VISIBILITY:WINDSHIELD
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2025-2026 Lincoln Corsair, Lincoln Aviator, and Explorer vehicles. The windshield glass may have visible air bubbles, which can obscure the driver's visibility. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 205, "Glazing Materials."
Safety risk
Reduced visibility while driving can increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the windshield as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed November 6, 2025. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 25C60. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall become searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning October 27, 2025.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
