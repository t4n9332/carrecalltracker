Ram heavy-duty diesel trucks can lose engine power

NHTSA campaign 22V642000 · Reported 2022-08-26 · Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) · high severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 RAM 3500
  • 2022 RAM 4500
  • 2022 RAM 5500

What's wrong, in plain English

The computer that controls your diesel engine may have a part inside it that was installed crooked. That part can short out, and when it does the computer stops talking to the rest of the truck. The result is that your truck can lose power while you are driving. This affects certain Ram 3500, 4500 and 5500 models with the 6.7L Cummins turbo diesel engine. A dealer will put in a new engine computer at no cost to you.

How serious is it?

Serious — losing power while driving could leave you stranded in traffic and lead to a crash; the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but get it fixed as soon as a dealer can take you.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included in recall 22V642000 (FCA number Z87).
2. Call your Ram dealer to book the engine computer replacement, or contact FCA US customer service at 1-800-853-1403.
3. Have the new engine control module installed — the repair is free, and owner letters were mailed October 20, 2022.

Official notice

Component
ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING:ENGINE:ENGINE CONTROL MODULE (ECU/ECM)
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) is recalling certain 2022 Ram 3500, 3500 Cab Chassis, Ram 2500, and 4500 and 5500 Cab Chassis vehicles equipped with Cummins 6.7L turbo diesel engines. The engine control module (ECM) may have been built with a misaligned capacitor that can short-circuit, resulting in an internal loss of communication.
Safety risk
An internal loss of communication can result in a loss of drive power, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the ECM, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed October 20, 2022. Owners may contact FCA US, LLC customer service at 1-800-853-1403. FCA US, LLC's number for this recall is Z87.
