Nissan Titan alternator wiring may short and stall engine

NHTSA campaign 19V495000 · Reported 2019-06-26 · Nissan North America, Inc. · critical severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2018 Nissan Titan
  • 2019 Nissan Titan

What's wrong, in plain English

The bundle of wires running to the alternator — the part that charges the battery while the engine runs — may have been pinched or rubbed when the engine was installed at the factory. Damaged wiring can short out. Nissan says a short can make the engine stall, which is dangerous in traffic, and it also raises the risk of a fire. The dealer will inspect the harness, check that it is routed correctly, and clip it back into place or replace it.

How serious is it?

This one carries both a stall risk and a fire risk; Nissan has not told owners to park the truck, but it should be inspected promptly rather than put off.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included. 2. Contact a Nissan dealer, or Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669, and reference recall 19V495000. 3. Have the alternator harness inspected and repaired or replaced — at no charge.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:WIRING
Summary
Nissan North America, Inc. (Nissan) is recalling certain 2017-2019 gasoline, light duty, Titan vehicles. The alternator harness may have been damaged during the engine installation process, possibly resulting in an electrical short.
Safety risk
An electrical short may cause an engine stall, increasing the risk of a crash. In addition, it increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Nissan will notify owners, and dealers will inspect the alternator harness for the proper routing and any damage. The harness will be clipped into the correct position or replaced as necessary, free of charge. The recall began August 8, 2019. Owners may contact Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669.
