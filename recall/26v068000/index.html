Hyundai IONIQ 5 battery short circuit creates fire risk

NHTSA campaign 26V068000 · Reported 2026-02-06 · Hyundai Motor America

critical How serious is it? About this label

This is serious because a short in the battery can lead to a fire; the notice does not tell owners to park outside or stop driving, but you should get the inspection done as soon as your dealer can take the car.

What's wrong, in plain English

A part inside the high-voltage battery pack, called a bus bar, may not have been tightened properly. A loose connection there can cause an electrical short, and Hyundai says that raises the risk of a fire. A dealer will check the bus bar bolts and tighten them, and will replace the entire battery pack if that turns out to be necessary. The work is free. Hyundai mailed owner letters on April 3, 2026, and VINs became searchable February 7, 2026.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your vehicle is covered. 2. Contact your Hyundai dealer, or Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460, and reference recall 294. 3. Schedule the free inspection and repair promptly.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY
Summary
Hyundai Motor America (Hyundai) is recalling certain 2025-2026 IONIQ 5 and 2026 IONIQ 9 vehicles. The high voltage battery system may contain an improperly tightened bus bar, which can cause a short-circuit.
Safety risk
An electrical short in the high voltage battery system increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and tighten the bus bar retention bolts and replace the battery system assembly, as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed April 3, 2026. Owners may contact Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460. Hyundai's number for this recall is 294. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov on February 7, 2026.
