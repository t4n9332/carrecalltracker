Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra hoods can fly open

NHTSA campaign 20V142000 · Reported 2020-03-12 · General Motors LLC

high How serious is it? About this label

Serious, because a hood that opens at speed blocks your view with no warning and can lead to a crash; the notice does not tell owners to park the truck, but get the repair done promptly.

What's wrong, in plain English

The hood on these trucks is held shut by a metal loop that the latch grabs onto. On the affected trucks that metal was not hardened properly during manufacturing, so the loop can snap. If it breaks, the hood can fly open while you are driving and block your view of the road. A dealer will replace the whole hood assembly, including the part and the labor, free of charge.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included and whether the repair was already completed — this recall began April 13, 2020. 2. Call your dealer, or Chevrolet at 1-800-222-1020 or GMC at 1-888-988-7267, and give them recall number N192284960. 3. Have the hood assembly replaced at no charge.

Official notice

Component
LATCHES/LOCKS/LINKAGES:HOOD:LATCH
Summary
General Motors LLC (GM) is recalling certain 2020 Chevrolet Silverado 2500 and 3500 and GMC Sierra 2500 and 3500 vehicles. The hood-latch striker wires may not have been heat-treated properly, possibly causing them to fracture.
Safety risk
If a striker wire fractures, the hood may open unexpectedly while driving, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
GM will notify owners, and dealers will replace the hood assembly, free of charge. The recall began April 13, 2020. Owners may contact Chevrolet customer service at 1-800-222-1020 or GMC customer service at 1-888-988-7267. GM's number for this recall is N192284960.
