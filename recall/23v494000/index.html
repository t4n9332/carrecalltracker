Nissan LEAF may speed up on its own

NHTSA campaign 23V494000 · Reported 2023-07-17 · Nissan North America, Inc.

high How serious is it? About this label

This is serious because a car that accelerates by itself can cause a crash; the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but schedule the free update right away.

What's wrong, in plain English

On these LEAF electric cars, the vehicle can pick up speed when you did not ask it to. It happens after you shut off cruise control and then change the way the car drives — moving the shifter from D to B, switching e-Pedal on, or selecting ECO mode. The fix is a free software update to the computer that controls the car, done at the dealer. Nissan planned to mail owner letters on August 30, 2023.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your LEAF is covered. 2. Contact a Nissan dealer or Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669 and mention recall R23A6. 3. Bring the car in for the free software reprogramming.

Official notice

Component
VEHICLE SPEED CONTROL
Summary
Nissan North America, Inc. (Nissan) is recalling certain 2018-2023 LEAF vehicles. The vehicle may accelerate unintentionally if the driving mode is changed ("D" to "B"; e-Pedal "On"; or "ECO" mode) after disengaging the cruise control.
Safety risk
Unintentional acceleration can increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will reprogram the vehicle control module (VCM), free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed August 30, 2023. Owners may contact Nissan's customer service at 1-800-867-7669. Nissan's number for this recall is R23A6.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
