What's wrong, in plain English

Some Kona Electric vehicles have a battery that can develop an internal short circuit. When this happens inside the battery pack, it raises the chance that the battery could catch fire. To fix this, dealers will update the software that manages the battery and check the battery pack itself, replacing the battery if there turns out to be a problem, all at no charge to the owner.

What you should do

1. Park your vehicle outside and away from structures until the repair is completed.
2. Check your vehicle's VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm it's included in this recall.
3. Contact your Hyundai dealer, or call Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460, to schedule the repair.
4. The software update and any needed battery replacement will be done free of charge.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 20V630000. Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY:MANAGEMENT SYSTEM/ENERGY CONTROL MODULE (BMS/BECM):SOFTWARE
Summary
Hyundai Motor America (Hyundai) is recalling certain 2019-2020 Kona Electric vehicles. The lithium-ion (Li-ion) battery may short-circuit.
Safety risk
An electrical short in the Li-ion battery increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Hyundai will notify owners, and dealers will update the Battery Management System (BMS) software and inspect the Li-ion battery, replacing it if necessary, free of charge. Owners are advised to park their vehicles outside and away from structures until the repair is complete. The recall began November 13, 2020. Owners may contact Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460. Hyundai's number for this recall is 196.
