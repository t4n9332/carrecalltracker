BMW X3 rear doors can unlock and open unexpectedly

NHTSA campaign 25V106000 · Reported 2025-02-20 · BMW of North America, LLC · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 BMW X3
  • 2023 BMW X3
  • 2024 BMW X3

What's wrong, in plain English

The locking parts in the two back doors may not work correctly. Federal safety rules require two separate actions to open a door from the inside so it cannot happen by accident. On these vehicles a single motion can unlock and open a rear door instead. That means a back door could come open when nobody meant to open it, and these vehicles do not meet the federal standard for door locks and door retention.

How serious is it?

A rear door opening unexpectedly can injure people riding in the back seat, so while the notice does not tell you to stop driving, treat it as urgent if you carry passengers or children back there.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your X3 is included.
2. Call your BMW dealer for an appointment, or BMW customer service at 800-525-7417; owner letters went out April 21, 2025.
3. The dealer replaces both second-row door locking modules free of charge.

Official notice

Component
LATCHES/LOCKS/LINKAGES:DOORS:LOCK
Summary
BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2022-2024 X3 sDrive30i and X3 xDrive30i vehicles. The second-row seat side door locking modules may not function properly and allow the doors to be unlocked with a single motion. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 206, "Door Locks and Door Retention Components."
Safety risk
Doors that can be inadvertently unlocked and unlatched may open unexpectedly, increasing the risk of injury to rear seat occupants.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the second-row seat side door locking modules, free of charge. A letter informing owners of the safety risk was mailed on April 21, 2025. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 800-525-7417.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
