Genesis G80 Airbag Sensor May Misread Passenger Seat

NHTSA campaign 18V496000 · Reported Jul 27, 2018 · Hyundai Motor America

high How serious is it? About this label

If a crash happens while a child or infant is in that seat and the airbag deploys instead of staying off, it can increase the risk of injury to them. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

The passenger airbag system may wrongly detect that an adult is sitting in the front passenger seat, even when no one is there. Because of this misreading, if a child or infant is actually riding in that seat during a crash, the airbag may inflate instead of staying switched off, which is what should happen when a small child is in that seat. The system is not correctly recognizing who, or what, is actually in the seat.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your vehicle is included.
2. Contact a Hyundai dealer, or call Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460, to schedule the repair.
3. The repair (reprogramming the airbag sensor system) is free.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 18V496000. Official notice

Component
AIR BAGS:SENSOR:OCCUPANT CLASSIFICATION
Summary
Hyundai Motor America (Hyundai) is recalling certain 2018 Genesis G80 vehicles. The Occupant Classification System (OCS) may incorrectly detect that an adult is in the passenger seat, even if the seat is unoccupied. In the event of a crash, if a child or infant is in the seat, the front passenger air bag may deploy instead of being deactivated.
Safety risk
In the event of a crash, if the front passenger air bag deploys instead of being deactivated when a child or infant are in the front passenger seat, it can increase the risk of injury.
Remedy
Hyundai will notify owners, and dealers will reprogram the OCS, free of charge. The recall began August 24, 2018. Owners may contact Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460. Hyundai's number for this recall is 002G.
