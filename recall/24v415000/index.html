Dodge Durango and Ram trucks lose stability control

NHTSA campaign 24V415000 · Reported 2024-06-06 · Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) · medium severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Dodge Durango
  • 2022 RAM 3500

What's wrong, in plain English

A software error in the computer that runs the anti-lock brakes can switch off the electronic stability control system. Stability control senses when the vehicle is starting to slide or spin in a turn and automatically brakes individual wheels to help you stay on course. With it shut off, you still have normal brakes and steering, but you lose that automatic help. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule that requires stability control to work.

How serious is it?

Moderately serious — you can keep driving, but with stability control disabled your risk of a crash goes up, especially on wet, icy or slippery roads.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your vehicle is covered.
2. Contact your Dodge or Ram dealer, or FCA customer service at 1-800-853-1403 (recall 55B), to book the visit.
3. The dealer updates the anti-lock brake computer's software at no cost to you.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRONIC STABILITY CONTROL (ESC):CONTROL MODULE
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) is recalling certain 2022 Dodge Durango and Ram 2500 and 3500 vehicles. Due to a software malfunction, the ABS control module may disable the electronic stability control system. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 126, "Electronic Stability Control Systems."
Safety risk
Driving with a disabled electronic stability control system can increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will update the ABS control module software, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed beginning July 11, 2024. Owners may contact FCA US, LLC customer service at 1-800-853-1403. FCA US, LLC's number for this recall is 55B.
