Jeep Wrangler plug-in hybrid battery fuse may overheat

NHTSA campaign 22V768000 · Reported 2022-10-13 · Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Jeep Wrangler
  • 2023 Jeep Wrangler

What's wrong, in plain English

These plug-in hybrid Wranglers have a large high-voltage battery pack. Inside the pack, a 200-amp fuse was fastened with the wrong hardware, and that can make the fuse build up too much heat. If the fuse fails from the heat, the vehicle can lose the power that drives the wheels. Dealers will check the fuse and replace it if needed, check the battery pack for damage and replace that if needed, all at no cost.

How serious is it?

This is serious because your Jeep could lose driving power on the road, which raises crash risk; the notice does not tell owners to park the vehicle, but the inspection should not be put off.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Jeep is affected. 2. Contact a Jeep dealer to book the free inspection and repair, or call Chrysler customer service at 1-800-853-1403 (recall Z71). 3. Until it is fixed, be extra careful in situations where losing power suddenly would be dangerous.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) is recalling certain 2022-2023 Jeep Wrangler Plug-In Hybrid Electric (PHEV) vehicles. The high voltage (HV) battery pack assembly was built with incorrect fasteners to secure the 200-amp fuse, which can generate excessive heat.
Safety risk
Excessive heat may cause the fuse to fail and result in a loss of drive power, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the HV battery fuse, as necessary. Dealers will also inspect the battery assembly for damage and replace it, if necessary. Repairs will be performed free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 8, 2022. Owners may contact Chrysler customer service at 1-800-853-1403. Chrysler's number for this recall is Z71.
