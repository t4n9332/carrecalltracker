Kia Telluride can roll away while in park

NHTSA campaign 24V214000 · Reported 2024-03-20 · Kia America, Inc. · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2020 Kia Telluride
  • 2021 Kia Telluride
  • 2022 Kia Telluride
  • 2023 Kia Telluride
  • 2024 Kia Telluride

What's wrong, in plain English

Parts that send power to the front wheels may not be fully joined together on these Tellurides. When that happens, shifting into PARK may not actually hold the vehicle, and it can roll away on its own after you get out. Kia says that until the repair is done, you should set the electronic parking brake every time before leaving the vehicle. Dealers update the parking brake software and replace damaged shafts if needed, free of charge.

How serious is it?

A vehicle that rolls away can cause a crash or injure someone nearby, so use the parking brake every single time until the free repair is completed.

What you should do

1. Set the electronic parking brake before you get out of the vehicle, every time, starting now.
2. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Telluride is included.
3. Contact your Kia dealer, or Kia customer service at 1-800-333-4542, and reference recall SC303 for the free repair.

Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:DRIVELINE:DRIVESHAFT
Summary
Kia America, Inc. (Kia) is recalling certain 2020-2024 Telluride vehicles. The intermediate shaft and right front driveshaft may not be fully engaged, which can result in vehicle roll away while in PARK.
Safety risk
A vehicle roll away increases the risk of a crash or injury.
Remedy
Owners are advised to manually engage the emergency parking brake prior to exiting the vehicle until the recall remedy is performed. Dealers will update the electronic parking brake software and replace any damaged intermediate shafts as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 15, 2024. Owners may contact Kia customer service at 1-800-333-4542. Kia's number for this recall is SC303.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
