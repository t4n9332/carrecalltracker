Volkswagen Golf R Radiator May Leak Coolant

NHTSA campaign 22V707000 · Reported Sep 21, 2022 · Volkswagen Group of America, Inc.

critical How serious is it? About this label

A coolant leak can cause the engine to overheat, increasing the risk of a fire. Coolant spilling onto the road can also increase the risk of a crash for other vehicles. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

In some Volkswagen Golf R vehicles, the radiator may not have been attached to the intercooler correctly. This can damage a coolant hose, causing it to leak coolant from the cooling system.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
2. Contact a Volkswagen dealer, or call 1-800-893-5298, to have the radiator inspected.
3. The repair is free of charge.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 22V707000. Official notice

Component
ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING:COOLING SYSTEM:RADIATOR ASSEMBLY
Summary
Volkswagen Group of America, Inc. (Volkswagen) is recalling certain 2022-2023 Golf R A8 and Golf GTI vehicles. The radiator may not have been attached correctly to the intercooler, which can result in a damaged coolant hose.
Safety risk
A damaged coolant hose can leak, causing the engine to overheat, and increasing the risk of fire. In addition, large amounts of coolant spilling onto the roadway can increase the risk of a crash to other vehicles.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect the radiator and replace any damaged parts, as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed November 18, 2022. Owners may contact Volkswagen's customer service at 1-800-893-5298. Volkswagen's number for this recall is 19Q8.
