Tesla Model 3 and Model Y Taillight Failure

NHTSA campaign 22V844000 · Reported Nov 15, 2022 · Tesla, Inc.

high How serious is it? About this label

A taillight that isn't working can make the vehicle harder for other drivers to see, which increases the risk of a crash. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

One or both taillights on certain vehicles may sometimes fail to light up. This is an intermittent problem, meaning the taillight might work at times and stop working at other times. When a taillight isn't lit, the vehicle becomes harder for other drivers to see, which reduces its visibility on the road.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your vehicle is included.
2. Tesla will send the fix automatically as an over-the-air software update; you can also contact Tesla customer service at 1-877-798-3752.
3. The update is free of charge.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 22V844000. Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING:TAIL LIGHTS
Summary
Tesla, Inc. (Tesla) is recalling certain 2023 Model 3 and 2020-2023 Model Y vehicles. One or both taillights may intermittently fail to illuminate.
Safety risk
A taillight that fails to illuminate may reduce the visibility of the vehicle, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Tesla has released an over-the-air (OTA) software update, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed January 14, 2023. Owners may contact Tesla customer service at 1-877-798-3752. Tesla's number for this recall is SB-22-00-016. (over-the-air software update)

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
