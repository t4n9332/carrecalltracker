BMW i4, iX and i7 can lose drive power suddenly

NHTSA campaign 22V944000 · Reported 2022-12-20 · BMW of North America, LLC · critical severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 BMW I4
  • 2022 BMW IX
  • 2023 BMW IX

What's wrong, in plain English

The software running the computer that controls the big high-voltage battery can cut off electrical power. If that happens while you are driving, the vehicle can suddenly lose the power that turns the wheels — it simply stops pulling. Losing power without warning in traffic, on a highway, or partway through a turn leaves you very little time to react and steer over to the side of the road.

How serious is it?

A sudden, unannounced loss of drive power can cause a crash, so get the software update as soon as you can; the notice does not tell owners to park the vehicle.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is on the list.
2. Contact your BMW dealer to schedule the update, or call BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417; owner letters were expected February 10, 2023.
3. The dealer updates the high-voltage battery control software free of charge.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:TRACTION BATTERY THERMAL:MANAGEMENT:SOFTWARE
Summary
BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2022-2023 iX xDrive40, iX xDrive50, iX M60, i4 eDrive35, i4 eDrive40, i4 M50, and 2023 i7 xDrive60 vehicles. The high voltage battery electronic control unit (ECU) software may cause an interruption of electrical power.
Safety risk
An interruption of electrical power may result in a sudden loss of drive power, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will update the high voltage battery ECU software, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed February 10, 2023. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
