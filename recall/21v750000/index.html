Mazda CX-9 rear seat can slide forward in crash

NHTSA campaign 21V750000 · Reported 2021-09-27 · Mazda North American Operations

medium How serious is it? About this label

The seat works normally in everyday driving, but in a crash a belted passenger in that left rear seat could be hurt worse than they should be, so the car can still be driven while you arrange the free repair.

What's wrong, in plain English

These CX-9s have a second-row bench seat that splits 60/40. On the left-hand section, the seat may slide forward unexpectedly during a crash instead of staying put. Because the seat belt anchors to the seat, that movement pushes extra force onto whoever is buckled in there. The vehicle does not meet the federal safety standard for seat belt anchorages. Dealers will replace the internal parts of the affected second-row seats free of charge.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to check whether your CX-9 is included. 2. Contact your Mazda dealer or Mazda customer service at 1-800-222-5500, Option 4 (recall 5221I), to schedule the free seat repair. 3. If you can, seat passengers elsewhere in the vehicle until the left second-row seat has been fixed.

Official notice

Component
SEATS:MID/REAR ASSEMBLY
Summary
Mazda North American Operations (Mazda) is recalling certain 2020-2021 CX-9 vehicles equipped with 60/40 split bench second-row seats. The left-side second-row seat may unexpectedly slide forward during a crash. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 210, "Seat Belt Assembly Anchorages."
Safety risk
A seat that slides forward during a crash can transfer excessive force onto a belted occupant, increasing their risk of injury.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the internal parts of the affected second-row seats, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed November 4, 2021. Owners may contact Mazda customer service at 1-800-222-5500, Option 4. Mazda's number for this recall is 5221I.
