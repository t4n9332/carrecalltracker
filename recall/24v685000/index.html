Ford Explorer Hybrid battery cable can rub and spark fire

NHTSA campaign 24V685000 · Reported 2024-09-13 · Ford Motor Company · critical severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2024 Ford Explorer

What's wrong, in plain English

On Explorers with the 3.3-liter hybrid engine, the cable running from the 12-volt battery can rub against the belt-driven starter-generator under the hood. Over time that rubbing wears through the cable's covering, and the bare wire can short out. A short circuit in that spot can start a fire. The repair is quick and free: the dealer adds a tie strap that holds the battery cable away from the high-voltage cable so the two cannot rub together.

How serious is it?

This one carries a fire risk, so treat it as urgent and get the free repair done as soon as your dealer can take the vehicle.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see if your Explorer is affected. 2. Call your Ford dealer or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332 (recall 24S61) and ask for the earliest appointment. 3. Have the tie strap installed — it costs you nothing.

Official notice

Component
HYBRID PROPULSION SYSTEM
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2024 Ford Explorer vehicles equipped with 3.3L Hybrid engines. The 12-Volt battery cable may chafe against the Belt-integrated Starter Generat (BiSG) and short circuit.
Safety risk
A short circuit increases the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Dealers will install a tie strap between the battery cable and high voltage cable, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed November 8, 2024. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 24S61.
