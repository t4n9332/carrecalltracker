Honda Accord Hybrid can lose drive power while driving

NHTSA campaign 25V785000 · Reported 2025-11-13 · Honda (American Honda Motor Co.)

high How serious is it? About this label

Losing power in traffic without warning can cause a crash or injury, so while Honda has not told owners to park the car, this one should not sit on your to-do list.

What's wrong, in plain English

A software mistake in one of the computers that manages your hybrid system can make it reset itself while you are driving. When that happens, the car can suddenly stop sending power to the wheels, so you lose the ability to accelerate. This can happen with no warning while the vehicle is moving. Honda has a corrected version of the software, and a dealer will load it into your car at no cost to you.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Accord Hybrid is affected.
2. Call your Honda dealer, or Honda customer service at 1-888-234-2138 (recall TN2), and book the software reprogramming.
3. Have the dealer reprogram the control module software — the repair is free.

Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:HYBRID MANAGEMENT:POWER CONTROL UNIT/MODULE (HPCU):SOFTWARE
Summary
Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2023-2025 Honda Accord Hybrid vehicles. Due to a software error, the integrated control module (ICM) central processing unit (CPU) may reset while driving, causing a loss of drive power.
Safety risk
A loss of drive power increases the risk of a crash or injury.
Remedy
Dealers will reprogram the ICM CPU software, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed January 6, 2026. Owners may contact Honda's customer service at 1-888-234-2138. Honda's number for this recall is TN2. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning November 18, 2025.
