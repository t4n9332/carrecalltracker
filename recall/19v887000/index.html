Ram 1500 tire pressure warning light may not work

NHTSA campaign 19V887000 · Reported 2019-12-12 · Chrysler (FCA US LLC)

medium How serious is it? About this label

Moderately serious — you may not be warned about a low tire, which raises crash risk, but you can keep driving; check your tire pressure with a gauge regularly until it is repaired.

What's wrong, in plain English

Some 2019 Ram 1500 trucks were built with the wrong tire pressure sensor. Because of that, the dashboard warning light that should come on when a tire is low may never light up. You could be driving on a badly underinflated tire without knowing it, which can lead to poor handling or a tire failure. Federal rules require a working tire pressure warning system. The dealer will replace the incorrect sensor with the right one.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included. 2. Contact your Ram dealer or Chrysler customer service at 1-800-853-1403 (recall number VE1). 3. Have the tire pressure sensor replaced at no cost.

Official notice

Component
TIRES:PRESSURE MONITORING AND REGULATING SYSTEMS
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US LLC) is recalling certain 2019 Ram 1500 trucks. An incorrect Tire Pressure Monitoring System (TPMS) sensor could have been installed during production causing the sensor to not display the low tire pressure warning indicator. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 138, "Tire Pressure Monitoring Systems."
Safety risk
Without a warning indicator, the driver will be unaware if low tire pressure occurs, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Chrysler will notify owners, and dealers will replace the TPMS sensor, free of charge. The recall began January 9, 2020. Owners may contact Chrysler customer service at 1-800-853-1403. Chrysler's number for this recall is VE1.
