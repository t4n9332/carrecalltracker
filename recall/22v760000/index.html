Kia K5 and Sorento can lose drive power

NHTSA campaign 22V760000 · Reported 2022-10-11 · Kia America, Inc. · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2021 Kia K5
  • 2021 Kia Sorento
  • 2022 Kia K5
  • 2022 Kia Sorento
  • 2023 Kia K5

What's wrong, in plain English

These Kia vehicles have a backup mode that is supposed to let you keep driving slowly if the transmission's oil pump acts up. On the recalled vehicles that backup mode may not work, so instead of limping along you could lose drive power completely and the vehicle would coast to a stop. A Kia dealer will check the transmission and replace it if needed, and will also update the transmission's software. Everything is done free of charge.

How serious is it?

A complete loss of drive power raises the risk of a crash, so this is serious; Kia has not told owners to stop driving, but get the inspection and software update done without delay.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your car is covered by recall SC250. 2. Make an appointment at a Kia dealer for the free inspection, software update, and transmission replacement if needed. 3. Call Kia customer service at 1-800-333-4542 with any questions.

Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION
Summary
Kia Motors, Inc. (Kia) is recalling certain 2021-2022 Sorento and 2021-2023 K5 vehicles. The vehicle's "fail-safe" limited-mobility drive mode may be impaired, when prompted by a transmission oil pump malfunction, which can result in a complete loss of drive power.
Safety risk
Loss of drive power increases the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the transmission, as necessary. Dealers will also update the transmission control unit software. All repairs will be performed free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 9, 2022. Owners may contact Kia customer service at 1-800-333-4542. Kia's number for this recall is SC250.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
