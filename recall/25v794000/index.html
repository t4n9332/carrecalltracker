Kia K5 Fuel Tank Damage Risk of Fire

NHTSA campaign 25V794000 · Reported Nov 17, 2025 · Kia America, Inc.

critical How serious is it? About this label

Kia says a melted fuel tank can leak, increasing the risk of a fire. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

Kia says a damaged check valve in some K5 vehicles can let air get into the fuel tank. That extra air can make the tank expand, and the expanded tank can touch hot parts of the exhaust system. Contact with the hot exhaust can melt the fuel tank, so Kia is having dealers check and fix the part before that happens.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
2. Contact a Kia dealer or call 800-333-4542 to schedule the inspection and repair.
3. The check valve replacement and any needed fuel tank replacement are free.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 25V794000. Official notice

Component
FUEL SYSTEM, GASOLINE
Summary
Kia America, Inc. (Kia) is recalling certain 2021-2024 K5 vehicles. A damaged check valve can allow air to enter the fuel tank, causing it to expand and contact hot exhaust components, melting the tank.
Safety risk
A melted fuel tank can leak, increasing the risk of a fire.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the check valve. The fuel tank will be inspected for damage and replaced as necessary. All repairs will be performed free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed January 16, 2026. Owners may contact Kia customer service at 800-333-4542. Kia's number for this recall is SC356. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning December 1, 2025.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
