Volvo VAH truck passenger seat belt anchor may fail

NHTSA campaign 25V753000 · Reported 2025-11-03 · Volvo Trucks North America · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2023 Volvo VAH

What's wrong, in plain English

Volvo built certain VAH trucks from 2019 through 2026 without enough reinforcement in the floor under the passenger seat belt. The spot where the belt bolts to the floor can pull loose or break. In a crash, the belt would not hold the passenger in place the way it is supposed to. These trucks do not meet the federal rule for seat belt anchors. A dealer welds in a new nut and washer to make the anchor strong again.

How serious is it?

Serious but not an emergency: the truck is not listed as unsafe to drive, and the danger only shows up in a crash, when the passenger belt may not protect the person in that seat.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your truck is included. 2. Call Volvo Trucks customer service at 1-800-528-6586 or your Volvo dealer and ask for recall RVXX2510. 3. Schedule the repair — the new weld nut and washer are installed free of charge.

Official notice

Component
SEAT BELTS:FRONT:ANCHORAGE
Summary
Volvo Trucks North America (Volvo Trucks) is recalling certain 2019-2026 VAH trucks. The passenger seat belt anchor may fail due to inadequate floor reinforcement. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 210, "Seat Belt Assembly Anchorages."
Safety risk
A seat belt anchor that fails will not properly restrain an occupant in a crash, increasing the risk of injury.
Remedy
Dealers will install a new weld nut and washer, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 16, 2025. Owners may contact Volvo Trucks' customer service at 1-800-528-6586. Volvo Trucks' number for this recall is RVXX2510. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning November 10, 2025.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
