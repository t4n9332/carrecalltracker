Nissan Titan and Frontier may roll away in Park

NHTSA campaign 22V671000 · Reported 2022-09-07 · Nissan North America, Inc. · high severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2020 Nissan Titan
  • 2022 Nissan Frontier

What's wrong, in plain English

In certain 2020 through 2023 Nissan Titan, 2020 through 2022 Frontier, and 2023 Z vehicles, the part inside the transmission that locks the vehicle in place when you shift into Park may not catch. If it does not, the vehicle can roll away on its own after you think it is parked. Nissan tells owners to set the parking brake every single time they park until the repair is made. Dealers will update the vehicle's computer software.

How serious is it?

A vehicle rolling away can hit a person, another car, or a building, so it is serious — but you can keep driving as long as you use the parking brake every time you park.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to check whether your vehicle is on the list. 2. Use the parking brake every time you park, starting now. 3. Contact your Nissan dealer or Nissan customer service at 1-800-867-7669 to get the free software update.

Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION
Summary
Nissan North America, Inc. (Nissan) is recalling certain 2020-2023 Titan, 2020-2022 Frontier, and 2023 Z vehicles. The transmission parking pawl may not engage when the vehicle is shifted into park, which can result in a vehicle rollaway.
Safety risk
A vehicle rollaway increases the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Owners are advised to apply the parking brake every time they park their vehicle. Dealers will perform the applicable repairs below, free of charge:  Model Year 2020-2022 Frontier vehicles: reprogram Transmission Control Module (TCM)  Model Year 2020-2022 Titan vehicles: reprogram TCM and Engine Control Module (ECM)  Model Year 2023 Titan vehicles: reprogram TCM  Model Year 2023 Z vehicles: reprogram TCM. Owner notification letters were mailed October 28, 2022. Owners may contact Nissan's customer service at 1-800-867-7669.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
