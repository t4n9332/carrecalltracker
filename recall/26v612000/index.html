What's wrong, in plain English

On some Cadillac Optiq vehicles, the front power windows may not sense when something is in the way when they are closed using the key fob's express-up function. Instead of detecting the obstruction and reversing direction, the window can keep closing, which could pinch a passenger before it retracts. Because the windows don't reverse as expected in this situation, these vehicles do not meet a federal safety standard for power windows.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is included.
2. Contact a Cadillac dealer for the software update, or call Cadillac customer service at 1-800-458-8006; the update may also be available over-the-air.
3. The repair is free.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 26V612000. Official notice

Component
VISIBILITY:POWER WINDOW DEVICES AND CONTROLS
Summary
General Motors, LLC (GM) is recalling certain 2025-2027 Cadillac OPTIQ vehicles. When closed using the key fob's express-up function, the front windows may fail to detect an obstruction and reverse direction. As such, these vehicles fail to conform to Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 118, "Power-Operated Window Systems."
Safety risk
A closing window that does not reverse direction as expected can pinch a passenger before retracting, increasing the risk of injury.
Remedy
The body control module (BCM) software will be updated by a dealer or through an over-the-air (OTA) update, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed November 9, 2026. Owners may contact Cadillac customer service at 1-800-458-8006. GM's number for this recall is N262567230. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov on September 24, 2026.
