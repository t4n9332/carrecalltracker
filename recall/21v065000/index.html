Volvo VNL flywheel bolts may be loose, can detach

NHTSA campaign 21V065000 · Reported 2021-02-11 · Volvo Trucks North America · high severity what's this?

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Volvo VNL

What's wrong, in plain English

On these VNL trucks with Cummins X15 diesel engines, the bolts holding the flywheel to the engine's crankshaft may not have been tightened correctly. If they loosen, the flywheel can come off. That can leave the truck with no drive power, and pieces from the driveline can be thrown out of the engine area and hurt someone nearby. These trucks were still at the factory, so most owners have not received them yet.

How serious is it?

Serious — a detached flywheel means losing power on the road plus flying parts, but the trucks are being corrected at the Volvo plant before delivery.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm whether your VNL is affected. 2. Call Volvo Trucks customer service at 1-800-528-6586 or your dealer to confirm the bolts were tightened and inspected before you take or keep driving the truck. 3. The tightening, inspection, and any repair are free of charge.

Official notice

Component
ENGINE
Summary
Volvo Trucks North America (Volvo Trucks) is recalling certain 2022 VNL vehicles equipped with Cummins X15 diesel engines. The capscrews that attach the flywheel to the crankshaft may have been improperly torqued, allowing the flywheel to detach.
Safety risk
A detached flywheel can cause loss of drive power, increasing the risk of a crash. Additionally, driveline pieces may be ejected from the engine compartment, increasing the risk of injury.
Remedy
The affected vehicles are still within the manufacturer's control. The Volvo Trucks manufacturing facility will properly torque the capscrews and inspect for possible damage and, if necessary, repair any damage found, free of charge. The recall began January 5, 2021. Owners may contact Volvo Trucks customer service at 1-800-528-6586.
