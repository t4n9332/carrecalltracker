Hyundai Sonata brake lights flash instead of staying lit

NHTSA campaign 24V583000 · Reported 2024-08-07 · Hyundai Motor America · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2024 Hyundai Sonata

What's wrong, in plain English

A software problem can make the brake lights flash rather than coming on and staying on steadily when you press the brake pedal. Drivers behind you may be confused about whether you are slowing or stopping, which makes it more likely someone runs into the back of your car. Dealers will reload the rear brake light software at no cost, and Hyundai is also sending the fix to cars wirelessly as an over-the-air update.

How serious is it?

A moderate concern that mainly raises the chance of being rear-ended; the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but get the update done soon.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Sonata is covered by recall 267.
2. Accept the over-the-air software update if your car offers it, or book a visit with your Hyundai dealer.
3. Call Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460 with questions — the fix is free.

Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING:BRAKE LIGHTS
Summary
Hyundai Motor America (Hyundai) is recalling certain 2024 Hyundai Sonata vehicles. The software for the brake lights may cause the brake lights to flash and not illuminate properly. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."
Safety risk
Flashing brake lights may cause confusion for other drivers on the road, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will update the rear brake light software, free of charge. Additionally, Hyundai will release the software update over-the-air (OTA). Owner notification letters were mailed September 12, 2024. Owners may contact Hyundai customer service at 1-855-371-9460. Hyundai's number for this recall is 267. (over-the-air software update)

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
