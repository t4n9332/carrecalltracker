Ford F-53 and F-59 chassis brake lights stay on

NHTSA campaign 26V155000 · Reported 2026-03-17 · Ford Motor Company

medium How serious is it? About this label

Moderate: the vehicle can still be driven, but other drivers get no warning when you brake, so have it checked soon and be extra careful in traffic.

What's wrong, in plain English

On these Ford F-53 and F-59 commercial chassis, the brake lights at the back can stay lit even when the driver's foot is off the brake pedal. Drivers behind you then have no way to tell when you are actually slowing or stopping, which can lead to a rear-end crash. Federal lighting rules do not allow this. A dealer will check the brake pedal and fix the push rod on the brake booster if needed, at no charge.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls (VINs have been searchable since March 20, 2026) to see if recall 26V155000 applies.
2. Contact your Ford dealer, or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332 (Ford recall number 26C17), to book the inspection.
3. Have the brake pedal inspected and the brake booster push rod repaired if needed — the work is free.

Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING:BRAKE LIGHTS
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2025-2026 F-59 and F-53 commercial stripped chassis vehicles. The exterior brake lights may remain illuminated when the driver is not applying the brakes. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."
Safety risk
Brake lights that remain illuminated can fail to alert other vehicles of the driver's intention, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect the brake pedal and repair the brake booster push rod as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed March 27, 2026. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 26C17. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning March 20, 2026.
