Ford and Lincoln EcoBoost engines can break, losing power

NHTSA campaign 24V635000 · Reported 2024-08-23 · Ford Motor Company

high How serious is it? About this label

Serious: the notice does not tell you to park the vehicle, but losing engine power while driving — especially at highway speed or while merging — raises the risk of a crash, so have the engine tested promptly.

What's wrong, in plain English

Some 2021-2022 Ford and Lincoln models with the 2.7L or 3.0L EcoBoost engine can have an intake valve break while you are driving. Those valves let air into the engine, and if one snaps, the engine can fail and stop making power. That means you could suddenly lose the ability to accelerate or hold speed in traffic. Dealers run a test on the engine and, if it fails the test, replace the whole engine at no charge.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is part of recall 24S55.
2. Contact your Ford or Lincoln dealer, or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332, to schedule the engine test (owner letters were mailed July 3, 2025).
3. Have the test done and, if the engine fails it, accept the free engine replacement.

Official notice

Component
ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING:ENGINE:HARD PARTS INTERNAL/MECHANICAL
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2021-2022 Bronco, F-150, Edge, Explorer, Lincoln Nautilus, and Lincoln Aviator vehicles equipped with either a 2.7L or 3.0L Nano EcoBoost engine. The engine intake valves may break while driving, which can result in engine failure and a loss of drive power.
Safety risk
A loss of drive power increases the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will perform an engine cycle test and replace the engine as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed July 3, 2025. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 24S55.
