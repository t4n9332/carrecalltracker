Porsche 911 rear seat belt may not hold child seat securely

NHTSA campaign 24V123000 · Reported 2024-02-20 · Porsche Cars North America, Inc.

medium How serious is it? About this label

A child seat that is not secured properly increases the risk of injury during a crash. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

Porsche is recalling certain 911 vehicles. The rear seat belts have a locking feature that is meant to hold a child seat firmly in place. On these vehicles that lock can switch off too early, so the child seat may not end up properly secured. Because of this, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety standard for occupant crash protection. Dealers will look at the rear three-point belts and replace them where needed.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether your vehicle is included.
2. Contact a Porsche dealer for the inspection, or call Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243 (Porsche's recall number is ARA2).
3. The inspection and any replacement are free of charge.

Official notice

Component
SEAT BELTS:REAR/OTHER:RETRACTOR
Summary
Porsche Cars North America, Inc. (Porsche) is recalling certain 2023 911 Carrera T, 911 Carrera 4 GTS, 911 Carrera GTS, 911 Turbo S, 911 Carrera 4 Cabriolet, 911 Carrera 4S Cabriolet, 911 Carrera S, 911 Turbo S Cabriolet, 911 Carrera 4, 911 Carrera 4 GTS Cabriolet, 911 Targa 4 GTS, 911 Carrera, 911 Carrera GTS Cabriolet, 911 Carrera 4S, 911 Targa 4S, 911 Turbo, and 911 Edition 50 Years Porsche Design vehicles. The seat belt automatic locking retractor (ALR) may deactivate early, which can prevent the child restraint system from securing properly. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 208, "Occupant Crash Protection."
Safety risk
An unsecured child restraint system can increase the risk of injury during a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the rear three-point belts as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed April 11, 2024. Owners may contact Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243. Porsche's number for this recall is ARA2.
