Subaru Impreza, Crosstrek valve breakup can kill engine power

NHTSA campaign 19V744000 · Reported 2019-10-17 · Subaru of America, Inc.

high How serious is it? About this label

Serious, because an unexpected loss of power while driving can lead to a crash; the notice does not tell owners to stop driving, but get the valve inspected soon.

What's wrong, in plain English

A small aluminum valve that lets pressure escape from the engine can come apart. The broken pieces can then be pulled into the engine, and the engine can lose power while you are driving. A dealer will look at the valve and replace it if needed. If the valve has already broken and the missing pieces cannot be located, Subaru will replace the entire engine. Everything, including an engine replacement, is free. Repairs have been available since December 2019.

What you should do

1. Enter your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm recall 19V744000 (Subaru's number WUP-01) applies to your car. 2. Book the inspection with a Subaru dealer, or call Subaru customer service at 1-844-373-6614. 3. The inspection, the new valve, and an engine replacement if needed are all free.

Official notice

Component
ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING:EXHAUST SYSTEM:EMISSION CONTROL:CRANKCASE (PCV)
Summary
Subaru of America, Inc. (Subaru) is recalling certain 2017-2018 Impreza and 2018 Crosstrek vehicles equipped with an aluminum Positive Crankcase Ventilation (PCV) valve. The PCV valve may come apart, allowing the separated components to enter the engine, possibly resulting in a loss of power while driving.
Safety risk
An unexpected loss of power while driving can increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Subaru will notify owners, and dealers will inspect and, if necessary, replace the PCV valve, free of charge. If the PCV valve has separated and the components cannot be found, the engine will be replaced, free of charge. The recall began December 2, 2019. Owners may contact Subaru customer service at 1-844-373-6614. Subaru's number for this recall is WUP-01.
