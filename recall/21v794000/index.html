Porsche Taycan hazard lights may flash when not needed

NHTSA campaign 21V794000 · Reported 2021-10-13 · Porsche Cars North America, Inc. · low severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2020 Porsche Taycan
  • 2021 Porsche Taycan

What's wrong, in plain English

These cars can turn on the hazard flashers automatically when the vehicle senses a possible collision ahead. On these vehicles, the flashers may switch on in ordinary driving situations when there is no emergency. Because federal rules cover how warning lamps behave, these cars do not meet that standard. Drivers around you may not understand why your flashers are on and may react in an unexpected way. Dealers fix this with a free software update.

How serious is it?

This is a lower-level concern because it can confuse drivers around you and slightly raise crash risk, and the notice does not tell owners to stop driving the car.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Taycan is covered. 2. Schedule the free reprogramming with a Porsche dealer, or call Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243 (recall AMC1). 3. If the flashers come on unexpectedly before the fix, switch them off once you confirm there is no emergency.

Official notice

Component
EXTERIOR LIGHTING:HAZARD FLASHING WARNING LIGHTS
Summary
Porsche Cars North America, Inc. (Porsche) is recalling certain 2020-2021 Taycan vehicles. The pre-collision automatic hazard lights may activate in non-emergency situations. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."
Safety risk
An activation of hazard lights in non-emergency situations could confuse other drivers, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will reprogram the hazard lights, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 2, 2021. Owners may contact Porsche customer service at 1-800-767-7243. Porsche's number for this recall is AMC1.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
