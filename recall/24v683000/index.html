Ford Bronco Sport software may miss low battery charge

NHTSA campaign 24V683000 · Reported 2024-09-13 · Ford Motor Company

high How serious is it? About this label

Serious — a sudden loss of drive power or hazard lights can put you in a crash, so schedule the update soon rather than putting it off.

What's wrong, in plain English

The computers that manage your engine and electrical system may not notice when the 12-volt battery is running low. If the charge drops without being detected, things can stop working — including your hazard lights — or the vehicle can lose drive power while you are on the road. The fix is a free software update at the dealer. Important: if your vehicle already had the earlier repair for this same problem under recall 24V-267, it still needs this new fix.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, and check it again even if you already had the earlier 24V-267 repair. 2. Contact your Ford dealer or Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332 (recall 24S60) to book the software update. 3. Have it done — there is no charge.

Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION:CONTROL MODULE (TCM/PCM/TECM)
Summary
Ford Motor Company (Ford) is recalling certain 2024 Bronco Sport vehicles. The body and power train control modules may fail to detect a change in the 12-volt battery state of charge.
Safety risk
An undetected low battery charge can result in a loss of electrical accessories such as hazard lights, or cause a loss of drive power, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will update the body control module and powertrain control module software, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed October 23, 2024. Owners may contact Ford customer service at 1-866-436-7332. Ford's number for this recall is 24S60. Vehicles in this recall that were previously recalled for the same issue under 24V-267 will need to have the new remedy performed.
