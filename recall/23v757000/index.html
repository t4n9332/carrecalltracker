Toyota Tundra label lists wrong load carrying capacity

NHTSA campaign 23V757000 · Reported 2023-11-09 · Southeast Toyota Distributors, LLC · low severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Toyota Tundra
  • 2023 Toyota Tundra
  • 2024 Toyota Tundra

What's wrong, in plain English

There is a sticker on your Tundra that tells you how much weight the truck can safely carry once passengers and cargo are added up. On these trucks that sticker shows the wrong number. If you trust it and load up to that figure, you could end up carrying more weight than the truck and its tires are built to handle. Nothing is mechanically broken. The distributor will mail you a corrected sticker to put in place of the wrong one.

How serious is it?

This is a paperwork problem, not a mechanical one, and the truck is safe to drive as long as you do not load it near the limit printed on the current label.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Tundra is covered by campaign 23V757000 (Southeast Toyota's number SET23B).
2. Watch your mail for the free replacement label, or call Southeast Toyota customer service at 1-866-405-4226 if it has not arrived.
3. Apply the new label over the old one, and avoid heavy loads until you have the correct figure.

Official notice

Component
EQUIPMENT:OTHER:LABELS
Summary
Southeast Toyota Distributors, LLC (SET) is recalling certain 2022-2024 Toyota Tundra vehicles. The load carrying capacity modification label states the incorrect load carrying capacity, which can result in an overloaded vehicle. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 110, "Tire Selection and Rims."
Safety risk
An overloaded vehicle increases the risk of a crash.
Remedy
SET will mail a new modified label, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 18, 2023. Owners may contact SET's customer service at 1-866-405-4226. SET's number for this recall is SET23B.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
