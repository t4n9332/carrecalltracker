Jeep Wagoneer door trim can come off while driving

NHTSA campaign 25V593000 · Reported 2025-09-11 · Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) · medium severity

Affected vehicles

  • 2022 Jeep Grand Wagoneer
  • 2022 Jeep Wagoneer
  • 2023 Jeep Grand Wagoneer
  • 2023 Jeep Wagoneer
  • 2024 Jeep Grand Wagoneer
  • 2024 Jeep Wagoneer
  • 2025 Jeep Grand Wagoneer
  • 2025 Jeep Wagoneer

What's wrong, in plain English

The decorative trim strips on the driver and passenger doors were not attached properly on some of these Jeeps, and they can work loose and fall off. A piece of trim lying in the road, or flying off at speed, is a hazard for the drivers behind you who have to swerve or run over it. Dealers will look at the door trim and replace it where it is needed, free of charge.

How serious is it?

Your own vehicle stays safe to drive, but detached trim can cause a crash for other drivers, so have it checked at your next convenient opportunity.

What you should do

1. Look up your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your Jeep is covered by campaign 25V593000.
2. Contact your Jeep dealer for an inspection appointment, or call FCA customer service at 1-800-853-1403 (recall 77C).
3. Have the door trim inspected and replaced if needed — there is no charge.

Official notice

Component
STRUCTURE:EXTERIOR TRIM
Summary
Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) is recalling certain 2022-2025 Jeep Wagoneer and Jeep Grand Wagoneer vehicles. The trim on the driver and passenger doors may not have been installed properly, which can allow it to detach.
Safety risk
Detached trim pieces can create a road hazard for other vehicles, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will inspect and replace the door trim, as necessary, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed September 25, 2025. Owners may contact FCA customer service at 1-800-853-1403. FCA's number for this recall is 77C. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning September 18, 2025.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
