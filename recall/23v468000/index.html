Toyota Tundra, Sequoia Spare Tire May Detach

NHTSA campaign 23V468000 · Reported Jul 7, 2023 · Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing

high How serious is it? About this label

A spare tire detaching from the vehicle raises the chance of a crash. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

The steel chain that holds the spare tire in place underneath the vehicle may not have been welded strongly enough during manufacturing. A chain that is not welded well enough can break. If the chain breaks, it can no longer hold the spare tire in place, which would allow the tire to detach and fall away from the vehicle while it is being driven.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls to confirm your vehicle is included.
2. Contact a dealer, or call Toyota customer service at 1-800-331-4331.
3. Dealers will replace the spare tire carrier assembly free of charge.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 23V468000. Official notice

Component
TIRES:TEMPORARY/EMERGENCY SPARE TIRE
Summary
Toyota Motor Engineering & Manufacturing (Toyota) is recalling certain 2023 Tundra, Tundra Hybrid, and Sequoia Hybrid vehicles. The steel chain used to secure the spare tire to the underside of the vehicle may have been insufficiently welded.
Safety risk
Chains that are not welded sufficiently can break and allow the spare tire to detach, increasing the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Dealers will replace the spare tire carrier assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed August 14, 2023. Owners may contact Toyota's customer service at 1-800-331-4331. Toyota's number for this recall is 23TB06/23TA06.

Verify your specific vehicle by VIN at nhtsa.gov. Recall repairs are free of charge by law.
