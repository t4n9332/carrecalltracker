Honda Odyssey Transmission Computer May Shift Into Park Unexpectedly

NHTSA campaign 19V299000 · Reported Apr 12, 2019 · Honda (American Honda Motor Co.)

medium How serious is it? About this label

A damaged parking rod can let the vehicle roll after it's parked, which increases the risk of a crash or injury. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

On certain Honda Odyssey vehicles, a loose battery connection or a weak battery can cause the transmission's control computer to unexpectedly restart. When this happens, the computer may shift the transmission into Park on its own, and doing so can damage the parking rod. If the parking rod is damaged, the vehicle may not stay in place after being parked and could roll, increasing the risk of a crash or injury.

Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 19V299000. Official notice

Component
POWER TRAIN:AUTOMATIC TRANSMISSION
Summary
Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2018-2019 Odyssey vehicles. Loose battery terminal connections or a degraded battery may cause the Transmission Control Unit (TCU) to unexpectedly reboot. Upon rebooting, the TCU may shift the transmission to the "Park" position, possibly damaging the parking rod.
Safety risk
A damaged parking rod can allow the vehicle to roll when parked, increasing the risk of a crash or injury.
Remedy
Honda will notify owners, and dealers will ensure the battery terminal connections are secure and will update the TCU software, free of charge. Additionally, the parking rod effectiveness will be checked. The recall began May 31, 2019. Owners may contact Honda customer service at 1-888-234-2138. Honda's numbers for this recall are Z4J, Y4K.
