Honda Accord, CR-V Hybrid, Insight Power Loss Risk

NHTSA campaign 20V798000 · Reported Dec 17, 2020 · Honda (American Honda Motor Co.)

high How serious is it? About this label

Honda says a loss of drive power can increase the risk of a crash. The notice does not tell owners to stop driving.

What's wrong, in plain English

Honda says the DC-DC converter in some CR-V Hybrid, Accord Hybrid, and Insight vehicles may shut down on its own. When the converter shuts down, it can stop the 12-volt battery from being recharged while the vehicle is being driven. If the 12-volt battery isn't recharging, the vehicle may end up losing the drive power it needs to keep moving down the road.

What you should do

1. Check your VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
2. Contact a Honda dealer or call 1-888-234-2138 to schedule the software update.
3. The repair is free.
Where do I find my VIN, and what do I tell the dealer?

Your VIN is the 17-character code on the lower left of the windshield, on your registration and usually on your insurance card. Enter it at nhtsa.gov/recalls to see whether this recall is still open on your car. When you call the dealer, say you have an open safety recall and give them NHTSA campaign number 20V798000. Official notice

Component
ELECTRICAL SYSTEM:PROPULSION SYSTEM:DC/DC CONVERTER
Summary
Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2020 CR-V Hybrid, Accord Hybrid, and 2020-2021 Insight vehicles. The DC-DC converter may shut down and prevent the 12-volt battery from recharging, possibly resulting in a loss of drive power.
Safety risk
A loss of drive power can increase the risk of a crash.
Remedy
Honda will notify owners, and dealers will update the power converter unit (PCU) software, free of charge. The recall began February 12, 2021. Owners can contact Honda customer service at 1-888-234-2138. Honda's number for this recall is K9E.
