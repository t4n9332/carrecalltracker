Buying a used 2023 Toyota Highlander Hybrid? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 9 owner complaints on file

This model year carries six recalls: two high-severity recalls involving a steering defect that may disable the airbag, a high-severity issue where the second-row seat back may not lock in place, two medium-severity items covering a front bumper cover that may detach and a rearview camera that may freeze, and a low-severity accessory weight label error. NHTSA has logged 9 owner complaints (unverified reports), with 0 crashes, 0 fires, and 0 injuries. The most-cited complaint areas are structure (3), seats (1), vehicle speed control (1), and electrical system (1).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm both steering-defect airbag recalls have been completed
  3. Confirm the second-row seat back recall has been addressed
  4. Ask about structure or seat-related issues, the top complaint areas
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
