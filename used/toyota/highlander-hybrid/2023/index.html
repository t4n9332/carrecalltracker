Buying a used 2023 Toyota Highlander Hybrid? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 9 owner complaints on file

This year carries two separate high-severity driver's airbag recalls — steering wiring that can disable it, and vehicles that may have shipped with it already disabled — plus second-row seat backs that may not lock. Rearview camera freezing, a loosening bumper cover, and Gulf States weight capacity labels fill out the list. Nine complaints were filed with NHTSA, unverified owner reports with no crashes, fires or injuries; structure and seats lead the areas cited.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Run the VIN and confirm both driver's airbag recalls were repaired, not just noticed
  3. Check the airbag warning light behavior at startup and turn the wheel lock to lock
  4. Test the backup camera repeatedly for freezing and re-latch the second-row seats
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
