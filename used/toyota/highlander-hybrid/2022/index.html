Buying a used 2022 Toyota Highlander Hybrid? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 6 owner complaints on file

A lighter recall year than 2021: the passenger airbag campaign drops off, leaving second-row seat backs that may not lock, a hybrid loss of stability control, a bumper cover that can loosen or fall off, and weight capacity labels that can fade. None advise against driving or parking indoors. Just six complaints appear in NHTSA's file — unverified owner reports with no crashes, fires or injuries — spread thinly across electrical, engine, brakes and steering.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Fold and re-latch both second-row seat backs to confirm they lock
  3. Ask for recall completion records on the seat back and stability control campaigns
  4. Read the door-jamb weight label; ask for a replacement if it has faded
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
