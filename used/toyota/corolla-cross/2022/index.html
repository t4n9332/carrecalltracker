Buying a used 2022 Toyota Corolla Cross? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 46 owner complaints on file

The recall record is narrow and repetitive: both listed actions concern the front passenger airbag possibly not opening properly, so the same defect area accounts for the model year's entire recall history. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside advisory. Owner complaints to NHTSA are modest in number but include crashes, injuries and one fire; they cluster in engine and power train rather than airbags, and these reports are unverified.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the passenger airbag recall work is closed out on the VIN
  3. Check the passenger airbag status light with someone in that seat
  4. Ask about engine and transmission history — the top complaint areas
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
