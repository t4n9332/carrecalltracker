Buying a used 2023 Toyota Highlander? Check these 9 recalls first

9 NHTSA recalls · 179 owner complaints on file

The 2023 Highlander carries a heavy recall load for a recent model year: nine campaigns, five rated high, including two separate driver's airbag deactivation issues tied to steering wiring, two tire load-rating errors (one specific to the Blackout Package), and second-row seat backs that may not lock. None carry stop-driving or park-outside warnings. NHTSA lists 179 unverified owner complaints with 12 crashes and 12 injuries; structure, engine, and power train are the largest identifiable areas.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Run the VIN through NHTSA or a dealer to confirm both airbag wiring recalls were completed.
  3. Check whether it is a Blackout Package and that the tires match the door-jamb load rating.
  4. Fold and latch both second-row seat backs, and check the bumper cover and weight capacity label.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
