Buying a used 2022 Toyota Tundra? Check these 13 recalls first

13 NHTSA recalls · 428 owner complaints on file

This model year has ten recalls, including a critical fuel tube that may chafe and leak, plus high-severity issues covering axle shaft nuts, parking brake engagement, an instrument display software bug, unexpected movement in neutral, and engine stalling from debris or bearing failure. Medium-severity items cover a rearview camera that may not display and a detaching tonneau cover. NHTSA has logged 428 owner complaints (unverified reports), including 5 crashes and 3 injuries; top categories are engine (164), power train (104), fuel/propulsion system (75), and unknown or other (44).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the fuel tube chafe/leak recall was completed
  3. Verify axle shaft nut and parking brake recalls were addressed
  4. Ask about engine stalling history (debris or bearing failure)
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
