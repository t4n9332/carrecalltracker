Buying a used 2020 Toyota Tundra? Check these 5 recalls first

5 NHTSA recalls · 45 owner complaints on file

Five recalls cover this year, two rated critical: headlight wiring that can overheat and cause a fire, and a steering component that can leak fluid. Two more address a Toyota/Lexus fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine, plus possible loss of power steering assist. Dim front turn signals are the minor one. NHTSA logged 45 unverified owner complaints, clustered in fuel/propulsion and engine, with four crashes, one fire and three injuries.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Run the VIN through NHTSA and ask for receipts showing the headlight wiring and fuel pump recalls were completed.
  3. Inspect the steering rack and lines for leaks or seepage, and note any heavy or inconsistent steering effort.
  4. Drive long enough to check for stalling or hesitation, and confirm both front turn signals flash at normal brightness.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
