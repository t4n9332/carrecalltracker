Buying a used 2018 Toyota Sienna? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 70 owner complaints on file

The recall list splits in two: fixes for all Siennas — alloy wheels that can crack, a fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (covered by two separate campaigns), and a low-severity floor-mat weight-capacity label — and three that apply only to wheelchair-conversion or steering-extension-modified vans, involving rear braking power, rubbing brake hoses and cracking steering extensions. Of 70 unverified NHTSA owner complaints, power train, fuel/propulsion and electrical lead; 5 crashes and 6 injuries were reported.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Inspect all four alloy wheels for cracks and confirm the wheel recall was performed on this VIN.
  3. Ask whether the fuel pump recall was completed and about any stalling or hard-start history.
  4. Determine if the van is a wheelchair conversion or has steering extensions; if so, verify the rear brake and steering recalls.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
