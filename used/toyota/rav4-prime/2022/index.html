Buying a used 2022 Toyota RAV4 Prime? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 21 owner complaints on file

Carries the same critical power converter overheat and fire recall as the 2021 car, but without the cold-weather stall recall; it adds a high-severity passenger seat sensor that may misread the occupant, plus a medium stability control recall. Owner reports are far fewer than 2021 — 21 unverified complaints filed with NHTSA, with five crashes and two injuries. Electrical system leads with seven, followed by unknown or other, service brakes and power train.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Check the VIN and get documentation that the power converter recall was performed
  3. With a passenger seated, confirm the passenger air bag status light reads correctly
  4. Ask about any electrical, charging or braking faults, and whether warning lights have been cleared
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
