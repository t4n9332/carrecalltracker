Buying a used 2023 Toyota RAV4 Prime? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 62 owner complaints on file

Both open recalls on this model year are display-related: the instrument panel may not show speed or warning lights, and the rearview camera image can freeze. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning. Owner complaints filed with NHTSA — unverified reports — number 62, with electrical system by far the largest category at 41, plus smaller counts for vehicle speed control and forward collision avoidance. Five of those reports mention a crash, one an injury.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for dealer paperwork showing both the instrument-panel and rearview-camera recalls were completed
  3. At startup, confirm the dash shows speed and all warning lights before they extinguish
  4. Shift into reverse several times and watch that the camera image refreshes rather than freezing
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
