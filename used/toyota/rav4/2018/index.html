Buying a used 2018 Toyota RAV4? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 331 owner complaints on file

The standout on this year is a critical recall for a replacement battery that can shift out of position and cause a fire; because it involves a service part, what matters is whether the battery currently fitted was addressed. The other two recalls are routine load-capacity label corrections. Complaints are heavy for the year — 331 unverified owner reports to NHTSA, 32 citing a fire and 31 a crash — and electrical system dominates the categories at 129.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether the battery is original or a replacement, and whether the fire recall was performed
  3. Open the hood and check the battery sits square and is held by its factory hold-down
  4. Look for the corrected weight-capacity label on the driver's door jamb, including with floor mats fitted
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Toyota dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
