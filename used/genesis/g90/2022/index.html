Buying a used 2022 Genesis G90? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 4 owner complaints on file

A short list for this year: one critical turbocharger oil leak recall tied to fire risk, and a high-severity fuel-pump failure campaign that could leave the car stranded. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning. Only four owner complaints reached NHTSA — unverified reports with no crashes, fires or injuries — scattered singly across engine, electrical, rear-camera and unclassified categories, with no clear pattern. The two recalls, not the complaint record, are what needs documenting here.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify the turbo oil leak recall was performed and check for oil residue underhood.
  3. Ask whether the fuel pump remedy was applied, and about any stalling or no-start events.
  4. Test the rear camera and reverse detection systems in a parking lot.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Genesis dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
