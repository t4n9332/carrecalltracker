Buying a used 2023 BMW X5? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 31 owner complaints on file

Only four recalls here, but one is serious: a windshield leak that can cause a fire. A second covers sudden loss of drive power, shared with other BMW SUVs and the Toyota Supra. The remaining two are interior fixes — a loose driver seat back bolt and second-row head restraints missing locking tabs. Owners filed 31 unverified NHTSA complaints, spread across electrical, power train and engine, with no fires reported.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for proof the windshield leak recall was completed, and check headliner, A-pillars and footwells for water staining or damp carpet.
  3. Ask whether the car has ever stalled or lost power while driving, and whether the power-loss recall remedy was applied.
  4. Push and pull the driver's seat back for play, and confirm the second-row head restraints lock in position.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
