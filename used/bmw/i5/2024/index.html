Buying a used 2024 BMW I5? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 20 owner complaints on file

Seven recalls, and braking dominates: one critical campaign for loss of brake assist power plus two high-severity ones covering power assist loss and integrated brakes needing inspection. A separate critical recall covers battery overheating and fire risk, and software recalls address motor power cuts, the hands-on steering sensor and the pedestrian warning sound. NHTSA's 20 owner complaints lean the same way, led by service brakes, stability control, electrical and hydraulic. Complaints are unverified reports, but the alignment is notable.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get dealer records showing all three brake recalls, including the inspection, were completed
  3. Ask whether any brake pedal feel or warning light complaints were reported by the owner
  4. Confirm the battery overheating recall remedy and the motor software update were applied
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
