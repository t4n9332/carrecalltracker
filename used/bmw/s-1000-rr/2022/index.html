Buying a used 2022 BMW S 1000 RR? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 4 owner complaints on file

The standout here is a critical-severity recall for clutch cover bolts that can loosen and let oil leak; alongside it is the same ignition lock failure that can stall the engine. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Only four owner complaints are on file with NHTSA — unverified reports — but one lists an injury, with no crashes or fires. Complaint areas are engine (3), electrical, fuel/propulsion, and unspecified, overlapping the clutch cover issue.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get service records showing the clutch cover bolt recall was done, and look for oil residue around the cover and on the rear tire.
  3. Verify the ignition lock recall repair and check for any stalling history during the test ride.
  4. Ask whether the bike has had electrical or fuel-system work, since those show up in the small complaint pool.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
