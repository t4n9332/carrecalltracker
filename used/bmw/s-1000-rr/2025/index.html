Buying a used 2025 BMW S 1000 RR? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 1 owner complaints on file

As a current-generation bike, the record is short. One high-severity recall covers the ignition lock failure that can stall the engine; a medium-severity recall covers BMW motorcycles whose emergency call system was switched off by software. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. NHTSA shows a single owner complaint — an unverified report — with no crashes, fires, or injuries, noted under electrical and unspecified areas. Recall status and software level are the practical things to verify.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the ignition lock recall was performed and that the engine does not cut out during a test ride.
  3. Check that the emergency call (eCall) system is active and that the software update addressing it was installed.
  4. Ask the seller for the dealer service history and run the VIN for any recall still open.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
