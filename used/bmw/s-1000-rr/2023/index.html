Buying a used 2023 BMW S 1000 RR? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 2 owner complaints on file

This year carries two high-severity recalls: a hand brake lever that can reduce stopping power, and the ignition lock failure that can stall the engine. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The NHTSA complaint record is thin — two filings, all unverified owner reports, with no crashes, fires, or injuries — touching electrical, engine, and unspecified areas. With so few complaints, recall paperwork and a hands-on brake feel check matter more than complaint trends.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the hand brake lever recall was completed and test front brake lever firmness and stopping power.
  3. Verify the ignition lock recall repair and watch for stalling or ignition cut-out on the ride.
  4. Ask about any electrical faults or warning lights, the area most named in the few complaints filed.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
