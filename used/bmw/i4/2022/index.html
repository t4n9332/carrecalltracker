Buying a used 2022 BMW I4? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 9 owner complaints on file · 1 do-not-drive · 1 park-outside

This is the heaviest recall year for the i4, with eight campaigns. One is a critical battery fire risk that carried both a do-not-drive and park-outside warning; two more critical ones cover sudden loss of drive power and failing battery modules. The rest range from power loss while driving to valet mode hiding warning lights, a missing pedestrian sound, and doors not auto-locking. NHTSA shows nine unverified owner complaints, three crashes and two injuries, mostly electrical.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm in writing that the do-not-drive battery fire recall was fully remedied
  3. Ask whether battery modules were replaced or inspected under the module recall
  4. Verify the power loss, valet mode, pedestrian sound and door lock software fixes
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
