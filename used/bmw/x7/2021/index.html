Buying a used 2021 BMW X7? Check these 9 recalls first

9 NHTSA recalls · 46 owner complaints on file

The fire-risk recall here is a 48-volt battery connection that can overheat, a different cause than the earlier starter motor campaigns. Also open: a steering column bolt that may loosen and the brake unit failure, plus rear head restraints that can detach and the recurring sunroof and third-row seat items. Of 46 unverified owner complaints, engine and power train lead with 11 each; two reported fires and one injury.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether the 48-volt battery connection recall has been completed.
  3. Confirm the steering column bolt and brake unit recalls were performed.
  4. Tug the rear head restraints and check the tire placard matches the fitted tires.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
