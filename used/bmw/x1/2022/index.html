Buying a used 2022 BMW X1? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 1 owner complaints on file

Two recalls stand out despite an almost empty complaint file: a critical-severity fuel rail that can crack on the X1, X3 and 228i, and a high-severity defect where the driver airbag may not deploy on the iX and X1. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. NHTSA lists only one owner complaint — an unverified report — with no crashes, fires, or injuries, filed under engine. Recall completion, not complaint volume, is what to focus on here.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get documentation that the fuel rail recall was completed, and ask about any fuel smell or hard starting.
  3. Confirm the driver airbag recall repair and check that no airbag warning light is showing.
  4. Ask about the single engine-related concern pattern: any check-engine lights or engine work in the service history.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
