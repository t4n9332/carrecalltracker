Buying a used 2023 BMW X1? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 70 owner complaints on file

Brakes dominate this year. Five of the six recalls involve braking: a critical-severity loss of brake assist power, plus separate high-severity recalls for loss of power assist, a brake module failure that can weaken brakes and stability control, and integrated brakes needing inspection; a medium-severity recall covers rear seat belts pinched by trim. NHTSA shows 70 owner complaints — unverified reports — with 2 crashes and no fires or injuries, led by service brakes (53), ESC (31), and hydraulic (22).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for repair orders covering each brake recall individually, since several separate brake actions apply to this year.
  3. Test drive specifically for a hard or unassisted brake pedal, and check for brake or stability-control warning lights.
  4. Inspect the rear seat belts for trim pinching and confirm that recall was completed.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
