Buying a used 2018 BMW X1? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 59 owner complaints on file

This is the busiest S 1000-era X1 year on record. Four recalls apply, led by a critical-severity steering tie rod that may break and a high-severity crankshaft sensor fault that can stall the engine, plus taillights that can loosen and roof pillar trim that may not cushion head impact. NHTSA carries 59 owner complaints — unverified reports — citing 6 crashes, 2 fires, and 6 injuries, concentrated in structure (22), electrical (8), and air bags (6).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get written confirmation the steering tie rod recall was completed, and have the steering and front suspension inspected.
  3. Ask whether the crankshaft sensor recall was done and whether the car has ever stalled while driving.
  4. Inspect taillight mounting and roof pillar trim, and ask about any airbag or structural repairs given the complaint pattern.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
