Buying a used 2020 BMW M340I? Check these 10 recalls first

10 NHTSA recalls · 53 owner complaints on file

This year carries the heaviest recall load in this group — ten campaigns, including a critical starter motor fire risk, plus high-severity tie rod breakage, loss of power brake assist, and poorly welded seat frames. None carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. Complaint volume is also the highest here: 53 unverified NHTSA reports, no crashes, fires or injuries logged, but 38 concern the engine — an area none of the listed recalls addresses.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get a full VIN recall report and confirm the starter motor fire recall is closed
  3. Ask about engine repair history and any warranty or goodwill work, given the 38 engine complaints
  4. Verify the tie rod, brake assist, and seat frame weld recalls were all performed
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
