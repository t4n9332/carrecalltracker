Buying a used 2018 BMW K 1600 GT? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 5 owner complaints on file

Two critical recalls apply: rear wheel lock-up risk and the K 1600 touring fire campaign, neither issued with a stop-riding or park-outside instruction. Complaint volume is low at 5 unverified NHTSA reports, but this is the only vehicle in this group where injuries appear — 2 injuries reported, with no crashes or fires logged. Complaint areas are power train (3), unclassified (2) and steering (1). Treat those injury entries as unverified owner claims.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get dealer confirmation by VIN that the rear wheel and fire recalls were completed
  3. Ask directly whether the bike has been down or had any accident or injury history
  4. Test ride for driveline noise, shifting problems and steering feel
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
