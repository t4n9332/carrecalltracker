Buying a used 2025 BMW X3? Check these 9 recalls first

9 NHTSA recalls · 35 owner complaints on file

The 2025 X3 carries an unusually long recall list for a new model: nine campaigns spanning a battery cable connection that can overheat, loose starter wiring that can stall the engine, an incorrectly attached steering shaft, a steering wheel that may turn by itself, two airbag defects (shipping-damaged passenger airbag fabric, misassembled side curtain), a cracking front left wheel bearing, and leaking rear brake hoses plus damaged front brake discs. NHTSA shows 35 unverified owner complaints — 3 crashes, 1 fire — led by electrical (12), steering (8) and forward collision avoidance (8).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get a dealer printout by VIN showing which of the nine recalls are closed and which are still open.
  3. Ask whether the brake hoses, brake discs and front left wheel bearing were inspected or replaced.
  4. Drive it long enough to feel for steering pull or self-steering, and note any false forward-collision alerts.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
