Buying a used 2023 BMW X3? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 12 owner complaints on file

A shorter list than the years before it: one critical recall for a starter that can overheat and catch fire, plus a sudden loss of drive power affecting BMW SUVs, rear doors that can unlock and open unexpectedly, and a low-severity cargo rail item. None carries a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. With 12 unverified complaints (1 crash, 1 injury) spread thinly across structure, electrical, engine and steering, no clear owner-reported pattern has formed yet.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the starter fire recall is closed by VIN
  3. Ask whether the vehicle has ever lost power or gone into limp mode while driving
  4. Test the rear doors for the unlocking recall and confirm the repair
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
