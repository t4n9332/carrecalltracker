Buying a used 2021 BMW X6? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 7 owner complaints on file

Fire and tire risk lead this year: a 48-volt battery connection that can overheat and a Pirelli tire recall for sudden air loss. A steering column bolt that may loosen and a brake unit failure are also covered, alongside backup camera, sunroof and blue-headlight recalls. Only 7 unverified NHTSA complaints exist — among them service brakes, electrical and forward collision avoidance reports — too few to read as a reliability pattern either way.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the 48-volt battery connection recall was completed and ask about any electrical or charging faults.
  3. Check whether the recalled Pirelli tires are still fitted, and inspect the date codes and brand on all four.
  4. Ask about the steering column bolt and brake unit recalls; note steering play or brake warnings on the test drive.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
