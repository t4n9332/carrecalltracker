Buying a used 2019 BMW X6? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 5 owner complaints on file

Two suspension-related recalls stand out: rear control arms that can break apart, and wheel bolts that may not have been tightened properly. A medium recall covers child seat anchor bars that can become damaged, and a low-priority one addresses a backup camera image that can vanish. Only 5 unverified NHTSA complaints are on file, but they include one crash, one fire and two injuries — a very small sample rather than an established pattern.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the rear control arm recall was performed, and have the rear suspension inspected for play or damaged bushings.
  3. Ask for documentation on the wheel bolt recall and have the bolts torque-checked.
  4. Test the backup camera through several ignition cycles and check the child seat anchor bars.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any BMW dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
