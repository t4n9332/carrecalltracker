Buying a used 2022 Tesla Model 3? Check these 18 recalls first

18 NHTSA recalls · 764 owner complaints on file

This model year has 10 recalls, mostly software-related: Full Self-Driving Beta and Autosteer misuse, FSD rolling through stop signs, a seat belt chime that may not sound, reduced windshield defrosting power, and the Boombox pedestrian-sound feature (listed twice). Hardware items include reassembled rear seat belts, a hood latch that may not detect an unlatched hood, and headlights described as too bright. NHTSA logged 764 owner complaints (unverified reports), including 53 crashes, 4 fires, and 45 injuries. Top complaint categories are forward collision avoidance (532), vehicle speed control (171), service brakes (167), and unknown or other (91).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether all listed software recalls have been applied, including the FSD and windshield defrost updates
  3. Confirm the hood latch recall was completed
  4. Test the seat belt chime and rear seat belt assembly
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Tesla dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
