Buying a used 2022 Tesla Model 3? Check these 18 recalls first

18 NHTSA recalls · 764 owner complaints on file

The recall list shifts away from suspension and brake fasteners toward feature and display issues: hood latch warning failure, FSD Beta behavior, a weaker defroster, center screen freezing after fast charging, pedestrian warning sound silenced by a factory reset, a tire warning light fault, plus rear seat belts possibly reassembled wrong. NHTSA has 764 unverified complaints (53 crashes, 4 fires, 45 injuries), with forward collision avoidance at 532 far ahead of speed control and service brakes.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether a factory reset was ever performed and confirm the pedestrian warning sound is audible at low speed.
  3. Fast charge or check for center screen freezing, and verify the defroster, hood latch warning and tire pressure warning light all function.
  4. Ask if the rear seats were ever removed or serviced, given the seat belt reassembly recall.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Tesla dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
