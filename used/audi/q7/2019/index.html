Buying a used 2019 Audi Q7? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 61 owner complaints on file

The recall and complaint records point the same direction. A critical recall covers sudden loss of steering control, and a high-severity one covers front shock components that can crack, alongside side curtain airbags that may not inflate properly and roof pillar padding. Of 61 unverified NHTSA complaints, the two largest groups are steering and suspension at 10 each, with electrical and fuel/propulsion behind. Five of those reports involve crashes, though no injuries are recorded in the file.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the steering recall remedy is documented for this VIN
  3. Have the front shock components inspected for cracks before buying
  4. On the test drive, note any steering warning lights, wandering or unusual effort
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Audi dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
