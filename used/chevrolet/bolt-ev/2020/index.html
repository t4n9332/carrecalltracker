Buying a used 2020 Chevrolet Bolt EV? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 172 owner complaints on file · 3 park-outside

This model year carries eight recalls, including three critical battery fire risk campaigns tied to charging plus a seat belt pretensioner fire risk, alongside a rear door that may open while driving, a brake caliper that may fracture, incorrect seat belt bolts, and a post-crash seat belt fire risk. NHTSA has logged 172 owner complaints, including 7 crashes and 4 fires reported, though these are unverified accounts. Electrical system leads at 93, followed by fuel/propulsion system (30), steering (22), and unknown or other (21).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm all battery fire risk recalls and any battery replacement have been completed
  3. Verify the brake caliper and seat belt bolt recalls have been repaired
  4. Ask about electrical system complaint history, the largest category for this year
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
