Buying a used 2018 Chevrolet Malibu? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 695 owner complaints on file

This model year carries six recalls, including one critical repair for missing transmission bolts that GM says can cause a leak and fire risk. Other recalls cover a fuel pump that may detach, a passenger air bag sensor defect, a rear brake issue, a software error that can shut off fuel injectors, and a loose seat belt. NHTSA has logged 695 owner complaints (unverified reports), with 23 crashes, 4 fires, and 17 injuries cited; engine and electrical system are the most-mentioned categories.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the transmission bolt recall (leak/fire risk) has been completed
  3. Ask if the fuel pump and air bag sensor recalls were addressed
  4. Ask about any engine or electrical system repair history
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
