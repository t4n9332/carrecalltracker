Buying a used 2023 Chevrolet Suburban? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 123 owner complaints on file

Three recalls, none flagged critical: rear suspension bolts that can break, potential 6.2L V8 engine failure, and daytime running lights that stay on. The engine issue is the one with the most weight behind it — 65 of the 123 unverified owner complaints filed with NHTSA cite the engine, with power train and electrical well behind. No stop-driving or park-outside instructions apply. Reported outcomes include 1 crash and 4 injuries, and no fires.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for engine service records; the 6.2L V8 failure recall applies
  3. Have the rear suspension bolts inspected for the recall remedy
  4. Verify the daytime running lights switch off as intended
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
