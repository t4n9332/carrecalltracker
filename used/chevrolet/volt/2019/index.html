Buying a used 2019 Chevrolet Volt? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 34 owner complaints on file

This year shares the 2018 Volt's two moderate recalls — potentially weak rear brakes and rear seat belts that may not lock. Owner complaints drop sharply to 34 unverified NHTSA reports, but the pattern is similar: power train and electrical system lead, with three crashes and one injury among them. Lower volume can reflect fewer of these cars on the road rather than a better record, so weigh it accordingly.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the rear brake and seat belt recalls were performed by a dealer.
  3. Check rear belt retractors lock under a quick pull on all seating positions.
  4. Ask for hybrid drivetrain service history and any charging or propulsion fault codes.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
