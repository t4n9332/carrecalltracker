Buying a used 2023 Chevrolet Traverse? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 87 owner complaints on file

Two recalls, both drivetrain: an axle shaft that can come apart and a transmission gear that can cause loss of power. That matches where complaints land — powertrain is the largest category at 29 of 87 unverified filings. The overall count is low for the model year, but 5 crashes and 8 injuries were reported within it, a higher share than the older Traverse years listed here.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the axle shaft and transmission gear recalls were completed on this VIN
  3. Test drive under acceleration and listen for clicking, clunking or vibration from the driveline
  4. Ask whether the vehicle has ever lost power or gone into reduced-power mode
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
