Buying a used 2019 Chevrolet Tahoe? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 105 owner complaints on file · 1 do-not-drive

This model year carries a critical recall for wheels that can lock up, flagged by GM as a stop-driving issue, plus a recall covering unexpected braking and pulling in Chevrolet/GMC trucks and SUVs. NHTSA has logged 105 owner complaints for this model year, including 8 crashes, 6 injuries, and 0 fires; these are unverified consumer reports, not confirmed defects. Complaint categories cluster in power train (38), engine (29), fuel/propulsion system (25), and electrical system (16).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the wheel lock-up (stop-driving) recall has been completed
  3. Ask whether the brake pull/unexpected braking recall was performed
  4. Ask about any power train, engine, or fuel system repair history
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
