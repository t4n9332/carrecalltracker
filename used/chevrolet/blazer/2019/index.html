Buying a used 2019 Chevrolet Blazer? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 186 owner complaints on file

Two open recall campaigns define this year: a critical transmission fluid leak affecting several GM brands, and possibly defective Continental tires on GM SUVs and trucks. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning. The 186 NHTSA owner complaints, which are unverified reports, cluster in power train (64) and electrical system (59), with engine third at 40; four crashes and two injuries, no fires. The powertrain theme overlaps the transmission recall, so drivetrain history matters here.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the transmission fluid leak recall was completed, by VIN
  3. Check whether the tires are the recalled Continental set or replacements
  4. Look under the car and on the dealer's lift for transmission seepage
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
