Buying a used 2023 Chevrolet Blazer? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 42 owner complaints on file

Both recalls here are drivetrain-related: a Blazer and Traverse axle shaft that can come apart, and a faulty transmission gear that can cause a loss of power. Neither is flagged stop-driving or park-outside. With 42 NHTSA complaints, which are unverified owner reports, the count is low, but three crashes and three injuries sit within it. Complaint areas run engine (12), power train (10), service brakes (9) and forward collision avoidance (7), echoing the axle and transmission recalls.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the axle shaft recall repair was performed, by VIN
  3. Ask about any loss of power, hesitation, or transmission work
  4. On the test drive, watch for false forward collision alerts and brake feel
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
