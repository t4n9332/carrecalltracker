Buying a used 2021 Chevrolet Corvette? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 12 owner complaints on file

Only two recalls on this year: half-shafts that can fail and cost drive power, and an airbag warning light that may not illuminate. The complaint file is small — 12 unverified reports to NHTSA — but 7 of them fall under power train, the same area as the half-shaft recall, so the two may describe related trouble. Electrical accounts for 4 more. One crash and one injury were reported, no fires.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get documentation that the half-shaft recall repair was performed
  3. Confirm the airbag warning light illuminates at startup
  4. Ask about any loss of drive power or drivetrain vibration
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
