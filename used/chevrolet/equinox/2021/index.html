Buying a used 2021 Chevrolet Equinox? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 93 owner complaints on file

The recall mix shifts here: a critical one for tires that can lose tread, plus high-rated recalls for incorrect seat belt bolts and a fuel pump problem that can stall the engine, and blocked child seat anchors. No stop-driving or park-outside instruction applies. Complaint volume is much lower than the 2018-2020 years at 93 unverified NHTSA reports (6 crashes, no fires, 5 injuries), spread fairly evenly across electrical, power train, fuel and engine.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether the recalled tires were replaced, and note the brand and date codes on the car
  3. Confirm the seat belt bolt and fuel pump recall repairs on the VIN
  4. Ask if the car has ever stalled or lost power while driving
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
