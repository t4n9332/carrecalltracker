Buying a used 2023 Chevrolet Equinox? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 95 owner complaints on file

A lighter recall record than earlier years and no critical entries. The highest-rated item is a passenger airbag that may not switch off as intended; the other two are a loose driver seat bolt and blocked child seat anchors. No stop-driving or park-outside instruction applies. NHTSA holds 95 unverified owner complaints (4 crashes, no fires, 3 injuries), with fuel and propulsion the largest area at 44, then engine and service brakes.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the passenger airbag recall repair and watch the airbag status light with a passenger seated
  3. Check the driver seat for looseness and the child seat anchors for access
  4. Ask about any fuel, propulsion or drivability issues and request service records
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
