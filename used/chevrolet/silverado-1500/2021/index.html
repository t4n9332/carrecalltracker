Buying a used 2021 Chevrolet Silverado 1500? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 817 owner complaints on file

This model year carries eight recalls, none rated critical, covering overcured tires, a seat belt bracket issue, seat belts that may use wrong bolts, a driver air bag that may not deploy, an engine that may fail, and a transmission issue with wheel lockup risk; one recall for rear wheel lock-up risk is listed for diesel trucks. NHTSA has logged 817 owner complaints (unverified reports), with 20 crashes, 11 fires, and 17 injuries cited; engine and power train are the most-mentioned categories.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the engine-failure and transmission wheel-lockup recalls have been completed
  3. Ask about the driver air bag and seat belt bolt/bracket recalls
  4. If diesel-equipped, confirm the rear wheel lock-up recall was addressed
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
