Buying a used 2021 Chevrolet Silverado 1500? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 817 owner complaints on file

Two critical recalls cover rear wheels that can suddenly lock, one of them diesel-specific. Others address a driver airbag that may not deploy properly, wrong or unsecured seat belt hardware, Continental tires, and 6.2L V8 engines that may fail. Engine is also the largest complaint area, 372 of 817 unverified owner reports to NHTSA, which include 20 crashes, 11 fires and 17 injuries. The engine theme runs through both the recall and complaint records.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Find out whether it has the 6.2L V8 and whether that engine recall remedy was performed.
  3. Get VIN confirmation on both rear-wheel lock recalls and the driver airbag recall.
  4. Inspect seat belt anchor bolts and the center bracket, and confirm the spare matches the road tires.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
