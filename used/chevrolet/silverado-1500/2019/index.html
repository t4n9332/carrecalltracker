Buying a used 2019 Chevrolet Silverado 1500? Check these 10 recalls first

10 NHTSA recalls · 1,076 owner complaints on file

This year carries the heaviest list here: a critical battery cable recall that can stall the truck, plus recalls for a passenger airbag that may not inflate, seat belt fire risk, quiet loss of anti-lock brakes, electronic brake assist, a driveshaft that may separate, an unsecured center seat belt bracket, and two for potentially defective tires. NHTSA lists 1,076 owner complaints — unverified — with 27 crashes, 8 fires, 15 injuries, led by engine (364) and powertrain (322).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get a full VIN recall report and confirm the battery cable and passenger airbag repairs specifically.
  3. Check tire brand and DOT dates against the two tire recalls, and whether the spare matches the road tires.
  4. Ask about driveshaft, ABS and brake-assist work, and watch for warning lights that disappear after a restart.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
