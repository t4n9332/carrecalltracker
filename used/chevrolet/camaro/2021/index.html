Buying a used 2021 Chevrolet Camaro? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 17 owner complaints on file

Four recalls, three rated high: incorrect seat belt bolts, an axle shaft that can separate while driving, and a transmission that may lock the wheels; a steering wheel emblem recall rounds it out. No stop-driving or park-outside orders were issued. NHTSA logged 17 unverified owner complaints, with 1 crash and 3 injuries, and power train accounted for 8 of them — echoing the axle and transmission recalls. Drivetrain is clearly the theme for this year.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify the axle shaft recall was performed, not just scheduled
  3. Ask whether the seat belt bolt recall inspection was done
  4. Listen for driveline noise or clunking on a test drive
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
