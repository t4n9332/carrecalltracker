Buying a used 2020 Chevrolet Camaro? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 31 owner complaints on file

Three recalls cover this year: a critical fuel pump leak with fire risk, a transmission defect that can lock the wheels, and a steering wheel emblem that may come loose in a crash. None carried stop-driving or park-outside warnings. Owners filed 31 unverified complaints with NHTSA — 7 crashes, 4 injuries, no fires — concentrated in power train and electrical. The wheel-lock and fuel pump items are the serious ones; the emblem recall is a minor fix.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get dealer proof the fuel pump recall repair was completed
  3. Confirm the transmission recall remedy; test drive for shifting or lock-up
  4. Ask about any power train or electrical repairs in service records
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
