Buying a used 2020 Chevrolet Silverado 3500? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 28 owner complaints on file

A recall-dense year. Two critical recalls address diesel rear wheels that can suddenly lock. Alongside them are hoods that can fly open — the subject of two recalls — a seat belt fire risk, an unsecured center belt bracket, weak roof airbags and a self-unlatching tailgate. Just 28 unverified NHTSA complaints were filed, led by powertrain and service brakes, with one crash and one fire alleged. Nothing here carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm both rear wheel lock-up recalls are closed and ask about any rear axle noise or braking irregularity.
  3. Check the hood's primary and secondary latches, since two recalls cover hoods flying open at speed.
  4. Ask whether the seat belt fire risk and center bracket recalls were performed, and inspect the belts.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Chevrolet dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
