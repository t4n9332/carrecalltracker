Buying a used 2019 Honda Pilot? Check these 13 recalls first

13 NHTSA recalls · 902 owner complaints on file

This year carries the heaviest recall load of these Pilots: two critical actions — tires that may fail suddenly and engines that can seize from a faulty bearing — plus a timing belt that can lose teeth, missing frame welds, fuel pump stalling, a hood that can fly open, and rear frame rust. None carry stop-driving or park-outside warnings. Owners filed 902 unverified NHTSA complaints, concentrated in electrical system and engine, with 13 crashes and 5 fires reported.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get a VIN recall check showing the tire and engine bearing recalls were completed.
  3. Have a shop inspect the rear frame, underbody welds and hood latch.
  4. Ask whether the timing belt and fuel pump recall work was done, and about any stalling.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Honda dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
