Buying a used 2021 Honda Pilot? Check these 10 recalls first

10 NHTSA recalls · 430 owner complaints on file

This model year carries ten recalls, including high-severity items covering certain Continental tires that may fail, brake master cylinder separation, fuel pump failure, a passenger seat sensor crack, front passenger airbag unintended deployment, and rear subframe corrosion, plus medium-severity issues with the instrument panel, rearview camera lag, side mirror glass, and a separate rearview camera problem. NHTSA logged 430 owner complaints (unverified reports), with 12 crashes, 1 fire, and 8 injuries. Electrical system (126) was the top complaint category, followed by engine (104), forward collision avoidance (97), and unknown/other (82).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether the Continental tire recall applies and was addressed
  3. Confirm the brake master cylinder and fuel pump recalls were completed
  4. Check for rear subframe corrosion and camera/mirror recall repairs
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Honda dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
