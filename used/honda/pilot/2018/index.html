Buying a used 2018 Honda Pilot? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 439 owner complaints on file

Seven recalls, led by a critical one for engines that can seize from a faulty bearing, plus a timing belt that can lose teeth and a fuel pump that can stall the engine. Also covered: seats that may loosen, a hood that can fly open, a seat sensor tied to unintended airbag deployment, and rear frame rust-through. NHTSA has 439 unverified owner complaints — the most here — dominated by engine (156), electrical system and power train.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm engine bearing and timing belt recall work by VIN
  3. Request full service records for engine and transmission repairs
  4. Check seat mounting security, hood latch, and rear frame rust
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Honda dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
