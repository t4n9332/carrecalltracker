Buying a used 2022 Honda CR-V? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 254 owner complaints on file

Two high-severity recalls define this year: a fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine, and a seat sensor that may cause unintended airbag deployment. A third, lower-stakes recall corrects a door label listing wrong weight and tire information. None carry a stop-driving or park-outside warning. Of 254 unverified owner complaints filed with NHTSA (5 crashes, 1 fire, 6 injuries), steering accounts for 133 — more than half — with forward collision avoidance a distant second at 43.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Run the VIN for the fuel pump and seat sensor recall repairs
  3. Drive at highway speed feeling for steering wander, clunks or heaviness
  4. Confirm the door jamb label matches the tires and load rating actually fitted
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Honda dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
