Buying a used 2020 GMC Sierra 2500? Check these 9 recalls first

9 NHTSA recalls · 74 owner complaints on file

This is the heaviest recall year in the range: two critical actions on diesels whose rear wheels can suddenly lock, plus hoods that can fly open while driving, a seat belt fire risk, an unsecured center seat belt bracket, weak roof airbags, a spare tire that can disable anti-lock brakes, and a self-unlatching tailgate. Owners filed 74 unverified NHTSA complaints, with one crash, three fires and one injury; power train reports dominate at 22, followed by electrical and brakes.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify both rear-wheel-lock recalls on the diesel and ask about any hard braking or lockup episodes
  3. Check the hood latch and secondary latch and confirm the hood recall repair
  4. Ask whether the seat belt fire risk and center belt bracket recalls were completed, and tug-test the center belt
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any GMC dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
