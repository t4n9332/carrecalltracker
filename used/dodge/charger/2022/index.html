Buying a used 2022 Dodge Charger? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 14 owner complaints on file

The notable recall here is a high-severity one for a shifter that may slip out of Drive; two separate medium recalls cover tire pressure sensors that fail or quit early. NHTSA shows only 14 unverified owner complaints for this year, spread thinly across air bags, powertrain, fuel/propulsion and electrical at four or fewer each, with three crashes and one injury reported. The complaint volume is low compared with earlier Chargers, though the shifter recall is worth confirming.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the shifter recall remedy was applied and Drive engages firmly
  3. Ask whether the tire pressure sensors were replaced under either recall
  4. Watch for a lit tire pressure warning light on a cold start
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Dodge dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
