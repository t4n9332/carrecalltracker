Buying a used 2018 Dodge Challenger? Check these 4 recalls first

4 NHTSA recalls · 75 owner complaints on file

Three of the four recalls involve unintended vehicle motion: a transmission that may not stay in Park, cruise control that can stick on, and a performance module that can trap cruise control. A fourth covers an engine computer that may cause stalling. None require you to stop driving. Owners filed 75 NHTSA complaints, concentrated in electrical system and unclassified categories, with 9 crashes, 2 fires and 8 injuries reported. Those complaints are unverified consumer reports.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm by VIN that the Park-engagement and cruise-control recalls were completed
  3. Ask whether a performance module is fitted and whether that recall was addressed
  4. Ask the seller about any stalling episodes or recurring electrical warning lights
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Dodge dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
