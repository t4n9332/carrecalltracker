Buying a used 2023 Dodge Hornet? Check these 5 recalls first

5 NHTSA recalls · 26 owner complaints on file · 1 park-outside

The Hornet's launch year carries one critical recall — loose battery cables that can cause a fire, with NHTSA advising the vehicle be parked outside until repaired. The rest are lower severity: a missing backup warning sound, a backup camera image that may not appear, rear seat belts that may not retract, and an incorrect load-weight sticker. Of 26 unverified owner complaints, 2 involved crashes; forward collision avoidance leads at 12, then electrical at 10.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the battery cable recall was completed before parking the car in a garage
  3. Start the car and verify the backup camera image appears and the reverse warning sound works
  4. Pull and release each rear seat belt to check that it retracts fully
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Dodge dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
