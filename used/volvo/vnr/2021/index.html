Buying a used 2021 Volvo VNR? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 1 owner complaints on file

Four critical recalls stand out here: loose steering bolts, a loose or missing front axle nut, parking brakes that can apply without warning, and — on VNR Electric units — a battery coolant leak that can cause fire. High-severity items add a brake pedal that may break off and Electric models braking too hard on their own. More routine: hubcap loss and a weight label that overstates the axle rating. One unverified owner complaint is on file, with no crashes, fires, or injuries.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify the steering bolt, front axle nut, and parking brake recalls were all completed on this VIN
  3. If it is a VNR Electric, confirm the battery coolant leak and hard-braking remedies were performed
  4. Inspect the brake pedal assembly and compare the door weight label against the actual axle rating
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Volvo dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
