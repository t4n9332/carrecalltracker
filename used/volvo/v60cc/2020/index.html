Buying a used 2020 Volvo V60CC? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 4 owner complaints on file

One recall stands out: a critical-rated steering gear screw defect that can lock the steering wheel. The other two are rated medium — automatic emergency braking that may fail to stop the car, and a fuel pump fuse that can blow, an issue shared with the V90 Cross Country. None carry a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The complaint file is very thin, four unverified owner reports to NHTSA with no crashes, fires, or injuries logged.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get the steering gear screw recall confirmed complete by VIN through a Volvo dealer, not just the seller's word.
  3. Ask whether the automatic emergency braking recall update was applied and if any AEB fault messages have appeared.
  4. Ask about no-start or stalling episodes that could point to the fuel pump fuse.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Volvo dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
