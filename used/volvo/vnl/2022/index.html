Buying a used 2022 Volvo VNL? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 2 owner complaints on file

This model year carries a long recall list: five high-severity campaigns covering flywheel bolts that can loosen and detach, wheels that may bend or break, unexpected shifts into neutral, air tank bolts that can break and drop the tank, and a side airbag that may not work. Two medium recalls cover hubcap loss and a wiper motor that can quit. None carry stop-driving or park-outside instructions. Only two owner complaints are on file with NHTSA — unverified reports — with no crashes, fires, or injuries.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Get a VIN-specific recall report and confirm the flywheel bolt and air tank bolt repairs were completed
  3. Ask whether the side airbag and unintended-neutral recall remedies were performed, with dealer paperwork
  4. Inspect all wheels for bends or cracks, confirm hubcaps are present, and test the wipers
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Volvo dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
