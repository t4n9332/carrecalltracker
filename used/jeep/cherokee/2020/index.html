Buying a used 2020 Jeep Cherokee? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 292 owner complaints on file

A much shorter recall list than 2019 — the steering gear that may break, the power transfer unit rollaway defect, and a minor recall for a backup camera image that stays on screen — but the complaint pattern has a similar shape. NHTSA received 292 unverified owner complaints, including nine crashes, five fires and nine injuries. More than half concern the power train, with engine, electrical and unspecified issues well behind it.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm by VIN that the steering gear and power transfer unit recalls were completed.
  3. Ask directly about power train work: transfer case, driveshaft or transmission replacement, and any rollaway incident.
  4. On the test drive, watch for steering noise or play, and check the camera image clears when you shift out of reverse.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Jeep dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
