Buying a used 2023 Jeep Grand Cherokee L? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 91 owner complaints on file

Two of this year's critical recalls cover the same problem — rear coil springs that can fall off — and suspension is also the largest owner-complaint category, 36 of 91 unverified NHTSA reports. Two more recalls involve side airbags that may not deploy or may deploy too late. The remainder are less severe: a blower motor that can disable the defroster, and second-row headrests that may not lock. Electrical is the next complaint area at 23. No recall here carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm both rear coil spring recalls were completed by VIN
  3. Verify the side airbag recall repairs (non-deployment and late deployment) are done
  4. Test the defroster and lock both second-row headrests
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Jeep dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
