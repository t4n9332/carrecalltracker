Buying a used 2020 Jeep Gladiator? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 483 owner complaints on file

This model year carries multiple recalls, including two critical notices for a clutch that can overheat and possibly fracture, plus a rear driveshaft that may lack grease, front seat belts that may not lock, a backup camera image that may stay on screen, and an instrument panel cluster that may fail. NHTSA has logged 483 owner complaints (unverified reports) for this year, including 11 crashes, 7 fires, and 6 injuries. Electrical system (183) and steering (181) are the leading complaint categories, followed by power train (54) and engine (45).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm both clutch overheat/fracture recalls were completed
  3. Verify the seat belt and rear driveshaft grease recalls were addressed
  4. Have the steering and electrical systems inspected
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Jeep dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
