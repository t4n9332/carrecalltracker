Buying a used 2020 Jeep Gladiator? Check these 7 recalls first

7 NHTSA recalls · 483 owner complaints on file

The 2020 Gladiator carries the longest recall list of the run: three critical actions covering manual-transmission clutch overheating and fire, plus high-severity recalls for a rear driveshaft that can break and fall off and front seat belts that may not restrain. A dash cluster short-out and a lingering backup camera image are lower-rated. NHTSA logged 483 unverified owner complaints — 11 crashes, seven fires, six injuries — dominated almost evenly by electrical system (183) and steering (181).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether it is a manual — the three clutch recalls apply to manual gearboxes.
  3. Get VIN recall status for the rear driveshaft and front seat belt repairs.
  4. Test-drive for steering wander and check the cluster and backup camera from cold start.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Jeep dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
