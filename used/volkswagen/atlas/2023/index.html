Buying a used 2023 Volkswagen Atlas? Check these 5 recalls first

5 NHTSA recalls · 130 owner complaints on file

A lighter recall list than surrounding years: five campaigns, one critical, for engine bearings that can fail (shared with the Audi Q5). The rest are high-severity but narrower — brake lines that may leak fluid, a side airbag deploying too late, a passenger airbag switching off wrongly, and loose bolts shared with the ID.4. Owners filed 130 unverified NHTSA complaints, led by engine (35) and electrical (32), with air bags at 22; 4 crashes, no fires and 6 injuries were reported.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify the engine bearing recall status by VIN and ask about any engine noise history
  3. Have the brake lines inspected for leaks and check brake fluid level and condition
  4. Confirm the airbag recalls were completed and the passenger airbag light behaves normally
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Volkswagen dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
