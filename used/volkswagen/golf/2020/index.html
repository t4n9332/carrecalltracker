Buying a used 2020 Volkswagen Golf? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 6 owner complaints on file

Only two recalls are listed for this year: a critical fuel leak risk shared with the Jetta and Audi A3, and a low-severity issue where the tire-pressure warning may be delayed. Neither carried a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. The complaint record is thin — six unverified owner reports to NHTSA with no crashes, fires or injuries, scattered across electrical, fuel and vehicle speed control. Compared with 2018 and 2019 Golfs, the recall list here is short.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for documentation that the fuel leak recall was performed on this VIN.
  3. Check for fuel odor at the tank and lines during a cold start and after the test drive.
  4. Confirm the tire-pressure warning illuminates at key-on and responds when a tire is low.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Volkswagen dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
