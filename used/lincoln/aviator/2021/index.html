Buying a used 2021 Lincoln Aviator? Check these 28 recalls first

28 NHTSA recalls · 39 owner complaints on file

This model year has three critical recalls for wiring fire risk, battery fire risk, and hybrid battery short-circuit/fire risk, along with high-severity recalls covering a steering knuckle fracture risk, motor mount bolts, side airbag weld defects, rearview camera failure, wrong suspension parts, engine valve failure, and seat belt bolts. NHTSA logged 39 owner complaints (unverified), including 1 crash, 1 fire, and 2 injuries, with electrical system (12) and engine (6) as the top categories.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the three fire-risk recalls (wiring, battery, hybrid battery) were completed
  3. Ask about the steering knuckle recall completion status
  4. Ask the seller about any engine or electrical issues experienced
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Lincoln dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
