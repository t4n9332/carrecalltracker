Buying a used 2020 Lincoln Aviator? Check these 30 recalls first

30 NHTSA recalls · 108 owner complaints on file

This model year carries five critical recalls covering fuel line leaks, driveshaft cracking with fire risk, wiring fire risk, battery fire risk, and hybrid battery short-circuit/fire risk, plus five high-severity recalls for seat recliners, airbag bolts, motor mount bolts, contaminated washer fluid, and rear suspension bolts. NHTSA has logged 108 owner complaints (unverified), including 2 crashes and 1 fire, with electrical system (39), unknown/other (24), and power train (22) as the top categories.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify all five critical recalls (fuel line, driveshaft, wiring, battery, hybrid battery) are completed
  3. Ask about any electrical system problems the seller has experienced
  4. Check that motor mount and rear suspension bolt recalls were done
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Lincoln dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
