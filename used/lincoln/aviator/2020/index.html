Buying a used 2020 Lincoln Aviator? Check these 30 recalls first

30 NHTSA recalls · 108 owner complaints on file

This year carries a long recall list — ten filings, five rated critical, covering fuel leaks, a drive shaft that can break, rear suspension bolts, and plug-in hybrid battery failure or fire. Others address rollaway risk from a missing cover, weak seat backs, loose side airbag bolts, power loss, and wiring that can rub an A/C pulley. None is flagged stop-driving or park-outside. 108 unverified owner complaints to NHTSA cluster in electrical system, power train, and back-over prevention, with two crashes and one fire.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Run the VIN and get repair orders for each of the five critical recalls, especially fuel leak, drive shaft, and rear suspension bolts
  3. If it is the plug-in hybrid, confirm the high-voltage battery recall work was completed and ask about any charging or battery faults
  4. Test every electrical function and the backup camera, the two heaviest owner-complaint areas, and check seat backs for looseness
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Lincoln dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
