Buying a used 2022 Hyundai Ioniq 5? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 404 owner complaints on file

All three recalls involve drivetrain electronics rather than crash protection: the car may roll away after parking, and two separate defects can cut drive power, one tied to the charging unit. None carry stop-driving or park-outside warnings. Complaint volume is high — 404 unverified reports to NHTSA (7 crashes, 1 fire, 1 injury) — and 304 fall under electrical system, with propulsion and power train next, echoing the power-loss recalls.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. VIN-check the rollaway recall and both power-loss recalls
  3. Ask whether the charging unit was replaced and get the invoice
  4. Fast-charge once on the test drive and watch for power-loss warnings
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Hyundai dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
