Buying a used 2018 Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 13 owner complaints on file

Both recalls on this year are critical and describe the same hazard from different angles: a power relay that can overheat and catch fire, one specifically citing heat at the rear seat. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside advisory. Owner reporting is thin — 13 unverified complaints filed with NHTSA, with 2 crashes and 2 injuries — spread evenly across engine, power train, electrical and unspecified, so the complaint data says little on its own.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. VIN-check both relay recalls; an older car may still have one open
  3. Ask for documentation of the relay replacement, not just a dealer visit
  4. Inspect the rear seat area for heat damage, odor or discoloration
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Hyundai dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
