Buying a used 2022 Hyundai Kona N? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 7 owner complaints on file

Both recalls here are drivetrain-related rather than fire- or restraint-related: one for a sudden, complete loss of drive power, and one for a fuel pump that can fail on Hyundai and Genesis performance models. Neither carries a stop-driving or park-outside warning. The NHTSA complaint file is small — 7 unverified reports with no crashes, fires or injuries — spread across electrical system, engine and power train, so it offers little signal either way.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the fuel pump recall was completed, not just scheduled
  3. Ask the seller about any sudden power loss or no-start events
  4. Check for open recall work on the drive-power recall by VIN
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Hyundai dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
