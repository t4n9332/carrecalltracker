Buying a used 2022 Porsche Taycan? Check these 12 recalls first

12 NHTSA recalls · 79 owner complaints on file

Battery fire risk still dominates — six critical campaigns covering high-voltage battery short circuits and the overheating portable charging cable — but the mechanical items narrow to cracking front brake hoses and a passenger airbag that may switch itself off, alongside blank screen/camera and false brake-pad-warning fixes. None carried a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. NHTSA lists 79 unverified owner complaints, 36 in electrical system and 18 in power train, with 2 crashes, 2 fires and 1 injury.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify every battery-related recall shows completed on the VIN report
  3. Check the passenger airbag status light with someone seated up front
  4. Look for cracking or seepage at the front brake hoses
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
