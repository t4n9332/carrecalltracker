Buying a used 2020 Porsche Taycan? Check these 12 recalls first

12 NHTSA recalls · 91 owner complaints on file

First model year, and the record shows it. Alongside several battery short-circuit fire recalls are a brake pedal that may fail, software that can shut down power while driving, loose rear suspension control arm bolts, seat wiring that can disable airbags, cracking front brake hoses, and screen, camera and child-anchor fixes. NHTSA holds 91 owner complaints — unverified — with 53 in electrical system and 22 in engine, including one crash and one reported fire.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Request a full VIN recall history; many 2020 campaigns are software-based
  3. Ask whether the car ever lost power or shut down while driving
  4. Inspect front brake hoses and confirm the seat wiring/airbag recall was done
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
