Buying a used 2021 Porsche Taycan? Check these 21 recalls first

21 NHTSA recalls · 69 owner complaints on file

This model year has 10 recalls, six of them critical: two separate battery fire-risk campaigns, a portable charging cable/outlet overheating issue, a high-voltage battery short-circuit risk, and two more battery short-circuit fire-risk recalls. High-severity recalls also cover suspension bolts that may loosen, a front suspension arm defect shared with the Panamera, a software fault that can cut power, and seat belts that may not lock child car seats properly. NHTSA lists 69 unverified owner complaints (4 crashes, 2 fires, 2 injuries), led by electrical system (37) and engine (16).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether all battery-related recalls (fire, short-circuit, charging cable) are completed
  3. Inspect suspension bolts and the front suspension arm for looseness
  4. Test child seat belt locking function
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
