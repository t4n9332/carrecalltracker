Buying a used 2019 Porsche Cayenne? Check these 6 recalls first

6 NHTSA recalls · 42 owner complaints on file

Recall activity here centers on occupant safety and electronics rather than the drivetrain: a rear seat belt buckle that may break in a crash, plus repeated actions covering a missing worn-brake-pad warning and a backup camera that is slow or goes blank. One loose-front-shock recall applied to a single Turbo, so it is unlikely to affect a given car. NHTSA logged 42 unverified owner complaints, one crash, no fires or injuries, clustered in air bags and electrical system.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for VIN proof the rear seat belt buckle recall was completed, and tug-test each rear buckle.
  3. Start the car and confirm the backup camera image appears promptly and does not go blank.
  4. Have brake pad thickness measured, since the worn-pad warning may not appear.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
