Buying a used 2023 Porsche 911 GT3? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 3 owner complaints on file

The 2023 GT3 has a lighter record than the prior year: two recalls, neither with a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. One covers cars with bucket seats, where the seat airbag may deploy unnecessarily; the other is a low-severity Touring issue where the brake lights flash together. NHTSA lists three owner complaints for this model year, unverified reports spread across electrical, engine and fuel-system areas, with no crashes, fires or injuries recorded.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Check seat configuration, since the seat-airbag recall applies to bucket-seat cars.
  3. If it is a Touring, ask whether the brake light recall work was done.
  4. Watch for electrical glitches and warning lights during the test drive.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
