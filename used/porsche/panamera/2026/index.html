Buying a used 2026 Porsche Panamera? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 1 owner complaints on file

As a brand-new model year, the file is small but includes two structural items: the fuel pump leak recall shared with the Cayenne, and a front suspension that can come apart, which also covers the Taycan. Neither was issued with a stop-driving or park-outside instruction. A single unverified owner complaint appears in NHTSA's database, filed under air bags and noting an injury. Low complaint counts on a new car reflect limited miles rather than a proven record.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify with a dealer that both open recalls have been performed on this VIN
  3. Ask whether the front suspension recall parts were replaced or only inspected
  4. Confirm no airbag or restraint warning lights appear at startup
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
