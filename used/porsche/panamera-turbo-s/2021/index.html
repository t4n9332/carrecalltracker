Buying a used 2021 Porsche Panamera Turbo S? Check these 3 recalls first

3 NHTSA recalls · 2 owner complaints on file

Two critical recalls define this year: a front suspension arm that can break, also affecting the Taycan, and a coolant pump that can short out and catch fire. Porsche attached no stop-driving or park-outside instruction to either. A medium recall addresses seat belts that may not secure child seats properly. Only two unverified owner complaints reached NHTSA for 2021 — one power train, one unspecified — with no crashes, fires or injuries reported.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Verify both critical recall repairs are recorded against the VIN
  3. Have the front suspension arms inspected during a pre-purchase check
  4. If carrying children, confirm the seat belt child-seat recall remedy was applied
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Porsche dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
