Buying a used 2018 RAM 2500? Check these 10 recalls first

10 NHTSA recalls · 2,570 owner complaints on file

Two critical recalls target the same area — a steering link that can come apart, and a related break on trucks fitted with an AEV lift kit — alongside side airbags that can burst apart. Anti-lock brakes and stability control can also fail. What stands out most is complaint volume: 2,570 unverified owner reports, with service brakes cited 2,333 times, far ahead of electrical and hydraulic. Thirty-seven crashes and 12 injuries were reported among them.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask about brake repair history and test hard stops — service brakes dominate this year's owner complaints.
  3. Verify the steering link recall was performed, and ask whether an AEV lift kit was ever installed.
  4. Confirm the ABS/stability control recall is closed and that no warning lights stay lit.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any RAM dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
