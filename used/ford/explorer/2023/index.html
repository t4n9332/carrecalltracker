Buying a used 2023 Ford Explorer? Check these 15 recalls first

15 NHTSA recalls · 108 owner complaints on file

Recalls for this model year include two critical fire-risk items (turbo oil leak, block heater) alongside high-severity recalls for a park-system defect, a loose strut bolt, second-row seats that may move or shift, a fuel pump that may stall the engine, and seat belt anchor bolts that may loosen; two camera-display recalls are rated medium. NHTSA lists 107 owner complaints (unverified reports), with 7 crashes, 2 fires, and 9 injuries described; top categories are unknown/other (24), suspension (13), electrical system (13), and steering (12).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm the turbo oil leak and block heater fire recalls were completed
  3. Verify the second-row seat and seat belt anchor bolt recalls were addressed
  4. Ask about strut bolt and fuel pump recall repair records
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Ford dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
