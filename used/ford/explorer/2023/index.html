Buying a used 2023 Ford Explorer? Check these 15 recalls first

15 NHTSA recalls · 107 owner complaints on file

The 2023 Explorer's standout is the backup camera: four separate recalls address an image that blanks, vanishes, or shows a blue screen, and one exists because an earlier remedy needed redoing. More serious items include rolling away from Park, a turbo oil leak that may cause an engine fire, block heater fires, a loose or missing front suspension bolt, loose seat belt anchor bolts, and fuel pump failure that can stall the engine. Owners filed 107 unverified NHTSA complaints.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Shift into reverse several times and confirm the camera image appears every time.
  3. Ask specifically whether the redone backup camera remedy was applied, not just the first one.
  4. Have the seat belt anchor bolts and front suspension bolt checked for presence and torque.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Ford dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
