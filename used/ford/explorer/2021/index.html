Buying a used 2021 Ford Explorer? Check these 26 recalls first

26 NHTSA recalls · 419 owner complaints on file

This model year has three critical recalls, including battery and block heater fire risks and an engine fire risk tied to the Police Interceptor variant. High-severity recalls cover loose motor mount bolts, a side airbag weld defect, a rearview camera that may fail, and a transmission park system that may fail after a reset. NHTSA has logged 419 unverified owner complaints, including 5 fire reports, 14 crash reports, and 16 injury reports, with power train (118) and unknown-category (84) issues most cited, followed by engine (47) and electrical system (42).

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask whether the battery and block heater fire-risk recalls were completed
  3. Verify the side airbag weld defect and motor mount bolt recalls were repaired
  4. Confirm the transmission park system recall (fail-after-reset) was addressed
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Ford dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
