Buying a used 2020 Ford Mustang? Check these 8 recalls first

8 NHTSA recalls · 119 owner complaints on file

Two braking recalls headline this year: a brake pedal bracket that can break during hard stops and a low brake fluid warning light that may not work. The remaining six are camera, display and park-warning items, including a misaligned forward camera that can disable driver aids. No stop-driving or park-outside instructions. NHTSA logs only 119 owner complaints, unverified, but they carry 13 crashes, five fires and eight injuries — a heavier outcome count than the volume suggests.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Confirm both brake recalls, pedal bracket and fluid warning light, are closed on this VIN
  3. Check brake fluid level and that the warning lamp illuminates at startup
  4. Test the forward-facing driver aids and the backup camera
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Ford dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
