Buying a used 2020 Ford Explorer Police Intercept? Check these 2 recalls first

2 NHTSA recalls · 1 owner complaints on file

This is a police-duty Explorer, so its service history matters as much as the recall list, which is short: a rear axle bolt that can break and allow a rollaway, plus contaminated windshield washer fluid. Only one unverified NHTSA complaint is on file, under power train — a very small sample rather than evidence of reliability. Fleet vehicles typically accumulate idling hours and hard-use wear that an odometer reading alone will not reveal.

Before you buy

  1. Get the 17-character VIN from the seller and run it at nhtsa.gov/recalls. It shows which recalls are still unrepaired on that exact car.
  2. Ask for the fleet's maintenance records and engine idle hours, not just mileage.
  3. Verify the rear axle bolt recall was performed and inspect the rear axle area.
  4. Ask whether the washer fluid was drained and replaced under that recall.
  5. Open recalls are repaired free at any Ford dealer, whoever owns the car — an open recall is a to-do item, not a bill.
