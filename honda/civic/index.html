Honda Civic recalls

11 recalls covering model years 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022. To check whether your vehicle is affected, look up your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — a recall may apply only to some vehicles of a given year.

Honda and Acura seat sensor may cause unintended airbag deployment high

Reported 2026-05-21 · Campaign 26V332000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022

A sensor under the front passenger seat measures the weight of whoever is sitting there so the car knows how to use the airbags. That sensor can crack and short out. If it does, the airbags can go off in an unintended way during a crash, which can hurt the people inside. This recall covers a long list of Honda and Acura models and expands an earlier recall. Dealers will put in new seat weight sensors.

Honda and Acura steering gearbox may make steering difficult high

Reported 2024-10-03 · Campaign 24V744000 · Model years: 2022

The steering gearbox — the part that turns your steering wheel motion into the wheels turning — may have been built wrong on these Honda and Acura models. Parts inside can rub too hard against each other, which can make the vehicle difficult to steer. Difficulty steering raises the chance of a crash. The dealer will replace a spring inside the gear and add or move grease around as needed, all free of charge.

Honda and Acura passenger seat sensor may crack, airbag risk high

Reported 2024-02-01 · Campaign 24V064000 · Model years: 2020, 2021, 2022

There is a sensor in the front passenger seat that senses the weight of whoever is sitting there. It tells the vehicle whether to turn the passenger airbag off — for example when the seat is empty or a small person is in it. On these vehicles the sensor can crack and short out, so the airbag may not switch off when it should. It could then go off in a crash when it was supposed to stay shut. Dealers replace the sensor free.

Honda and Acura fuel pump can fail, stalling engine high

Reported 2023-12-18 · Campaign 23V858000 · Model years: 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021

The fuel pump sits inside the gas tank and sends fuel to the engine. On these vehicles that pump can fail. When it does, the engine can stall while you are driving — losing power in traffic or on the highway raises the chance of a crash. A dealer will replace the entire fuel pump unit for free. This recall is an expansion of two earlier Honda fuel pump recalls covering additional vehicles.

Honda Civic replacement steering rack may damage a tire high

Reported 2023-10-19 · Campaign 23V704000 · Model years: 2022

Some 2022-2024 Civic 4-door and 5-door cars got a new power steering rack during a service visit, and that part may have been put together wrong. When it is, a front tire can rub against the lower suspension or the tie rod end. That constant rubbing wears into the tire and can damage it. Only cars that actually received a replacement rack are affected. A Honda dealer will inspect the electric power steering rack and replace it if needed, free.

Honda Civic, Pilot and Acura MDX Brake Failure Risk critical

Reported 2023-06-29 · Campaign 23V458000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

A fastener that connects the brake pedal booster to the brake master cylinder may have been put together wrong at the factory. If it fails, the master cylinder can separate from the booster and you could lose the ability to stop the vehicle. A dealer will inspect the brake booster assembly and repair it if needed at no cost. Honda mailed owner letters on August 7, 2023, so the repair is available now.

Honda and Acura fuel pump can fail and stall engine high

Reported 2021-03-25 · Campaign 21V215000 · Model years: 2019, 2020

The pump that pushes gasoline out of the fuel tank toward the engine sits inside the tank, and on these Honda and Acura vehicles it can stop working. When it fails, the engine can stall, meaning it shuts off while you are driving, and that can happen at any speed. Your dealer will replace the whole fuel pump assembly at no cost to you. This recall is a larger version of an earlier Honda recall, 20V-314.

Honda and Acura fuel pump failure can stall the engine high

Reported 2020-05-28 · Campaign 20V314000 · Model years: 2018, 2019

The low-pressure fuel pump sits inside the gas tank and pushes fuel toward the engine. On these Honda and Acura vehicles, that pump can quit working. If it does, the engine can stall — shut off — while you are driving, which can leave you stopped in traffic or unable to keep up with the cars around you. Dealers replace the entire fuel pump assembly at no charge, and this repair has been available since July 2020.

Honda Civic Hatchback: owner's guide missing child-seat info low

Reported 2018-11-16 · Campaign 18V817000 · Model years: 2018

Certain 2017-2018 Honda Civic Hatchback and Civic Type R vehicles either did not come with an owner's guide, or the guide they came with did not include all the required information about the vehicle's child car seat anchors. Because of that missing information, these vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule covering child restraint anchorage systems. Honda dealers will give you a replacement owner's information kit with the correct information.

Honda Civic and CR-V power steering can turn wrong way high

Reported 2018-09-27 · Campaign 18V663000 · Model years: 2018

In these Civics and CR-Vs, a small magnet inside the power steering system may not be held in place properly and can come loose. If it does, and you turn the wheel all the way to one side, the power steering can push in the opposite direction from the way you are steering. The car suddenly fights your steering input instead of helping it. Dealers will replace the whole steering gearbox assembly at no cost to you.

Honda Civic certification label printed with garbled characters low

Reported 2018-06-20 · Campaign 18V421000 · Model years: 2018

On some 2018 Honda Civics, the label that lists the vehicle's build date was printed with random, jumbled characters. Nothing on the car itself is broken. The trouble is that you or a dealer may not be able to tell whether your Civic is included in other safety recalls, which could mean missing a repair you need. Honda dealers will put on a correct label free of charge. This recall began in August 2018.
