Dodge Challenger recalls

Dodge Challenger left rear seat back may not lock medium

Reported Aug 1, 2024 · Campaign 24V573000 · Model years: 2023

On some 2023 Dodge Challenger cars, the left rear seat back may not lock in its upright position. That means it may not stay firmly in place the way the seat is designed to. Because of this, these cars do not meet the federal safety rule covering seating systems. Dealers will check the latch piece that the seat back catches on and adjust it if needed. Chrysler mailed letters to owners in August 2024.

Chrysler 300 and Dodge Parking Brake Roll-Away Risk high

Reported Feb 15, 2024 · Campaign 24V112000 · Model years: 2023

The parking brake on some Chrysler 300, Dodge Charger, and Dodge Challenger vehicles may not be adjusted correctly. If the parking brake isn't holding properly, the vehicle could roll away on its own. This roll-away risk increases the chance of a crash or injury.

Chrysler 300, Dodge Challenger, Charger Tire Sensor Failure medium

Reported Oct 27, 2022 · Campaign 22V808000 · Model years: 2022

The battery in the tire pressure monitoring sensors may fail early, causing the sensor to stop working. If a warning light is already on because of a failed sensor, it can hide a real low tire pressure problem.

Chrysler 300, Challenger, Charger TPMS Sensor Failure medium

Reported Jul 14, 2022 · Campaign 22V504000 · Model years: 2022

On certain 2022 Dodge Challenger, Dodge Charger, and Chrysler 300 vehicles, the battery inside a tire pressure sensor may fail earlier than it should, which can make the sensor stop working. When a sensor stops working this way, it will not warn the driver if a tire's air pressure gets low. If the tire pressure warning light is already on because of a failed sensor battery, it can also hide a real, separate tire pressure problem from the driver.

Dodge Challenger Dash Warning Lights Not Visible medium

Reported Aug 26, 2021 · Campaign 21V664000 · Model years: 2021

FCA is recalling certain 2021 Dodge Challenger vehicles because the safety system status indicators in the instrument cluster don't light up brightly enough to be seen in daylight or at night. This means drivers may not get information about the vehicle's safety systems when they need it. Dealers will reprogram the instrument cluster with corrected software to fix the issue.

Chrysler 300, Challenger, Charger windshields may detach in crash high

Reported Jul 8, 2021 · Campaign 21V516000 · Model years: 2020, 2021

During manufacturing, the windshield may not have been properly bonded, or glued, to the vehicle. If the vehicle is involved in a crash, a windshield that isn't properly bonded could come loose and separate from the vehicle. A windshield that detaches during a crash may not provide the protection it's designed to give the occupants inside. This increases the risk of injury to those inside the vehicle.

Jeep, Ram and Dodge backup camera image stays on screen medium

Reported Sep 10, 2020 · Campaign 20V191000 · Model years: 2019

Chrysler is recalling certain Jeep, Ram, Dodge and Chrysler vehicles with 8.4-inch or 12-inch radio screens. A software error can leave the backup camera picture on the screen after the driver has shifted out of reverse. Because of this, the vehicles do not meet the federal safety rule covering rear visibility. Dealers will update the radio display software. Owners can also choose to have the software updated remotely, over the air, instead of visiting a dealer.

Dodge Challenger and Charger Front Tire Damage high

Reported Oct 24, 2019 · Campaign 19V758000 · Model years: 2019

Certain Dodge Challenger and Charger vehicles were built with a front wheel and brake package that isn't compatible with the vehicle. This mismatch can allow the front tire to rub against a steering part called the steering knuckle, and that contact can damage the tire.

Chrysler 300, Challenger, Charger Cluster Warning Failure medium

Reported Mar 14, 2019 · Campaign 19V203000 · Model years: 2019

On certain 2019 Dodge Challenger, Dodge Charger, and Chrysler 300 vehicles, the instrument cluster may not properly light up warnings that are meant to alert the driver. These are the same warning lights and messages that tell a driver about problems with the vehicle's systems. If the cluster fails to illuminate them correctly, the driver may not receive that information as intended.

Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep and Ram Vehicles May Stall high

Reported Aug 9, 2018 · Campaign 18V524000 · Model years: 2018

A voltage regulator chip on the circuit board inside the powertrain control module may fail. If this chip fails, the vehicle may stall while it is being driven, or it may fail to start at all. To correct this problem, Chrysler dealers will replace the entire powertrain control module with a new one so the vehicle does not experience this stalling or no-start condition again.

Dodge Challenger Cruise Control May Not Shut Off high

Reported Jul 12, 2018 · Campaign 18E053000 · Model years: 2018

Certain engine control modules sold as part of performance upgrade kits have a defect that could stop the cruise control from turning off. If a short circuit happens in the vehicle's wiring while cruise control is on, the driver may not be able to turn it off by pressing the brake pedal or using the switch, so the vehicle could keep going the same speed or speed up on its own.

Dodge, Jeep, Ram and Chrysler cruise control may not cancel high

Reported May 17, 2018 · Campaign 18V332000 · Model years: 2018

Chrysler is recalling certain Dodge, Jeep, Ram and Chrysler vehicles from several model years because the cruise control may not shut off. If a wire inside the vehicle shorts out while cruise control is in use, the driver may not be able to cancel it by pressing the brake pedal or by switching the system off. The vehicle could keep going at its set speed or even speed up. Dealers will inspect and reprogram the engine or powertrain computer.

Multiple Chrysler Group Models Have Wrong Park Lock Rod high

Reported May 1, 2018 · Campaign 18V280000 · Model years: 2018

On certain 2018 Dodge Charger, Durango and Challenger, Jeep Grand Cherokee and Wrangler, Chrysler 300, and Ram 1500 vehicles, the wrong part may have been installed in the transmission that locks it into park. Because of this, the transmission may fail to shift into 'park' and hold the vehicle in place, meaning the vehicle could move on its own when the driver thinks it is safely parked.

Dodge Challenger recall questions

How many recalls does the Dodge Challenger have?

NHTSA lists 13 safety recalls covering the 2018–2023 Dodge Challenger. 7 of them are rated high or critical on this site. The most recent is "Dodge Challenger left rear seat back may not lock" (NHTSA campaign 24V573000), reported Aug 1, 2024.

Which Dodge Challenger model year has the most recalls?

The 2018 Dodge Challenger has the most, with 4 recalls. The 2020 model year has the fewest of the years we cover, with 1. Newer model years have had less time to collect recalls, so a low count on a recent year is not a guarantee.

Is there a do-not-drive or park-outside recall on the Dodge Challenger?

No. None of the 13 Dodge Challenger recalls on file carries a do-not-drive or park-outside advisory from NHTSA.

How do I check whether my Dodge Challenger has an open recall?

Enter your 17-character VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. A recall often applies to only some vehicles of a model year, and the VIN lookup shows the ones still unrepaired on your car. Recall repairs are free at any franchised Dodge dealer, including for second owners.
